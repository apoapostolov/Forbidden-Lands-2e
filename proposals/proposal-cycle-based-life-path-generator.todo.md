This TODO breaks the proposal into eleven deliberate passes.
The goal is not merely to make the system bigger.
The goal is to make it publishable:

- mechanically complete

Each pass should end in a document state that is reviewable on its own.
Do not rush forward while an earlier pass is still soft.
Each pass should also end with two editorial closure steps:

- a professional text polish of the active chapter-scale draft or proposal section
- movement of stabilized design notes and design goals into [chapter-design-goals-reference.md](/mnt/c/git-public/Forbidden-Lands-2e/proposals/chapter-design-goals-reference.md)

## Writing Style Notes

These notes come from reading Chapters 1, 2, and 3 of the manuscript.
They are not optional preferences.
They are the working standard for whether the life-generator replacement sounds professional enough to belong in this book.

### What The Manuscript Actually Does

- It opens with direct statements, not design framing.
- It alternates short forceful sentences with medium explanatory ones.
- It keeps one clear job per paragraph.
- It uses examples, tables, and bullets to clarify procedure rather than to decorate the page.
- It grounds rules in concrete play situations.
- It lets setting texture enter through physical nouns, social pressures, and practical consequences.
- It speaks with confidence and does not apologize for the rules.

### Paragraph Standards

- A paragraph should usually do one thing: define, explain, warn, distinguish, or transition.
- Most rules paragraphs are short to medium and do not wander.
- When a section needs procedure, it should move quickly into bullets, steps, or a table.
- Flavor paragraphs in Chapters 1 and 2 are still controlled. Even when they are atmospheric, they remain legible and pointed.

### Sentence Standards

- Prefer short and medium sentences over sprawling analysis-heavy ones.
- Land sentences on concrete nouns, verbs, or consequences.
- Use direct verbs: `choose`, `roll`, `gain`, `suffer`, `begin`, `must`, `can`.
- Avoid padded openings like `the aim is to`, `this proposal seeks to`, `the system should explicitly`.
- Avoid repeating the same explanatory cadence over and over.

### Rules-Prose Standards

- State the rule plainly before explaining why it matters.
- Put procedure before commentary.
- Put exceptions after the main rule, not before it.
- If a table can carry the load, let the table carry it.
- Examples in Chapter 3 are concrete and specific. They show play, they do not summarize intention.

### Tone Standards

- Harsh, clear, practical, and confident.
- No blog voice.
- No workshop voice.
- No design-journal filler.
- No abstract system-talk where the manuscript would use action, danger, gear, weather, bodies, silver, or travel.

### What To Cut

- `The aim is`
- `the cleanest way is`
- `this creates the right shape`
- `the system should`
- `for proposal purposes`
- `if adopted`
- stacked explanation that tells the reader what the text is doing instead of just doing it

These phrases are acceptable in rough design notes, but not in polished chapter-facing prose.

### What To Prefer Instead

- direct rule statements
- concrete outcomes
- pressure stated through play consequences
- local setting detail
- examples with named characters, places, or immediate hazards

### Structural Guidance For This Proposal

- The early proposal sections still read too much like design briefing and not enough like chapter-grade rules writing.
- Pass 8 should not be the first time the prose starts sounding native to the book.
- Every pass from here on should polish toward manuscript texture, not merely mechanical completeness.
- When in doubt, compare against Chapter 2 setup prose and Chapter 3 rules explanation prose, not against generic TTRPG proposal habits.

## Pass 11: Optional Oaths And Life Commitments

**Goal:** Consider an optional pre-mustering-out layer of personal obligation for location-based or goal-driven play.

If adopted, this phase would slot into the life-generator flow **before Mustering Out**.
It is optional by design.
The default system must still support adventurers who leave home with no active oath beyond survival, ambition, and opportunity.

**Purpose**

- give a character an active oath, burden, duty, or life-task that still pulls at them
- support campaigns centered on one settlement, valley, kin-group, shrine, hall, or feud
- give players a clean hook for short-term or long-term personal commitment without forcing every party into the same mode of play

**Deliverables**

- [ ] Define the optional oath or life-commitment procedure
- [ ] Determine whether the obligation is rolled, chosen, or chosen from rolled options
- [ ] Separate short-term obligations from long-term vows
- [ ] Define acceptable obligation sources: kin duty, sworn friendship, blood debt, promised support, revenge, pilgrimage, guardianship, inheritance, unfinished work
- [ ] Define how an oath creates pressure without chaining the whole party to one settlement against its will
- [ ] Determine whether fulfilling, breaking, delaying, or twisting an oath changes Standing, contacts, or advancement hooks
- [ ] Professionally polish the stabilized Pass 11 chapter text
- [ ] Move the stabilized design goals from Pass 11 into the chapter design-goals reference

**Design Guardrails**

- [ ] The oath system must remain optional
- [ ] It must support both individual and party-level commitments
- [ ] It must not punish roaming sandbox play by default
- [ ] It must create motive and pressure, not a mandatory quest log
- [ ] It must stay grounded in Ravenland obligations, not modern character-arc language

**Done When**

- [ ] The optional oath layer can be added before Mustering Out without disturbing the rest of the subsystem

## Execution Order

Use the passes in order.
Do not collapse them into one drafting sprint.

The safe sequence is:

1. spine
2. childhood foundations
3. path coverage
4. fallback logic
5. numerical calibration
6. synergy audit
7. lore grounding
8. final prose

10. publishability pass
11. optional oaths and life commitments

## Working Rule

If a later pass reveals that an earlier design choice was wrong, go back and correct the earlier pass rather than layering exceptions on top.
This system should grow by sharpening, not by accretion.
