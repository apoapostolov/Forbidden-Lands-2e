# Settlement Play and Journeys Compatibility Analysis

## Scope

This analysis checks the Chapter 10 settlement-play procedures against the Book 01 Journeys chapter, especially the village-facing rules for Hospitality, First Impression, gates, local Reputation, rumors, settlement visits, quarter-day activities, and crime.

## Current Book 01 Mechanics

Book 01 already gives the player-facing side of settlement play:

- Travel is hex-based. The map is open and players can leave a settlement in any direction.
- Roads modify travel speed, but do not replace the hex map.
- Hospitality and First Impression resolve how unknown travelers are received.
- Gates, palisades, night arrival, sponsorship, and presentation affect entry.
- Local Reputation, Standing, stronghold reputation, circle reputation, and rumors decide who recognizes the fellowship.
- Settlement visits use quarter-day activities: ask around, seek work, petition, trade, repair, carouse, heal, rest, and train.
- Crime and judgment already cover public offense, hue and cry, judgment, sentencing, outlawry, and flight.

Chapter 10 should therefore support the GM behind the screen. It should not make players roll a second arrival procedure, replace hex travel, or duplicate the crime chapter.

## Compatibility Verdict

The current Chapter 10 settlement-play rules are compatible with Book 01 if treated as GM-side state procedures.

They work best as a layer that answers:

- What has changed here since the last visit?
- Which household is hungry, angry, owed, or ashamed?
- What shortage is shaping local choices?
- What does the First Impression roll look like in the fiction?
- Who carries resentment after the formal judgment ends?

They should not answer:

- Which direction the players may leave the settlement.
- Whether a village can be entered in place of Book 01 Hospitality.
- How legal sentencing works in place of the Book 01 crime rules.

## Procedure-by-Procedure Check

### Settlement Turn

Compatible.

The procedure advances a settlement once per month or after a major event. It does not consume player-facing quarter days and does not interfere with journey actions. Its risk is overlap with the existing vicissitudes table, so Chapter 10 now states that the GM should use one or the other for the same month.

Design fit:

- Uses existing settlement problems, locations, and conditions.
- Creates a GM clock without adding player-facing complexity.
- Supports rumor and return-visit play in Book 01.

Risk:

- The `2D6` result can feel lighter than the existing `3D6` vicissitudes table. This is acceptable because it is a short form.

### Household Ledger

Compatible.

Need and Heat give the GM a local memory tool. They do not replace Standing. Standing measures the settlement's broad attitude toward the fellowship. Heat measures a specific household or authority that has reason to act.

Design fit:

- Explains why two villages with the same Standing may react differently.
- Gives crime, debt, insult, and favor a local face.
- Supports Book 01 petitions, work, hospitality, and judgment.

Risk:

- Heat can be confused with Standing if the text is not clear. Chapter 10 now frames Heat as household pressure, not public reputation.

### Stores And Shortages

Compatible.

Stores and shortages fit the survival economy of Book 01. They explain why Hospitality changes, why prices shift, and why a settlement may refuse aid even when Standing is positive.

Design fit:

- Supports food, lodging, labor, trade, and winter pressure.
- Gives the GM a reason for changing Hospitality between visits.
- Keeps scarcity concrete: grain, firewood, feed, salt, water, seed, coin.

Risk:

- Tracking too many stores slows play. Chapter 10 now tells the GM to track only the stores that matter in play.

### Route Links

Compatible only as an optional structure.

Book 01 is a hex crawl. Route links must not become mandatory exits, required paths, or a replacement for the open map. They are useful only when the GM intentionally runs a shorter road-bound adventure, caravan structure, or pointcrawl.

Design fit:

- Matches Book 01 rumor travel, roads, rivers, ferries, and market access.
- Helps short adventures where every stop on a road matters.
- Gives trade and road condition a simple state.

Risk:

- If used casually, route links can make players think the map has fixed exits. Chapter 10 now explicitly says not to use route links to fence the world in.

### First Arrival

Compatible as GM advice.

Book 01 already owns the player-facing arrival roll. Chapter 10 should not create a second roll. It now gives the GM questions to answer before using Hospitality and First Impression.

Design fit:

- Improves the fiction of the First Impression roll.
- Ties settlement condition, stores, and households into the gate scene.
- Preserves the Book 01 procedure.

Risk:

- If expanded into a separate procedure, it would duplicate Book 01. Keep it as advice.

### Justice And Retaliation

Compatible as aftermath tracking.

Book 01 already covers crimes, judgment, sentencing, outlawry, and flight. Chapter 10 should track who still carries the grievance, not replace the law.

Design fit:

- Heat explains consequences after formal judgment.
- Gives offended households agency.
- Supports blood price, denial of shelter, armed pursuit, and revenge without rewriting the legal system.

Risk:

- Overlap with Book 01 crime rules. Chapter 10 now tells the GM to use Book 01 for charge and sentence, and Heat for who keeps the matter alive.

## Recommended Integration Notes

- Keep `Settlement Turn` and `Vicissitudes` as alternate time-advance tools.
- Keep `Route Links` visibly optional and tied to pointcrawl or road-bound play.
- Keep `First Arrival` as advice that feeds Book 01 Hospitality and First Impression.
- Keep `Justice And Retaliation` as household aftermath, not sentencing.
- Use `Need` and `Heat` only for named households or powers that matter. Do not track every family.

## Final Assessment

The rules are compatible with Book 01 after the rewrite. They add GM-side settlement state without taking agency away from the hex map or duplicating the player-facing settlement procedures. The only fragile point is Route Links, and the current text now marks it clearly as optional for pointcrawl play.
