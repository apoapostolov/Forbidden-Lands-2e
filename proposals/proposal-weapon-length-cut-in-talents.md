# Weapon Length Talents

## Verdict

The CUT IN action and weapon-length rules in Chapter 5 create a reach game worth playing. Closing costs a FAST action and a MOVE roll. Against a LONG-REACH weapon it costs −2. BRACE punishes the rush. INTERCEPT covers the ambush. The core game handles the basics.

What it lacks is a reason to specialize. No talent rewards the knife fighter who reads the overextension and slips inside the haft. No talent rewards the spear holder who keeps fighters at the steel's edge round after round.

This proposal adds two general talents:

- **INSIDE THE GUARD** — the closer, who steps past longer weapons
- **KEEP THEM OUT** — the holder, who punishes every step forward

The original draft included a third talent (Pass The Measure) for pressing advantage after closing. That role is folded into Inside The Guard's higher ranks. Two talents with clear identities are better than three with overlapping ones.

## Design Goals

These talents must:

- reward reading distance and choosing the right moment to commit
- create real decisions about whether to close, hold, or break off
- preserve BRACE as the deliberate anti-charge stance
- preserve INTERCEPT as the generic ready action
- cost Willpower at every activation — nothing fires for free
- feel worth the XP at ranks 4–5, where the investment is steep

These talents must not:

- stack three reactive punishments on one approach attempt
- make LONG-REACH irrelevant by letting everyone close cheaply
- remove the cost of closing from the game
- create a mandatory talent tax for melee fighters
- duplicate what BRACE and SPEAR FIGHTER already do

## Shared Rule: One Reactive Attack Per Approach

A single CUT IN attempt, charge, or approach can trigger at most **one reactive attack** from any source — BRACE, INTERCEPT, SPEAR FIGHTER rank 2, INSIDE THE GUARD, or KEEP THEM OUT. If more than one could fire, the defender chooses which one triggers. Other non-attack effects (such as KEEP THEM OUT's lockout at rank 1) can still apply alongside the chosen reactive attack.

This prevents a spear user from stacking BRACE, SPEAR FIGHTER, and KEEP THEM OUT into three hits on one closing attempt.

## INSIDE THE GUARD

_General talent. Applies against any enemy whose melee weapon has longer reach than yours. Monsters and animals with a defined natural weapon reach are valid targets; those without a defined reach are immune._

You read the overextension and move before the haft recovers.

- ✦ **RANK 1:** When an enemy attacks you with a melee weapon of longer reach than yours and rolls no ⚔️, you may spend 1 WP to close the distance. This counts as a successful CUT IN but costs no action. Once per round.
- ✦ **RANK 2:** After you successfully CUT IN — through this talent or a normal MOVE roll — your next melee attack against that enemy this round gains +1.
- ✦ **RANK 3:** After you CUT IN against a LONG-REACH weapon, the enemy suffers −2 to their next roll to restore distance before your next turn.
- ✦ **RANK 4:** When a BRACE attack hits you during a CUT IN, you may spend 2 WP to press through and complete the close. You suffer the damage and any critical injury as normal. The enemy's BRACE stance ends — they do not get a second BRACE trigger until they spend another action to set one.
- ✦ **RANK 5:** Once per encounter, when you CUT IN against a LONG-REACH weapon, you may spend 2 WP to strike in the same motion. Make one SLASH or STAB against that enemy immediately. The target suffers −2 to PARRY this attack unless they hold a shield. If the blow deals at least 1 damage, the target cannot use KEEP THE DISTANCE until after your next turn.

### Design Notes

Rank 1 is the identity: the enemy overextends, you step inside. It costs WP and requires them to miss — neither is guaranteed.

Rank 2 converts the close into a slight edge. Rank 3 makes it harder for them to push you back out. Both are passive bonuses that reward what you already did.

Rank 4 is the answer to the set spear. BRACE is supposed to stop a rush cold. At rank 4, you take the point in the gut and close anyway. You still eat the damage. This is not a counter — it is a willingness to trade blood for position, and it costs 2 WP on top of whatever the stab inflicted.

Rank 5 is the killing close. One chance per fight to drive a knife under the haft before the polearm can recover. The −2 to PARRY is real but not absolute — a shield still blocks it, and armor still rolls. If it connects, the enemy loses the ability to reopen distance for one round. That is the moment the knife fighter trained for.

## KEEP THEM OUT

_General talent. Applies when you hold a melee weapon with longer reach than your opponent's. Monsters and animals are valid targets if their natural weapon reach is shorter than yours._

You fight from the edge of your steel and punish every step forward.

- ✦ **RANK 1:** When an enemy fails a CUT IN roll against you, you may spend 1 WP to lock the distance. They cannot attempt CUT IN against you again until after their next turn. This lockout cannot trigger on the same approach that triggered BRACE or INTERCEPT — choose one or the other.
- ✦ **RANK 2:** While your weapon has longer reach than your enemy's and they have not closed the distance, your melee attacks against them gain +1.
- ✦ **RANK 3:** When you use rank 1 to lock an enemy out, they also suffer −1 to PARRY and DODGE your attacks until after their next turn. The failed rush left them short of guard.
- ✦ **RANK 4:** Once per round, when an enemy fails a CUT IN against you, you may spend 1 WP to make one immediate STAB against them if your weapon can reach. This follows the one-reactive-attack-per-approach rule — if BRACE, INTERCEPT, or SPEAR FIGHTER already fired on this approach, rank 4 cannot.
- ✦ **RANK 5:** Once per encounter, you may spend 2 WP to root your stance (FAST action). Until the start of your next turn: you cannot voluntarily move; all CUT IN attempts against you suffer an additional −2 penalty; you may trigger rank 4 against each enemy that fails a CUT IN (1 WP each, ignoring the once-per-round limit); and any enemy you hit with that reactive STAB is also forced Prone.

### Design Notes

Rank 1 is the identity: the enemy lunged and missed, and now they cannot try again for a full round. Tempo control, not damage.

Rank 2 rewards the discipline of keeping distance. It only applies while the gap holds — one successful CUT IN and the bonus vanishes.

Rank 3 stacks on rank 1. The enemy who failed to close is now worse at defending against your reach-advantage attacks. Combined with rank 2, the effective swing is +1 attack, −1 defense — a net +2 pressure for one round, only after a failed CUT IN.

Rank 4 draws blood. The failed rush triggers a reactive STAB, but only one reactive attack per approach, so it never stacks with BRACE on the same attempt. The cost is 1 WP per trigger — not free.

Rank 5 is the wall. You plant your feet, give up all movement, and dare anyone to come through. Against a LONG-REACH weapon with the base −2 penalty, rank 5 adds another −2 for a cumulative −4 to CUT IN. A fighter with MOVE 3 rolls 3 dice at −4 — near-impossible without pushing or bonuses. Any enemy who does try and fails eats the reactive STAB and lands Prone. The cost is 2 WP plus immobility plus every trigger costs 1 WP more. Ranged attacks, magic, and patience all bypass it. It lasts one round.

This is the spear in the doorway. The pike wall in the pass. It does not make you invincible. It makes closing on you terrifying for exactly long enough to matter.

## Balance Safety

These talents are safe only if the base CUT IN action stays expensive. If CUT IN becomes free, both talents collapse into dominant lines.

The shared reactive-attack rule is the primary safety valve. Without it, a spear user with BRACE + SPEAR FIGHTER rank 2 + KEEP THEM OUT rank 4 would trigger three separate hits on one approach. The rule limits the stack to one reactive attack plus non-attack effects (lockout, prone, penalties).

Inside The Guard rank 4 does not negate BRACE — it lets you complete the close despite taking the hit. A BRACE STAB with a polearm can deal 3–5 damage before armor. Pressing through that costs 2 WP on top. The fighter who walks through a set spear pays in blood and Willpower.

Keep Them Out rank 5 trades movement for denial. A rooted fighter cannot dodge area attacks, cannot reposition, cannot chase, and cannot retreat. Any enemy with a bow, spell, or patience waits it out.

## Integration Points

These talents belong in Chapter 4 among the general talents.

They interact with:

- **CUT IN** (Chapter 5) — the core action both talents modify
- **KEEP THE DISTANCE** (Chapter 5) — Inside The Guard rank 3 and 5 restrict it
- **BRACE** (Chapter 5) — Inside The Guard rank 4 allows pressing through; shared reactive rule governs stacking
- **INTERCEPT** (Chapter 5) — shared reactive rule governs stacking
- **SPEAR FIGHTER** (General talent) — shared reactive rule prevents rank 2 from stacking with KEEP THEM OUT rank 4 on the same approach
- **MELEE CHARGE** (General talent) — a charge from NEAR to ARM'S LENGTH counts as an approach for the shared reactive rule
- **COMBAT EXPERIENCED** (General talent) — positional maneuvers (push, prone, overextend) apply after the close resolves, not during it
- **PATH OF THE BLADE** (Fighter) — damage bonuses apply normally to Inside The Guard rank 5's strike
- **PATH OF THE ENEMY** (Fighter) — rank 2's parry/dodge blocking does not stack with Inside The Guard rank 5's PARRY penalty; use one or the other
- **Mounted combat** — mounted charges that close distance count as approaches for the shared reactive rule; KEEP THEM OUT applies against riders normally

## Recommendation

Accept both talents if the manuscript keeps CUT IN as a paid FAST action and BRACE as the dedicated receive-the-rush stance.

The shared reactive-attack rule should be stated once in Chapter 5 near the BRACE and INTERCEPT definitions, then referenced by name in both talent entries. That prevents the stacking problem at the source instead of patching it in each talent.
