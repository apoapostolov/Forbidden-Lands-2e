# Proposal - `Makeshift Tools` as an Adventuring Resource

Goal: add a single cheap, practical gear item called `Makeshift Tools` to cover the rough field gear adventurers constantly need in the wild: shovel, hand pick, hammer, pry iron, wedges, awl, nails, cord, oil, spare straps, and similar mundane implements, without colliding with the existing proper shovel and pickaxe entries.

## Problem

The game already assumes adventurers sometimes need cheap but essential field tools, yet these items are awkward to track one by one and too easy to handwave until the moment they suddenly matter.

This produces two bad outcomes:

- either the group ignores such gear entirely
- or the table is forced into tedious shopping-list accounting for low-value but mandatory expedition items

## Rule Change

Add a new gear item called `Makeshift Tools`.

`Makeshift Tools` is a catch-all adventuring kit, tracked with a Resource Die instead of as a single permanent item.

## Gear Entry

| Item | Price | Availability | Weight | Effect |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `Makeshift Tools` | 1 silver | Common | Normal | Resource Die `D8`. Covers rough field tools needed for digging, prying, hammering, cutting, fastening, clearing, patching, and similar practical work. |

## What `Makeshift Tools` Includes

A typical `Makeshift Tools` kit may include:

- small shovel or entrenching spade
- hand pick or mattock head
- hammer
- pry iron or wedges
- awl, knife, and files
- nails, pegs, hooks, and scrap cord
- spare straps, bindings, oil, and simple repair bits

This is not a fine artisan's kit. It is expedition gear at its meanest: ugly, practical, cheap, and always in danger of wearing out, bending, snapping, or being used up.

## Using `Makeshift Tools`

Whenever `Makeshift Tools` is used for a significant field task, roll its Resource Die.

Significant use means work such as:

- digging through earth, roots, snow, or rubble
- breaking into blocked passages, crates, doors, or shutters
- prying stones, floorboards, lids, spikes, or fittings loose
- driving stakes, tent pegs, braces, or supports
- cutting away brush, light roots, or debris
- making rough camp repairs
- patching tack, packs, shields, carts, sledges, boats, or harness
- improvised climbing, wedging, anchoring, or bracing work
- extracting arrows, spikes, nails, or embedded junk from gear or structures

Do not roll for trivial use. Roll when the tools are being meaningfully expended, stressed, dulled, bent, or broken down by hard living.

## Depletion

`Makeshift Tools` starts at `D8`.

Each significant use requires a Resource Die roll, using the normal resource-die rules.

When `Makeshift Tools` is reduced to `NONE`, the kit is treated as broken, spent, scattered, or missing vital pieces. It no longer counts as usable `Makeshift Tools` until repaired, replaced, or resupplied.

Broken `Makeshift Tools` might mean:

- the shovel head cracks
- the pick haft splits
- the hammer loosens
- the pry iron bends
- the wedges shatter
- the nails, hooks, pegs, and cord are used up
- too many little losses finally make the kit worthless

## What `Makeshift Tools` Does Not Replace

`Makeshift Tools` does not replace:

- a forge
- a full saw-and-hammer building set for serious work
- a smith's proper equipment
- a healer's supplies
- a rope supply
- artisan tools required by crafting tables

It is not meant to satisfy specialized crafting requirements in Chapter 10. It is meant to cover the ugly little practical jobs that every expedition eventually faces.

If a check calls for a proper hand tool and the character only has `Makeshift Tools`, they may still attempt it at a penalty:

- at `D8`: `-1` die
- at `D6`: `-2` dice

This reflects bad balance, bent edges, weak hafts, poor fit, and the general misery of trying to do real work with field junk. It does not allow `Makeshift Tools` to stand in for a forge, a workshop, or a full professional toolset.

## Adjudication Guidance

- If the task is something a real traveler, miner, scout, grave-robber, caravan hand, or ruin-plunderer would obviously need rough implements for, `Makeshift Tools` is the right item.
- If the task requires skilled manufacture or a named workshop toolset, `Makeshift Tools` is not enough.
- If two or more Quarter Days of hard labor are spent on the same obstacle, roll `Makeshift Tools` once per Quarter Day of serious use.
- If several adventurers contribute their kits to the same task, only the kit actually doing the work is rolled, unless the group clearly burns through several kits.

## Why This Works Better

- It gives expeditions a real logistics item without forcing absurd item-by-item accounting.
- It makes ruins, wilderness travel, camp life, and improvised engineering feel more grounded.
- It creates attrition in a place that feels realistic rather than arbitrary.
- It gives the group a practical reason to restock in villages and settlements.
- It distinguishes ordinary field capability from real artisan gear.

## Optional Follow-On

If this proposal is adopted, Chapter 10 could later mark certain exploration actions or obstacle types with a short note such as `Requires Makeshift Tools` or `Makeshift Tools` roll required after success.
