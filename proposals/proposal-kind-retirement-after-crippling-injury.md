# Proposal - Kind Retirement After Crippling Injury

Goal: give groups a humane, story-fitting way to let a badly injured adventurer step away from danger without treating that choice as failure, disposal, or punishment.

## Why This Matters

Some injuries are memorable because they leave scars and change how an adventurer moves through the world. Those are good for play.

Some injuries leave a person alive, but make the daily business of adventuring so hard that every scene must bend around the wound. That can still be played for a time, but not every player wants their companion's story to become one of constant helplessness, pain, or dependence.

When that happens, the game should offer mercy and dignity, not just mechanics.

## Core Thought

If an injury leaves an adventurer difficult to play without slowing the story, or simply no longer enjoyable for the player, the table should treat retirement as an honorable outcome.

The adventurer has not failed. They have survived.

They may:

- remain with the company as a trusted follower NPC
- keep the camp, stores, horses, or stronghold accounts
- become a guide, quartermaster, healer's assistant, watch-keeper, or counselor
- settle in a village, keep, temple, or hall where they can receive better care
- go to kin, friends, or a household that can tend them in safety

## Tone And Framing

The language around this should be kind.

Do not frame the retired adventurer as a burden to be dropped.
Do not frame care as weakness.
Do not frame the player's choice as giving up.

Instead, frame it like this:

- the road has taken enough from them
- they have earned warmth, safety, and a roof
- their name still matters to the company
- they may yet advise, warn, bless, remember, and be remembered

The world of Forbidden Lands is harsh. That is exactly why small acts of mercy matter.

## Table Guidance

When a crippling injury is rolled, the GM should say plainly that the player has three honorable options:

1. keep playing the adventurer with the injury's full consequences
2. retire the adventurer from active danger and keep them in the story as an NPC ally or follower
3. let the adventurer withdraw from the tale into safety, and bring in a new PC

The player should never be pressured into choosing the most punishing option merely because the injury was severe.

## Best Use

This rule works best as a sidebar or short boxed text near permanent critical injuries, prosthetics, or recovery.

It is not a mechanical subsystem. It is a statement of tone:

survival may end a warrior's road, but it does not strip away their worth.
