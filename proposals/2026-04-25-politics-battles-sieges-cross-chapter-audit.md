# Cross-Chapter Audit: Politics and Battles & Sieges After the Second Pass

## Scope

This audit reviews the current handoff between:

- `02-gamemasters-guide/11-politics-of-the-forbidden-lands.md`
- `02-gamemasters-guide/12-battles-and-sieges.md`

The focus is narrow.

It is not another battlefield audit.
It is not another prose audit.
It is an audit of the war-to-rule seam:

- hostages
- surrender terms
- punitive extraction
- occupation
- revolt
- status shift after conquest
- post-war political fallout

## Overall verdict

The bridge is stronger than it was.

Chapter 12 now speaks the language Chapter 11 needs:

- Standing
- Feud
- burden
- hostages
- occupation
- coercion

That is good. The chapters are finally looking at the same people.

But one major procedure is still missing.

**Occupation has consequences, modifiers, and even talent support, but it still does not have a weekly resolution loop in Chapter 11.**

That is now the clearest remaining seam in the war system.

## What now works well

### 1. Burden language is aligned

Chapter 11 defines campaign burden politically.
Chapter 12 defines extraction ceilings physically.

That split is healthy.

- Chapter 11 says what it costs politically to take
- Chapter 12 says what can be taken before bodies start dropping

The newer `Refusal, Hostages, and Example` section improves this further by giving visible methods of coercive collection rather than leaving them as GM inference.

### 2. Occupation now has immediate texture in Chapter 12

The added `Occupation After Taking a Place` section gives three immediate holding modes:

- oaths and hostages
- garrison rule
- terror rule

This was an important improvement. It means the fall of a place now changes the campaign in a specific way before the next political turn begins.

### 3. Hostages now exist on both sides of the bridge

Chapter 11 already had:

- hostage compact as a political instrument
- `Take Hostage` as a political act
- hostage return as a valid feud-reduction outcome

Chapter 12 now adds hostages inside:

- surrender and parley
- ransom terms
- village coercion
- occupation after assault

That shared vocabulary is correct and valuable.

### 4. Bad faith now has campaign memory

Chapter 12's bad-faith rule and open-sack consequences properly state that treachery poisons future negotiations.

That is exactly the kind of long shadow Chapter 11 can use.

## Main remaining problems

### 1. Occupation has no weekly test

This is the biggest remaining issue.

Evidence in Chapter 11:

- Occupation is a defined settlement status.
- Occupation is said to require men, food, pay, and an answer to resistance.
- Occupation always creates Hard burden.
- A bad occupation becomes Crushing fast.
- `Path of Terror` rank 2 says: **"Occupied settlements do not test immediate revolt on the first week of occupation."**

The problem is simple.

There is no actual occupation or revolt test written anywhere around that rule.

Chapter 12 now says:

- under `Oaths and hostages`, the first attempt at revolt, refusal, or smuggling in the next campaign week fails automatically unless hostages are freed
- under `Terror rule`, the place does not revolt in the next campaign week

These lines imply a weekly occupation check exists.
It does not.

This is now the clearest broken bridge in the combined system.

### 2. Status conversion after conquest is still underdefined

Chapter 12 now tells us how a place is taken and how it is held immediately after the fall.

Chapter 11 defines the long-form settlement statuses:

- Ruled
- Protected
- Tributary
- Vassal
- Occupied
- Allied

What is still missing is the explicit conversion rule.

Questions the current manuscript does not answer cleanly enough:

- Does every place taken by assault become **Occupied** by default?
- Can a pre-breach surrender with clean terms skip straight to **Protected** or **Tributary**?
- Does `Mercy` after storming a place still count as occupation until a later political act softens it?
- When does a held place stop being merely occupied and become ruled again?

Right now the reader can infer answers, but the bridge would be stronger if the manuscript stated them plainly.

### 3. Hostage lifecycle is only half procedural

The system now does a good job of taking hostages.
It does a weaker job of living with them.

The following are clearly important in play but not yet fully procedural:

- hostage escape
- hostage rescue by raid or intrigue
- hostage death by neglect, illness, or deliberate killing
- hostage return as a formal political act after war
- what hostage loss does to Feud, Standing, or Mandate when the loss was accidental rather than malicious

This is not as urgent as the missing occupation check, but it is the next cleanest seam.

### 4. Post-war fallout is still advisory, not procedural

Chapter 11's `After the War` section is good design advice.
It is not yet a compact resolution step.

It says battle aftermath should change faction play through:

- lost troops
- pillaged villages
- unpaid armies
- broken roads
- damaged walls and mills
- Mandate loss after defeat

All true.
But there is still no short end-of-campaign or end-of-battle reckoning procedure that turns those truths into one repeatable table behavior.

This is a lesser problem than the missing occupation test, but it is real.

## Recommended fixes

### P7.1 — Add an Occupation Check to Chapter 11

**Priority:** critical

Insert into Chapter 11 near `### Occupation` or inside the `Campaign Week` structure.

**Rule goal:** make occupation playable week by week.

**Minimum procedure needed:**

Once per campaign week, each occupied settlement makes an **Occupation Check**.
The occupying faction rolls one of:

- `Iron + Discipline`
- `Mandate + Decree`
- `Mandate + Accord` if the place is being softened through sworn terms rather than naked force

Set difficulty from the settlement's current condition:

- `1` if burden is Light and Standing is neutral or better
- `2` if burden is Hard or Standing is cold
- `3` if burden is Crushing, recent sack occurred, or hostages were mishandled
- `4` if terror was used, pay failed, garrison is thin, or a rival is actively stirring resistance

**Success:** the occupation holds for now.

**Failure:** choose or roll one:

- open refusal of dues
- smuggling or messenger traffic gets through
- households flee
- sabotage of stores, mill, road, gate, or ferry
- one hostage is freed, lost, or traded away
- the place shifts from Occupied to active Feud and requires suppression

This single procedure would pay off several dangling references immediately.

### P7.2 — State the default status after conquest

**Priority:** high

Add a short bridge note between Chapter 12 aftermath and Chapter 11 occupation.

**Recommended rule:**

- any place taken by assault is **Occupied** by default
- a place that surrenders before breach may become **Protected** or **Tributary** if terms were accepted and kept
- a place under oath-and-hostage rule remains **Occupied** until a later successful political act changes its status
- mercy changes burden and Standing; it does not erase the fact of conquest by itself

This would remove a lot of interpretation drift.

### P7.3 — Add a bridge note for hostages

**Priority:** medium

Add one sentence in Chapter 12 and one in Chapter 11.

**Recommended bridge:**

- hostages taken through surrender, coercive collection, or occupation count as normal hostage assets in Chapter 11
- returning them cleanly can reduce Feud by 1 or improve Standing by 1 where the fiction supports it
- harming or losing them without agreement worsens Feud or Standing even if the death was not deliberate

This may be enough without a full hostage subsystem.

### P7.4 — Add a short War Reckoning step

**Priority:** medium

Insert at the end of Chapter 11's `After the War` section.

**Rule goal:** give GMs a one-minute conversion from battle outcome to faction damage.

**Possible structure:** after any major battle, siege fall, or failed campaign:

- reduce `Iron` for destroyed contingents
- reduce `Hearth` for pillaged or burned productive ground
- reduce `Road` for cut crossings, broken routes, or failed courier lines
- reduce `Mandate` for public defeat, unpaid troops, dishonored terms, or a sack that turned opinion hard

Then shift one or more settlement statuses if the fiction demands it.

This would make the return from Chapter 12 into Chapter 11 cleaner and faster.

## Implementation order

1. `P7.1 Occupation Check`
2. `P7.2 Default status after conquest`
3. `P7.3 Hostage bridge note`
4. `P7.4 War Reckoning step`

## Final judgment

The war system is now very close.

The battlefield is not the weak part anymore.
The siege is not the weak part anymore.
The weak part is the week after the gate falls.

Chapter 12 can now take a place.
Chapter 11 can describe what occupation means.
What is still missing is the rule that tells us, next week, whether the place is actually staying taken.
