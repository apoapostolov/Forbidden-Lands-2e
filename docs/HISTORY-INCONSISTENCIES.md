# History and Chronology Inconsistencies

## Scope and method

This document audits the historical continuity of the Forbidden Lands setting as presented in the repository. The principal authority is the detailed chronology in `02-gamemasters-guide/03-history.md`, checked against:

- `01-corebook/01-front-matter.md`
- `02-gamemasters-guide/04-gods.md`
- `02-gamemasters-guide/05-kin.md`
- `02-gamemasters-guide/09-adventure-sites.md`
- `skills/forbidden-lands-lore/references/history.md`
- `skills/forbidden-lands-lore/references/setting.md`
- `skills/forbidden-lands-lore/references/places.md`

Campaign spoiler files were not consulted. Findings that might depend on a campaign revelation are marked unresolved rather than forcibly reconciled.

The audit distinguishes four kinds of discrepancy:

- **Contradiction:** two authoritative statements cannot both be literally true.
- **Chronology error:** a date, duration, or sequence conflicts with the established timeline.
- **Reference drift:** a derived lore or writing reference no longer matches the manuscript source.
- **Intentional uncertainty:** myths, propaganda, and incomplete player knowledge differ by design and should remain unresolved.

## Recommended authority order

When correcting conflicts, use this order unless an author decision explicitly overrides it:

1. GM-facing detailed history and operational adventure-site facts.
2. Other GM-facing chapters.
3. Player-facing summaries, legends, and opening fiction.
4. Repository lore references derived from the books.

Player-facing material repeatedly announces that history has become myth. Its inaccuracies can therefore be retained as in-world beliefs. A derived reference, by contrast, should identify those beliefs as such rather than silently presenting them as GM truth.

## Confirmed inconsistencies

### 1. The Blood Mist era ends after the present year

**Severity:** High chronology error.

The History chapter labels the period `900–1200 AS — Era of the Blood Mist` (`03-history.md:241`), but the same chapter says the Mist lifts in 1160 AS (`03-history.md:253`) and establishes the present as 1165 AS (`03-history.md:259`). The heading therefore extends the Blood Mist thirty-five years beyond the present and forty years beyond its stated lifting.

The repeated phrase “three centuries” is acceptable as a rounded description of roughly 260 years. The endpoint `1200 AS` is not.

**Recommended resolution:** Change the heading to `900–1160 AS — Era of the Blood Mist`. Retain “roughly three centuries” or “ten generations” in prose.

### 2. The lifting of the Blood Mist is both an event and a multi-year process

**Severity:** Medium chronology ambiguity.

The History chapter and lore references treat 1160 AS as the year the Mist lifted. The detailed account under that heading says the Mist “slowly disappeared over the next few years” after Merigall began spreading homesickness among the bloodlings (`03-history.md:253–257`). The player-facing introduction instead says it disappeared “suddenly” a few years ago (`01-front-matter.md:101`, `131–133`).

The player account can be understood as incomplete public knowledge, but the GM chronology still needs to decide whether 1160 marks:

- the beginning of the thinning;
- the year ordinary travel became possible; or
- the completion of the disappearance.

**Recommended resolution:** Define 1160 AS as the year the Mist began to fail and long-distance travel first became viable. State that it thinned unevenly until approximately 1163 AS, with isolated bloodlings remaining in 1165. Player-facing claims of a sudden disappearance can remain.

### 3. The Ailanders have two incompatible origins

**Severity:** High reference contradiction.

The History chapter is explicit: the Ailanders were a persecuted sect from Alderland who crossed into Ravenland around 510–540 AS (`03-history.md:49–57`). The Gods and Kin chapters agree that Jamharda led them from Alderland (`04-gods.md:21–23`; `05-kin.md:52–56`).

The setting reference instead says their homeland is unclear, specifically says it was not Alderland, places them in eastern Ravenland, and suggests that they fled a conflict related to the Alder Wars (`references/setting.md:171–179`). This cannot be reconciled with the detailed history.

**Recommended resolution:** Rewrite the setting reference to state that the Ailanders originated as Raven-worshipping refugees from Alderland, crossed around 510–540 AS, and later spread eastward within Ravenland.

### 4. The setting reference places all human arrival during the Alder Wars

**Severity:** High reference chronology error.

The setting reference says humans arrived in Ravenland “during the period of the Alder Wars” (`references/setting.md:88–94`). The detailed chronology establishes several different arrivals:

- the first human migration at the Shift in 0 AS, south into Alderland;
- the Ailander exodus into Ravenland around 510–540 AS;
- the Quard arrival in 610 AS;
- the first Alder War in 820–821 AS;
- the large Alderlander Man Flood in 825 AS.

Only the later Alderlander colonists arrived as part of the Alder Wars. The Ailanders and Quards were already in Ravenland for centuries.

**Recommended resolution:** State that Ravenland's present human population formed through successive migrations, with only the Alderlander military influx belonging to the Alder Wars.

### 5. The Vond Wall has the wrong builders in the lore chronology

**Severity:** High reference contradiction.

The History chapter states that the dwarves built the Vond Wall and fortress in 620 AS to stop further migration from Aslene (`03-history.md:69–71`). The lore chronology says the Aslenes raised it against incursions from the south (`references/history.md:130–137`).

These accounts assign the same fortification to opposing parties.

**Recommended resolution:** Treat the dwarves as the builders. The wall was intended to stop additional Aslene migration through Shadowgate Pass.

### 6. Mount Bilica is crossed in the year it closes the route

**Severity:** Medium reference chronology error.

The detailed chronology places the Ailander exodus in 510–540 AS and says the dwarves raised Mount Bilica in 550 AS to close the passage after additional migrations (`03-history.md:49–61`). The lore history and places references say the Ailanders crossed Mount Bilica in 550 AS (`references/history.md:122–128`; `references/places.md:497–498`).

This wording makes the Ailanders cross the mountain in the same year that its raising closes the route, and it moves the principal exodus outside its stated date range.

**Recommended resolution:** Say that the Ailanders crossed through the passage later blocked by Mount Bilica, mostly during 510–540 AS. In 550 AS the dwarves raised the mountain behind the last refugee groups.

### 7. Lumra is said to fall in the battle the dwarves win

**Severity:** High reference contradiction.

The First Alder War occurs at Lumra in 820–821 AS. The dwarves win, and Teramalda's armor rises after Garmar Four-Beard attempts to cook her (`03-history.md:101–109`). Lumra remains a dwarven refuge in 852 AS, when Martea and her children flee there. The mines are then consumed by the demon manifested through Marga (`03-history.md:151–159`).

The lore history and places references instead say Lumra fell in 820 or 820–821 AS (`references/history.md:139–147`; `references/places.md:244–245`). That turns a dwarven victory into the fall of the hold and ignores its later use in 852.

**Recommended resolution:** Record 820–821 AS as the Battle of Lumra and creation of the wandering armor. Record 852 AS as Lumra's fall or destruction.

### 8. Algarod is buried in The Hollows and also exists at Weatherstone

**Severity:** High location and character contradiction.

The detailed history says Algarod survives the destruction of his army, is captured at Alderstone, killed by Therania, reanimated, and sent to command Weatherstone (`03-history.md:193–213`). The Weatherstone adventure confirms that his undead body remains there and can awaken (`09-adventure-sites.md:1400–1416`, `1489–1500`).

The Hollows is built over a cemetery containing victims of the battles between Algarod and Zygofer (`09-adventure-sites.md:881–899`). It does not say the king is buried there.

The lore history and places references nevertheless call The Hollows Algarod's burial cairn and list him as buried there (`references/history.md:163–170`, `268`; `references/places.md:43–49`). These claims are incompatible with the physical presence of Algarod at Weatherstone.

**Recommended resolution:** The Hollows is the burial ground of soldiers and leaders from Algarod's army, and possibly the site of part of the final campaign. Algarod himself was taken to Alderstone and later placed at Weatherstone. Remove every claim that he is buried beneath The Hollows.

### 9. Algarod's manner of death is simplified into an incorrect cause

**Severity:** Medium reference error.

The lore history says Algarod was killed by Zygofer's demons in 874 AS (`references/history.md:69–73`). The detailed account says the demons destroyed his army, but his guards carried him alive to Alderstone. Therania killed him after he refused her offer, then raised him as an undead lover (`03-history.md:193–197`).

Player legends may reasonably say he “fell” against the demonic hordes, but the GM reference should preserve the actual sequence.

**Recommended resolution:** Record that the demon horde defeated Algarod's army; Therania personally killed and reanimated the captured king.

### 10. Zytera's creation has conflicting dates and components

**Severity:** High reference contradiction.

The detailed History chapter dates Zytera's creation to 880 AS. Zygofer and Therania are fused body-to-body, after which Merigall attaches them to the headless body of a giant spider-like demon (`03-history.md:221–227`).

The lore chronology dates the event around 875–878 AS and says father and daughter merged with a demon prince (`references/history.md:176–183`). That changes both the date and the nature of the being involved.

**Recommended resolution:** Use 880 AS. Describe Zytera as the fusion of Zygofer and Therania, subsequently joined to a demonic spider carcass. Do not call the carcass a demon prince unless another explicit author decision establishes that fact.

### 11. Zytera, Zygofer, and Therania are simultaneously one being and three active figures

**Severity:** High unresolved continuity conflict.

The History chapter says the bodies, necks, brains, and souls of Zygofer and Therania were united into the creature Zytera (`03-history.md:221–227`). The lore chronology's “Now” section nevertheless lists Zytera as ruler while also saying the independent Zygofer broods and the independent Therania plots (`references/history.md:219–237`). Its named-figure index likewise lists all three separately (`references/history.md:249–265`).

The player-facing opening fiction also speaks of “the demon-king and his daughter” as separate rulers (`01-front-matter.md:63–71`), but that passage is explicitly fiction and can function as outdated human belief. The GM-facing lore reference cannot do so without explanation.

**Recommended resolution:** Pending any campaign-specific revelation, treat Zytera as the current fused identity and mark reports of separate Zygofer and Therania as rumor, dream-presence, or an unresolved mystery. Do not list all three as physically independent in 1165 AS without an explicit explanation.

### 12. Vond's ruler is assigned inconsistently

**Severity:** Medium reference contradiction.

The detailed history assigns Vond to the demon lord Krasylla by treaty in 877 AS (`03-history.md:207–213`). The places reference agrees that Vond is Krasylla's seat and places Zytera's Mogarium above it on Mount Ora (`references/places.md:181–192`).

The lore history instead says Zytera holds the fortress of Vond in 880 AS (`references/history.md:184–192`). This collapses two adjacent but distinct seats of power.

**Recommended resolution:** Krasylla holds Vond. Zytera holds Alderstone and the Mogarium at Mount Ora. Their forces and domains may overlap, but the seats should not be treated as identical.

### 13. Quard ancestry switches from Ailander to Alderlander

**Severity:** Medium core-text contradiction.

The History chapter says the early Aslenes mixed with the Ailanders (`03-history.md:73–75`). The Quard entry repeats that they mixed with Ailanders over time (`05-kin.md:112`). The Galdane entry then says the Quards have mixed with Alderlanders for centuries (`05-kin.md:118`).

The last statement contradicts two earlier statements and is likely a single-word error.

**Recommended resolution:** Change “Alderlanders” to “Ailanders” in the Galdane entry unless the intended history is that Quards mixed with both groups at different periods. If both are intended, state that sequence explicitly.

### 14. Falender's burning is incorrectly identified as part of the Demon Flood

**Severity:** Medium reference chronology error.

The History chapter dates Falender's destruction to 869 AS and attributes it to western soldiers who remained as bandits after Margelda's liberation (`03-history.md:171–173`). The Demon Flood occurs in 874 AS (`03-history.md:193–201`).

The places reference says Falender was “burned 869 AS in the Demon Flood” (`references/places.md:240–243`). The date and event label cannot both be correct.

**Recommended resolution:** Retain 869 AS and attribute the burning to dispersing western soldiers or bandits. Remove “in the Demon Flood.” The later demonic devastation may still have affected the ruins.

### 15. Wailer's Hold falls two years too early in the references

**Severity:** Low chronology error.

The History chapter describes an abandonment from 865–867 AS and says Zygofer's troops took the city in 867 (`03-history.md:161–163`). The lore history and places references say it fell in 865 AS (`references/history.md:154–157`; `references/places.md:246–247`).

**Recommended resolution:** Use 865 AS for the beginning of the Meromannian withdrawal and 867 AS for the capture of the city.

### 16. The Galdane devastation and arrival are compressed into one year

**Severity:** Low chronology imprecision.

The detailed history distinguishes the westward movement of the demons in 875 AS from the Galdane arrival in Ravenland in 876 AS (`03-history.md:199–205`). The Kin chapter and lore chronology describe the homeland's destruction in 875 and immediately summarize the survivors reaching Margelda (`05-kin.md:114`; `references/history.md:173–175`).

This can describe a winter-spanning migration, but the reference currently makes the arrival appear to occur in 875.

**Recommended resolution:** State that the Galdane homeland was devastated in 875 AS and the surviving clan reached Margelda in 876 AS.

### 17. Resolved: calendar order differed between the chapter and lore reference

**Status:** Resolved.

The phase sequence is now standardized everywhere as:

`Springrise → Springwane → Summerrise → Summerwane → Fallrise → Fallwane → Winterrise → Winterwane`

The eight festival boundaries are likewise standardized as:

`Awakening Day → Springturn → Lushday → Midsummer → Harvest Day → Fallturn → Rotday → Midwinter`

Each festival boundary opens the corresponding phase in the same position in these two sequences. Springturn and Fallturn therefore count among the eight boundaries rather than being additional observances outside the calendar structure.

These do not alter the timeline, but they make historical cross-referencing unreliable.

## Intentional differences that are not errors

### The origin of the orcs

The detailed Shift story says Clay pulled the orcs from the mud when the land was divided (`03-history.md:27–31`). The Kin chapter also records an alternative belief that an older orc people already existed and was enslaved during the division (`05-kin.md:346–348`). The opening of the Kin chapter calls orcs native (`05-kin.md:7`).

These accounts can remain incompatible because the Kin chapter explicitly frames the origin as myth and identifies competing beliefs. The lore reference should describe the origin as disputed rather than selecting both versions as simultaneous fact.

### Zygofer and Therania in the player introduction

The opening fiction calls them separate rulers and greatly simplifies Zygofer's entry into Ravenland (`01-front-matter.md:61–75`). The same book tells players that the history of the land has been forgotten or become myth (`01-front-matter.md:109–113`). This is appropriately unreliable player knowledge. The spelling `Zygefor` should still be standardized unless the variant is intentionally part of the tale.

### The cause of the Mist's disappearance

Player-facing text calls the disappearance inexplicable. GM-facing history attributes it to Merigall's manipulation of the bloodlings. This is a purposeful knowledge boundary, not a contradiction.

### Religious accounts of the Protector

Wyrm, Raven, and Rust/Heme traditions interpret the raven-and-serpent image differently. These are sectarian claims, not competing editorial facts. They should remain unresolved unless the setting deliberately establishes a hidden cosmological truth.

### Rounded dates in oral history

“Twelve hundred years ago,” “seven hundred years ago,” “six hundred years ago,” “three centuries,” and “ten generations” are credible oral approximations. Exact AS dates in the GM chronology should control calculations; rounded language need not be mechanically exact.

## Overall conclusion

The setting's main historical spine is coherent: migration leads to religious conquest, the Alder Wars empower Zygofer, the Demon Flood destroys the old order, the Iron Lock seals the land, and the Blood Mist freezes the consequences until the present. Most contradictions entered through compressed summaries and derivative lore references.

The most consequential continuity problems concern the Ailanders' origin, the builders of the Vond Wall, Lumra's fall, Algarod's body, and the identities and seats of Zytera and Krasylla. Correcting those points would leave a stable chronology while preserving the setting's intended use of disputed myth, sectarian history, and unreliable player knowledge.
