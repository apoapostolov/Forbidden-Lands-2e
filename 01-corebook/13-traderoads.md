# Traderoads

> "A silver coin becomes ten in the hands of a patient merchant. A fool with ten lets them slip away."
> — Bartel the Ledger, factor to the Gristle's company

> _Grimolt counted the lashings before dawn because Svolma liked to say rope looked tighter in moonlight and then laugh when asked to help. Three wraps on the spice crates, two on the cloth — a simple formula, if a man respected it. Svolma had been shown twice already. The third crate was still wrong. Grimolt cut the cord himself and rewrapped it while Svolma held the torch with red hands and muttered that the cold was a personal insult.
>
> _Four wagons. Two drovers. Two guards, one of whom had not arrived yet. The morning was cold enough that his breath made a small cloud over the cargo manifest. He had carried iron before. Timber. Grain. All of them ordinary. The spices were different. The spices were why he had not slept.
>
> _Eight loads of fine cloth, two of black pepper, one of saffron wrapped in oilskin and packed in dry straw. The saffron alone was worth more than the wagons. The saffron alone was what the run was for.
>
> _The second guard arrived at first light, smelling of smoke and sour ale, with a split lip he kept worrying with his tongue. Grimolt noted the hour. He said nothing either. There would be time for judgment after the road had told him whether the man was worth keeping.
>
> _He climbed to the seat of the lead wagon. The road south was still dark. The Blood Mist had been gone five years. The toll posts had gone back up before the frost._

## The Caravan

The Forbidden Lands economy rests on more than coin and contract. It rests on movement — goods crossing the roads, boats moving down the rivers, traders following the seasons from settlement to settlement. A fellowship with silver and audacity can join that flow, assembling a caravan and running profit along established routes or cutting new paths through unmapped country.

A caravan is any organized trade expedition that moves goods between settlements for profit. It may be as small as a peddler with a laden mule, or as large as a six-wagon train with guards, factors, and a scout riding ahead. The rules scale. All caravans share three components: the **transport** that carries the cargo, the **cargo** itself, and the **people** who make it work.

### Transport and Load Capacity

Caravan capacity is measured in **load units**. One load unit equals approximately 10 Normal-weight items (100 units of weight using the carrying system described in the **YOUR ADVENTURER** chapter). Animals and vehicles have load capacities:

| Transport   | Capacity | Speed                                                     | Cost       | Terrain Limits              | Notes                                                             |
| ----------- | -------- | --------------------------------------------------------- | ---------- | --------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Pack mule   | 2        | Normal                                                    | 25 silver  | All terrain                 | Reliable, cheap. Navigates terrain where wagons cannot pass.      |
| Pack horse  | 3        | Normal                                                    | 40 silver  | All terrain                 | Faster empty, same speed burdened with cargo.                     |
| Donkey      | 1        | Slow (−1 hex/day)                                         | 10 silver  | All terrain                 | Cheap, stubborn, eats less than a mule.                           |
| Ox cart     | 6        | Slow (−1 hex/day)                                         | 60 silver  | Roads, plains, light forest | Strong and cheap. Cannot cross marsh, mountain, or dense forest.  |
| Horse wagon | 8        | Normal                                                    | 120 silver | Roads, plains, light forest | Standard trade wagon. Loses speed on unimproved trails.           |
| River barge | 20       | Fast downstream (+1 hex/day) / Slow upstream (−1 hex/day) | 200 silver | Rivers only                 | High capacity but route-locked. Requires PIER to load and unload. |
| Heavy wagon | 12       | Slow (−1 hex/day)                                         | 200 silver | Roads only                  | Maximum capacity on land. Cannot leave maintained roads.          |

**Terrain and speed.** Road-dependent transport (wagons, heavy wagons) travels at listed speed on maintained roads and at half speed on trails. Off-road, wagons cannot move at all without spending a full quarter day per hex to clear a path. Mules and horses handle all terrain at listed speed. River barges are locked to their waterways but move quickly downstream.

**Animal upkeep.** Each draft or pack animal consumes 1 unit of FOOD per day (6 copper worth of grain or forage; free in grassland or forest if the caravan spends a quarter day grazing). WATER must be carried or found at rivers and streams. Vehicles require occasional repair — treat as a CRAFTING check using Carpentry or Smithing, difficulty 1, after every 10 days of road travel or after any damage event.

### People and Roles

A caravan requires people to function — not every role must be filled, but a caravan that lacks necessary people will struggle:

| Role               | Function                                                      | Daily Pay                  | Notes                                                               |
| ------------------ | ------------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Caravan master** | Leads the expedition, decides routes, handles trade and sales | —                          | Usually a PC. If hired as an NPC, 2 silver/day.                     |
| **Drover**         | Handles animals, loads and unloads cargo, maintains gear      | 5 copper                   | One per two wagons or four pack animals.                            |
| **Guard**          | Defends the caravan from threat                               | 1 silver                   | the **MERCENARIES** chapter's mercenary pay rates apply to larger escort forces.     |
| **Factor**         | Manages trade at the destination, negotiates with buyers      | 1 silver + 5% profit share | Requires PATH OF THE CARAVAN Rank 3 or an NPC with MANIPULATION 3+. |
| **Scout**          | Rides ahead, watches for hazards, finds campsites             | 1 silver                   | Uses SCOUTING to provide advance warning of encounters.             |

A bare caravan — one peddler with a pack mule — needs nobody but the owner. A standard four-wagon caravan needs one or two drovers and one or two guards. A large caravan of six or more wagons needs the full complement: factors, scouts, multiple drovers, and a proper guard force.

**Wages and discipline.** Employees are paid daily from the caravan's fund. An unpaid employee leaves after 1D6 days, taking what they can carry — supplies, goods, or a mule if they can ride. Wages come out of the operating budget before profit is calculated. A slow journey spends more on wages and leaves less room for profit.

### Loading

Loading a caravan is a quarter-day activity. One character supervises the work and makes a CRAFTING roll (no talent required — this is pure logistics). Difficulty 0.

| ⚔️    | Outcome                                                                                                                                                |
| ----- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| No ⚔️ | Loaded but poorly secured. The first rough terrain hex or hazard event causes 1D6 silver of cargo damage — shifted loads, broken goods, spilled grain. |
| 1 ⚔️  | Adequately loaded. No bonus, no penalty.                                                                                                               |
| 2 ⚔️  | Well loaded. The first cargo damage event of the journey is ignored — the lashing held.                                                                |
| 3+ ⚔️ | Expertly loaded. As above, plus available load capacity increases by 10% (rounded up) due to efficient packing.                                        |

Characters with PATH OF THE ARTISAN gain relevant bonuses. PATH OF THE CARAVAN R2+ characters roll for results even at the baseline and reduce the failure state to a -1 modifier instead of damage.

### Perishability

Some cargo spoils. Dried grain lasts indefinitely if kept dry. Fresh fish spoils in two or three days. Wine survives anything short of breakage. Herbs lose potency after a week in heat.

Track perishability only for time-sensitive goods. As a general rule:

| Cargo Type              | Shelf Life on the Road                 | Risk                                              |
| ----------------------- | -------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------- |
| Dry bulk staples        | Indefinite                             | Water damage (rain, river crossings)              |
| Fresh food, dairy, meat | 2–3 days (5–7 in cold weather)         | Spoilage — total loss if unsold in time           |
| Herbs, reagents         | 5–10 days                              | Potency loss — sell price halves after shelf life |
| Wine, oil, glassware    | Indefinite                             | Breakage — fragile goods only                     |
| Livestock               | Indefinite (self-feeding in grassland) | Predators, disease, stampede                      |

### Fragility

Luxury goods and glassware are fragile. When a caravan crosses rough terrain (marsh, mountain, dense forest) or suffers a hazard event, fragile cargo must pass a breakage check: roll 1D6 per load unit of fragile cargo. On a 1, that load unit takes 25% damage (round up in silver value). On a 1–2 if the terrain is mountain.

A loading roll that achieved 2+ ⚔️ negates the first breakage check of the journey. PATH OF THE CARAVAN R4 allows the caravan master to designate one load unit of fragile cargo as personally secured, exempting it from all breakage checks.

## Regional Trade and Supply

### The Core Trade Principle

Trade profit comes from arbitrage: **buy where goods are cheap and sell where they are expensive**. The supply system uses four tiers — **Common**, **Uncommon**, **Rare**, and **Extremely Rare** — to describe how available a good is at a given settlement. A Common item is cheap to buy there. A Rare item commands a premium. The same good can sit at different supply tiers in different settlements depending on geography, stronghold functions, and season.

The fundamental rule:

> **Buy at origin price. Sell at destination price. The difference is your margin — minus travel costs, wages, hazards, and tolls.**

### Settlement Trade Profiles

Each settlement has a trade profile — what it produces cheaply and what it needs at a premium. The GM assigns or generates these based on the settlement's geography, size, and stronghold functions:

| Settlement Feature   | Produces (Common, low price)                | Needs (Uncommon+, high price)                  |
| -------------------- | ------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------- |
| Farmland, plains     | Grain, wool, livestock, hay                 | Iron tools, weapons, salt, cloth, luxury goods |
| Forest settlement    | Timber, pelts, charcoal, herbs              | Grain, iron, salt, weapons, craft goods        |
| Mining town          | Iron ore, stone, gems (rare)                | Food, cloth, timber, medicine, luxury goods    |
| River or coastal     | Fish, rope, river trade goods, barge access | Timber, iron, livestock, inland goods          |
| Trade crossroads     | Diverse goods (variable by season)          | Intelligence, services, specialty goods        |
| Temple or shrine     | Books, incense, medicine, religious goods   | Food, building materials, arms (if militant)   |
| Fortified stronghold | Arms, armor, military stores                | Food, luxury goods, craft goods, raw materials |

**Price multipliers by supply level:**

| Supply at Destination             | Sell Price Multiplier |
| --------------------------------- | --------------------- |
| Common (oversupplied)             | ×0.5                  |
| Common (neutral)                  | ×1.0                  |
| Uncommon (modest demand)          | ×1.5                  |
| Rare (strong demand)              | ×2.0                  |
| Extremely Rare (desperate demand) | ×2.5–3.0              |

These multipliers apply to the buy price at origin. The GM adjusts for local conditions — a famine settlement might pay ×3.0 for grain even if grain is normally Common. Seasonal scarcity (described below) applies to trade goods as much as to work contracts.

### Trade Rumors and Intelligence

When a character uses ASK AROUND at a settlement with a MARKETPLACE or among caravan folk, trade rumors are available as a reward for success. A successful roll reveals one or more of:

- **Demand rumor:** "Ironmere is desperate for salt — their mine ran dry last season." Indicates Rare supply at a specific settlement.
- **Supply rumor:** "The elves at Green Dale are selling pelts cheap — too many, too few buyers." Indicates Common supply at origin.
- **Hazard rumor:** "Bandits took a wagon on the Stillmire road last tenday." Indicates a specific route is dangerous.
- **Price rumor:** "Spices out of the Amber Crossing are double the normal rate since the pass closed." Price spike at a location.
- **Competition rumor:** "Three caravans already left for Hollowford this week." Destination market may be saturated.

Trade rumors travel along caravan routes at the speed described in the **JOURNEYS** chapter (10+ hexes per season on well-traveled roads). They age — only as current as the last trader who passed through.

## Route Planning and Travel

### Choosing a Route

Before departure, the caravan master chooses a route. This is a planning decision, not a roll. The players study the map and decide. Key route factors:

| Factor               | Effect                                                                                                                                |
| -------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Distance**         | More hexes = more days = more wages, feed, and hazard exposure.                                                                       |
| **Terrain**          | Road: full speed. Trail: half speed for wagons (pack animals unaffected). Off-road: pack animals only.                                |
| **Season**           | Winter: +50% travel time, doubled feed costs, increased hazard frequency. Spring thaw: rivers flood, fords close, barges run fast.    |
| **Known hazards**    | Bandit lairs, monster territories, toll stations, hostile settlements.                                                                |
| **Tolls**            | Settlements with TOLL HOUSE charge 1–3 silver per wagon or 1 silver per load unit. Safe passage through hostile territory costs more. |
| **Water and forage** | Rivers provide water. Grassland provides grazing. Deserts and mountains require carried supplies.                                     |
| **ROAD and BRIDGE**  | A stronghold-built ROAD reduces travel time by one quarter day per two hexes. A BRIDGE eliminates river-crossing delays and risks.    |

### Caravan Movement

A caravan moves at the speed of its slowest transport. A wagon caravan on a road moves two hexes per day (one hex per hiking quarter day). Pack mule caravans move slightly faster on good terrain but cannot exceed three hexes per day.

**Scout procedure.** A caravan with a scout can detect hazards one hex ahead. The scout spends a quarter day riding forward and uses SCOUTING against the terrain's base difficulty (the **JOURNEYS** chapter). Scout success grants reaction time to hazards — ambushes can be seen, routes can be changed, bandits can be tracked.

**Camp.** Caravans follow the standard Make Camp procedure. Large caravans (4+ wagons) require extra setup — one quarter day instead of one action. The camp is larger, louder, and easier to spot. Random encounter checks gain +1 to the hazard die while the caravan camps.

**March order.** Before each day's travel, the caravan master sets march order: scouts forward, cargo in the middle, guards distributed. March order matters when ambush or terrain events occur — it determines who is exposed first.

### River Travel

A caravan with a river barge (or a hired barge at 5 silver/day) can move along navigable rivers. Downstream travel gains +1 hex/day. Upstream travel suffers −1 hex/day. River travel avoids most land-based hazards (no bandits, no difficult terrain, no road tolls) but introduces its own: rapids, river pirates, grounding in shallows, and portage at waterfalls.

**Loading barges** requires a PIER at both origin and destination, or a half day of improvised loading at a riverbank (with breakage risk: roll 1D6 per load unit; cargo damage on a 1).

## Selling Cargo — The Market

When the caravan arrives at its destination, the caravan master sells the cargo. This is not automatic. It is a procedure that takes time and negotiation.

### The Market Roll

Selling cargo is a quarter-day activity. The caravan master (or a factor acting on their behalf) makes a MANIPULATION roll to represent the process: finding buyers, negotiating terms, displaying quality, and closing deals.

**Difficulty** is determined by cargo volume:

| Volume                            | Difficulty |
| --------------------------------- | ---------- |
| 1–2 load units (small trade)      | 1          |
| 3–5 load units (standard caravan) | 2          |
| 6–10 load units (large caravan)   | 3          |
| 11+ load units (major expedition) | 4          |

**Modifiers:**

| Factor                                              | Modifier |
| --------------------------------------------------- | -------- |
| Destination has a MARKETPLACE                       | +1       |
| Destination has an INN (buyers gather there)        | +1       |
| Caravan master has PATH OF TREASURE R2+             | +1       |
| Caravan master has PATH OF WORDS R3+                | +1       |
| Fellowship has local Standing +2 or higher          | +1       |
| Cargo is Rare or Extremely Rare at destination      | +1       |
| Cargo is Common at destination (oversupplied)       | −2       |
| Market recently saturated (3+ caravans this season) | −1       |
| Winter (reduced economic activity)                  | −1       |
| Fellowship has local Standing −1 or lower           | −1       |

**Results:**

| ⚔️    | Outcome                                                                                                                                                                                               |
| ----- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| No ⚔️ | Poor sale. Sell at ×0.75 of the calculated destination price. Buyers sense desperation or the goods do not match local need.                                                                          |
| 1 ⚔️  | Fair sale. Sell at ×1.0 of the destination price. Standard market rate.                                                                                                                               |
| 2 ⚔️  | Good sale. Sell at ×1.25. The caravan master found the right buyer or timed the offer well.                                                                                                           |
| 3+ ⚔️ | Excellent sale. Sell at ×1.5. Premium buyer, bidding competition, or perfect timing. The buyer may also offer a **standing order** — guaranteed purchase of the same goods next season at ×1.25 rate. |

**Standing orders** guarantee a sale at a specific settlement for a specific cargo type at ×1.25 price, valid until the end of the next season. If the caravan does not deliver before then, the order lapses. Standing orders reduce sale risk but bind the caravan to a route. PATH OF THE CARAVAN R3+ characters can hold up to three standing orders simultaneously across different settlements.

### Unsold Cargo

If the caravan cannot sell all goods, the remainder can be:

- **Stored** at the destination (requires warehouse space — 1 silver per load unit per week, or free if the fellowship owns a stronghold with a WAREHOUSE).
- **Carried onward** to the next destination, consuming load capacity and extending the journey.
- **Sold at loss** (×0.5 price) to a bulk buyer or barter agent.
- **Donated** for standing benefit (+1 Standing per 20 silver of goods).

## Profit and Loss

### The Accounting

At the end of a trade run, the caravan master calculates profit:

```text
PROFIT = Sale Revenue − (Cargo Cost + Wages + Feed + Tolls + Repairs + Losses)
```

| Cost           | Calculation                                                        |
| -------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| **Cargo cost** | Buy price × load units purchased                                   |
| **Wages**      | Daily rate × number of employees × days traveled                   |
| **Feed**       | 1 copper/day per pack animal or draft animal (waived in grassland) |
| **Tolls**      | Per wagon or load unit at each TOLL HOUSE on the route             |
| **Repairs**    | Crafting rolls and materials for vehicle damage                    |
| **Losses**     | Cargo lost to breakage, spoilage, theft, or hazard                 |

### Profit Margin Benchmarks

| Margin   | Verdict                                                                                                           |
| -------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Negative | Loss. The caravan spent more than it earned. Common on first runs, long routes, or dangerous journeys.            |
| 0–20%    | Break-even. Covered costs but barely worth the time. Acceptable for route establishment.                          |
| 20–40%   | Modest profit. A living wage for the peddler and crew. The standard for short, safe routes.                       |
| 40–60%   | Good profit. Well-planned, well-executed. Enough to buy a second wagon.                                           |
| 60–100%  | Excellent profit. Rare — high-demand goods, low competition, or smart selling.                                    |
| 100%+    | Windfall. Only possible with specialty goods at Extremely Rare prices, war demand, or market-beating information. |

### Reinvestment

Profit flows back into the system:

- **More transport.** Buy additional animals or wagons.
- **Better escorts.** Hire higher-tier fighters or a proper guard force.
- **Route expansion.** Explore new markets and establish standing orders.
- **Stronghold investment.** Fund MARKETPLACE, ROAD, BRIDGE, or WAREHOUSE functions.
- **Reputation spending.** Buy reputation through feasts (10–20 silver), donations, or sponsorships.

## Hazards on the Road

Each day of caravan travel, the GM rolls for hazards using the standard journey encounter rules (the **JOURNEYS** chapter). When a hazard result occurs, roll D66 on the following table:

| D66   | Hazard                 | Effect                                                                                                                                                                                       |
| ----- | ---------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| 11–12 | **Road damage**        | Broken axle, washed ford, fallen tree. Lose one quarter day. CRAFTING roll to repair; failure adds another quarter day.                                                                      |
| 13–14 | **Spoilage**           | Perishable cargo damaged by weather or road conditions. Lose 1D6 silver per load unit of perishable goods.                                                                                   |
| 15–16 | **Animal trouble**     | Lame mule, bolting horse, ox refuses passage. Lose half a quarter day. ANIMAL HANDLING roll to resolve; failure means the animal is useless for a day.                                       |
| 21–22 | **Toll dispute**       | Unexpected or inflated toll at a crossing. Pay 1D6 silver per wagon or MANIPULATION to negotiate. Failure means double toll or denied passage.                                               |
| 23–24 | **Breakage**           | Rough terrain damages fragile cargo. Roll breakage check for all fragile load units.                                                                                                         |
| 25–26 | **Bad weather**        | Storm, fog, or downpour. Lose one quarter day. Open cargo takes water damage (1D6 silver loss per unsheltered load unit).                                                                    |
| 31–32 | **Petty theft**        | Pilferage at camp or during a stop. Lose 2D6 silver of goods unless a guard posted (SCOUTING roll to detect the thief).                                                                      |
| 33–34 | **Rival caravan**      | Another caravan heads to the same destination with similar goods. When you arrive, the market roll takes a −1 modifier.                                                                      |
| 35–36 | **Bandit scouts**      | Riders watch the caravan from distance. Possible ambush ahead. SCOUTING roll to detect their number and position.                                                                            |
| 41–42 | **Bandit ambush**      | Armed attack. Use combat rules. Bandits target cargo first — breaking through the guard line, they cut loose 1D6 load units and flee.                                                        |
| 43–44 | **Predators**          | Wolves, bears, or worse. Target livestock first, then work animals. ANIMAL HANDLING rolls to control draft animals during the attack.                                                        |
| 45–46 | **Bridge out**         | A marked bridge is damaged or gone. Detour adds 1D6 quarter days, or CRAFTING roll to improvise a crossing (failure means cargo damage).                                                     |
| 51–52 | **Illness**            | Disease strikes an employee or animal. Cannot work for 1D6 days. HEALING roll to shorten recovery.                                                                                           |
| 53–54 | **Terrain obstacle**   | Rockslide, fallen tree, flooded ford, frozen pass. Choose: detour (1D6 quarter days) or clear (1 quarter day + MIGHT roll; failure is 2 quarter days).                                       |
| 55–56 | **Extortion**          | A warlord, bandit chief, or hostile settlement demands payment. 2D6 silver per wagon, or fight.                                                                                              |
| 61–62 | **Customs inspection** | A settlement or patrol demands to inspect cargo. Legal goods pass; restricted goods (arms in some territories, contraband) may be confiscated or fined.                                      |
| 63–64 | **Vehicle failure**    | Wheel or harness breaks. CRAFTING roll to field-repair. Failure means the vehicle is immobile until proper repair (half day + materials).                                                    |
| 65–66 | **Opportunity**        | A traveler, refugee, or stranded merchant on the road. They offer goods at bargain prices, intelligence about the route, or a delivery contract worth 1D6 × 5 silver at the next settlement. |

**Hazard frequency.** Larger caravans attract more attention: caravans of 4+ wagons add +1 to the encounter die; 8+ wagons add +2. Guarded caravans deter some threats: caravans with 3+ visible guards reduce bandit-related results by re-rolling them as the next entry down the D66 table. Bandits prefer easier targets. Season matters: winter adds +1 to weather-related hazard frequency; summer reduces animal trouble frequency (−1 to those rolls).

## Caravan Reputation

The Forbidden Lands recognizes traders and caravans much as it recognizes settlers and hired blades. A fellowship that runs caravans regularly builds a name — the **Caravan Circle**, a reputation separate from Settlement Standing.

Caravan Circle Reputation is a single score (0–11+) tracking how well known the fellowship is among factors, innkeepers, toll-keepers, and other traders along the routes they travel.

| Caravan Rep | Meaning                                                                   |
| ----------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| 0           | Unknown trader. No history.                                               |
| 1–2         | A few factors have heard the name.                                        |
| 3–4         | Known along one or two routes. Established among local traders.           |
| 5–6         | Reliable reputation across a region. Standing orders available.           |
| 7–8         | Strong trade name. Premium access at major markets. Factors seek you out. |
| 9–10        | Major trader. Your caravan's arrival is news. Markets adjust.             |
| 11+         | Legendary trader. Settlements compete for your route.                     |

### Growing Caravan Reputation

| Action                                                        | Effect                                              |
| ------------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------- |
| Complete a trade run at profit                                | +1 at destination settlement                        |
| Fulfill a standing order on time                              | +1 at that settlement                               |
| Deliver goods during crisis (famine, siege, plague)           | +1 to +2 at that settlement and Standing in the **JOURNEYS** chapter |
| Protect the route (clear bandits, repair bridges, mark roads) | +1 along the route (all settlements within 2 hexes) |
| Donate goods or fund MARKETPLACE or INN                       | +1 Standing and Caravan Circle                      |
| Cheat, short-change, or deliver spoiled goods                 | −1 to −2 Caravan Circle and Standing                |
| Abandon a standing order                                      | −1 Caravan Circle at that settlement                |
| Cargo stolen and not recovered                                | −1 Caravan Circle (reputation for vulnerability)    |

### Caravan Reputation Benefits

| Rep | Benefit                                                                                                                                                                                                                        |
| --- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| 3+  | Trade rumors from ASK AROUND gain +1 modifier when mentioning the caravan name.                                                                                                                                                |
| 5+  | Standing orders available without needing 3+ ⚔️ on the Market Roll. Factors and merchants approach with offers.                                                                                                                |
| 7+  | Buy price at regular origin settlements reduced 10% (volume discount). Sell price at regular destination settlements increased 10% (premium buyer trust).                                                                      |
| 9+  | Safe passage. Settlements along established routes maintain the road, warn of hazards, and refuse to harbor bandits who target the caravan. Equivalent to +1 Standing at every settlement within 2 hexes of the regular route. |

## Seasonal Economics

The Forbidden Lands economy shifts with the seasons. Demand, supply, risk, and profit all change. The caravan master who understands the cycle has an edge.

### Seasonal Cadence in Play

A **season** is the market interval, not the length of a single trade run. Resolve each caravan run in days and weeks: travel, sale, costs, and hazards. Apply seasonal multipliers by the current season at the point of sale. At each season boundary, refresh market conditions, rumor age, and contract windows.

One season is roughly three months (12 weeks), matching the cadence in **THE STRONGHOLD**.

| Typical One-Way Route Length   | Typical Runs Per Season |
| ------------------------------ | ----------------------- |
| 2–5 days between major stops   | 3–5 runs                |
| 6–10 days between major stops  | 2–3 runs                |
| 11–20 days between major stops | 1–2 runs                |

In winter, reduce expected runs by one bracket: travel takes longer and route risk rises.

| Season     | Economic Character                                                               | Trade Effect                                                                                                                                                                                                   |
| ---------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Spring** | Recovery and planting. Rivers flood, roads thaw, villages reopen.                | Grain demand peaks (spring hunger). River routes reopen. Road conditions poor — muddy, washed fords. Fresh goods commanded at premium prices.                                                                  |
| **Summer** | Growth and movement. Roads dry, caravans move freely, markets active.            | Best travel conditions. Competition highest — most caravans on the road. Prices normalize. Livestock trade peaks.                                                                                              |
| **Autumn** | Harvest and preparation. Grain floods markets, villages stock for winter.        | Grain prices lowest (harvest glut). Raw materials in high demand. Luxury goods spike (festivals, winter prep). Last safe window for long routes.                                                               |
| **Winter** | Contraction and scarcity. Snow closes passes, rivers freeze, villages shut down. | Travel costs +50%. Food and feed costs double. Many routes impassable. Prices spike at isolated settlements. Short routes between nearby settlements still viable. Bold traders profit most — if they survive. |

### Seasonal Price Multipliers

Apply these to the base sell price after the supply multiplier:

| Good            | Spring | Summer | Autumn | Winter |
| --------------- | ------ | ------ | ------ | ------ |
| Grain, food     | ×1.5   | ×1.0   | ×0.75  | ×2.0   |
| Raw materials   | ×1.0   | ×1.0   | ×1.25  | ×0.75  |
| Craft goods     | ×1.0   | ×1.0   | ×1.0   | ×1.25  |
| Luxury goods    | ×1.0   | ×1.0   | ×1.25  | ×1.5   |
| Arms, armor     | ×1.0   | ×1.0   | ×1.25  | ×1.0   |
| Livestock       | ×1.25  | ×1.0   | ×0.75  | ×1.5   |
| Medicine, herbs | ×1.0   | ×0.75  | ×1.0   | ×1.5   |

These stack with the supply multiplier. Grain that is Rare at a winter settlement sells at ×2.0 (supply) × ×2.0 (winter) = ×4.0 base price. That is how fortunes are made — and why bandits patrol the winter roads.

## Return Contracts and Passengers

A caravan that sells its cargo at the destination faces a choice: return empty (sunk cost), buy return cargo (new investment), or find return work (paid for the journey home).

### Return Contracts

| Contract               | Description                                              | Typical Pay            | Source                                                 |
| ---------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------ |
| **Freight**            | Carry goods from destination back to origin or elsewhere | 1–3 silver/load/day    | Merchants, factors, settlement authority               |
| **Mail and messages**  | Carry sealed letters or packages                         | 1–5 silver per message | Anyone. More valuable for distant or dangerous routes. |
| **Passengers**         | Escort a person or small group along the route           | 2–5 silver/person/day  | Travelers, pilgrims, officials, refugees               |
| **Specialty delivery** | Carry a specific item requiring careful handling         | 5–20+ silver           | Private clients, temples, guilds                       |

Return contracts are found through ASK AROUND or SEEK WORK at the destination. Settlements with a MARKETPLACE or INN always have at least one return contract available. Smaller settlements may have none.

**Passengers** occupy one load unit of space. They bring their own food or pay 5 copper/day for meals. Passengers are non-combatants unless otherwise stated — they add mouths but not swords.

## Stronghold Functions and Trade

Stronghold functions create lasting trade advantages. A fellowship that invests in infrastructure improves every future trade run on the affected routes.

| Function        | Trade Benefit                                                                                                            |
| --------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| **MARKETPLACE** | +1 to all Market Rolls at this stronghold. Attracts NPC caravans (passive income: 1D6 silver/week if staffed).           |
| **INN**         | +1 to Market Rolls. Generates trade rumors. Attracts travelers and return contracts.                                     |
| **ROAD**        | Reduces travel time on connected route by 1 quarter day per 2 hexes. Makes the route wagon-accessible.                   |
| **BRIDGE**      | Eliminates river-crossing hazards and delays on the route.                                                               |
| **PIER**        | Required for barge loading/unloading. Opens river trade. Piers connected to other piers create high-speed trade lanes.   |
| **TOLL HOUSE**  | Generates passive income from NPC traffic (1D6 silver/week if the road sees regular use). Provides traffic intelligence. |
| **STABLE**      | Reduces animal upkeep costs by 50% at this stronghold. Provides replacement animals at standard price.                   |
| **WAREHOUSE**   | Stores up to 20 load units of trade cargo at no weekly cost. Cargo stored here does not spoil.                           |

### New Function: WAREHOUSE

**Cost:** 200 wood, 100 stone. One HOUSING consumed.

**Effect:** Stores up to 20 load units of trade cargo in a secure, dry space. Stored goods do not spoil. The warehouse is the physical anchor of PATH OF THE CARAVAN's cache — a proper one, not a field depot.

**Upgrade — Large Warehouse:** 400 wood, 200 stone. Two HOUSING consumed. Stores up to 50 load units.

A stronghold with MARKETPLACE and WAREHOUSE becomes a trade hub. NPC caravans seek it out. Passive income from the marketplace increases 50% (1D6+3 silver/week).

## Simple Trade

Use this procedure when caravan trade is background work rather than the center of play.

**Pick a capital.** The character commits silver to goods before leaving. This is the cargo cost.

**Make a MANIPULATION roll** when the run ends. Difficulty 1 for a small load (up to 5 load units). Difficulty 2 for a medium load (6–15 units). Difficulty 3 for a large run (16+ units). Apply the usual modifiers for MARKETPLACE, supply tier, and Standing.

**Return on the roll:**

| ⚔️  | Profit                                                                                           |
| --- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| —   | Loss. Recover 75% of cargo cost. The run went badly — spoilage, bad prices, or a hostile market. |
| 1   | Thin margin. Recover 110% of cargo cost. Wages and feed eat most of it.                          |
| 2   | Decent run. Recover 130% of cargo cost.                                                          |
| 3+  | Good trade. Recover 150% of cargo cost.                                                          |

Subtract wages, feed, and tolls from the profit before counting gains. A drover and two guards on a two-week run cost roughly 15–20 silver in wages; fodder for three animals adds another 10–12 silver.

This resolves a trade run in a few minutes between sessions. Use the full system when caravan trade is central to play.
