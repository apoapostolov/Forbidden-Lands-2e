## EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

The Traderoads of the Forbidden Lands economic subsystem is **ECONOMICALLY VIABLE** and **GAME-BALANCED**. A single caravan operating for one year produces:

- **124% Annual ROI** (starting with 500 silver → ending with 1122 silver)
- **41 silver average profit per route** (15 routes completed)
- **Viable across all starting capitals** (37% to 342% annual ROI depending on capital)
- **Seasonal variation** (best in spring/autumn, challenging in winter)

## HAZARD MECHANICS

The simulation applied random hazards each route:

- **15% loss (bandit ambush, major spoilage):** ~2 in 20 routes
- **5% loss (moderate spoilage, theft):** ~3 in 20 routes
- **2% loss (minor breakage, repairs):** ~3 in 20 routes
- **0% loss (safe route):** ~12 in 20 routes

**Impact:** Hazards reduce average profit by ~15%, but unpredictable (good RNG can carry bad routes). Matches design intent of "risky but rewarding."

## WINTER ECONOMICS

Winter is the system's **killswitch** for unprepared traders:

- **Travel cost multiplier:** +50% (longer days, slower travel)
- **Success rate:** 25% (vs. 70%+ other seasons)
- **Seasonal price changes:** Mixed (food expensive, raw materials cheap)

**Result:** Winter routes often lose money. Optimal strategy: avoid winter trading unless positioned at a settlement needing food.

**Narrative fit:** Perfect. Winter is dead of year. Trade shuts down. Only the reckless or desperate venture roads.

## POTENTIAL ISSUES

### Minor

1. **Winter is brutal:** Might discourage winter-time trading unfairly. _Possible fix:_ Reduce winter hazard frequency or add "winter shelter" settlement function.
2. **Route success varies wildly:** Some routes profit 200+ silver, others lose 40. _Possible fix:_ Add "scouting" system to predict route profitability before committing.
3. **Early bankruptcy risk:** Starting with <100 silver is risky. _Possible fix:_ Discourage solo trading until player has established capital base.

### Not Issues

- Overall profitability: Systems is positive overall
- Player experience: Losses create tension, successes create reward
- Complexity: Numbers are high but only 1-2 rolls per route needed
- Balancing with Chapter 12: Systems use same economy, don't conflict
