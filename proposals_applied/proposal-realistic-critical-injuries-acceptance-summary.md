# Proposal - Realistic Critical Injuries Acceptance Summary

Purpose: identify which parts of `proposal-realistic-critical-injuries.md` should move into `corebook/06-critical-injuries.md` now, and which parts should remain in proposal space until a broader rewrite is approved.

## Accept Now

These parts fit the current Chapter 6 framework and can be integrated without rebuilding the rest of the manuscript.

### 1. The core `65` / `66` split for physical injuries

Accept:

- `65` becomes survivable catastrophe rather than automatic death
- `66` stays immediate death or bodily destruction beyond field rescue
- the victim still needs a normal save-their-life `HEALING` roll if the entry is lethal
- on a successful save, `65` sends the result to a matching permanent injury subtable

Reason:

- this is the heart of the proposal
- it fits the existing slash, stab, blunt, and burn structure
- it creates a stronger spectrum than the current "alive, dead, or small penalty" spread

### 2. Permanent injury subtables for slash, stab, blunt, and burn

Accept in principle, with line editing during integration:

- `Permanent Injuries - Slash`
- `Permanent Injuries - Stab`
- `Permanent Injuries - Blunt`
- `Permanent Injuries - Burn`

Reason:

- these are the direct payoff for the new `65` result
- they distinguish damage types in a way the current chapter does not
- they now use more consistent manuscript-facing language and clearer hooks

Editorial caution:

- keep only entries whose effects map cleanly to current rules
- if an entry still feels too fiddly in play, simplify it while preserving the same severity band

### 3. The retirement-default guidance for catastrophic survivors

Accept as a short sidebar or editorial note, not as a hard subsystem.

Reason:

- the proposal now correctly distinguishes "alive" from "practical adventurer"
- this avoids dishonest table language
- it gives players a clear and humane off-ramp when a result is technically survivable but no longer fun

Best placement:

- beside permanent injuries, prosthetics, or recovery text in Chapter 6
- or as a short boxed note tied to permanent critical injuries

### 4. Compatibility notes with existing recovery tools

Accept in editorial form:

- `LUCKY` rank `4`
- `PHYSICIAN` rank `3`
- `MEND WOUNDS`
- `REGENERATION`

Reason:

- these interactions must be stated clearly during integration or the chapter will contradict Chapters 4 and 7

## Accept With Simplification

These parts are useful, but should be trimmed during manuscript integration.

### 1. Severity-band framing

Accept the idea of severity bands, but do not necessarily print the band table in the final rules text.

Reason:

- useful for design and editing
- not strictly needed at the table once the actual subtables are written cleanly

### 2. Prosthetics and collapse-state guidance

Accept the tone and intent, but fold it into concise prose.

Reason:

- the manuscript needs guidance
- the current proposal wording is better as design support than final copy

## Reject For Now

These should not move into `corebook/06-critical-injuries.md` in the current pass.

### 1. New non-typical parent tables

Reject for now:

- `Critical Injuries - Poison`
- `Critical Injuries - Acid / Corrosion`
- `Critical Injuries - Cold / Freeze`
- `Critical Injuries - Swallow`
- their associated permanent injury subtables

Reason:

- current Chapter 6 still routes most non-typical physical harm through Burn
- poison already has its own handling elsewhere in the chapter
- integrating these tables cleanly would require wider updates across spells, monsters, hazards, and references outside this chapter

### 2. Replacement horror tables

Reject for now:

- `Critical Injuries - Horror`
- `Permanent Trauma - Horror`

Reason:

- the horror table is a larger tonal and systemic rewrite than the physical `65` fix
- it deserves its own focused acceptance discussion instead of being smuggled in under the injury proposal

### 3. Design-support text that belongs in proposal space

Reject for direct manuscript integration:

- long design-principle sections
- future-expansion gateway logic
- extensive rationale prose explaining why the proposal works

Reason:

- useful in proposal history
- not final-rules material

## Recommended Integration Order

If this proposal is accepted in part, integrate it in this order:

1. rewrite the parent physical tables so `65` routes to permanent subtables and `66` remains terminal
2. add the four permanent physical subtables
3. add a brief compatibility note for healing, physician care, luck, and regeneration
4. add a short compassionate retirement sidebar or note

## Bottom Line

Move now:

- the physical `65` rework
- the four physical permanent injury subtables
- the compatibility clarifications
- a short retirement-default note

Hold back:

- the new poison / acid / cold / swallow framework
- the horror rewrite
- all wider expansion material

That keeps the accepted manuscript change sharp, coherent, and proportionate to the actual problem being solved.
