# Long-Reach Engagement Simplification

## Audit Verdict

`LONG-REACH` still carries too much of a free `BRACE` effect.

The current chapter gives long weapons three things at once:

- a distance advantage
- a close-in penalty for the enemy
- a built-in punishment when the enemy fails to close

That last part is the problem. `BRACE` now exists as the clean, intentional receive-the-rush rule. If `LONG-REACH` keeps its own passive punish-on-fail behavior, the two rules blur together and the polearm stance loses its job.

## What The Rule Rewards

The current `LONG-REACH` package rewards control of space. That is good.

The issue is that it rewards control of space twice:

- once through reach itself
- once through a reactive strike for failed approach

That second reward is what makes the feature feel like a hidden `BRACE`.

## What Players Will Believe It Rewards

Players will read `LONG-REACH` as:

- the best weapon length for preventing contact
- a passive trap on anyone who tries to move in
- a safer version of `BRACE` without the action cost

That perception is unstable. Once players see the passive punish, they will stop treating `BRACE` as a distinct choice and start treating it as a redundant flourish.

## What The GM Must Manage

The GM has to solve one of two problems:

- either enemies avoid closing on long weapons and the fight stalls
- or the GM ignores the passive punish and the rule becomes soft fiction

Neither outcome is ideal. The rule is strong enough to shape enemy behavior, but not clean enough to stay fun without repeated judgment.

## Design Read

`LONG-REACH` should own distance.

`BRACE` should own the deliberate receive-the-rush attack.

`INTERCEPT` should own the general ready action.

Those jobs are separate. They should not bleed into one another.

## Proposed Revision

Strip the passive punish from `LONG-REACH`. Keep the distance rules.

### Keep

- `LONG-REACH` fighters attack from `_Near_`
- enemies must still cut in to close
- closing remains harder against a long weapon
- range still matters in normal play

### Remove

- the automatic strike when an enemy fails to close
- any language that makes `LONG-REACH` behave like a free `BRACE`

## Draft Manuscript Text

**LONG-REACH:** This weapon uses the long-reach engagement rules. It fights from _Near_ range and applies a -1 penalty to MOVE rolls. Enemies must cut in to reach you, and if they fail they stay outside your reach until they try again.

## Why This Is Better

This keeps the weapon length meaningful without handing out a free reactive attack.

The result is cleaner:

- `LONG-REACH` sets the reach game
- `BRACE` gives the polearm fighter the sharp, dramatic anti-charge stance
- `INTERCEPT` stays the generic ready action

## Recommendation

Accept this simplification and move the receive-the-rush pressure fully onto `BRACE` and `INTERCEPT`.

If the passive punish stays in `LONG-REACH`, the chapter keeps two rules for the same job and the polearm identity gets split across them instead of sharpened.
