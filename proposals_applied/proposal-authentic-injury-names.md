# Proposal - Authentic Critical Injury Names

Goal: keep the existing Forbidden Lands 2E critical injury mechanics, effects, and healing times, but rename entries so the names sound plausible for the amount of time they actually take to heal in the game while still fitting the hard, low-tech voice of the setting.

This proposal does **not** change any dice, penalties, lethality, or healing times. It only changes the labels.

## Core Problem

Several current names sound like permanent mutilations or major surgical trauma, but the table then says the injury heals in `D6`, `2D6`, or `3D6` days. That breaks verisimilitude.

Examples:

- `Severed finger` healing in `D6`
- `Broken arm` healing in `2D6`
- `Severed nose` healing in `D6`
- `Gouged eye` healing in `2D6`

Those names imply permanent loss or long-term disability. The mechanics beneath them often imply something more like a bad cut, a wrenched joint, a deep bruise, a torn sinew, or a hard thrust that missed the worst of the flesh.

## Naming Rule

Use injury names that match the table's recovery band.

| Healing Time | Naming Band | Appropriate Language |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `—` | Momentary / cosmetic | stunned, winded, nicked, bruised, scraped |
| `D6` days | Minor but real injury | split, gashed, pierced, wrenched, strained, badly bruised, scratched eye |
| `2D6` days | Moderate trauma | deep gash, torn sinew, bad wrench, cracked rib, deep thrust, ugly bruise |
| `3D6` days | Serious but still non-permanent trauma | fracture, severe crush, snapped sinew, torn cord, smashed joint |
| `Permanent` | True maiming | severed, destroyed, amputated, crushed joint, lost eye, permanent paralysis |

## Design Standard

For authenticity:

- `Severed`, `amputated`, `destroyed`, and `ruptured` should generally be reserved for `Permanent` or near-death results.
- `Broken` should usually imply at least `3D6` unless it is a tiny bone with mostly functional recovery.
- `Gouged eye` and similar names should be reserved for permanent or near-permanent loss.
- `D6` and `2D6` entries should mostly use plain, bodily language: split, gashed, torn, wrenched, bruised, pierced.
- If the effect is temporary but severe, name the temporary structure that is damaged, not the entire body part as lost.

## Recommended Editorial Principle

When the game wants a temporary injury, the label should describe:

- what flesh is hurt,
- what part of life is hindered,
- and what degree of harm fits the listed recovery time.

It should not describe the body part as permanently lost unless the rules actually make it permanent.

## Suggested Manuscript Policy

Use these naming filters when editing Chapter 6:

1. If it heals in `D6`, the name should usually sound superficial, partial, or moderate.
2. If it heals in `2D6`, the name can sound serious, but not permanently mutilating.
3. If it heals in `3D6`, fractures, ruptures, and heavy crush injuries become acceptable.
4. If it is `Permanent`, then severed, destroyed, amputated, and similar language is appropriate.
5. If a result is lethal but still heals in days after successful treatment, the name should describe a survivable severe wound, not a body part already fully lost.

## Why This Works Better

- The tables stop sounding cartoonishly fatal.
- The fiction matches the mechanical recovery window.
- Serious permanent injuries remain distinct and properly feared.
- The game keeps its harsh tone without accidentally implying miraculous regrowth every few days.

## Reference Notes

This proposal is based on broad real-world recovery bands:

- minor cuts and grazes start healing within days
- sprains, strains, and bruising usually take weeks rather than months
- broken noses often take several weeks
- concussions can range from days to weeks
