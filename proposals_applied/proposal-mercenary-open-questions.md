# Proposal — Mercenary Chapter Open Questions

**Source:** Part 6, Open Questions from `proposal-mercenaries-chapter-audit.md`

**Status:** Draft — six design decisions required

## 1. Can a Caster Be Appointed Sergeant?

**Question:** The chapter does not address whether a hired caster can fill the Sergeant role. A caster with high EMP and MANIPULATION could theoretically do the work.

**Proposal: No.**

The Sergeant role requires two things a caster typically cannot provide: presence in the shield wall and credibility with the rank and file. Common fighters follow a sergeant because they have seen that sergeant hold a line, drag a wounded man clear, and make the call that kept five men alive when the flank collapsed. A caster who was behind the line casting HEAL or LIGHTNING when those moments happened does not carry the same authority — not because of prejudice, but because the lived experience is different.

A caster who wants to influence the band's direction does so through the advisory channel already implied by the Steward role. A captain who values their caster's counsel grants them a voice at council. That voice can be decisive without carrying the title.

**Rule text (for Section 7, as a note under Caster Loyalty):**

> A hired caster cannot be appointed Sergeant. The Sergeant must come from the Named Men — fighters who hold the line and are seen holding it. A caster who advises the captain does so as a counselor, not an officer. Their influence is real but informal.

**Placement:** Add as a final paragraph under the Caster Loyalty subsection in Section 7.

## 2. Can Named Men Have Multiple Triggers?

**Question:** Each Named Man is assigned one Trigger. Can a GM assign a second to a Named Man with a complex history? If so, does each fire independently?

**Proposal: Yes, with limits.**

A Named Man who has served through two or more seasons of active contracts may acquire a second Trigger — either through play (a witnessed event that creates a new breaking point) or at the GM's discretion when the character's history justifies it. The second Trigger is recorded alongside the first.

Both Triggers fire independently. If an event touches both Triggers simultaneously, only the more severe consequence applies — the Named Man does not break twice in a single moment.

The cap is two. A Named Man with three alleged Triggers is not complex — they are unstable. The GM should retire them from the band or promote them to a recurring NPC who appears in the region but no longer serves.

**Rule text (for Section 6, after the Trigger table):**

> **Second Triggers.** A Named Man who has served the band through two or more seasons may acquire a second Trigger — through play or at the GM's discretion. Both Triggers fire independently. If a single event touches both, only the more severe consequence applies. No Named Man carries more than two Triggers. A man with that many breaking points is not reliable enough to keep under arms.

**Placement:** Add after the existing Trigger table or guidance in Section 6.

## 3. Does the Warmaster Pay Himself from Host Treasury?

**Question:** No wage line exists for the Warmaster. If the Warmaster is a PC, this needs a rule.

**Proposal: Yes — the Warmaster draws a fixed share.**

The Warmaster draws a share equal to the highest-paid band captain's weekly rate, taken from the treasury before allocation. This is not a salary negotiated with anyone — it is the traditional cost of the office. A Warmaster who takes more is embezzling. A Warmaster who takes nothing is either a saint or hiding a different income, and the captains will wonder which.

If the Warmaster is a PC, state the share at the table when the Host is formed. The share is deducted from treasury automatically each week. If the treasury cannot cover the Warmaster's share and full allocation to all bands, the Warmaster's share is the first thing deferred — a Warmaster who pays himself while the bands go short loses 1 Ledger per week this continues.

**Rule text (for Section 10, under Host Treasury):**

> **Warmaster's Share.** The Warmaster draws a weekly share equal to the highest-paid band captain's rate, taken from treasury before allocation. If the treasury cannot cover both the share and full band pay, the Warmaster's share defers first. A Warmaster who draws their share while bands are on deferred pay loses 1 Ledger per week until the deferral is resolved or the share is suspended.

**Placement:** Add under the Host Treasury section in Section 10, after the strategic reserve paragraph.

## 4. Can Host Treasury Favor One Band?

**Question:** The Warmaster's authority over treasury allocation is described but not limited. Can a Warmaster pre-allocate a bonus to one band at the expense of another?

**Proposal: Yes, but it is visible and costs Ledger.**

A Warmaster may direct a bonus allocation to any single band — extra coin for a difficult contract, hazard pay for a dangerous route, resupply after a costly engagement. The bonus draws from the Host treasury's operating pool, not from another band's allocation directly. But the other captains see the ledger. They talk.

Each bonus allocation that visibly favors one band costs 1 Ledger with every other band captain who learns about it. A MANIPULATION roll at difficulty 2 keeps the allocation discreet for one season. On failure, the other captains know, and the Ledger loss is immediate.

If a Warmaster directs bonus allocations to the same band twice in one season without a clear operational justification (contract terms, emergency resupply), the bonused band's captain gains +1 to their next Host Council authority roll and every other captain gains a GRIEVANCE.

**Rule text (for Section 10, under Band Budgets):**

> **Bonus Allocations.** The Warmaster may direct a bonus allocation to a single band from the operating pool. Each bonus that visibly favors one band costs the Warmaster 1 Ledger with every other band captain. A MANIPULATION roll at difficulty 2 keeps the bonus discreet for one season; on failure, every captain learns and the Ledger loss is immediate. Two bonuses to the same band in one season, without clear operational need, grants the favored captain +1 to their next Council authority roll and gives every other captain a GRIEVANCE.

**Placement:** Add under the Band Budgets and the Purser section in Section 10.

## 5. Guard Hireling → Named Man Promotion

**Question:** Can a GUARD from the stronghold chapter be promoted to Named Man status? Or are they permanently Common-tier?

**Proposal: Yes, with a transition.**

A GUARD hireling who has fought alongside the band through two engagements and survived both may be promoted to Named Man. The promotion requires one session of downtime where the captain (or a Named Man acting on the captain's behalf) invests time in the fighter — assessing their capability, learning their history, and assigning them a place in the band's hierarchy.

On promotion, the GUARD's old stat block is replaced by the Named Man stat block for their assigned Role and Tier (Veteran). They receive a Loyalty score (start at 1), a Trigger (rolled or assigned), and an Agenda (chosen by the GM based on what has been observed during play). Their name and history carry over. They are no longer a hireling — they are on the band's roster and subject to Named Men rules.

The promotion costs nothing in coin, but the band now pays Named Man rates for a man who was previously cheaper. That is the trade: better fighter, higher cost, more political weight in the band.

**Rule text (for Section 6, under Creating a Named Man):**

> **Promotion from Hireling.** A GUARD hireling who has survived two engagements alongside the band may be promoted to Named Man. The captain spends one session of downtime assessing the fighter and assigning them a role. On promotion, the GUARD adopts the Veteran stat block for their assigned Role, receives Loyalty 1, and is assigned a Trigger and Agenda. Their hireling contract ends — they are now on the band's roster at Named Man rates.

**Placement:** Add after the Creating a Named Man subsection in Section 6.

## 6. Multiple Call Names

**Question:** Section 9 implies one Call Name per character. Can a character with a long career earn a second? Which do settlements recognize?

**Proposal: Yes — a second name replaces the first mechanically.**

A character may earn a second Call Name through a deed that eclipses the first. The conditions are the same: witnessed by three company members, sponsored by a Named Man. The new name replaces the old for all mechanical purposes — it is the name settlements recognize, the name that opens or closes gates, the name that appears in bounty postings.

The old name does not vanish. Men who served with the character when they earned it still use it at fires and in private. It becomes lore — a marker of history, not authority. But settlements, employers, and strangers know only the most recent name. A man called the Door who later earns the Wall is the Wall to everyone who was not there when the door mattered.

A character cannot carry two Call Names simultaneously for mechanical purposes. The game does not track dual reputations — it tracks the current one.

**Rule text (for Section 9, after the Call Name section):**

> **Second Call Names.** A character who earns a second Call Name replaces the first for all mechanical purposes. Settlements, employers, and bounty postings recognize the newer name. The old name survives as lore — spoken at fires by men who were there, but no longer the name that opens gates. A character carries one Call Name at a time.

**Placement:** Add after the existing Call Name subsection in Section 9.
