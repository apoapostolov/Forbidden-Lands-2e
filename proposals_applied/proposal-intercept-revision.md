# INTERCEPT Audit And Revision Proposal

## Verdict

`INTERCEPT` is usable, but in its current form it creates two problems in the combat engine:

1. it is a very strong anti-approach button because the attack cannot be parried or dodged
2. it overlaps too much with `BRACE`, especially now that `BRACE` also answers movement into reach

The rule does not break combat by itself, but it pushes the system toward a hard denial posture that can narrow enemy behavior and force the GM to work around the rule rather than play against it.

## What The Rule Rewards

The current action rewards a fighter who is willing to give up both actions in exchange for one guaranteed strike before an enemy completes movement or closes for a shot.

That is a clear payoff. The problem is that the payoff is too clean.

Because the intercepted attack cannot be `PARRIED` or `DODGED`, the target has very little room to answer it once the stance is set. That makes `INTERCEPT` feel less like a reaction and more like a preloaded punishment for movement.

## What Players Will Probably Believe It Rewards

Players will read it as:

- a way to punish anyone who tries to close
- a universal overwatch stance
- a reliable ranged denial tool

That expectation is not wrong, but it is stronger than the combat chapter needs. The game already has `BRACE` for the classic receive-the-charge moment and `LOCK SHIELDS` for holding a line. `INTERCEPT` should not become the best answer to every approach.

## What The GM Will Need To Manage

The GM will need to manage:

- enemy reluctance to move into melee at all
- repeated soft-countering of `INTERCEPT` by refusing to advance
- a temptation to make some foes ignore the rule entirely so combat keeps moving

That is a warning sign. A good rule changes behavior because it is strong in the fiction. A bad rule changes behavior because the table has to work around it.

## Actual Balance Risks

### Action compression

`INTERCEPT` spends both actions, but it still compresses offense and defense into one prepared moment. In practice, that is a premium effect, not a basic one.

### Reaction pressure

It creates a very strong reaction window against movement, and the attack cannot be answered once triggered. That can make the first fighter to close feel like they were never allowed to play.

### Niche overlap

`BRACE` now covers the polearm receive-the-rush fantasy in a cleaner way. `INTERCEPT` no longer has a sharply different job unless it is narrowed.

### Table behavior

The rule is strongest when the GM and players both remember it perfectly. That makes it a high-memory, high-pressure exception. Those are usually acceptable only if they create a very distinct role.

## Proposed Revision

Keep `INTERCEPT`, but make it a normal reactive strike instead of an uncounterable one.

### Revised rule intent

- spend both actions to set a single intercept
- when the trigger you are prepared for happens, you may make one attack before the enemy's action resolves
- that attack resolves normally and can be `PARRIED` or `DODGED` if the enemy could see it coming
- if the attack hits, the enemy stops where the blow lands unless they spend `1 WP` to force through
- you still cannot `PARRY` or `DODGE` until your next turn

## Draft Manuscript Text

**INTERCEPT:** You prepare a reactive stance and wait for the opening. During your turn, you spend both your FAST and SLOW actions. Until the start of your next turn, when the trigger you are prepared for happens, you may make one **ATTACK** or **SHOOT** roll before the enemy's action resolves. The attack can be **PARRIED** or **DODGED** as usual if the enemy could see it coming; if you catch them from behind a corner or from another blind angle, they cannot react in time. If the attack deals any 🩸, the target must stop where the blow lands unless they spend 1 WP to press through. You can do this only once per round. After using **INTERCEPT**, you cannot **PARRY** or **DODGE** until the start of your next turn.

## Why This Revision Fits

This version keeps the rule useful in the current combat engine:

- it still punishes careless movement
- it still gives non-polearm fighters a defensive answer
- it still feels like a real commitment

But it removes the part most likely to distort table behavior:

- the strike is no longer automatic pressure with no answer
- `BRACE` keeps the polearm identity
- `INTERCEPT` becomes a general reactive stance instead of the best possible one

## Recommendation

Promote a simplified `INTERCEPT` revision before treating the current text as stable.

If the current text stays unchanged, the likely long-term result is not mathematical collapse. It is play-pattern drift: players will lean on `INTERCEPT` as a default anti-approach line, and the GM will have to route around it.
