## Purpose

Use this reference when several enemies should act as a meaningful force rather
than as a queue of separate minor turns.

This module distinguishes four scales:

- **individuals**, who use complete NPC or monster rules
- **minions**, who are individually fragile and activated in small groups
- **bands**, which share initiative but remain separate bodies
- **troops**, which are resolved as one collective combatant with cohesion,
  formation, and mass attacks

The purpose is not to place more attacks on the table. The purpose is to make
numbers create different tactical problems: enclosing, screening, suppressing,
carrying, holding ground, overrunning, rescuing, deserting, and obeying orders.

## Scale Models

### Individual

Use full attributes, skills, gear, actions, and injury rules. Choose this scale
for named foes, specialists, officers, beasts with distinct behavior, and any
enemy whose personal survival matters.

### Minion

Use an ordinary NPC or lesser-creature profile with simplified defeat. Minions
occupy space as individuals but activate in a small group. Choose this scale
when each body matters fictionally but should not require a complete damage
track.

### Band

Use one shared initiative for 2-6 identical enemies. Each retains its own
Strength, position, and actions. Choose this scale when ordinary combat rules
already produce the desired behavior and the only problem is handling time.

### Troop

Use one collective Strength track and one turn for a mass of roughly 6-20
similar enemies. Choose this scale when cohesion, frontage, morale, and orders
matter more than individual blows.

### Horde or host

Do not assign one enormous troop statblock to dozens or hundreds of enemies.
Represent a horde as several troops, each with a role, route, and morale. At
larger scales, treat the host as a campaign or site pressure and resolve only
the part the adventurers can meaningfully affect.

## Choosing An Abstraction

Ask what the scene is about.

| Scene Question | Best Model |
| --- | --- |
| Can we capture this scout alive? | individual |
| Can the hero cut through disposable guards to reach the ritualist? | minions |
| Can we outfight four ordinary raiders? | band |
| Can we break the shield line before reinforcements arrive? | troop |
| Can the settlement survive the army? | several troops plus campaign objectives |

Do not change scale during a fight without a clear trigger. A troop may fragment
into individuals when Broken, when scattered through several zones, or when an
adventurer enters an interior objective where only a handful can follow.

## Minions

### Minion rule

A minion uses a normal profile but has one point of effective Strength. Any
unprevented Strength damage defeats it. The GM decides whether a defeated
minion is dead, Broken, unconscious, trapped, or fleeing according to the
attack and fiction.

Minions:

- do not roll critical injuries
- cannot push rolls unless they are individually important
- use ordinary armor, dodge, and parry
- can perform ordinary actions and help one another
- do not gain attack dice merely because more minions exist

This rule changes durability only. It does not make weak people foolish,
suicidal, or morally insignificant.

### Group activation

Activate 2-5 identical minions together. They share initiative and declare
their actions before resolving them. Resolve movement as a group, then resolve
attacks. Use one roll only when they all attempt the same collective task
against the same obstacle.

### Combined minion attack

When 2-4 minions at ARM'S LENGTH attack the same target in the same segment,
one leads the attack and each additional minion grants +1 Skill Die, up to +3.
Resolve one hit and one damage total. The combined attack cannot multiply
critical injuries, poison exposures, forced movement, or special riders.

Use separate attacks when the minions have different positions, weapons,
targets, or intentions.

### Minion pressure roles

Give a minion group one role.

| Role | Behavior |
| --- | --- |
| Screen | block routes, intercept, take cover, and protect a leader |
| Harrier | force retreats, deny easy aim, and pursue separated prey |
| Bearer | carry ammunition, wounded allies, ritual objects, or loot |
| Binder | use nets, hooks, poles, chains, or grapples to set up another foe |
| Watcher | raise alarms, mark targets, hold lights, or maintain signals |
| Feeder | bring corpses, fuel, blood, or captives to a monster or engine |

Minions become tactically distinct through jobs, not through stacks of special
bonuses.

### Cleaving through minions

If an attack plausibly sweeps an area and inflicts at least one damage after
armor, one extra ⚔️ beyond the first may defeat one additional minion within
the attack's reach, up to the attacker's weapon or effect limit. Do not apply
this to precise stabbing attacks unless the fiction supports passing through
several bodies.

This option lets strong attacks feel strong without granting a free new action.

### Minion surrender and flight

Check morale when half the group has been defeated, its leader falls, or its
purpose becomes impossible. Most human minions seek cover, flee, surrender, or
drag away comrades. Fanatics, enthralled servants, and mindless dead need a
stated reason to ignore morale.

## Bands

A band is the lightest abstraction and should be the default for several normal
enemies.

### Band procedure

- Roll initiative once for identical members.
- Declare the band's shared intention first.
- Move members together where practical.
- Roll separately for attacks that target different adventurers.
- Track Strength and conditions per member.
- Roll one morale decision for the band.

### Band maneuvers

Use ordinary actions to create coordinated behavior.

- Two members LOCK SHIELDS while another attacks from behind them.
- One uses HELP while another grapples or shoots.
- One forces a RETREAT while another occupies the opened route.
- One draws attention while another moves through cover.
- Several brace a narrow passage rather than all making attacks.

Do not invent a special bonus when the core HELP, cover, grapple, brace, and
positioning rules already express the tactic.

## Troops

A troop is a collective enemy. It has a footprint, Strength, Armor Rating,
morale, movement, and a short attack table or action list.

### Troop stat line

Use this minimum form:

`TROOP: Name; Size; Strength current/maximum; Armor Rating; Morale; Movement;
Formation; Attacks; Weakness or disruption condition.`

Morale is the force's dice rating under the optional **MORALE & ROUT** rule in
the core combat chapter. Six dice is the hardened baseline. Record a short
motive or profile beside the number when its likely response is not obvious.

### Size

Use three practical sizes.

| Size | Approximate Bodies | Footprint | Base Strength Guideline |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Small | 6-8 | part of one zone | 6-10 |
| Standard | 9-14 | one zone or a narrow line across two | 10-16 |
| Large | 15-20 | one broad zone or two connected zones | 15-22 |

Strength is cohesion and fighting capacity, not a literal count of living
bodies. Damage may represent casualties, panic, lost frontage, broken shields,
scattered members, or exhausted discipline.

### Troop activation

A troop receives one initiative and normally performs one slow and one fast
action. A mass attack is a slow action. Movement, reforming, receiving an order,
seizing an objective, or changing formation may consume the fast or slow action
as stated.

Do not grant one attack per represented member.

### Troop footprint

A troop occupies enough space to matter.

- It can block a narrow border or fill a small zone.
- Enemies at ARM'S LENGTH are engaged with the troop, not every body in it.
- Moving through an unbroken hostile troop requires a stated gap, successful
  maneuver, special ability, or breaking its formation.
- Area effects that cover most of the footprint may receive a troop-specific
  benefit stated in the attack or troop profile.

### Attacking a troop

Resolve attacks normally against the troop's Armor Rating. One attack can
normally inflict no more than 2 Strength damage to a troop unless it is:

- an area attack covering much of the footprint
- an explosive, collapsing, burning, trampling, or similarly mass-relevant
  effect
- an attack made against a declared weak formation or trapped troop
- a monster attack whose scale clearly reaches several bodies

The cap prevents one dueling strike from erasing a formation while preserving
the importance of fire, terrain, ambush, and large monsters.

### Troop Strength bands

Use current Strength to determine cohesion.

| Current Strength | State | Effect |
| --- | --- | --- |
| more than half | Ordered | full attack list and formation benefits |
| one quarter to half | Shaken | lose the highest attack results or one formation benefit |
| less than one quarter | Fragmenting | only core attacks, withdrawal, surrender, or desperate objective actions |
| zero | Broken | routs, surrenders, scatters, or is destroyed according to fiction |

Use D10/D8/D6 attack access if the troop has a numbered table: D10 while
Ordered, D8 while Shaken, and D6 while Fragmenting. Place formation-dependent
attacks at 7-10 and basic attacks, withdrawal, and self-protection at 1-6.

### Being inside a troop

An adventurer who deliberately cuts into a troop's footprint is surrounded
unless allies open a route or the formation is Disordered. The adventurer may
attack vulnerable members, a leader, banner, drum, beast handler, or other
interior target, but loses easy retreat and may be selected by close mass
attacks.

Make entering the formation a high-agency risk, not automatic damage. State
the route and what objective can be reached.

## Formation States

A troop uses one formation state at a time. Changing formation normally costs
a fast action if the troop is Ordered and a slow action if Shaken. A Fragmenting
troop cannot adopt a complex formation without a successful command intervention.

### Open order

**Purpose:** move through rough ground, reduce vulnerability to area effects,
and screen a broad area.

**Tradeoff:** loses dense melee and shield benefits.

### Shield line

**Purpose:** hold a border and resist frontal missiles or charges.

**Tradeoff:** slow to turn, weak from rear or flank, vulnerable to disrupted
footing and attacks from above.

### Spear hedge

**Purpose:** deny charges and long-reach approaches.

**Tradeoff:** requires clear frontage and breaks if entered from a flank or
forced into cramped ground.

### Column

**Purpose:** move through roads, gates, tunnels, and bridges.

**Tradeoff:** limited frontage and vulnerable if the route is blocked at both
ends.

### Encircling ring

**Purpose:** contain one target, prisoner group, beast, or ritual site.

**Tradeoff:** spreads the force thin and is vulnerable to a concentrated break
at one point.

### Volley line

**Purpose:** deliver coordinated ranged pressure.

**Tradeoff:** requires ammunition, visibility, and protection from close
engagement.

### Swarm mass

**Purpose:** climb, engulf, distract, or flow through small openings.

**Tradeoff:** vulnerable to fire, flooding, smoke, wide sweeps, or the swarm's
specific ecological deterrent.

### Formation disruption

Disorder a formation by applying a fictionally sufficient event:

- attack its exposed flank or rear
- kill, capture, or silence the guide or signaler
- break the surface it braces upon
- force movement through fire, mud, ice, caltrops, a narrow door, or fleeing
  civilians
- frighten mounts or beasts that give the formation its structure
- split it across zone borders

The exact roll should use an existing action or skill. Do not require a bespoke
“formation check” if MOVE, MELEE, PERFORMANCE, MARKSMANSHIP, CRAFTING, animal
handling, or a spell already resolves the intervention.

## Command And Orders

Command makes leaders tactically valuable without giving every follower more
independent actions.

### Command capacity

A leader may sustain one order at a time. Giving an order is normally a slow
action for an NPC leader unless a talent or statblock says otherwise. The order
affects one band or troop that can perceive the signal.

An order grants one defined behavior, not a universal dice bonus.

### Order catalog

#### HOLD

The force keeps a border, objective, or captive position. It gains a narrow
benefit against forced movement but cannot voluntarily pursue beyond the named
line.

#### ADVANCE

The force moves while preserving one named formation benefit. It cannot use its
strongest stationary attack in the same round.

#### FOCUS

The force declares one target or zone. Its next mass attack gains +2 Base Dice
or one targeting privilege. The intention is visible before resolution.

#### SCREEN

The force protects a named leader, engine, retreat, or objective. Attacks through
the screen suffer the ordinary consequences of cover or must first open a route.

#### ENCIRCLE

The force closes routes around a target. It spends the current attack to improve
position. On the next round, escape becomes harder if the ring remains intact.

#### LOOSE

Ranged troops release a synchronized volley into a declared zone. The order
requires line of sight, ammunition, and a preceding ready state or signal.

#### WITHDRAW

The force disengages by bounds, carries wounded, and preserves cohesion. It
gives up ground or an objective in exchange for avoiding a rout.

#### TAKE THEM ALIVE

The force replaces lethal attacks with grapples, nets, shields, and forced
movement. It may gain control benefits but reduces or removes Weapon Damage.

### Signals

Attach command to a perceptible channel: voice, horn, drum, flag, lantern,
scent, psychic pulse, pheromone, or ritual chain. Smoke, darkness, distance,
noise, fear, broken line of sight, or a captured signaler can disrupt the
channel.

### Command tokens

For exceptional leaders, use up to three Command tokens. Spend one to issue an
order as a fast action, rally a Shaken troop, or preserve formation after a
disruption. Tokens do not refresh during the encounter unless a concrete
reserve, drum, shrine, or reinforcement restores one.

## Morale And Breaking

Morale determines whether intelligent enemies continue paying the cost of the
fight.

### Morale triggers

Check intent or morale when:

- the force falls to half Strength
- its leader, standard, brood parent, or handler falls
- it is attacked from an unexpected rear or interior position
- its escape route closes
- the promised reward or coercive threat disappears
- the enemy demonstrates a feared weapon, monster, spell, or betrayal
- the force's objective becomes impossible

### Morale outcomes

Choose the outcome supported by motive and position:

- hold but abandon aggressive orders
- fall back to a defensible border
- attempt to recover a leader or wounded companion
- bargain, surrender, or offer information
- fragment into small fleeing groups
- turn on a coercive officer or summoner
- fight desperately to open an escape route

Morale failure does not always mean running directly away. It means cohesion no
longer overrules individual survival, loyalty, fear, or grievance.

### Fear and troops

If a fear effect targets an entire troop, make one fear attack against the force
and use the core **MORALE & ROUT** trigger for three or more ⚔️. For lesser fear
results, describe individual panic without reducing the troop's Strength unless
the effect explicitly damages collective cohesion. Do not roll separately for
every body.

Mindless troops may ignore fear but should have another disruption channel:
command rune, necromancer, scent trail, brood impulse, light, salt, fire, or
loss of animating terrain.

## Mass Attacks

Mass attacks threaten space and priorities. They should not simply multiply a
normal weapon attack by the number of bodies.

### Attack templates

#### PRESS OF BODIES

Targets at the troop's frontage test position or suffer low damage plus forced
movement. It is strongest in a doorway, bridge, stair, or shield line and weak
when the troop is flanked.

#### HOOK AND DRAG

Several enemies use hooks, nets, roots, tentacles, or jaws to pull one target
into the footprint. The target has a resistance or escape action; allies can
cut lines or hold the target.

#### VOLLEY

Declare a zone before resolving. Roll one attack against each exposed target or
one area attack according to the manuscript's established form. Cover and
leaving the declared zone are valid answers. Ammunition or readiness limits
repetition.

#### OVERRUN

The troop moves through a route and attacks everyone who remains in it. It must
begin Ordered, have suitable mass and ground, and end Disordered or exposed.

#### SUPPRESS

The troop makes movement and exposed actions costly without high direct damage.
It fixes targets behind cover, closes a lane, or forces them to spend actions
moving cautiously.

#### SEIZE

The troop attacks an objective: gate, wagon, prisoner, ritual object, bridge,
or supply pile. Adventurers must choose between attacking the troop and
protecting the objective.

#### SCATTER

Beasts, swarms, or loose skirmishers spread through connected terrain. They give
up formation offense to occupy more routes, search, or deny hiding places.

#### REFORM

The troop makes no mass attack. It ends one disruption, changes formation, and
may recover one narrow benefit. Reforming must be interruptible by continued
pressure or loss of command.

### Attack ceiling

A troop's strongest attack should normally combine only two of these:

- high Base Dice
- multiple targets
- Weapon Damage 2
- severe control
- armor bypass
- long range

If it combines three, require setup, formation, telegraphing, expendable
resources, or a severe post-attack vulnerability.

## Casualties, Recovery, And Pursuit

### Reading troop damage

Describe each point according to the attack: killed, wounded, knocked down,
scattered, pinned, terrified, separated, or stripped of equipment. This keeps
nonlethal and supernatural attacks meaningful.

### Recovering cohesion

A troop may recover one Strength of cohesion if it spends a round reforming in
safety, receives effective leadership, gathers scattered members, and has not
lost bodies or equipment that make recovery impossible. State a hard encounter
limit, normally once.

Healing magic does not automatically restore collective cohesion. It heals
individuals; time and command restore formation.

### Broken troops

When troop Strength reaches zero, decide what remains:

- defeated bodies and wounded survivors
- scattered individuals who no longer pose a force-scale threat
- surrendered enemies
- a rout that may be pursued
- mindless remnants deprived of coordination

Do not erase the represented people. Their survival, capture, treatment, and
return affect the sandbox.

### Pursuit

Resolve pursuit as a new situation, not free slaughter. Consider terrain,
fatigue, mounts, local knowledge, surrendered weapons, wounded people, and the
cost of abandoning the original objective.

## Creature-Type Applications

### Humans and kin

Use officers, signals, formation, morale, gear, fatigue, hostages, and prepared
ground. Human forces usually want pay, survival, reputation, duty, revenge,
escape, or protection of kin. Their tactics should reveal training and motive.

### Beasts

Use packs, herd movement, dominance, scent, alarm, protection of young, and
flight thresholds. A beast troop may be a herd or mass, but most predators work
better as a band with PACK RESPONSE than as a troop.

### Monsters

Use broods, linked bodies, spawn, cult-servants, parasitized hosts, or swarms.
Let the central monster spend its attack commanding or feeding from the force;
do not give the monster and every subordinate full independent pressure without
budgeting for it.

### Demons

Use bound thralls, projected fragments, possessed crowds, ritual choirs, and
unstable lesser manifestations. Give the force a control dependency such as a
name, seal, summoner, rhythm, shared host, or breach. Breaking it should scatter,
free, or destabilize the group rather than merely deal damage.

## Worked Force Chassis

### Border levy shield troop

`TROOP: Village Levy; Standard; Strength 12; Armor Rating 4; Morale 6;
Movement 1; Formation Shield Line; attacks D10/D8/D6.`

- Results 1-2: HOLD THE GAP; no damage, resist forced movement and protect a
  named retreat.
- Results 3-4: SPEAR THRUSTS; ordinary frontal pressure against one target.
- Results 5-6: SHIELD PRESS; low damage and forced movement.
- Results 7-8: LOCK AND STAB; formation-dependent attack with a control rider.
- Results 9-10: ORDERED SURGE; telegraphed advance that threatens the frontage.
- Breaking the drum signal prevents reforming and cancels active orders.
- Half the levy will surrender if offered credible safety for the village.

### Wolf hunting band

Use 3-5 separate wolves on one initiative. One drives prey from cover, one
closes the escape line, and one attacks. `PACK RESPONSE` permits one wolf to
reposition after prey escapes a hold, not to make another full damaging attack.
The band withdraws when badly hurt unless defending cubs or starving.

### Corpse-moth swarm troop

`TROOP: Corpse-Moth Cloud; Small; Strength 8; Armor Rating 0; Morale —;
Movement 2; Formation Swarm Mass.`

- Ordinary targeted attacks inflict at most one damage.
- Fire, violent wind, smoke, or broad water effects ignore that cap or inflict
  double damage as stated.
- The cloud can distract, enter armor gaps, obscure sight, or settle on corpses
  to reproduce; it does not need six versions of direct damage.
- Burning corpses denies reproduction but destroys evidence and resources.

### Demon-bound procession

`TROOP: The Procession; Large; Strength 18; Armor Rating 2; Morale bound;
Movement 1; Formation Column.`

- A chain of spoken names links the possessed bodies. The cantor spends one
  action each round sustaining an order.
- The troop can press forward, seize a named victim, or emit a shared fear
  attack, but only while the cantor is audible.
- Silencing the chant makes the troop Shaken. Reversing three names frees
  survivors; killing bodies reduces Strength but strengthens the demon's corpse
  anchor.
- The design makes rescue and ritual knowledge mechanically distinct from
  efficient slaughter.

## Balance And Agency

### Action economy

One troop replaces many turns. It should not preserve the attack volume of all
represented individuals. A leader's order changes or improves the troop's one
turn; it does not normally add another full turn.

### Damage economy

Troop Strength can be high because damage is capped for narrow attacks, but
Armor Rating should remain legible and weaknesses should reward preparation.
Do not combine very high Strength, high armor, a universal damage cap, and easy
cohesion recovery.

### Spotlight economy

Give different approaches distinct jobs:

- heavy or area attacks damage mass
- duelists enter the formation to reach leaders and signals
- scouts find routes, reserves, and morale facts
- speakers negotiate surrender or exploit divided loyalty
- crafters sabotage structures, wagons, bridges, and signals
- magic alters terrain, visibility, fear, and formation
- healers preserve civilians, captives, and defeated enemies

### Ethical weight

Abstraction must not erase who is being fought. State whether a point of troop
damage represents death, injury, panic, or separation. Preserve surrender,
capture, desertion, coercion, and aftermath. This is especially important for
levies, enthralled civilians, child soldiers, famine mobs, prisoners, and
religious or ethnic conflicts.

### Complexity budget

In one encounter, use at most:

- three troop profiles
- one formation state per troop
- one active order per leader
- one shared morale or clock marker per force
- one exceptional reaction across the whole force

Use bands or individuals for everything else.

## Construction Procedure

1. State what the force wants and what holds it together.
2. Choose individual, minion, band, or troop scale.
3. Choose one force role: hold, seize, screen, hunt, carry, suppress, or escape.
4. Set its footprint and routes before its attack numbers.
5. Choose one formation or coordination principle.
6. Write one way to disrupt cohesion besides dealing damage.
7. If it has a leader, give the leader one order channel and a motive.
8. Put basic actions at low attack-table results and formation-dependent actions
   at high results.
9. Define half-Strength behavior and the Broken outcome.
10. Write the aftermath for survivors, prisoners, wounded, leaders, and the
   settlement or faction that supplied the force.

## Audit Checklist

- Does the abstraction reduce handling time?
- Do numbers change space and objectives rather than only attack volume?
- Can the force be disrupted through terrain, signals, morale, or leadership?
- Does the strongest mass attack require a visible formation or setup?
- Are individual specialists still targetable and meaningful?
- Does current Strength visibly change cohesion or attack access?
- Are area attacks useful without making every other character irrelevant?
- Do human and animal enemies preserve believable motives and flight behavior?
- Can coerced or possessed members be saved?
- Is surrender, fragmentation, or pursuit supported?
- Does the force leave survivors and consequences instead of disappearing at
  zero Strength?
- Can the GM run the encounter with one state marker per troop?
