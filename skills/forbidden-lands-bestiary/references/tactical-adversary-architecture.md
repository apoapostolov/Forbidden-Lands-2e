## Purpose

Use this reference to make enemy types differ in how they think, organize,
occupy space, respond to injury, and pursue objectives. It expands the bestiary
beyond attack delivery and conditions into encounter architecture.

The module covers humans, kin, beasts, monsters, and demons. It does not make
them mechanically interchangeable. The same tactical function must arise from
different causes:

- a human controls space through training, tools, law, and cooperation
- a beast controls space through anatomy, senses, appetite, and territory
- a monster controls space through an impossible body or curse
- a demon controls space through manifestation, bargain, taboo, and corruption

Design from cause to rule. Do not give every enemy a teleport, counterattack,
summon, and second phase because those effects are tactically convenient.

## Design-Space Expansion

The established bestiary provides strong coverage of attack delivery, target
selection, damage, conditions, defense, weakness, and six-result attack-table
construction. Add the following independent axes when the encounter needs them:

1. **Time:** preparation, delay, cooldown, phase, clock, and deterioration.
2. **Organization:** command, formation, pack, troop, brood, and linked bodies.
3. **Intent:** declared target, lane, objective, bait, and conditional response.
4. **Position:** facing, perch, interior, elevation, route, border, and pursuit.
5. **Anatomy state:** intact, exposed, disabled, severed, molted, or unstable.
6. **Morale and appetite:** warning, commitment, frenzy, surrender, feeding, and
   flight.
7. **Information:** tells, false tells, learned responses, signals, names, and
   hidden dependencies.
8. **Objective pressure:** capture, carry, breach, defend, corrupt, rescue, and
   escape.
9. **Resource economy:** ammunition, breath, venom, brood, corpses, command,
   anchors, and safe ground.
10. **Relationship:** loyalty, coercion, symbiosis, summoning, worship, rivalry,
    and predation among enemies.

A design uses one or two primary axes and at most two supporting axes. The
catalog is a choice space, not a checklist.

## Universal Tactical Axes

### Time patterns

#### BUILD

Spend the current attack or action to strengthen one named future effect. Show
where the stored force resides. Limit accumulation and provide disruption.

#### DELAY

Declare an effect that resolves after a condition or at a later initiative.
The delay creates time for movement, rescue, or interference.

#### COOLDOWN

After an apex effect, temporarily remove an attack, lower defense, or require
return to a source. A cooldown matters only if players can exploit it.

#### DECAY

An ability, summoned body, formation, ward, or environmental effect weakens
each round unless fed or maintained. Maintenance must consume position, action,
or resources.

#### DEADLINE

A visible three-step clock changes the site or completes an enemy objective.
It pressures priorities without adding attack dice.

### Organization patterns

#### LINK

Two or more enemies share a defense, sense, movement permission, or attack
setup while a visible link remains. Breaking distance, line, chain, song, scent,
or formation removes the benefit.

#### HANDOFF

One enemy passes a mark, captive, weapon, charge, signal, or objective to
another. The handoff costs an action or requires position; interception becomes
meaningful.

#### SCREEN

One enemy protects another through bodies, shields, mist, webs, darkness, or
social consequence. The screen changes access rather than granting universal
immunity.

#### RELAY

Several enemies extend perception, command, magic, or a ranged effect through
intermediate nodes. Removing one node shortens reach or creates a gap.

#### SACRIFICE

An enemy consumes a follower, body part, anchor, future resource, or escape
route for an immediate effect. The cost must remain lost.

### Intent patterns

#### MARK

Select one target through scent, blood, command, curse, or observation. Grant
one narrow privilege until the mark's clear end condition.

#### LANE

Threaten a route with charge, breath, missile, collapse, or sweep. Mark the
geometry before resolution.

#### BAIT

Expose a desirable opening, objective, wounded body, loose weapon, or apparently
safe route. The enemy gains one response only if the bait is accepted. The clue
must allow suspicion; avoid pure GM gotchas.

#### GUARD

Name an anchor, dependent, border, or objective. The enemy's behavior and one
mechanical benefit change while defending it. Threatening the guarded thing can
redirect the enemy.

#### HUNT

Choose prey by a stated rule: wounded, isolated, armored, magical, carrying
food, bearing a scent, or breaking taboo. Target logic becomes discoverable
personality.

### Position patterns

#### PERCH

Use a limited network of elevated or protected positions. Perches give movement,
observation, or attack permissions and can be occupied, destroyed, smoked out,
or bypassed.

#### FACING

Give a large, shielded, or sensory-limited foe a front, flank, and rear. Facing
must be easy to mark and usually changes only when the foe moves or spends a
fast action.

#### INTERIOR

Allow characters to enter a troop, shell, gut, shadow, carriage, ritual circle,
or body cavity. Interior play must give the isolated player decisions and
targets, not merely recurring damage.

#### TETHER

Tie movement to a chain, host, nest, shadow, territory line, water source, or
ritual radius. The tether limits the enemy and creates leverage.

#### WAKE

Movement leaves a temporary trail of fire, ice, spores, churned ground, fear,
or scent. Limit the trail's length or duration and make its origin physical.

### Resource patterns

#### AMMUNITION

Count a small number of nets, venom shots, boulders, lightning stores, or ritual
seals. Strong ranged or control actions consume them.

#### RESERVOIR

Store up to three points of blood, fear, heat, water, corpses, or command. Name
how the enemy gains and spends the resource.

#### HARVEST

Let the enemy take a resource from the battlefield: fallen bodies, broken gear,
light, memories, spilled blood, or living roots. Harvesting takes tempo and can
be denied.

#### EXHAUST

Repeated use reduces potency, range, mobility, or control until rest or feeding.
Use when bodily effort and scarcity should be visible.

## Encounter Roles

Use roles to compose behavior. Role is not profession or species. One enemy may
change role once during a phase transition.

### Vanguard

Enters dangerous space, breaks a line, absorbs attention, and opens a route.
Give it movement and forced-position tools, not complete battlefield control.

### Anchor

Holds a border, maintains a formation, protects a site, or sustains allied
rules. Give it durability or stability but limited pursuit.

### Harrier

Punishes isolation, withdrawal, aiming, spell preparation, or exposed movement.
Give it speed and target logic but modest durability.

### Controller

Changes zones, routes, visibility, elevation, or access. Make the change
temporary, local, and answerable.

### Artillery

Threatens from range through volleys, breath, thrown objects, curses, or site
engines. Require sight, ammunition, setup, or vulnerable position.

### Binder

Restrains, marks, carries, grapples, or separates a target for another enemy.
Preserve rescue and escape play.

### Support

Heals, commands, feeds, screens, repairs, summons, or maintains a link. The
support must be reachable through some route and should spend actions to help.

### Assassin

Uses preparation and target selection for a brief high-pressure window. Once
revealed or committed, it loses concealment or escape security.

### Scavenger

Feeds on aftermath, steals resources, drags bodies, and changes the value of
casualties. It complicates the scene without always joining either side.

### Keystone

Enables the force's unusual behavior: commander, queen, drummer, host, anchor,
or controlling demon. Removing it changes the encounter but should not always
kill every subordinate.

## Human Adversaries

Humans and kin should gain depth from intention, training, equipment, social
ties, and fear. Use normal combat actions whenever possible.

### Prepared positions

Human enemies dig, brace, conceal, range, stock, and signal. Give prepared
positions finite advantages:

- measured firing lanes
- covered withdrawal routes
- cached shields, stones, water, or ammunition
- alarm strings and horn relays
- barricades built to delay rather than become magical walls
- murder holes, smoke vents, loose stairs, or animal pens

The position can be scouted, approached elsewhere, sabotaged, negotiated around,
or made irrelevant by changing the objective.

### Tactical doctrines

Give a trained group one doctrine.

#### FIX AND FLANK

One group holds attention while another moves through cover. If either group is
forced to defend itself, loses the signal, or cannot reach the flank, the plan
fails without a replacement bonus.

#### ROTATING LINE

Injured front fighters withdraw behind fresh allies. This preserves people and
formation but costs movement and opens a window during rotation.

#### CROSSING FIRE

Two ranged groups cover intersecting lanes. The benefit exists only in the
overlap and collapses if one position is screened or engaged.

#### FALSE RETREAT

The force gives ground toward prepared terrain. It gains one ambush privilege
if pursued without caution, but risks a real rout if morale was already poor.

#### CAPTURE DOCTRINE

Enemies use nets, shields, hooks, commands, and threats to take prisoners. They
trade damage for control and must transport or guard captives afterward.

#### FIGHTING WITHDRAWAL

Part of the force screens while the rest moves wounded, treasure, civilians,
or evidence. Attacking the screen, racing the objective, or bargaining with the
retreating group are different responses.

### Human specialist mechanics

#### Standard bearer

Sustains one morale or formation benefit while visible. The standard may be
captured, cut down, imitated, or used in negotiation.

#### Sapper

Prepares a collapse, fire, breach, or obstruction across rounds. Tools, access,
and uninterrupted work are dependencies.

#### Handler

Directs beasts through voice, reins, scent, pain, food, or bond. Removing the
handler changes beast behavior according to training and relationship; it does
not automatically make beasts join the adventurers.

#### Field healer

Returns a Broken or defeated ally to withdrawal capability, stabilizes losses,
or preserves troop cohesion. Healing consumes action, supplies, and proximity.

#### Duelist

Marks one opponent and controls a small area through reach, stance, challenge,
or reputation. Allies and terrain can break the duel; it is not a supernatural
lock.

#### Agitator or priest

Raises courage, enforces fanaticism, interprets events, or turns bystanders into
a force. The effect depends on being heard and believed and may fail when the
speaker's claims are visibly disproved.

### Human morale profiles

Choose one:

- **Professional:** withdraws in order when the objective no longer justifies
  losses.
- **Levy:** holds near kin and familiar ground but fragments under isolation.
- **Desperate:** accepts great risk until an escape, food, cure, or hostage is
  secured.
- **Fanatical:** ignores ordinary loss while the sacred sign or authority
  remains; breaking that basis causes shock.
- **Coerced:** obeys while watched or threatened; separation creates defection
  or surrender opportunities.
- **Predatory:** attacks weak targets while safe and flees when the balance
  reverses.

## Beasts

Beast tactics arise from senses, learning, hunger, breeding, territory, and
energy. Do not make every predator fight to the death or coordinate like trained
soldiers.

### Hunt states

Use a four-state hunt when a beast encounter spans more than one exchange.

1. **Assess:** observe, scent, shadow, warn, or test the perimeter.
2. **Commit:** rush, pounce, separate, stampede, or seize prey.
3. **Secure:** drag, suffocate, carry, feed, or defend the kill.
4. **Disengage:** retreat from injury, noise, fire, lost advantage, or stronger
   resistance.

Only apex, starving, trained, diseased, cornered, or brood-defending beasts
should skip ordinary disengagement logic.

### Sensory maps

Give an important beast one dominant sense and one limitation.

| Sense | Tactical Privilege | Counterplay |
| --- | --- | --- |
| scent | tracks wounded or marked prey through obscured sight | water, smoke, strong competing odor, wind |
| vibration | detects movement through ground or web | stillness, irregular rhythm, leaving the surface |
| heat | locates living bodies in darkness | fire decoys, insulation, cold surroundings |
| echo | fights in darkness and around corners | soft barriers, loud clutter, silence, damaged organ |
| broad vision | detects flanking and distant movement | glare, clutter, confined terrain, eye injury |
| herd alarm | shares danger across the group | separate the alarm animal, imitate or drown signal |

Counterplay should reduce certainty, not render the beast senseless without
effort.

### Beast mechanics

#### TESTING PASS

The beast makes a low-damage rush or feint and repositions. If the target reacts
in the expected way, the beast gains a narrow benefit on its next committed
attack. Holding ground or changing response denies it.

#### DRAG TO COVER

After securing prey, the beast spends movement pulling the victim toward den,
water, height, brush, or pack. The victim and allies have recurring decisions.

#### EXHAUSTIBLE BURST

The beast can sprint, leap, constrict, discharge venom, or beat its wings at
full power a limited number of times. Recovery requires distance, stillness, or
feeding.

#### MATERNAL OR TERRITORIAL LINE

Crossing a visible boundary changes warning into attack. Leaving it, moving the
young, or redirecting the threat can end pursuit.

#### PACK CUT-OFF

Pack members move to close different routes rather than all attack. Their next
attack improves only if the prey remains isolated.

#### HERD CASCADE

Fear or injury spreads movement through nearby animals, creating a changing
lane rather than a stack of attacks. Calming, diverting, opening a route, or
removing the alarm source changes the cascade.

#### INJURED GAIT

At a Strength threshold, remove leap, charge, climb, flight, or constriction
results. Add defensive snapping, concealment, retreat, or den-seeking behavior.

#### PLAY DEAD OR FREEZE

The beast becomes still to evade notice, protect anatomy, lure approach, or
break pursuit. Provide sensory evidence and do not use it as an automatic free
attack against cautious characters.

## Monsters

Monster mechanics should reveal an impossible body, curse, appetite, or ecology.
Choose one law the monster imposes on the encounter.

### Monster laws

#### BODY AS TERRAIN

The monster is large or distributed enough to climb, enter, cross, shelter
behind, or destroy in sections. Give interior objectives and movement risks.

#### PREDATORY ENVIRONMENT

The monster acts through water, roots, mist, shadow, walls, fungus, or carrion.
Mark where it can manifest and how players can make areas unavailable.

#### CONVERTED HARM

One damage type feeds, divides, hardens, reproduces, or relocates the monster.
Signal the conversion and provide an ordinary alternative.

#### SHARED BODY

Several visible forms share one Strength pool, sensation, or death condition.
Distance, barriers, or destroying a coordinating organ changes the sharing.

#### STOLEN FUNCTION

The monster takes sight, voice, warmth, memory, shadow, or movement and uses it.
Keep the stolen category narrow and give recovery through rescue, rest, ritual,
or defeating the responsible organ.

#### METABOLIC TERRAIN

The monster consumes the site: rusting metal, drinking water, eating light,
breathing smoke, freezing heat, or digesting structures. Its resource creates
a route to starvation or redirection.

#### INCOMPLETE DEATH

Breaking Strength changes state instead of completing victory. The remaining
condition must be inferable and should create a short finishing decision, not a
hidden second full health bar.

#### ADAPTIVE SCAR

After suffering a specific tactic, the monster gains one temporary resistance
or changes position. Limit adaptation to one remembered tactic at a time and
give a visible body change. Players can rotate methods or exploit the new scar.

### Monster attack architectures

#### Chain

One result creates a state; another exploits it. The exploitation must not be
automatic, and allies must be able to break the chain.

#### Branch

One telegraphed setup can resolve in two ways depending on player response. The
monster chooses between pursuit and objective, not between two equally perfect
punishments.

#### Cycle

The monster moves through expose, strike, feed, and recover states. Players can
learn and interrupt the rhythm.

#### Collapse

High-result attacks disappear with injury or disabled anatomy. Use the
D12/D10/D8/D6 or D10/D8/D6 method in the boss reference.

#### Contamination

Attacks leave bounded traces that matter after the immediate hit: scent marks,
eggs, corrosive gear, spoiled water, haunted dreams, or attracting scavengers.
Give removal and aftermath procedures.

## Demons

Demons can violate reality more radically, but each demon needs a coherent
metaphysical dependency. Build the dependency from what permits it to exist
here.

### Manifestation states

#### Influence

The demon acts through dreams, compulsions, omens, animals, weather, or social
conflict. It cannot apply full bodily attacks and may be difficult to target.

#### Vessel

The demon inhabits a person, object, corpse, place, or constructed body. Damage
to the vessel and harm to the demon need separate consequences.

#### Emergence

The demon crosses through a breach or anchor and gains physical power while
becoming targetable. Emergence should be visible and interruptible.

#### Unbound

The demon reaches maximum freedom but also loses the protection of its vessel,
bargain, concealment, or anchor network.

### Demon mechanics

#### BARGAIN AS ARMOR

A promise, invitation, debt, hospitality, or accepted gift protects the demon
from one class of interference. Fulfilling, voiding, exposing, or transferring
the bargain changes the protection. Do not make obscure wording the only route.

#### NAME CIRCUIT

Names, titles, witnesses, or written marks act as nodes sustaining powers.
Learning and using one node disables a specific effect. The true name should
not be an all-purpose instant kill.

#### POSSESSION PRESSURE

The demon marks, isolates, enters, and stabilizes in a host across discrete
steps. Each step has signs and resistance or rescue. Do not resolve lasting
possession from one opaque failed roll.

#### CORRUPTING CHOICE

Offer immediate tactical benefit at a durable moral, bodily, social, or
spiritual cost. The choice must be genuine; refusing it cannot simply remove a
character from the encounter.

#### LAW INVERSION

Within a bounded area, reverse one concrete relationship: wounds feed light,
spoken orders bind the speaker, doors lead toward the anchor, or fear moves
between linked people. State the boundary and a way to collapse it.

#### DEBT COLLECTION

The demon can claim only what was promised, marked, named, or taken. Its target
logic becomes strict and exploitable. Players may contest ownership, fulfill a
lesser term, substitute value, or expose fraudulent authority.

#### FRAGMENT SPAWN

The demon splits off lesser manifestations by spending Strength, anchors, or
future actions. Fragments use minion or troop rules and do not create free power.

#### BANISHMENT WAKE

Defeating the demon releases stored weather, memories, hosts, corruption, or
site damage. Players may prepare the site to control the consequence before the
final blow.

#### CONTRADICTION

The demon cannot sustain two stated truths at once: invited and forbidden,
named and anonymous, reflected and unseen, fed and fasting. Engineering the
contradiction removes one power or forces manifestation. Base it on clues and
setting logic, not arbitrary riddle wording.

## Reactions, Movement, And Position

### Reaction discipline

Grant at most one named reaction per enemy profile and at most one use per
round. Pay with a fast action, stored resource, next attack, defense, or a
once-per-phase limit.

Useful reactions include:

- turn a protected facing toward one attacker, exposing another side
- interpose a shield, follower, wing, or possessed body at permanent cost
- retreat to a marked perch after a miss
- release a held victim to avoid forced movement
- collapse a prepared border instead of making the next attack
- spend Charge early for a weaker uncontrolled discharge

### Movement with consequence

Free movement should not merely negate player positioning. Attach one of these
costs:

- abandon an objective
- cross hazardous terrain
- lose formation or armor
- expose a route
- spend stored power
- leave prey, young, anchor, or support behind
- forfeit the next fast action

### Forced movement fairness

State direction, range, collision consequences, and rescue. Use the map's
existing hazards, but do not invent a lethal edge only after the hit lands.

## Battlefield And Objective Mechanics

### Dynamic borders

Doors break, bridges turn, water rises, roots close, crowds surge, and fire
spreads. Change one border per round at most. Mark whether it becomes blocked,
obscured, rough, open, or dangerous.

### Safe ground economy

Create a small number of positions protected from one enemy effect. Let the
enemy threaten, corrupt, or occupy them gradually. Players can defend, create,
or abandon safe ground.

### Moving objectives

Use captives, wagons, livestock, ritual bearers, wounded leaders, drifting boats,
or carried anchors. Give the objective a route and pace. Both sides can redirect
it through ordinary actions.

### Destructible terrain

Give important objects Armor Rating, Strength, tools that work well, and clear
consequences. Destroying terrain should trade one problem for another: collapse
the bridge and lose pursuit, break the dam and flood the ruins, burn the nest
and lose resources.

### Third forces

Scavengers, civilians, weather, rival hunters, livestock, spirits, or spreading
fire may respond to what both sides do. A third force needs one simple behavior
and should not add another full tactical faction unless the scene requires it.

## Information, Deception, And Adaptation

### Learnable pattern

Repeat a tell before repeating its consequence. Players should be able to say,
“It braces before charging,” or “the choir goes silent before possession.”

### False tell

An intelligent enemy may imitate a known preparation once. A false tell must
cost position, action, credibility, or a resource. Beasts use false tells only
if hunting behavior or training supports them.

### Signal reading

Allow INSIGHT, SCOUTING, LORE, PERFORMANCE, animal knowledge, military
experience, or direct observation to reveal orders and target logic. Success
should change a decision before resolution.

### Limited adaptation

An enemy may adapt to one repeated tactic after seeing it at least once. Show
the adaptation and let the players exploit what the enemy stopped doing. Never
grant universal immunity to the party's successful plan.

### Enemy memory

Recurring enemies remember routes, favored spells, bargains, injuries, and
betrayals. Memory should change preparation and motive, not give access to
information they could not have obtained.

## Costs, Counterplay, And Limits

For every exceptional mechanic, write four fields.

| Field | Question |
| --- | --- |
| Cause | What body, training, relationship, site, or metaphysical law creates it? |
| Cost | What action, resource, position, defense, ally, or future does it spend? |
| Tell | How can players perceive or infer it before maximum punishment? |
| Counterplay | How can at least two different approaches alter it? |

### Stacking limit

One enemy normally receives:

- one primary tactical engine
- one support mechanic
- one counterplay dependency
- one damage-state or morale change

A boss may add one phase architecture and one limited reaction. A force may add
formation and command. Do not stack the full allowances of every module.

### Numerical restraint

Prefer changing permission, target, position, timing, or resource over adding
dice. When adding dice:

- +1 or +2 expresses a repeatable situational edge
- +3 expresses a paid setup, severe exposure, or limited apex window
- increased Weapon Damage should rarely accompany the same dice increase
- extra targets count as power even if dice remain unchanged

## Composition Recipes

### Human checkpoint with depth

- Anchor: shield band at a barricade.
- Artillery: two crossbow minions with limited clear lanes.
- Support: officer with HOLD and WITHDRAW orders.
- Objective: wagon being searched while a prisoner can identify the party.
- Counterplay: approach by riverbank, bribe coerced levy, cut alarm cord, create
  a diversion, or challenge the officer's authority.

### Beast hunt with depth

- Harrier: two pack beasts close escape routes.
- Assassin: one apex beast uses TESTING PASS then marks isolated prey.
- Terrain: three visible perches and a scent-carrying wind.
- Damage state: injured gait removes pounce and changes goal to retreat.
- Counterplay: regroup, foul scent, occupy perches, protect food, or leave the
  den boundary.

### Monster site with depth

- Keystone: a rooted monster sharing Strength with three visible growths.
- Binder: minion tendrils drag prey toward wet ground.
- Clock: water reaches another growth each round, activating it.
- Objective: rescue trapped workers before breaking the dam.
- Counterplay: sever links, drain channels, burn growths, feed it tainted water,
  or bargain through the surviving host voice.

### Demon incursion with depth

- Influence phase: a possessed crowd repeats one accusation.
- Vessel phase: the demon uses the magistrate's seal as BARGAIN AS ARMOR.
- Force: fragment minions carry written names toward a breach.
- Objective: prevent the third name from reaching the gate.
- Counterplay: expose the false judgment, seize the seal, free named hosts,
  contradict the bargain, or force full manifestation.

## Tactical Stress Test

Run six imagined turns before approving a new adversary.

1. A melee specialist closes directly.
2. A ranged specialist stays behind cover.
3. A support character spends a turn rescuing or helping.
4. A player attacks the objective instead of the enemy.
5. The party withdraws or changes zones.
6. The strongest enemy mechanic succeeds once.

For each turn ask:

- Does the enemy have one coherent response rather than a perfect counter?
- Did prior information allow a meaningful prediction?
- Did the player's action change position, resources, state, or stakes?
- Can the affected player still make decisions?
- Does the aftermath differ depending on method?

Then test three enemy states:

- intact and in prepared terrain
- damaged or disrupted
- losing, isolated, or deprived of its objective

If the enemy behaves identically in all three, it lacks tactical state. If it
has entirely new rules in each, it is too complex.

## Design-Space Ledger

Use this ledger to verify that a creative round adds breadth rather than renamed
attacks. The entries below are distinct reusable families added by this module
and its two companion tactical references.

| Dimension | Added Families |
| --- | --- |
| Time | Build, Delay, Cooldown, Decay, Deadline, phase transitions |
| Organization | Link, Handoff, Screen, Relay, Sacrifice, formation, orders |
| Intent | Mark, Lane, Bait, Guard, Hunt, declared objective attacks |
| Position | Perch, Facing, Interior, Tether, Wake, dynamic borders |
| State | Degradation, Exposure, Frenzy, Desperation, Instability, discipline collapse |
| Force scale | Minion, band, troop, horde-as-objective, fragmentation |
| Resources | Ammunition, Reservoir, Harvest, Exhaust, Charge, Command |
| Information | Learnable patterns, false tells, signal reading, adaptation, enemy memory |
| Human depth | six doctrines, six specialists, six morale profiles |
| Beast depth | hunt states, sensory maps, eight beast behavior mechanics |
| Monster depth | eight monster laws and five attack architectures |
| Demon depth | four manifestation states and nine metaphysical mechanics |

This adds more than fifty separable mechanical families across twelve dimensions
without replacing the established attack, defense, condition, weakness, and
encounter grammar. Do not maximize the number used in one entry. The expanded
space exists so different enemies can each select a small, coherent subset.
