# Voice Test Protocol

Three sequential tests for any prose passage destined for this
manuscript. Apply in order: Quick Scan first, Full Pass only if
the Quick Scan flags, Author Lens Check when auditing register
consistency.

---

## Test 1 — Quick Scan (under 2 minutes)

Checks the 13 highest-frequency AI tells. Binary only.
Mark each item **PRESENT** or **ABSENT**.

A passage **passes** the Quick Scan if it has no more than
2 items marked PRESENT.

A passage **fails** if it has 3 or more items marked PRESENT.
Proceed to the Full Pass to locate and count specific violations.

---

### The 13-Item Quick Checklist

**Vocabulary**

- [ ] **P1 — Significance Inflation:** Does the passage use
  `ancient`, `primal`, `profound`, `vast`, `legendary`, `timeless`,
  `great`, or `immense` as plain description? (Not as a character's
  speech or oath — as neutral narration.)

- [ ] **P2 — Fantasy-Register Drift:** Does the passage use
  `common folk`, `shadowy`, `arcane`, `mystical`, `eldritch`,
  `mysterious`, or equivalent generic high-fantasy vocabulary?

- [ ] **P5 — Promotional Language:** Does the passage use
  `vibrant`, `thriving`, `flourishing`, `remarkable`, `incredible`,
  or `tapestry` (in the "rich tapestry of X" construction)?

**Sentence structure**

- [ ] **P8 — Rule-of-Three:** Does any sentence contain a
  three-item list? Count lists across the whole passage. More than
  one three-item list: PRESENT.

- [ ] **P16 — Not-Just-X-But-Y:** Does any sentence use the
  construction "not just [downplayed thing], but [elevated truth]"
  or the variant "not just tales" / "not merely" + elevation?

**Paragraph structure**

- [ ] **P17 — Topic-Sentence Paragraph:** Does every paragraph
  begin with a sentence that states what the paragraph is about?
  If yes: PRESENT.

**POV / Interiority**

- [ ] **P23 — Emotion Announcement:** Does the passage name a
  character's emotion directly? ("she felt fear," "grief washed
  over him," "he was angry.") If yes: PRESENT.

- [ ] **P24 — Sympathetic Narrator Insert:** Does the passage
  address the reader's potential discomfort with the text?
  ("this is not meant to suggest," "the intent here is not to,"
  "rightly or not.") If yes: PRESENT.

**Dialogue**

- [ ] **P28 — Expressive Dialogue Tag:** Does any dialogue line
  use a tag other than `said`, `asked`, or an action beat? (`he
  smiled`, `she exclaimed`, `he muttered darkly.`) If yes: PRESENT.

**World-building**

- [ ] **P33 — Generic Atmospheric Opener:** Does the passage open
  a scene with weather, time of day, or landscape as pure
  atmosphere? (Not character action, not sensory detail anchored
  to a character.) If yes: PRESENT.

- [ ] **P38 — Quest-Hook Framing:** Does the passage describe the
  situation as an opportunity, a chance, a destiny, or a
  challenge for the characters? ("adventurers who want to...",
  "here is a chance to...") If yes: PRESENT.

**Structure**

- [ ] **P39 — Sentimental Resolution:** Does the passage end with
  consolation, warmth, or earned peace after difficulty? If yes:
  PRESENT.

- [ ] **P43 — Lesson Stated Directly:** Does the passage state a
  moral or thematic conclusion in the narrator's voice? ("This is
  what war costs." "Loyalty means nothing without proof.") If yes:
  PRESENT.

## Test 2 — Full Pass (under 10 minutes)

Run after a Quick Scan failure, or on any final draft before
integration into the corebook.

Count occurrences per pattern. Score at end.

### Layer 2 — Sentence Structure (Patterns 7–16)

- **P7 Em-Dash Cluster** — occurrences: ___
  Count em-dashes in the passage. More than one per 300 words:
  flag.

- **P8 Rule-of-Three** — occurrences: ___
  Count three-element lists or three-part anaphoric structures.

- **P9 Negative-Then-Positive** — occurrences: ___
  Search for "It is not X. It is Y." or "Not X. Y." constructions
  used as clarification or elevation.

- **P10 Copula Avoidance** — occurrences: ___
  Search for: "standing," "rising," "waiting" at sentence starts
  where "He stood," "It rose" would be correct.

- **P11 Elegant Variation** — occurrences: ___
  Search for synonyms used to avoid repeating a noun. (First
  "the woman," then "the figure," then "the stranger" — all
  referring to the same person.)

- **P12 Hedging Stack** — occurrences: ___
  Search for: "perhaps," "somewhat," "arguably," "in some
  ways," "as it were."

- **P13 Gerund Opening** — occurrences: ___
  Search for sentences beginning with a gerund: "Watching the
  road, she..." / "Knowing the cost..."

- **P14 Balanced Compound Conclusion** — occurrences: ___
  Search for sentence endings that balance two abstract nouns
  or two parallel phrases.

- **P15 Trailing Qualifier** — occurrences: ___
  Search for sentence endings with: "at least," "in a way,"
  "or so he believed," "somehow," "as best she could."

- **P16 Not-Just-X-But-Y** — occurrences: ___
  Search for: "not just," "not merely," "more than just."

**Layer 2 subtotal:** ___

### Layer 3 — Paragraph Structure (Patterns 17–22)

- **P17 Topic-Sentence Paragraph** — occurrences: ___
  Count paragraphs that begin with a sentence stating what the
  paragraph is about.

- **P18 Continuity Bridge** — occurrences: ___
  Search for sentences that only connect the previous paragraph
  to the current one without adding information.

- **P19 Thematic Restatement Ending** — occurrences: ___
  Search for paragraph endings that restate the opening
  observation in a slightly elevated form.

- **P20 Even-Rhythm Paragraph** — occurrences: ___
  Test: read the paragraph aloud. Do all sentences have similar
  length and weight? If yes: flag.

- **P21 Signposting Sentence** — occurrences: ___
  Search for: "what follows is," "the next section covers,"
  "this chapter explains," or equivalent in fiction prose (not
  rules text).

- **P22 Backstory Dump on First Mention** — occurrences: ___
  Search for: first mention of a character or place immediately
  followed by a historical paragraph about them.

**Layer 3 subtotal:** ___

### Layer 4 — POV and Interiority (Patterns 23–27)

- **P23 Emotion Announcement** — occurrences: ___
  Search for: felt [emotion], was [emotion], [emotion] filled
  him/her.

- **P24 Sympathetic Narrator Insert** — occurrences: ___
  Search for sentences that address the reader's potential
  concerns about the text.

- **P25 Over-Explained Motivation** — occurrences: ___
  Search for character action followed by explanation of why
  they did it in the narrator's voice.

- **P26 Analytical Self-Awareness** — occurrences: ___
  Search for first-person narrators who analyze their own
  psychology mid-scene.

- **P27 Memory Flashback That Explains** — occurrences: ___
  Search for: memories triggered by a present object that
  then explain the character's current behavior.

### Layer 5 — Dialogue (Patterns 28–32)

- **P28 Expressive Dialogue Tag** — occurrences: ___
  Count every dialogue attribution that is not `said`, `asked`,
  or an action beat.

- **P29 Dialogue That Explains Backstory** — occurrences: ___
  Search for conversations where characters explain things they
  would both already know.

- **P30 Perfect Grammar in All Speech** — occurrences: ___
  Test: do all characters speak in complete, grammatically
  correct sentences? If yes: flag.

- **P31 Dialogue Tags for Emotion** — occurrences: ___
  Search for: `he said sadly`, `she replied bitterly`, any
  adverb after a dialogue tag.

- **P32 Character Who Summarizes for Reader** — occurrences: ___
  Search for characters who deliver exposition through dialogue
  that sounds written rather than spoken.

### Layer 6 — World-Building (Patterns 33–38)

- **P33 Generic Atmospheric Opener** — occurrences: ___
  Test: does the scene open on weather, time of day, or
  landscape before any character action?

- **P34 Over-Complete Information** — occurrences: ___
  Test: does the passage give the reader all information about
  a location, person, or situation at first mention?

- **P35 Religion-as-Flavor** — occurrences: ___
  Search for religious references that exist only as atmosphere
  with no economic or practical function.

- **P36 Monster-as-Obstacle** — occurrences: ___
  Search for monster descriptions that describe only what the
  monster does to the party, not what the monster is or needs.

- **P37 Village-as-Set** — occurrences: ___
  Search for village descriptions that give only visual texture
  without economy, social structure, or history.

- **P38 Quest-Hook Framing** — occurrences: ___
  Search for adventure-module voice: "the villagers need
  someone," "there is an opportunity," "someone willing to."

| 10+ | FAIL — voice reconstruction needed |

per 1000 words, double the threshold (0–4 = PASS).

## Test 3 — Author Lens Check

Use when auditing register consistency in a specific passage.
Identify which author register the passage is intended to use,
then apply the corresponding lens.

---

### Le Guin Lens

**Positive signals — these should be present:**

- Short declarative sentences naming what exists, not what
  seems.
- Ecological specificity: a named plant or animal as part of
  the scene.
- Authority — social or formal — treated as background fact,
  not introduced.
- The second significant character revealed through the first
  character's behavior, not described directly.
- The emotional fact of the scene present in the physical detail,
  not named.

**Negative signals — these must be absent:**

- The narrator explaining what the scene means.
- Any sentence of the form: "She realized that..."
- Three-part anaphoric structures or rule-of-three lists.
- Weather-opens-the-scene as pure atmosphere.
- Any promotional or significance-inflation vocabulary.

**Quick lens test:** Take the longest sentence in the passage.
Does it end on an action verb or a specific physical noun?
(Le Guin sentences end on weight.) If no: the passage
needs revision.

---

### Cornwell Lens

**Positive signals — these should be present:**

- First-person present-tense observation vocabulary.
- Tactical spatial awareness: near, far, left flank, the
  ridge above, the ford below.
- Short sentences for moments of danger or decision.
- Named equipment: not "his sword" but "the old hand-and-a-half
  with the nicked blade."
- Prior-experience framing: "I had seen this before" or
  equivalent in third-person.

**Negative signals — these must be absent:**

- Internal monologue that explains strategy mid-fight.
- Abstract moralizing about the nature of war or loyalty.
- Sympathetic narrator inserting reassurance.
- Rule-of-three constructions.
- Any sentence that ends on an abstract noun.

**Quick lens test:** Count the action verbs per sentence.
A Cornwell passage has at least one action verb per sentence
in a combat or tension scene. If a sentence lacks an action
verb, it is probably description-for-description's-sake.

---

### Sapkowski Lens

**Positive signals — these should be present:**

- Investigation logic: the character arrives knowing something
  specific that the reader does not yet know.
- Price mechanism: what does this cost? Who is paying?
- Dry wit in the narrative voice or in dialogue.
- The contract or arrangement made explicit before action begins.
- Prior intelligence: the character has already gathered
  information; the scene executes on it.

**Negative signals — these must be absent:**

- Generic monster framing (what the monster does as threat).
- Quest-hook voice.
- Emotion announcement.
- Backstory dump at first mention.
- Any sentence that performs wonder at the strangeness of the world.

**Quick lens test:** Does the character in the scene know
something specific that the reader does not? If the character
and reader are discovering the situation at the same pace,
the passage is not in Sapkowski register.

---

### Parker Lens

**Positive signals — these should be present:**

- Economic history: the passage tracks what things cost over
  time.
- Maintenance cost vocabulary: what does it take to keep this
  running?
- Dark comedy: the narrator's observation of practical
  dysfunction is wry, not outraged.
- Unreliable pragmatist voice: the narrator is observing
  their own compromises without excessive self-judgment.
- What survives: what still stands, what has been repaired,
  what has been given up.

**Negative signals — these must be absent:**

- The narrator admiring anyone.
- Heroic register of any kind.
- Sentimentality at scene endings.
- Any sentence of the form "despite everything, they
  endured" (sentimental survival formula).

**Quick lens test:** Does the passage contain at least one
specific number or material quantity? (Bushels, silver,
days, miles, weight.) Parker prose is specific and economic.
If the passage has no numbers, check whether the register is
drifting abstract.

---

### Gwynne Lens

**Positive signals — these should be present:**

- Body-first observation: the character's physical condition
  is given before their mental or emotional state.
- Proprioception: the character knows where their weight is,
  where their limbs are.
- Animal or ecological witness: something non-human that
  knows what is happening.
- Short sentences for the key revelation.
- Detail that requires proximity to observe (texture, smell,
  the specific sound).

**Negative signals — these must be absent:**

- Emotion announcement (name the physical experience,
  not the emotion).
- Abstract noun preference (the dread, the weight of it).
- Explanatory interiority (what the character understands
  about what is happening).
- Rule-of-three constructions.
- Generic atmospheric openers.

**Quick lens test:** Remove every sentence in the passage
that names an emotion or a mental state. Does the scene
still communicate the emotional content? If yes, the passage
is in Gwynne register. If no, the scene depends on announcement
and needs revision.

---

### Kay Lens

**Positive signals — these should be present:**

- Elegiac omniscience: the narrator knows how this ends.
- "Last time" marker: this moment is named as the final
  occurrence of something.
- Historical weight: the event is placed in a longer pattern.
- Humanizing detail: a specific small fact about a peripheral
  person (the ferryman's daughter, the name of the horse).
- What was lost and when.

**Negative signals — these must be absent:**

- Lesson stated directly (the pattern is: this is what it
  meant; not what we should learn from it).
- Sentimental resolution (Kay's endings are elegiac,
  not consoling).
- Present-tense immediacy (Kay's register is retrospective;
  the narration knows it is looking back).
- Backstory dump at first mention (the history arrives
  obliquely, through what is missing).

**Quick lens test:** Does the passage know something about
the future that the characters do not? Kay register is
retrospective by definition. If the narration is in the
same ignorance as the characters, the passage is not in
Kay register.

## Using the Tests Together

**Typical workflow for a passage being drafted:**

1. Draft the passage.
2. Run Quick Scan (2 minutes). If 0–2 flags: submit for
   integration. If 3+ flags: proceed.
3. Run Full Pass (10 minutes). Identify specific violations.
4. Revise against violations only — do not revise beyond
   what the tests flag.
5. Re-run Quick Scan to verify clean.

**Typical workflow for a passage being audited for register:**

1. Identify the intended author register.
2. Run Quick Scan.
3. Run the Author Lens Check for the intended register.
4. If the passage passes Quick Scan but fails the Lens Check,
   the voice is clean of AI tells but is in the wrong register.
   Revise using the relevant author reference file.

**When two registers are in the same passage:**

Identify the primary register and the secondary register
(see `composite-voice.md`, register map). The primary should
satisfy that register's Lens Check completely. The secondary
register contributes texture — one or two techniques —
and should not fail its own Lens Check's Negative Signals list.
