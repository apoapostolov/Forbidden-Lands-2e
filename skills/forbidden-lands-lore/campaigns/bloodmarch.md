## What Is an Expansion Region?

An expansion region is a neighboring country with its own kin,
geography, religions, magic traditions, terrain rules, and
creatures. It is not part of the core Ravenlands. Its content
does not appear in the main `/references/` files because most
Forbidden Lands stories do not involve it.

Load this file when the user's question, scene, or design
explicitly concerns the Bloodmarch / Aslene, the Legacy of Horn
campaign, or kin and creatures native to the Bloodmarch (Vasnians,
Caprids, red elves, horse clans, the Seventh, wingsteed, kton,
fraege, etc.).

Do not load this file for general Forbidden Lands questions that
do not reference the Bloodmarch.

## Region Overview

The Bloodmarch (also called **Aslene** by its native peoples) is
Ravenland's neighbor to the west. Climate is similar to Ravenland
but hotter and drier, farther from the sea. The central plains
have volcanic soil: dark sand mixed with fertile ash. The
surrounding mountains are tall with few passes. Strange rock
formations are common, especially in the east. All mountain
rivers converge southward and are swallowed by the earth.

### Historical Context

Three hundred years ago, during King Algarod of Alderland's wars
against the renegade sorcerer Zygofer, a demon horde was summoned
that slaughtered Alderlander armies in Ravenland. After losing
control, Zygofer redirected the horde into the Bloodmarch. He
eventually sealed most gates. Survivors of the demon flood were
left behind in both Ravenland and the Bloodmarch.

Afraid the demons would return, Zygofer made a deal with a demon
prince to seal Shadowgate Pass by perverting the laws of nature
at the passage. The pass stayed sealed for three hundred years.

During the events of Raven's Purge (at Vond), Shadowgate Pass
reopened. Nobody knows the exact cause — possibly the demons
guarding it died or found their way home. The Bloodmarch is now
open for the first time in three centuries.

### Present State

Adventurers, settlers, mercenaries, treasure hunters, and
missionaries from Ravenland flood through Shadowgate Pass.
Quards and Galdanes — horse clans that fled to Ravenland before
the Blood Mist — are returning to their ancestral lands.
The village of **Taregyll** was founded almost immediately on
the eastern slopes past the pass; it is the de facto entry point
to Aslene.

Nobody knows how much of the horse people's country survived.
The demons have grown stronger in recent years rather than
weaker. A demonic red plague has corrupted large tracts of
forest. The horse clans are attempting to unite under a single
leader for the first time since the Demon Flood.

## Terrain Types

### Standard Terrain

The Bloodmarch contains forests, plains, hills, and high
mountains that use standard Forbidden Lands journey rules.

### Ashlands

Black deserts of volcanic ash. Nothing grows. No hunting.
No edible water (dead pools are poison, Potency D6+3). Making
camp is hazardous; no healing or attribute recovery possible.
Ash storms are common. Sinkholes and gas pockets pose constant
threat.

### Firelands

Magma flows close to the surface; bare feet burn. No animals
to hunt. Foraging yields heat-resistant algae and hard roots
at best. Geyser water is undrinkable (Potency D6+1). Travelers
must make SURVIVAL rolls every Quarter Day or suffer Strength
damage from the hot ground. Sleeping here counts as bare ground
in sweltering heat; SURVIVAL roll required to sleep at all.
Waking always THIRSTY.

The Firelands are considered sacred by priests of Horn; several
temples stand on ground thick enough to build on.

### Crimson Forest

Blood-red demon jungle tangled like spilled entrails. Hot and
faintly luminous. Flowers turn toward warm-blooded creatures.
Air carries spores — imperceptible, potentially poisonous or
hallucinogenic. Plants have will of their own. Predatory
creatures are neither plant nor beast. Visitors are changed
by prolonged exposure.

**Crimson Sickness:** Spending time in a Crimson Forest risks
contracting Crimson Sickness. Symptoms progress along nine stages
(bloodshot eyes → blood vessels on skin → red pulsating vessels
on limbs → red hand with will of its own → flesh growths → facial
deformation and talon hands → back tentacles → boils and oozing
slime → death and dissolution into Crimson Forest). Each morning
an infected creature must ENDURE or drop a stage, retaining all
previous symptoms. Empathy penalties accumulate.

Crimson Sickness is halted by Blue Tar (consumed daily) and
cured if Blue Tar has been heated on Have's holy fire first.

Red elves are the only kin that live inside Crimson Forests
without harm.

## Regions of the Bloodmarch

**Ashenvale:** Eastern plain, once home to the Galdane horse
clan. Hit hardest and first by the Demon Flood in 875 AS.
The Galdanes were nearly wiped out; those who fled to Ravenland
only now return. The soil is grayish-black volcanic ash — highly
fertile when irrigated. Taregyll is here.

**The Black:** North of Ashenvale. Blacker volcanic ash, outcrops
of black obsidian (volcanic glass) cut through with white cinder.
Obsidian is used throughout the Bloodmarch for temples, weapons,
jewelry, and talismans. Originally Vasnian and Quard territory.
The Black has been reclaimed piecemeal since the Demon Flood.

**Vasnia:** Northern mountainous region. Dominated by the
indigenous Vasnians, orcs, and wolfkin. Horse clans rarely enter
without strong numbers — they are not welcome. Rumors of heavily
fortified villages around ore deposits. The Quards hold nominal
claim but no real control.

**Havenmark:** Once lush and beautiful, elves and humans living
in peace. Now the center of demonic plague spread — most
who aren't red elves avoid it. The Houn clan that once ruled
it is nearly extinct. Consensus holds Alderland responsible for
the Houn downfall, though the details are disputed.

**Firestead:** Dwarven realm north of the lava river Burn, which
flows westward from volcano Horn and crashes into Scorne Gorge.
The dwarves held the natural bridge Grinagap during the worst
demon attacks, preserving Firestead. Civil war between two
dwarven clans later allowed demons a foothold; the Caprid dwarves
have been hunting down miscreations ever since using magma singing
to dig lava ditches. Relatively unscathed by the demon plague.
The only Caprid city is Helya.

**The Wailing:** Wind-swept plain named after the goddess Wail.
Eastern section called the Forest of Stone for its ravines and
pillar-like cliffs. Horses here developed wings over millennia
to navigate the landscape and escape predators. Home territory
of the Sabirian horse clan.

**Kreysel:** The Screaming Land — where the Wailing narrows west.
Wind so strong few creatures survive long. Plants grow thick and
along the ground; animals evolved turtle-like legs. The wind grew
stronger during the Demon Flood, likely magically enhanced by
sorcerers in the Drawn Lands beyond, who closed the passage to
protect themselves. The western hurricane is permanent; the Drawn
Lands lie beyond and are unknown.

**Strilling:** Arid shrublands where carnivorous dread raptors
prey on wild horses. Caberian horse clan territory. Caberians are
said to be so horseback-oriented that adults can barely walk.

**Druma:** Undulating land with lush forests, fought over for
centuries between the Bloodmarch and Alderland at Gander's Pass.
Prepared battlefield plains, hardened for cavalry. Since the
pass closed during the Demon Flood three centuries ago, the
region fell quiet — which suits demons trying to establish
themselves in the world of humans.

**Varina:** Southwest hills, rising toward highlands. Beautiful
but avoided by horse clans who believe Horn's presence does not
reach the limestone ground here. Ill-suited for riding.

**Hadruma:** Highland rising toward Gander's Pass in the
southeast. Devastated for as long as anyone remembers — vultures,
wild dogs, and the bones of countless generations. The Gander, a
guild of mages honored by both sides of the Alderland wars,
once watched the pass from eight pillars (Gåsbenen). At the end
of each Gander's life they hurled themselves from their pillar.
During the Demon Flood the sides of the pass collapsed, sealing
it. Whether earthquake or deliberate is unknown.

## Kin of the Bloodmarch

### The Five Horse Clans

Nomadic human peoples who claim descent from the volcano god Horn.
Each clan controls a central plain named after one of Horn's five
dormant son-volcanoes. The horse clans believe Horn gave them the
land and the weapons (Horn's Astra) to drive out the Vasnians.
Historical scholarship suggests they arrived from the Drawn Lands
over a thousand years ago, expelled the Vasnians, and adopted
Horn's worship from the Caprid dwarves. The clans strongly reject
this version.

Clans permit friendly outsiders to traverse their land and take
what they need to survive. Permanent settlements and trading
caravans require chieftain approval and a tribute equal to
one-tenth of value.

**Houns:** Associated with volcano Houner. Most prestigious
lineage — Sella the Liberator was reportedly Houn. Decimated
during the Demon Flood; their territory is largely Crimson
Forest. Maintain good relations with Caprid dwarves of Firestead.
Current leader: Selligar Horne of Havenmark, young and
charismatic, trying to unite all clans.

**Sabirians:** Associated with volcano Sabira. Were warned of
the Demon Flood in advance and prepared, which they believe
makes them stronger and more capable than the Houns. Loosely
allied with the Caberians through marriages. Best riders by
their own assessment. Home of the legendary wingsteed horses.
Current leader: Mommodar the Gnawer, aged and cunning.
Opposed to Selligar Horne's claim to lead all clans.

**Caberians:** Associated with volcano Cabera. Traditionally
at war with Alderland, which attacks periodically through
Gander's Pass. Value weapon smithing, combat training, and the
skaldic arts. Easily offended; quick to challenge to a duel.
Current leader: Merdekai, scarred warrior in his mid-forties.
Supports Mommodar's claim over Selligar's.

**Galdanes:** Associated with volcano Gald. Hit first and
without warning by the Demon Flood (their territory borders
Ravenland). Their legendary chieftain Kanender the Mad broke
through the demons and fled to Ravenland, where his descendants
remained until Shadowgate Pass reopened. The Aslene Galdane
survivors consider Kanender a traitor. Now Aslene Galdanes
and returning Ravenland Galdanes regard each other with
complicated pride and contempt.
- Ravenland Galdane leader: Kormella Mira of Moldena,
  Kanender's descendant. Finest horse warrior alive. Hates
  demons and Rust followers. Seeks Quard alliance.
- Aslene Galdane leader: Trandesso Haveman, traditionalist
  who values pride over preparation.

**Quards:** Associated with volcano Quard. Long at war with the
Vasnians. Never fully recovered from the Demon Flood. Distrustful
and insular. Some Quard groups returned after Shadowgate Pass
reopened; Aslene Quards consider them soft and farming-corrupted.
Current leader: Jorgundos Ash, quiet, just, and notoriously
difficult to persuade.

### Vasnians

The Bloodmarch's indigenous people. Named after the northern
region of Vasnia, where they still live in large numbers.
Said to have some orc blood in their veins; live in close contact
with orcs and wolfkin of the region. No written language —
oral tradition only, which the horse clans use to dismiss them
as lesser people. Vasnians see themselves as part of nature,
interconnected with all living things rather than as individuals.
Worship the goddess Volitia (same as the orcs). Many dream of
reclaiming their land.

### Red Elves

The elves of the Bloodmarch. Red skin, red hair, red clothing.
Everyone else views them with suspicion — the common belief,
even among Ravenland elves, is that they were driven mad by the
demonic plague.

Red elves keep to themselves in the Crimson Forests. They follow
their leader Vaerefor van Reiben, who preaches that red is the
only natural color (all others are corruption by the evil god
Kolor) and that the Crimson Forest must spread across the world
for the sky god Rubor to descend among his people. Red elves
ride on sarcoptes and slimesnails. Some humans serve as their
agents beyond the Crimson Forest.

### Caprid Dwarves

The dwarves of Firestead. They have a horn jutting from the
forehead and call themselves Caprids. Kulli is their word for
hornless dwarves, whom they consider weak degenerates. Humans
and elves are baas kulli — lesser kulli. Hornless dwarves in
return imply that Caprids mated with goats.

Caprids worship Horn as Warm Huge, the warm and malleable
aspect of the stone god Huge. Caprid dwarves are proficient in
Magma Song. They abolished their clan system after clan wars
allowed demons to invade Firestead. Only city: Helya.

The horse clans get on well with the Caprids — the dwarves
were first to encourage Horn-worship and forged Horn's Astra
for Sella, intending to test the powerful new weapons on
someone else before using them themselves.

### Aslene Orcs

Unlike Ravenland orcs, never enslaved by elves or dwarves.
Consider themselves the oldest, most primordial kin — molded
from the first soil by the creator god Klei (their name for Clay).
Worship the all-mother Volitia. Move in small family units;
regular assemblies for pacts and justice. Female orc position
is as strong as in Ravenland.

Aslene orcs despise Ravenland orcs for allowing themselves to
be subjugated. They believe an orc simply is, and does not need
to become anything — anything new dilutes Volitia's message.
Leaders understand the power of orc unity, but the cultures
are too different for easy alliance. Only females can negotiate
between countries; Aslene male orcs mock and fight their
Ravenland counterparts on contact. The Sisterhood of Viraga
(Ravenland) has representatives in Vasnia.

**Étosh:** The orc male concept of fulfilling Volitia's
intention through manly deeds — war, song, competition,
procreation, dominance. A good male is capable, commanding,
strong-willed, and produces strong offspring. Worthiness is
displayed through religious symbols that can be ritually
challenged for ownership. Religion is oral: old trees, glades,
and rock formations used as altars. Ecstatic female hunts of
live prey, torn apart and devoured. No buildings or scriptures.

### Wolfkin

Wolfkin have been in the Bloodmarch for a very long time.
More confident than their Ravenland kin; nobody sees them as
demon-spawn here. Said to have been created by the Nightwalker
Guard (their word for the Nightwalker god) for a specific
purpose now forgotten, supposedly hidden in their noses.
Often mingle with and breed with orcs.

**Grargs:** Wolfkin-orc hybrids. Bulkier, more aggressive,
more animalistic than ordinary wolfkin. Have tusks. Sterile.
Cannot form packs; move around the fringes of settlements.
Temperamental — wolfkin do not hesitate to kill grargs who
threaten their young. In major battles, orcs arm grargs with
heavy knuckledusters, drug them into frenzy, and use them as
vanguards.

### Moon Elves

Rare — some doubt their existence. Pale bluish skin, white
hair. Said to be cursed by Wyrm and carry a moonstone in the
chest rather than a ruby. Skilled in illusory magic (mentalism).
Vanish without a trace when glimpsed. Their true history is
deeply entangled with the campaign's central mystery.

### Halflings and Goblins

Few native halflings in the Bloodmarch. Small shy groups
reportedly live in secluded valleys. Much more sympathetic
toward each other than in Ravenland — possibly because moon
elves and wolfkin once hunted both, and they never trusted
anyone else again. Halflings and goblins encountered in the
Bloodmarch are almost exclusively Ravenlander pilgrims seeking
moonstones they believe were torn from their kin at the time
of the Shift.

### Ravenlanders and Alderlanders

Settlers from Ravenland trying to farm or trade. Religious
refugees escaping persecution. Rust Church and Congregation
of the Serpent scouts from Alderland — both observing the
newly opened country.

## Gods and Religion

### Horn and Have

Horn is the active volcano at the center of the Bloodmarch —
worshiped by horse clans and Caprid dwarves alike as their
god and father. Horn gave the clans their horses and (in myth)
the Astra that let Sella conquer the land. His five dormant
son-volcanoes lent their names to the five clans. Horn erupts
with fiery rage periodically but mostly sends fertile ash.

Horn is the god of power, construction, and creation.

Have, "the Blue Goddess," is Horn's wife. She ignites inner
fire — rage, love, burning passion. To "have" is to be filled
with Have's fire. To "be having" is to carry a child, a dream,
or an ability. Have is the goddess of war, love, fertility,
learning, and creativity.

Devotees of Have are sworn enemies of all demons, particularly
the red vegetation that spreads in the name of Rubor.

**The Pyrolytes:** Demon-hunting cult within the Church of Have.
Produce holy fire and blue tar, both of which increase damage
against demons. Blue tar also halts and cures Crimson Sickness.
Found at the Temple of Have in Ashenstead.

The central temple is Ashenstead on Mount Faltenvil — a sacred
site and adventure location.

### Volitia and Étosh

Volitia is the all-mother worshiped by Vasnians and Aslene
orcs. A pantheistic forest goddess who ordered the creator
Klei to make the world simply because she could. The orcs
believe they are closer to Volitia than any other kin. Their
duty is to reflect her will through desire (pride, authenticity,
procreation, power, independence — considered female virtue)
and capacity (overcoming challenges, dominating weaker kin —
considered male virtue). Together these form Étosh.

### Nightwalker (Ranghöge)

The Bloodmarch name for the Nightwalker is Ranghöge — protector
of nighttime creatures. Wolfkin see him as lord of the night
and strive to become one with him under his leadership.
The moon goddess Eor is accepted as Ranghöge's helper, eyes,
and sometimes wife. Umbra creatures glimpsed at dusk and dawn
are believed to be Ranghöge's messengers.

**Order of Egression:** A Ranghöge cult that brings peace to
the dead so they can pass from this world. Practitioners
use Blood Magic.

### Eor

Worshiped by halfling and goblin pilgrims in the Bloodmarch.
The moon goddess is their mother. The Bloodmarch halflings
revere salt as holy dust fallen from the moon — it penetrates
anything and can be ritually purified into a powder so holy
it preserves food from rot.

### Rubor and Kolor (Red Elf Religion)

Created by the demon prince Vaerefor van Reiben. Rubor is
another name for the Red Wanderer of the sky (the star from
which elves fell as rubies). Kolor is the evil coloring force
that corrupted the world away from pure red. All colors except
red are Kolor's work. Red elves must spread the Crimson Forest
until the world deserves to receive Rubor descending among
them. White and black do not count as colors for this purpose.
The rainbow is Kolor's worldly manifestation.

## New Magic Traditions

These magic traditions do not exist in the core Ravenlands.
Characters from Ravenland or Alderland cannot begin the game
knowing them — they must find teachers.

### Magma Song

A sub-tradition of Stone Song, practiced by Caprid priests
of Horn. Magma singers control lava, fire, and hot water.
Caprid priests can learn both Stone Song and Magma Song but
must increase their ranks separately. Other kin who learn
Magma Song usually do not know Stone Song.

Core philosophy: Magma Song awakens the memory and potential
of matter rather than adding energy. Stone becomes lava by
remembering what it once was. Water boils by realizing its
potential. Magma Song does not work on limestone or sandstone
(never magma). Understanding and empathy are central. Poetry
is highly valued.

| Spell | Rank |
|---|---|
| Ignite | 1 |
| Fire's Friend | 1 |
| Mold Stone | 1 |
| Fire Memory | 2 |
| Water Memory | 2 |
| Steam Flight | 2 |
| Magma Cascade | 3 |
| Firewall | 3 |
| Summon Fire Wyrm | 3 |

**Ignite (1):** Causes an inanimate object within Near range to
burst into flames. Carried objects burn the bearer; armor worn
becomes an ongoing fire attack.

**Fire's Friend (1):** Personal immunity to all fire, including
dragon fire and lava, for one turn per Power Level. Additional
Power Levels protect nearby allies.

**Mold Stone (1):** Makes hot liquid stone or metal shape itself
and solidify in any rough-cut form. Up to one cubic meter per
Power Level. More precise shapes need CRAFTING rolls.

**Fire Memory (2):** Instantly melts volcanic stone into lava.
Up to one cubic meter per Power Level. Only affects stone that
was once lava — not marble or limestone.

**Water Memory (2):** Changes water state one step per Power
Level (ice → water → warm water → steam). Vaporized containers
explode. Frozen water shatters locks and makes enemies slip.
Cannot affect water inside living creatures. Bonus: always
temperature-correct beverages for free.

**Steam Flight (2):** Pressurized steam from a carried water
container lifts the caster — one meter vertical or one zone
horizontal per round until water runs out. Aimed at a creature
within Arm's Length, the steam deals Strength damage.

**Magma Cascade (3):** A fist-sized chunk of volcanic rock
explodes in burning magma. Everyone within Arm's Length of
the explosion suffers Power Level damage to Strength. Can be
placed with a MARKSMANSHIP roll, or set as a ground trap.

**Firewall (3):** A one-meter-thick horizontal or vertical wall
of fire bursts from volcanic rock. One meter per Power Level
in length; three meters per Power Level in width. Crossing
inflicts Power Level Strength damage.

**Summon Fire Wyrm (3):** Summons a fire wyrm from volcanic
ground. Arrives in one turn and attacks everything in sight
except the summoner. Size determined by Power Level.

### Mentalism

Moon elf magic tradition that affects the perception, memory,
and will of intelligent creatures. The moon elves of Anoma
(beyond the eastern seas) were masters; Bloodmarch moon elves
have lost much of this knowledge but remain excellent
manipulators.

| Spell | Rank |
|---|---|
| Mental Strength | 1 |
| Wordplay | 1 |
| Traceless | 1 |
| Confusion | 2 |
| Amnesia | 2 |
| Truth Sense | 2 |
| False Shape | 3 |
| Fata Morgana | 3 |
| Geas | 3 |

**Mental Strength (1):** One allied creature per Power Level
becomes immune to Wits and Empathy damage and fear attacks
for one turn per Power Level.

**Wordplay (1):** Makes one person per Power Level reinterpret
speech by exploiting an ambiguous word — permanent effect.

**Traceless (1):** Alters all traces (appearance, voice, scent,
body language) of the caster and one additional creature per
Power Level for one hour per Power Level. Cannot control how
others perceive the new appearance.

**Confusion (2):** Reduces one target's Wits to zero (Broken
without critical injury, incapable of rational thought) for one
round per Power Level. INSIGHT roll to resist.

**Amnesia (2):** Makes one person per Power Level forget the
previous turn. INSIGHT roll to resist. Person is not otherwise
changed but must reassess the situation.

**Truth Sense (2):** Lets the caster detect lies, see through
illusions, and understand hidden or distorted things. Each
Power Level reveals something significant about a thing's
true nature.

**False Shape (3):** Changes the caster's appearance, voice,
and smell into another living creature between half and double
their size. The change is an illusion — attributes and
languages are unaltered. INSIGHT roll to see through; contact
immediately reveals the illusion.

**Fata Morgana (3):** Creates a controlled illusion that looks
and acts by the caster's will but is immaterial, scentless,
and traceless. INSIGHT roll to see through; contact immediately
reveals the illusion. Requires concentration to maintain.

**Geas (3):** Forces a creature to perform one action of the
caster's choice. Cannot compel suicide or actions leading to
the creature's death. INSIGHT roll to resist. Lasts until the
action is performed or one day per Power Level.

### Oneiromancy

Dream magic. Taught by the Aslene prophet called the Dreamstress.
Usually performed while dreaming or in trance; a few spells
affecting others' sleep can be cast while awake.

A sleeping oneiromancer who has cast spells wakes without the
spent Willpower Points.

| Spell | Rank |
|---|---|
| Premonition | 1 |
| Nightmares | 1 |
| Restorative Sleep | 1 |
| Dream Visit | 2 |
| Mara | 2 |
| Lullaby | 2 |
| Dream Travel | 3 |
| Sand Sleep | 3 |
| Prophetic Dreams | 3 |

**Premonition (1):** Once per Power Level, make a skill roll
and decide after seeing the result whether the action was
actually taken. With Power Level 2+ this works for nearby
sleeping allies' rolls. Cannot push premonitioned rolls.

**Nightmares (1):** Injects nightmares into sleeping creatures
within Near range. Wits damage equal to Power Level; zero Wits
causes critical fear injury. Victims become SLEEPLESS next day.
No effect on monsters.

**Restorative Sleep (1):** After restorative dreams, increase
one attribute or skill by one step for the day, per Power Level.

**Dream Visit (2):** In sleep, travel invisibly to any
previously visited place and observe it. Additional Power Level
allows appearing before and communicating with people there.
Cannot act physically.

**Mara (2):** Summons a mara in dream form and sends it after
a target the caster can visualize. Mara attacks relentlessly.
Caster sees what the mara sees until waking. Mara disappears
after completing its task.

**Lullaby (2):** A quiet lullaby puts humanoid creatures within
Short range into deep slumber unless they make an INSIGHT roll.
Sleepers can be woken. Affects allies unless they plug their ears.

**Dream Travel (3):** After a Quarter Day's sleep, travel to
any previously visited location — bringing along any carried
items and one additional sleeping person per Power Level above 3.

**Sand Sleep (3):** Throwing a handful of sand renders all
living warm-blooded creatures of human size or smaller within
Near range unconscious. INSIGHT roll to resist. Only the caster
can wake them.

**Prophetic Dreams (3):** Conjures or repairs a non-magical
item of moderate value during a night's prophetic dreams.
Can also recreate a lost body part, heal a condition, or
resurrect the recently dead — but only if not slept since
the injury or death.

### Magnetism

Iron-based magic developed within the Rust Church's warrior
cult, the Ironbrows. Not typically taught to outsiders.

| Spell | Rank |
|---|---|
| Path of Iron | 1 |
| Iron Will | 1 |
| Stanch Blood | 1 |
| Attract | 2 |
| Repel | 2 |
| Bloodshock | 3 |

**Path of Iron (1):** Track any person carrying iron items up
to one day after they passed. Each iron item leaves a unique
trace. Cannot track through water.

**Iron Will (1):** Manipulate metal-containing objects — lock
or unlock shackles, doors; make a sword stick in its sheath
or shield. Power Level determines size/weight of affected items.

**Stanch Blood (1):** Manipulates the iron in blood to stanch
a fatal wound and automatically save the life of someone with
a fatal critical injury.

**Attract (2):** Magnetize an enemy in metal armor — adds bonus
dice equal to Power Level to all metal weapon attacks against
that enemy. Duration extends by one round per additional Power
Level.

**Repel (2):** Makes a metal armor anti-magnetic, repelling
metal weapons including metal-tipped arrows. Negative
modification equal to Power Level on all metal weapon attacks.

**Bloodshock (3):** Manipulates an enemy's blood to render them
immediately unconscious. Wits roll to resist. Cannot be woken
until the effect expires. Can affect additional targets for
every two Power Levels.

## Drugs and Potions

### Blue Tar

Produced by stripping bark from holy blue pines of Have and
tapping them for sap. Boiled into thick bluish-black liquid.
Boiling requires a large stationary facility.

At the temple of Ashenstead burns the sacred blue flame of
Have. Any fire lit inside the temple turns blue and holy.
All priests of Have can transfer holy fire to another
combustible material, but cannot multiply it — if five torches
are lit from one holy flame, only one flame turns blue.

**Against demons:** Blue Tar grants +1 damage against all demons.
Blue Tar combined with holy fire grants +2 total.

**Against Crimson Sickness:** Halts Crimson Sickness with daily
doses. Cures it completely if the tar has been heated on holy fire.

**Other uses:** Makes Horn's Astra glow briefly when smeared on
them. Used to claim ownership of an Astra (see Blue Blood).
Fuels the mechanical horse Witherbeam. Locally kills demonic
vegetation and neutralizes spore tissue on the ground.

### Blue Blood

Equal mixture of Blue Tar and one's own blood. Used for ritual
purposes — specifically to claim ownership of Horn's Astra.
Ownership lasts until the Astra is washed with Dense Water.

### Dense Water

Condensed water produced by Caprid dwarven druids. Looks like
ordinary water but lasts much longer (resource die only lowers
on a 1). Powerful cleaning agent said to wash away vermin,
mange, tattoos, scars, and disfigurements. Highly sought after
in Alderland.

Used in the campaign to wash away Astra ownership. Also used
as brake fluid in Witherbeam. Production requires a master,
a live kidney, and water run at specific astrological alignments.

### Crimson Ooze

Viscous red fluid found throughout Crimson Forests. Produced
by slime snails and also flows through Veinpearls. Crimson
Forest spreads along trails of Crimson Ooze. Ingesting it
contracts Crimson Sickness. An elven ruby placed in Crimson
Ooze grows into a red elf.

### Lycopodium Powder

**Weapon:** Blown at a demon within Arm's Length — MIGHT roll,
base damage 1, cannot be parried or dodged. On a hit, demon
burns for 1 Strength damage per round until a MOVE roll

**Healing:** Burning Lycopodium Powder in a wound caused by

burning is so painful the adventurer must ENDURE or pass out

## Bestiary of the Bloodmarch

### Smolderer

Small dark-gray humanoid creatures with glowing eyes. Faint
smoke wisps off their warm bodies; they smell of sulfur but
are cold to the touch — they draw heat from their surroundings.
Found only in ashlands. Believed to be intelligent; their
wheezing sounds may be language. Sprung from the earth's
depths, not related to other Forbidden Lands kin.

Feed on heat as a delicacy — living warmth is preferred over
the earth's heat, which is sufficient but unexciting. Attack
in hordes, often riding ktones. If damaged they sink back
into the ashland and do not return; a killed smolderer
disintegrates into ash.

*Special:* Sulfuric odor damages Empathy of nearby creatures.
Can drain body heat on a successful melee hit, inflicting COLD.

### Bloodbeech

Large red trees at the edges of Crimson Forests, with beautiful
white flowers. When flowers detect warm-blooded creatures, they
detach and fly down to draw blood for seed production. The
bloodbeech gauges how many flowers it has lost and sends only
enough to maintain its seeding needs — always holds back enough
to attack the next group that approaches.

### Prune

Red elves surgically "reformed" by Ashenstead priests through
anatomical procedures and injected Blue Tar. The results are
scrawny, blue-black creatures that follow orders. Levels of
consciousness vary; whatever remains is filled with hatred.

Some prunes escaped or were deliberately released to mock
the red elves. Elves from Ravenland hunt them to cut out
the rubies still in their chests.

*Special:* Explode on contact with fire — burning Blue Tar
cascade, Weapon Damage 2 against all within Near range.

### Wingsteed

A horse, almost always gray, born with wings on rare occasions
in the Wailing and Kreysel regions. The Sabirian clans value
them as gifts from the goddess Wail. Can take flight upwind
with powerful wingbeats; otherwise soars on updrafts like
an eagle. Cannot perform precise air maneuvers or fight while
airborne. Mainly used for exhibitions, messages, and crossing
ground obstacles. The famous stallion Barganos belongs to
Mommodar the Gnawer. About twenty wingsteed currently exist
in the Bloodmarch.

### Fraege

Heat-stealing ice demons. Always attack the warmest target
first — lamps, torches, and campfires before warm bodies.
Hide in dense vegetation; their cold can be felt meters away.
Freeze and paralyze limbs with their grip; a strong fraege
can kill a human with an embrace. Grow in strength after
absorbing heat. Split into two when Strength reaches 14.

Said by some scholars to be demon-tainted smolderers — both
feed on heat — but fraeges are far larger and more dangerous.

### Grave Lily

Predatory plant-insect hybrid of the Crimson Forest. Feeds
on animals dissolved in the corrosive liquid in its flower-cups.
Moves on four insect-like legs but usually stands motionless
waiting for prey. Catches prey with paralyze-poisoned tentacles.
Uses hand-like claws to push captured prey into flower-cups.
Thorns can be raked to saw oversized prey into smaller pieces.

### Kton and Fire Wyrm

Fire wyrms are abyss worms of Aslene. Smaller ones are ktones —
ravenous, fast in ashlands, smell of acetone. As they grow
they dig into the magma beneath volcanic plains and become
fire wyrms, feeding on fiery minerals and rarely surfacing.
Only dangerous to surface creatures near open lava fissures
or when summoned by magma singers.

Legendary fire wyrm: **Erinya** — ancient, much larger and
more heavily armored than ordinary fire wyrms. Mentioned in
connection with the campaign.

### Mecha

Living plant constructs of the Crimson Forest — mobile and
predatory, partially plant, partially animated matter.

### Salamander

Amphibious creature native to the Firelands.

### Sarcoptes

Red elf mount of choice. Found in Crimson Forests. Red elves
ride these in preference to horses.

### Forest Star

Creature unique to the Bloodmarch's varied terrain.

### Slime Snail

Massive snail native to Crimson Forests. Produces Crimson Ooze.

Creature unique to the Bloodmarch.

### Sporewalker

### Pearlyveine

## Horn's Astra (Public Knowledge)

Five legendary weapons said to have been given by the volcano
god Horn to Sella the Liberator for the conquest of the
Bloodmarch. After the liberation, Sella divided them among
the five horse clans to prevent internal strife. The plan
failed — the clans fought immediately. During the Demon Flood
three centuries ago, the Astra were lost or scattered.

Each Astra carries a Horn rune. They produce a faint blue glow
while they have a rightful owner. They can be identified by
coating them briefly with Blue Tar (they glow when coated).

- **The Goblet Staff of Have** — staff with a goblet at its end.
  Traditionally the Quard clan's Astra.
- **The Helm of Horn** — helmet with a moonstone in the forehead.
  Traditionally the Houn clan's Astra.
- **Sella's Dragonboot** — a left boot of dragon skin; magically
  summons a matching right boot. Traditionally the Sabirian
  clan's Astra.
- **Glasstooth** — a dagger. Traditionally the Caberian clan's
  Astra. The Caberians in Strilling claim to possess it; their
  version is widely believed to be a replica.
- **The Arrows of the Fire Wyrm** — three arrows that constitute
  one Astra only when all three are collected, summoning their
  quiver. Traditionally the Galdane clan's Astra. At least one
  arrow may be in Ravenland already (see Raven's Purge).
- **The Stonechest of Horn** — a breastplate that appears only
  when all five Astra are held by a single person, or summoned
  through ritual when four are held.

## ⚠️ The True History of the Astra and Sella

The public myth says Horn gave the Astra to Sella to conquer
the Bloodmarch for the horse clans. The true version recorded
in the Book of Winds at Watch of the Sisters:

The goddesses Flow and Wail sent Sella from the Drawn Lands
westward to seek **peace and trade** with the Vasnians.
Sella was offended by something during the meeting and let
peace collapse into open war. Flow and Wail were furious — they
rejected their own representatives and blocked the road back
with an everlasting hurricane. Godless, the horse clans turned
to Horn (encouraged by the Caprid dwarves).

The dwarves gave Sella Witherbeam the mechanical horse and
Horn's Astra — powerful weapons they did not dare test themselves
and expected to have serious side effects. The myth of Horn
granting the weapons arose from this situation. The dwarves
intended to let the horse clans clear the Vasnians from their
mining districts; Sella began a war of extermination, which
the dwarves now regard as a historical embarrassment.

## ⚠️ Binding the Astra

Ownership is claimed by smearing an Astra with Blue Blood
(equal parts Blue Tar and the owner's own blood).

**Accelerated aging:** A person bound to one or more Astra ages
faster. Rate: 10 × (number of bound Astra) × (maximum level
for bound Astra) faster than normal. One bound Astra = 10
months per actual month. All five Astra = 150× faster (roughly
3 years per week). Owner releases from aging when they give
up or lose the Astra. Carrying Astra inactive (unsmeared) has
no aging effect.

Ownership ends when the Astra is washed with Dense Water.
Only then can a new owner claim it.

## ⚠️ The Sons of Horn

The five dormant volcanoes (Houner, Sabira, Cabera, Gald, Quard)
awaken in sequence as each corresponding Astra is first bound
to a person. Each eruption:

- Pyroclastic flow destroys one adjacent non-mountain hex,
  turning it to Firelands.
- After a Quarter Day, a second adjacent non-mountain hex
  floods with lava.
- After a day, two hexes in a random direction receive
  meter-thick ash rain and turn to Ashlands.
- Column of smoke visible from ten hexes.

As more volcanoes become active, visibility across the entire
Bloodmarch decreases. Sunsets turn redder; daylight grows dull.
Red elves read this as the arrival of Rubor.

## ⚠️ Horn's Astra — True Powers

### The Goblet Staff of Have (Quard / Quard)

Suggested location: A Vasnian chieftain's hall in Vasnia,
mounted as a prize of war. Vaerefor van Reiben once owned it
before the Vasnians took it. Konana knows its worth and will
barter, but demands a replica in exchange. Other Vasnians refuse
to let it leave.

**Level 1:** One dose of liquid poured into the goblet becomes
any non-magical, non-Blue-Tar, non-Dense-Water beverage or
liquid of the bearer's choice.

**Level 2:** One dose becomes any drug, acid, poison, Blue Tar,
or Dense Water.

**Level 3:** Once per Quarter Day, one dose becomes any liquid
or a Goblet Tonic that heals all injuries (including critical),
cures all poisons and diseases, and can resurrect the recently
dead.

**Drawback:** Every Level 2 or 3 use requires a D6 roll. On a
1, the hex where the user stands turns to Crimson Forest over
one week (spider veins spreading from underground first).

---

### The Helm of Horn (Houns / Houner)

Has a moonstone embedded in its forehead. Fiena Fromelei can
sense the direction to all moonstones — including this one —
while the moon is up. Removing the moonstone ends its Astra
function (retains Level 1 magic). Reinserting a moonstone
restores it. Missing — can be placed at any adventure site.

**Level 1:** Wearer sees in the dark. Armor Rating 3.

**Level 2:** Owner's Wits +1 while wearing.

**Level 3:** Full protection against all non-magical ranged
attacks (diverted to another creature within Near range).
Protection against fear attacks. Immunity to MANIPULATION.
Wearer's hair turns completely white over one week.

---

### Sella's Dragonboot (Sabirians / Sabira)

A left boot of dragon skin that magically summons a matching
right boot. Missing — could be found at any adventure site,
or with the traveling jester Pottus Tynk (who thinks it suits
his costume and doesn't know it's magical).

**Level 1:** Perfect balance. No MOVE rolls to maintain
balance; cannot be knocked over.

**Level 2:** Walk on water and lava without damage or sinking.

**Level 3:** Leap to anything within Short range as a fast
action with perfect landing balance. Stomp the ground to
summon an earthquake — everyone within Near range must MOVE
or fall prone.

**Drawback:** All allies within Short range get −1 to all
Agility-based skills while Level 2 or 3 is active.

### Glasstooth (Caberians / Cabera)

Also known as Souldrinker. A jealous dagger — reduces Weapon
Bonus of all non-magical bladed weapons within Short range
by one (affected weapons have reduced hit chance and break
more easily). At the start of the campaign, Poansa Moulter
or the elf Mahogany (of the Inglourious Butchars random
encounter) carries the real dagger; the other carries a replica.
Neither knows they have one of Horn's Astra — both believe
they carry the cursed dagger Serpent's Bane, which allegedly
"kills sunlight, shadow, and the soul itself." The Caberians
in Strilling claim their revered dagger is Glasstooth; the
elders know it is a replica and keep the secret to protect
clan morale.

**Level 1:** Double damage. Counts as a magical weapon.

**Level 2:** Double damage also against creatures normally
resistant to physical violence (including Octomadria and
the Seventh).

**Level 3:** Each hit automatically inflicts a critical injury
in addition to normal damage. Armor has no effect. Against
Octomadria, every hit is fatal — but she must die eight times
to be banished.

### The Arrows of the Fire Wyrm (Galdanes / Gald)

Three arrows. Only constitute one Astra when all three are
collected — collecting all three summons their quiver.
Other arrows placed in this quiver crumble to ash. Adventurers
may already have one from Raven's Purge. One arrow may be
deep in the magma with demon Krasylla (pulled there by the
fire wyrm Erinya after being hit). This arrow attracts Erinya
— D6 every Quarter Hour in Firelands: on a 1, Erinya attacks.
The fire wyrm can be diverted by firing Krasylla's arrow.

**Level 1:** Gives the archer a D8 Artifact Die. Returns
to owner (or quiver) after three rounds.

**Level 2:** All three arrows fired simultaneously at the same
target — single attack with 3D8 Artifact Dice. Returns after
three rounds.

**Level 3:** All three arrows fired simultaneously at three
different targets in a single action. One D8 Artifact Die per
target. Returns to quiver after three rounds.

## ⚠️ Witherbeam — The Mechanical Horse

Witherbeam is Sella the Liberator's mechanical horse, built
by the Caprids who flayed his warhorse and filled the skin
with a mechanical body. Powered by Blue Tar and Dense Water
(used as brake fluid). Fueled through the nose: unscrew the
larger horn for Blue Tar, smaller horn for Dense Water.
Resource Dice are transferred to Witherbeam; roll each Quarter
Day of use.

Without Blue Tar, Witherbeam stops and remains motionless
until refueled. Without Dense Water, Witherbeam cannot stop
and keeps running until Blue Tar runs out.

Under each full moon, a living human must be sacrificed to
keep the machinery's spirits alive.

**Stats:** Strength 8, Agility 4, Movement 3.

**Effect:** Rider automatically gains Path of the Plains
and Path of the Knight at Rank 1 (or +1 Rank if already
possessed, max Rank 3). Can be extended with side pieces
to carry up to three riders.

**Suggested location:** Standing on the cliff by the Guardian
Temple (field temple to Horn and Have).

Currently posing as priests afflicted with skin rot seeking

appeared after Vinhelm's accident; they want to capture

psychic messages in all her students that will give her
complete control when the time comes to dream herself away.

Wants to obtain one or more Horn's Astra, dissolve them,
and charge her dream tower with their power for the escape.
Backup plan: rule this world alongside Vaerefor van Reiben
and dream beauty into existence.

## ⚠️ The Ancient History of the World (The Seventh's Origin)

Long ago on the eastern continent Anoma, humans discovered a
species of leech with primitive intelligence. Farmed as labor,
the worms killed their masters after several generations and
enslaved humanity. By the fifth generation the worms had
developed mythology and culture. They worship Meat — the
god they believe is resting inside all living things, who
will emerge along with the twelfth generation, at which point
everything living will be gathered inside the god's body.
The "Becoming" is their term for this event.

The sixth generation learned to control humans by placing
leeches on them. The seventh generation enslaved the continent's
moon elves, who mastered mentalism. The Seventh gave moon elves
favored status (promised a place on Meat's surface rather than
becoming her entrails) in exchange for their help magically
controlling other kin.

When the eighth generation was about to be born, the moon
elves refused. They prayed to Eor, who sent a giant raven to
help. The raven stole **Octomadria** — the Eighth Mother who
would birth the next generation. The moon elves fled Anoma
on ships. The Seventh sent a fleet of enslaved humans in
pursuit; the moon elves used mentalism to make the humans
believe they were following their raven god to a promised
land. Those humans arrived in Ravenland twelve centuries ago.

The moon elves, knowing the Seventh would come eventually,
hid Octomadria at their secret temple Agnostica and moved
it beyond the world. Because this couldn't be permanent,
they linked the temple's absence to a clock already in the
Bloodmarch — the Metrochrone, a giant clock at the Watch
of the Sisters. Then the moon elves split themselves into
two ignorant kin (halflings and goblins) to wait out the
Seventh's extinction, tearing the moonstones from their chests
and hiding them in Agnostica.

The Seventh did not go extinct. Now they have arrived in
the Bloodmarch.

## ⚠️ The Campaign Phases

### Phase 1: Arrival in the Bloodmarch

The adventurers arrive at Taregyll and learn the political
landscape: demon plague, clan strife, the legend of Horn's
Astra hidden for three centuries, and the threat of Vaerefor
van Reiben's expanding Crimson Forests.

### Phase 2: The Search for Horn's Astra

Adventurers and key players seek the Astra. Each time an Astra
is first bound to a person, one Son of Horn awakens. The phase
ends when someone (not necessarily the adventurers) has
claimed three related Astra.

### Phase 3: The Hunt for the Eighth Mother

Begins when someone owns three Astra. Fiena Fromelei finds
a moonstone and transforms into a moon elf — the first in
living memory. She recovers the full moon elf history.
The Seventh learn a moon elf has appeared and pursue.

### Phase 4: Time Is Reborn

Begins when the adventurers reach Watch of the Sisters.
The dead priestesses explain Agnostica — that it exists
beyond time and cannot return until the Metrochrone is
restarted. The Kogler murdered the priestesses and stole
clock parts to build his mechanical world in Tribolia.
The Metrochrone can be repaired with stolen parts from
Tribolia, or with parts crafted with the Dreamstress's help
from Oxengelder.

### Phase 5: The Return of Agnostica

Four Sons of Horn have awakened; the Metrochrone has been
restarted. Agnostica emerges in one of the six hexes surrounding
the last dormant Son of Horn. Simultaneously the Cauldron of
Flow activates. The fifth Son of Horn does not awaken —
Agnostica appears near its volcano.

### Phase 6: The Final Showdown

Adventurers reach Agnostica at the same time as at least
one key player. The battle for the temple and Octomadria
determines the fate of the Bloodmarch.

### Phase 7: Aftermath

Epilogue. The situation in the Bloodmarch after the finale.
Hints at future campaigns. Gander's Pass to Alderland is opened.

## ⚠️ Octomadria — The Eighth Mother

Not Wyrm reborn. The would-be queen of the Seventh — the
creature whose purpose is to birth the eighth generation of
worm people on the path toward the god Meat.

**First form:** A three-meter-long snake with the upper body
of a small child. Six subsequent forms appear as twisted women
of different kin (human, dwarf, orc, goblin, wolfkin, moon elf).
All first seven forms: same stats (Str 3, Agi 3, Wits 3,
Emp 3; Melee 3, Insight 3, Manipulation 3). All forms share
burning desire to know who they are — no memory, stressed,
frightened, aggressive. All first seven forms are killed
instantly on contact with any Horn's Astra.

**Final form:** Twelve-meter-long snake ending in a woman's
torso and head. Completely naked; demands fine clothing.
If none provided, projects exotic garments through illusion.
Considers herself an obvious goddess. Wants worship. Will
enter any alliance that accelerates this.

Must be killed **eight times** (once per form) to be banished.
Only Glasstooth can harm the final form.

**Final form stats:** Str 30, Agi 5, Wits 4, Emp 4; Insight 4.
Armor Rating 3 (scales).

## ⚠️ Adventure Site Secrets

### Agnostica

The secret moon elf temple, existing beyond time and the
world. It holds the Eighth Mother (Octomadria) and the
remaining 36 moonstones, including the chests of seven living
moon elves. It reappears near the last dormant Son of Horn
once both conditions are met: four Astra bound, Metrochrone
restarted.

The temple can be sent back beyond the world by activating
its altar of time.

### Watch of the Sisters

Contains the Metrochrone (great clock). Contains the Book
of Winds (the true history of Sella and the Astra). The dead
priestesses here explain the situation to adventurers.
The Kogler stole clock parts; they must be recovered from
Tribolia or rebuilt with the Dreamstress's help.

### Oxengelder

Located on limestone — Horn's volcanic domain does not reach
here. The Dreamstress's dream tower is here. The Dreamstress
can construct Metrochrone parts if needed. Contains creatures
called maras (summoned by Oneiromancy).

## ⚠️ Campaign Outcomes

The campaign's resolution depends on who controls the Astra,
what happens to Octomadria, and who enters Agnostica last.

**If the horse clans unite under Selligar:** A short-lived
but glorious unification of the Bloodmarch. Selligar dies
old within weeks (accelerated aging from the Astra) and
the clans fracture again. Or he learns to wield only one
Astra at a time and survives longer.

**If the Seventh get the Eighth Mother:** They birth the
eighth generation, then die themselves. The eighth generation
grows toward the ninth. The Bloodmarch gains an alien
new presence that begins enslaving kin. Ravenland is the
next target.

**If the moon elves hide Agnostica forever:** Octomadria
is never awakened. The moonstones are kept hidden. Halflings
and goblins remain split. The Seventh eventually go extinct.
The Bloodmarch must solve its demon problem without the Astra.

**If the Dreamstress completes her escape:** She dreams
herself back to Eoni, taking her students with her (they
have no choice — she controls them). Oxengelder empties.
The most peaceful site in Aslene becomes a silent ruin.

**If Vaerefor wins:** The Crimson Forest expands. Alderland
and Ravenland are next. The sky gradually reddens. The world
becomes Harachne.

**If Konana wins the Astra for the Vasnians:** A third
claimant on the Bloodmarch gains genuine power. The horse
clans are forced to negotiate for the first time in memory.
New possibility of a shared Bloodmarch or a long civil war.

After the finale, Gander's Pass to Alderland opens — a
new campaign direction for the future.
