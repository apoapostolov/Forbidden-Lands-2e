# Bitter Reach — Places

**Region:** The Bitter Reach
**Source:** Magnus Seter, Free League Publishing
**Campaign:** Wake of the Winter King

---

## Sub-Regions

### The Silver Coast

Thin coastal strip on the eastern edge of the Bitter
Reach. Black volcanic sand beaches — no white shores
here, no matter the name. The name comes from the dreams
of those who first arrived, not from the land itself.

Fishing villages are scattered along the coast. They
subsist on cod, seals, and whatever the ocean gives.
Hunters go after Nanuiks and Pikebeasts from these
villages. Adventurers pass through to resupply and
hear rumors. They rarely stay. Northfall is the
largest and most permanent settlement on the Silver
Coast. See Adventure Sites below.

### The Glacial Sea

The northern boundary of the Bitter Reach is open
ocean that quickly becomes impassable ice. The Silver
Coast runs roughly two hundred kilometers northward
from Northfall before curving west and vanishing under
the ice shelf.

Fishermen and seal hunters work the open water. Beyond
the open sea, enormous ice floes block any larger
vessel. Legends insist that some icebergs are large
enough to have ancient elven ruins sitting on them.
Whether anyone has confirmed this and lived to report
it is another question.

### Fallowmoor

Icy tundra west of the Silver Coast. Flat, wind-scoured,
inhospitable. Snowfall and storms are constant but the
wind keeps the snow thin — the ground is mostly bare.
Groves of petrified trees from forgotten ages stand
frozen across the moor; they do not burn well.

Smaller ruins dot Fallowmoor. Most have already been
plundered, as they are the closest ruins to the coast
and attract every treasure hunter who arrives without
the nerve to go deeper.

### The Morma Glacier

The tundra of Fallowmoor gives way west to an enormous
ice cap. Deep crevices cross the glacier; many are old,
but a growing number have opened recently as the cap
has begun to melt. Hot springs appear throughout,
where volcanic heat meets the magic that froze the
land three thousand years ago.

The glacier is melting. The cause is the breaking of
the Seal of Stars — the first of Blaudewedd's five
locks. As the other Seals break, the melt will
accelerate. See `campaigns/bitter_reach.md` for the
implications of each Seal's destruction.

### Icemoor

Beyond the Barren Hills, the ice cap continues as a
frozen plateau called the Icemoor. The ice here is
hundreds of fathoms thick. Few adventurers who have
gone this far west have returned to describe it.
Those who have report nothing except ice, wind,
and the absence of any other direction.

### The Barren Hills

A low mountain range west of the glacier. The cold
is harder here; the grip of the ice tightens. Life
exists in these hills — the orc clans live here, and
there are ancient ruins that no treasure hunter has
yet picked clean. The Barren Hills are frequently
visited by adventurers with the stamina to reach them.

The orcs do not welcome strangers to the Barren Hills
and will send scouts to observe any group that enters.

### The White Peaks

The highest mountains in the Bitter Reach. The wind
howls constantly and snow falls without stopping.
Legends say the largest strongholds of the lost elven
civilization were built here and remain frozen beneath
the ice, preserved and intact. Frozen corpses of lost
adventurers are the only proof anyone has tried to
reach them.

What lies beyond the White Peaks is not known. The
mountains are considered impassable.

### The Southern Range

The border with Ravenland. Insurmountable peaks that
reach through the clouds. Two passages exist through
the range.

The **Stillmist** is an elven dreamway — a magical
passage that requires an elven guide. Non-elves who
enter the Stillmist without a guide do not come out
where they intended, if they come out at all.

The **Winding Ways** is a system of dwarven underground
passages. The route is described only in dwarven
legends and songs. Only dwarves have a working
knowledge of the entrance and the route through. The
Gatekeepers and Pathkeepers guard both ends. See
`kin.md` for the dwarf clans of the Southern Range.

### The Ised and Keld

Two rivers flow from the Barren Hills down to the
coast, running partially beneath the Morma Glacier
before surfacing in Fallowmoor. Legends say artifacts
wash downstream from the ruins upstream; adventurers
pan the cold water near the coast for exactly this
reason. The more treasure hunters gather along a
river stretch, the more trouble arises.

It is possible to follow the rivers upstream under
the ice, though this requires preparation and a
willingness to navigate ice caverns in the dark.

## Adventure Sites

### Northfall

**Type:** Settlement — the largest town in the Bitter Reach
**Location:** Fixed — on the map, northern Silver Coast

Northfall is built on the ruins of a former winter
elven harbor. The town is protected by a thick wall
of stone, ice, and snow crested with sharpened Pikebeast
poles. The twin gates are made from petrified wood and
sea monster bones. They close at sunset.

The town is run between two competing authorities:
Marshal Volle enforces the law by force; Ingmarg Plump
controls the economy and — through it — most of
everything else. The conflict between them is visible
and constant. Whether it is real or theater is a
matter of local debate.

The Alderlander king's agents are in Northfall, posing
as peddlers and prospectors. They watch who comes and
goes through the gates.

**Key locations:**

- Gates (1) — Guarded at each gate; all arrivals and
  departures reported to Volle and, covertly, to Ingmarg
- Ice Wall (2) — High enough to deter Nanuiks and
  Dire Wolves; topped with Pikebeast pole weapons
- Ingmarg's Stronghold (3) — The merchant prince's
  residence and counting house
- Ogrin the Tailor (4) — Was personal tailor to the
  Alderlander king; fled north; now sews coats and
  keeps his past quiet
- Cuss and Dang's Inn (5) — The main gathering place;
  where rumors circulate and deals are struck
- Hillis' Store (6) — Buys and sells found items; if
  something has been lost in Northfall, Hillis probably
  has it
- Holbenda's Smithy (7) — Practical weapons and tools;
  Holbenda knows the ice demands different metal work
  than the south
- Marshal Volle (8) — Volle's headquarters and the
  de facto seat of law in Northfall; his guards are
  the only armed patrol
- The Stone Staircase (9) — Leads down to the black
  beach and fishing wharf below the town
- Fishing Wharf (10) — Where the catch comes in and
  outgoing boats launch; also the main arrival point
  for ships from the south
- The Whaler Camp (11) — Rough camp on the black beach
  outside the main walls. Bloody butchered Pikebeast
  cadavers litter the sand; waddlerbirds and rats feast
  on the remains. Whalers build their cabins from whale
  bones and whale skin. Large cauldrons bubble with
  rendered lard — the whale oil that heats and lights
  Northfall. The whalers are rowdy. The town guards
  push them back to the beach whenever they come up
  into town. They look down on the fishermen they share
  the beach with.
- Ruins (13) — Ancient elven structures partially
  incorporated into the town; strange markings; locals
  do not discuss what lives in the lower sections at
  night
- The Lighthouse of the Elves (12) — Old elven
  lighthouse outside the town, still operational by
  no mechanism anyone has identified. At night the
  beacon emits a ghostly glow that shifts color like
  the northern lights. Shadows of winter elf lighthouse
  keepers move through the tower on certain nights.

**Rumors (selection):**

- Ingmarg is a demon in disguise who intends to plunge
  the Bitter Reach into permanent darkness.
- Ice Trolls patrol the inside of Ingmarg's stronghold.
- Marshal Volle is on Ingmarg's payroll; the conflict
  between them is staged.
- Somewhere in Northfall there is an entrance to the
  winter elves' catacombs.
- Alderland is planning to invade. Every Alderlander
  in town is a spy.
- The lighthouse sends signals to ghost ships — the
  ancient fleet of Ferenblaud's navy — still visible
  at sea on certain nights.

### Tower of the Farseers

**Type:** Elven ruin — ruined observatory
**Location:** Open expanse or mountain summit; variable
placement on the campaign map; no fixed sticker

Ferenblaud built this tower to study the stars,
believing they were both predictors of the future
and gateways to other worlds. The tower was built
as high as winter elven engineering could manage.
At its peak was an observatory where scholars mapped
the sky and attempted to draw energy from deep space.

The Dragon Mul killed the stargazers and settled in
the tower to guard it. He scratched the signs of the
stars into the floor of the highest chamber and
has rested there ever since.

The Seal of Stars was placed in the tower three
thousand years ago. It has been broken. A group of
adventurers working for the demon Wurda reached the
top, destroyed the Seal, and died there. Mul survived,
badly wounded.

The breaking was marked by a shower of shooting stars
visible across the Bitter Reach on the night it happened.

See `campaigns/bitter_reach.md` for the consequences
of the first Seal's destruction and what remains
in the tower.

### Bleakness Keep

**Type:** Winter elven fortress — partially accessible ruins
**Location:** A mountain valley in the Barren Hills or
White Peaks; known among treasure hunters but its
exact position is only shared with trusted individuals

Namtarel was Ferenblaud's executioner, Prince of Death,
and the most feared of the Winter King's princes. He
was laid in deep slumber in his own keep by Ferenblaud
— not dead, but set aside, waiting for a ritual that
would restore him fully. The ritual was never completed.
The curse buried the keep along with everything else.

A permanent camp of treasure hunters has grown up
outside the wall. It is run by Wolftrap, an Alderlander
fugitive and bully who extorts passage fees from anyone
who wants to approach the keep. Inside the camp are
also traders, a field surgeon, and a store — all
charging three to five times normal rates.

Most of the ancient citadel remains sealed by ice.
The sections accessible to adventurers include an
outer courtyard, a labyrinth, an armory, an orchard,
and Namtarel's tomb.

Namtarel's Black Arrow is believed to rest in his
tomb. Two named artifacts — Headsbane and Night-and-day
— are in the armory. See `artifacts.md` for their
legends.

See `campaigns/bitter_reach.md` for what Namtarel's
status is during the campaign and what happens when
his tomb is disturbed.

### Hope's Last Rest

**Type:** Village — typical small coastal settlement
**Location:** Variable, placed adjacent to coast or
river; represents any one of the fishing villages
scattered across the Silver Coast and Fallowmoor

Hope's Last Rest was established a hundred years ago
by treasure hunters who arrived from Alderland hoping
to found a rival to Northfall. At its peak it held
several hundred people; today it holds around thirty
permanent residents, swelling to fifty when hunters
and treasure hunters pass through.

The original ambition died when the nearby ruin was
stripped and no new one was found. What remained was
a trading post for Ailander fishermen and hunters, and
a place where the orcs of the area conduct neutral-ground
barter with other kin.

Orilla the Outlaw runs a bandit crew that shelters
in Hope's Last Rest, tolerated because she pays for
protection in coin and not in threat. The village also
hosts Tryngar, a peddler of artifacts who operates at
lower prices than Ingmarg and is believed to be an
old enemy of the merchant prince.

The village wall has crumbled in sections; sharpened
poles fill the gaps. The gate is always closed and
requires a shout to open.

**Key locations:**

- The Gate (1) — Guarded by Kolm and Breda; they
  open for anyone who looks friendly enough
- Snowbank (2) — A reinforced snow berm covering the
  eastern approach; limited cover against Nanuiks
- Wharf (3) — Where boats anchor; freezes over in
  worst winters; fishermen make temporary camps
  in open water when it does
- The Bandits' House (4) — Orilla's crew; weapons
  visible from outside
- Feydor's Fishing Shed (5) — Old fisherman, doesn't
  ask questions about what comes in off the water
- Tryngar's Store (6) — Artifact peddler; unknown
  connection to Ingmarg
- The Whale's Lament Inn (7) — The inn; Larke runs
  it; Old Hutla sits in the corner and will tell
  you things for a drink
- Ruins of the Winter Elves (8) — Partially stripped
  ruin on the edge of the village; the original
  reason the village exists; not yet fully exhausted

### The Field of Swords

**Type:** Gladiatorial arena — winter elven ruins
**Location:** Variable; placed in Fallowmoor near the
border of the Barren Hills; known by reputation
throughout the Bitter Reach

An ancient winter elven arena where prisoners were
forced to fight for the elves' sport. The most skilled
could win status in elven society but never freedom.
Thirty years ago, the adventurer Kvaldor the Slayer
discovered the ruins, found an amphitheater behind two
immense gates, and spent years restoring it to active use.

Today the Field of Swords is a functioning arena with
permanent camps, a system of patron-backed fighters,
formalized dueling rules, and an economy built on
betting. Kvaldor takes a tenth of every wager. His
authority over the site is absolute.

Three types of combatants fight here: free fighters
backed by wealthy patrons called Sword Nobles, chained
fighters who are outlaws or slaves, and captured
monsters used as arena beasts. The audience is reliably
more bloodthirsty than the combatants.

Most adventurers reach the Field of Swords through
Northfall, where the innkeepers Cuss and Dang scout
for capable fighters to recruit to the arena. The
demon Wurda also visits here, directing treasure
hunters toward the Seals.

Sleeping at the Field of Swords causes shared
nightmares among all who camp here — visions of a
dragon and a locked stone door, growing more vivid
after a battle in the arena.

**Key locations:**

- The Camp (1) — Open camp outside the arena walls;
  Ailanders, exiled orcs, wolfkin adventurers, and
  various rogues share the space with visible tension.
  Smoke lies heavy over the camp. Small fires smolder.
  The atmosphere is strained and unfriendly. Eyes
  follow you between the tents.
- The Pits (2) — Cells for chained fighters and
  caged beasts; always guarded. The stench is
  overwhelming. Chains rattle. Here and there you
  hear moans and growls from the dark.
- Chambers of the Esteemed (3) — Restored ruins
  repurposed as lodgings for the Sword Nobles and
  their guards; ten silver per night minimum.
  Laughter and mirthful murmurs seep through the
  doors. Heavily armed guards bar the way.
- Gates of Battle (4) — Twin gates with betting odds
  boards outside; opened only for celebrated bouts.
  Behind the closed gates: shouts and screams from
  an excited audience.
- The Arena (5) — Enormous amphitheater cut from
  stone; pillars decorated with winter elven battle
  imagery; balconies for the Sword Nobles; Kvaldor's
  private observation balcony above all of it.
  Atmosphere always barbarous and loud. The audience
  is more bloodthirsty than the combatants in the pit.
  Rarely more than a hundred spectators today; thousands
  filled it in the elven era.
- The Cave of the Howler (6) — Surprisingly warm and
  lit dimly by luminescent moss. A dark shape prowls
  the far wall — Howler the captive Dire Wolf growls
  at anyone who enters. A stone door at the back
  conceals a staircase leading deeper underground.

**Notable figures:**

- **Kvaldor the Slayer** — Owner and ruler; immense,
  gray-haired, always armored; broadsword on his belt;
  knows about the underground chamber and what guards it
- **Meldendre** — A Sword Noble; vain, testy, secretly
  an Alderlander spy; believes Kvaldor's gold is hidden
  in the cave behind the Howler
- **Nalia the Nimble** — Popular summer elf fighter;
  quick and skilled with two long daggers
- **Vaina** — Fiery red hair, white eyes; secretly
  possesses the ability to briefly see the future;
  has fought here for five years; patron is Kvaldor

See `campaigns/bitter_reach.md` for the Seal of Fire,
its guardian Gibil, and what lies beneath the arena.

### Frostwind Hold

**Type:** Orc settlement — permanent fortified village
**Location:** Variable; Barren Hills or higher terrain
near the Morma Glacier

Frostwind Hold is the main settlement of the Frostwind
Clan — two hundred orcs and the most powerful
shamanic tradition in the Bitter Reach. Where the
human settlements on the coast are grim and quiet,
Frostwind Hold is loud, active, and smells of
cook-fires and reindeer.

The orcs are wary but not reflexively hostile. Visitors
who show respect are let through the gate and offered
food. Visitors who do not show respect are removed.

The air around Frostwind Hold is unusually clean and
fresh. A brisk wind always blows through the settlement.
Those who sleep here report lighter dreams than usual —
dreams of open sky and the sensation of flight. This
is an effect of the Seal of Wind, hidden in a winter
elven tower in a valley behind the settlement.

The Frostwind Clan knows the Seal is there and has
taken it upon themselves to protect it. They will
not discuss this openly with outsiders.

Hoyra leads the Frostwind Clan and is the most
powerful orc shaman in the Bitter Reach. She knows
Elemental Magic — a closely guarded secret even among
the orcs. She will not teach it to anyone who has not
earned genuine trust. See `kin.md` for more on Hoyra.

**Key locations:**

- The Eternal Wall (1) — Fortified outer wall;
  Snow Boar Riders patrol
- Gate (2) — Controlled entry; orcs evaluate all
  visitors before granting passage
- Dwellings (3) — Longhouse-style structures built
  into the hillside
- The Fire Hut (4) — Central communal fire; where
  food is shared and disputes adjudicated
- The Hunters' Circle (5) — Where the best hunters
  meet to plan and where clan lore is maintained
  orally
- The All-seeing Eye (6) — Hoyra's tower; the
  druidic focus of the settlement
- The Council Hall (7/8) — Where Hoyra and the
  clan elders meet; outsiders are not admitted
  without invitation
- The Trader (9) — Orc goods: reindeer meat,
  furs, dried fish, and the occasional artifact
  retrieved from ruins
- The Orc's Rest (10) — Sleeping quarters for
  honored guests; you must earn the right to
  be offered this
- Snow Boar Pen (11) — Snow Boar Rider mounts
- The Cold Caves (12) — Storage and secondary
  shelter cut into the rock; deeper passages
  lead toward the old winter elven tower

See `campaigns/bitter_reach.md` for the Seal of Wind
and what happens if it is threatened or destroyed.

### Palace of the Ice Giants

**Type:** Fortress — ancient winter elven stronghold,
repurposed by the Ice Giants
**Location:** Deep in the White Peaks; no map marker;
only reachable with a guide or by accident

Blaudewedd created the Ice Giants to guard the fettered
winter elves after the curse fell. Over three thousand
years their numbers have dwindled from a thousand to
roughly a dozen. They occupy a stronghold built from
enormous blocks of ice and ancient stone, originally
a winter elven fortress.

King Bele rules what remains. He is ancient, uncertain,
and torn between his two advisors Bor and Rym — Bor
holds to the original mission; Rym has grown tired
of it. The Ice Giants are not hostile by default, but
they are not simple to deal with. They have no use
for flattery.

The palace has no fixed path to it. Adventurers
typically reach it through the guidance of the orcs
(Hoyra will send escorts to those she trusts) or by
stumbling into it during a storm or a pursuit.

**Key locations:**

- The Crystal Gate (1) — A glass gate across a
  bridgeless ravine; Ferenblaud built it as a
  demonstration that he feared no one; it still
  opens for anyone who approaches
- The Hall of Statues (2) — Galleries of the Ice
  Giants' chronicle work — ice sculptures recording
  events in the Bitter Reach; some recent
- The Throne Room (3) — King Bele's seat; where
  he deliberates with Bor and Rym; the Seal
  of Earth is below this room
- Chamber of the Fallen (4) — Memorial to the
  Ice Giants who have died in three thousand years
  of service
- Observation Towers (5) — High enough to see
  across the Bitter Reach; Ice Giants use them
  as watch posts

See `campaigns/bitter_reach.md` for the Seal of
Earth, the conflict between Bor and Rym, and what
Wurda's involvement means for the campaign.

### Wyrm's Fist (Alderlander Flagship)

**Type:** Warship — mobile adventure site
**Location:** Variable; initially anchored outside
Northfall; may relocate based on campaign events

Wyrm's Fist is the command vessel of General Baraggor
of the Alderlander expeditionary force. Built from
black oak, several decks tall, armed with bolt
throwers capable of killing Frost Dragons. The ship
flies the royal seal of Alderland from its mainmast.

Three other Alderlander warships accompany Wyrm's Fist:
Wyrm's Blade, Wyrm's Shield, and Wyrm's Hope. Each
carries approximately one hundred sailors and two
hundred fifty soldiers. The arrival of the fleet came
as a shock to the entire Bitter Reach — even Ingmarg
did not know the Alderlander king had committed a force
this large.

Access to Wyrm's Fist requires a boat. The ship's
own rowboats are its only practical connection to
land, and they are hoisted back up after use to
prevent them freezing in.

See `kin.md` for General Baraggor's background and
public goals.

**Key locations:**

- Forecastle (1) — Lookout post; crew stationed here
  spot approaching vessels and threats
- Quarterdeck (2) — Navigation and command; Baraggor
  holds briefings here
- Baraggor's Private Chambers (3) — The general's
  quarters; where seized artifacts are stored
- The Demon's Den (4) — A locked compartment that
  houses Versipel, Baraggor's bound demon
- War Room (5) — Maps, plans, intelligence; the
  operational center of the Alderlander campaign
- Spoils of War (6) — Holds seized artifacts and
  valuables
- Cells (7) — Prisoners are kept here; also used
  as an interrogation staging point
- Torture Chamber (8) — Operational; the master
  torturer's tools are maintained and ready
- Dormitory (9) — Soldier barracks; overcrowded
  and demoralizing
- Galley (10) — Food; the cook and the scullion
  keep their own counsel
- The Bilge (11) — Where seized goods overflow
  the main hold and where rats work
- The Bilge access also connects to the cells
  via a maintenance hatch

See `campaigns/bitter_reach.md` for Baraggor's
true mission and Versipel's role in the campaign.

### The Sunken City (Harbor of Dreams)

**Type:** Underwater ruins — partially accessible
**Location:** Variable; must be placed adjacent to
coast or sea ice; its existence is known along the
Silver Coast but its exact location is not

This was Ferenblaud's foremost harbor during the reign
of Rodenvale. Blaudewedd's curse sank it — either
directly or as a consequence of the great magical
upheaval. The harbor now lies beneath the ocean,
partially frozen, partially flooded.

The Seal of Water is inside the tallest lighthouse
of the Sunken City, placed there by Blaudewedd to
draw magical energy from the harbor's power as a
former center of the Winter King's naval operations.
The lighthouse is underwater. Reaching it requires
protection against cold and a means of breathing
beneath the surface.

Fishermen and hunters along the coast know the
Sunken City attracts the largest fish in the ocean.
Pikebeasts are sighted here frequently. Anyone who
cuts a hole in the ice above it can catch exceptional
fish — if they survive the process.

The Guardian of the Seal of Water is the Giant Squid
Abzu, who hunts from the flooded lighthouse and
defends the surrounding waters.

**Key locations:**

- The Lonely Nanuik (1) — A cub sitting at an
  ice hole where its mother was taken by Abzu;
  the ice around the hole was broken from below
- The Misgrown (2) — A group of Misgrown have
  established a camp on the ice above the city;
  their presence is not coincidental
- The Plaza (3) — A partially above-water section
  of the old harbor; collapsed columns, broken
  paving, elven inscription still legible on one
  arch
- The Temple (4) — Underwater; whatever god was
  worshipped here is not one of Ravenland
- The Lighthouse (5) — The tallest remaining
  structure; mostly submerged; the Seal of Water
  is inside

See `campaigns/bitter_reach.md` for the Seal of
Water, Abzu, and what the Misgrown presence here
means.

### Palace of the Winter King

**Type:** Ancient fortress — the campaign's final site
**Location:** Highest reaches of the White Peaks;
no map marker; reachable only when the campaign has
advanced enough for the location to become findable

For three thousand years, Ferenblaud has been frozen
on his crystal throne. His palace has been empty,
haunted by illusions of what it once was. As the
Seals are broken one by one, the curse loosens, the
illusions give way to the living, and the palace
comes back to life.

At the start of the campaign the palace is silent.
By the time all five Seals are broken it is a
functioning stronghold with hundreds of winter elves,
active forges, and Ferenblaud himself free to move
and command.

The palace was built to demonstrate power, not to be
defensible. The great crystal gate opens for anyone
who approaches — Ferenblaud's statement that he feared
nothing. This remains true and remains a vulnerability.

The Frost Dragon Scrana — Ferenblaud's bonded dragon,
sister of Ravenland's Scarne — is imprisoned in the
palace with her master. When enough Seals are broken
she stirs alongside him.

**Key locations:**

- The White Gate (1) — The crystal bridge entrance;
  guards appear only after the third Seal is broken;
  until then it is unguarded and open
- The Courtyard (2) — Where awakened winter elves
  gather; where the army forms as the campaign
  advances
- The Well (3) — Functional; cold enough to be
  dangerous
- The Forges (4) — Ferenblaud's weapon forges;
  they reactivate when two or more Seals are broken
- Storehouses (5) — Supplies for the campaign to
  come; some have been raided by scavengers over
  the centuries
- The Dungeons (6) — Below the main level; prisoners
  from the early awakening period are held here
- The Hall of Prayers (7) — Not a Hall of Prayer to
  any god of Ravenland; the imagery is astronomical
  and entity-summoning in nature
- The Chamber of Dreams (8) — Where Ferenblaud's
  visions were sent outward during his torpor; still
  active even before he fully wakes
- The Throne of the Winter King (9) — Ferenblaud's
  crystal throne; encased in cursed ice that cannot
  be destroyed by mortal force until the Seals
  are broken; Scrana is imprisoned nearby

See `campaigns/bitter_reach.md` for the five-stage
awakening of the palace, the endgame confrontation,
and the three armies' competing offers to the PCs.

## Generic Site Types

The following categories appear across all regions
of the Bitter Reach. Adventure sites can be generated
using the tables in the campaign source.

### Ice Caves

Natural or artificial caverns beneath the ice. Used
as shelter, as storage by various kin, as hiding places
by fugitives, and as graves. Ice caves can be found
anywhere in the glacier or the Barren Hills. Many
collapse without warning. The older ones have been
here since Rodenvale and may contain frozen remnants
of the old kingdom.

### Elven Ruins

Structures from the age of Rodenvale — towers, halls,
fortresses, harbors, and temples scattered across
the Bitter Reach from the Silver Coast to the White
Peaks. Most are partially buried under ice. The degree
to which they have been explored varies. Ruins close
to the coast have usually been picked over; ruins in
the Barren Hills and deeper terrain may be intact.

Stone Ravens and Stone Guardians are frequently found
still active in elven ruins. See `bestiary.md`.

### Fishing Villages

Small permanent settlements on the Silver Coast and
along rivers. Population between twenty and a hundred.
Family-based, suspicious of outsiders, but bound by
tradition to offer what aid can be spared to those in
genuine need. Most have a basic inn, a store, and a
wharf. See Hope's Last Rest above as a named example.
