# Bloodmarch — Kin

**Region:** Aslene (the Bloodmarch)
**Source:** Erik Granström, Free League Publishing
**Campaign:** Legacy of Horn

---

## The Five Horse Clans

Five distinct peoples who crossed from the Drawn Lands.
They are called "horse clans" because their economy and
war culture centers on horses. Intermarriage is common;
cultural identity is clan-based, not genetically enforced.

### Houns (Clan of Horn)

Western plains. Sedentary farming and stock-breeding culture.
The most established clan in terms of permanent settlements.

**Keeper of the Astra:** Helm of Horn — full-face helm with
moonstone set in the forehead. Currently held by the Houn
chieftain **Selligar Horne**, a pragmatic old man who has
held his position through careful neutrality during clan
conflicts.

**Cultural notes:**
- Houn hospitality is deliberate and formal. Travelers
  are assessed before being invited in.
- The Houns farm the black volcanic soil extensively.
  Their food culture uses ash-cured meats and root vegetables
  most horse clans do not grow.
- Selligar Horne is not a warrior. He runs the western
  territory through alliances and trade rather than force.
  He distrusts the Pyrolytes.

### Sabirians (Clan of the Grasslands)

Central grasslands and river valleys. The Sabirians trace
their lineage to Sella the Liberator. Their culture is
matrilineal — inheritance, leadership, and clan membership
pass through the female line.

**Keeper of the Astra:** Sella's Dragonboot — a single
left boot, armored, knee-high. Currently held by the
chieftain **Mommodar**, a young woman who inherited the
position recently.

**Cultural notes:**
- The Sabirians are the fastest riders in the Bloodmarch.
  Their horses are bred specifically for the open grassland.
- Sabirian women lead both domestically and in war. Men
  hold secondary positions but are not suppressed — the
  culture is matriarchal, not anti-male.
- Mommodar is young enough to be underestimated. She is
  not.

### Caberians (Clan of Craft and Trade)

Eastern hills and river passes. Traders and craftspeople.
The Caberians work the most extensively with non-clan kin —
including Vasnians and Caprid dwarves.

**Keeper of the Astra:** Glasstooth — a dagger of translucent
material, also called Serpent's Bane. Currently held by
the chieftain **Merdekai**, a merchant-warrior of middle
age who views most things in terms of their practical value.

**Cultural notes:**
- Caberian towns are the most cosmopolitan in the Bloodmarch.
  Vasnians, dwarves, and halflings all have quarters in
  the larger Caberian settlements.
- Merdekai is not a fan of the Pyrolytes, who he regards
  as expensive and politically unpredictable.
- The Caberians maintain the best road network inside the
  Bloodmarch.

### Galdanes (The Northern Clans)

Northern uplands and mountain valleys. Struck hardest by
the Demon Flood. Three hundred years later they remain the
most scarred and the most suspicious of outsiders.

**Keeper of the Astra:** Arrows of the Fire Wyrm — three
arrows (all three together constitute one Astra). Currently
held by the clan authority, with the Rubor-Kolor movement
complicating ownership questions.

The Galdanes are internally fractured:

- **Kormella Mira** — traditional Galdane leader. Older.
  Wants the clan to survive with its culture intact. Does
  not embrace Rubor-Kolor but does not actively oppose it
  either.
- **Trandesso Haveman** — leader of the Rubor-Kolor faction
  within the Galdanes. Actively converts sub-clans by force
  and has begun eliminating those who resist. Wears all red.

**Cultural notes:**
- Galdane hospitality to outsiders is minimal and conditional.
  A Ravenlander who enters Galdane territory without invitation
  is likely to be turned around or killed.
- The mountain communities are tight-knit. Everyone knows
  everyone. Strangers are remembered.
- The Arrows of the Fire Wyrm are a contested object even
  within the clan.

### Quards (Southern Pastoralists)

Southern plains and border territories near former Gander's
Pass. The most heterodox clan — the Quards absorbed
significant Vasnian and orc influence over the centuries.

**Keeper of the Astra:** Goblet Staff of Have — a walking
staff that also functions as a ritual goblet. Currently
held by the chieftain **Jorgundos Ash**, a heavyset older
man who runs a trading hub at the southern edge of the
Bloodmarch.

**Cultural notes:**
- The Quards are the most relaxed of the horse clans.
  Their southern territory is less touched by Crimson
  Forests and farther from the volcano.
- Jorgundos Ash is a fixer. He knows everyone, remembers
  old debts, and doesn't ask too many questions.
- Quard towns welcome outsiders more readily than any
  other clan territory.

## Vasnians

The original settled people of Aslene. Predated the horse
clans. Did not flee when the clans arrived — they absorbed
the newcomers over generations while being absorbed in turn.

Modern Vasnians are largely indistinguishable culturally
from the horse clan people they live among. They maintain
their own family lineages and oral traditions but do not
operate as a separate political bloc. Some Vasnian towns
exist independently; most Vasnians live in mixed communities.

## Red Elves

Unique to the Bloodmarch. Live inside Crimson Forests. Not
enslaved at any point in Bloodmarch history — the Aslene
orcs specifically refused to participate in elf slavery
from before the founding period. Red elves in the Bloodmarch
do not experience the general elven cultural trauma around
slavery in the same way Ravenland elves do.

Red elves are immune to Crimson Sickness — they emerged from
the same process as the forests themselves.

**Prune:** A red elf who has been injected with Blue Tar
becomes a Prune. The transformation is irreversible. Prunes
are blue-veined and explosive — physical contact with fire
causes them to detonate. They are used as weapons by some
factions. See `bestiary.md` for statblock.

**Sarcoptes:** Scorpion-like creatures that red elves breed
and ride as mounts.

## Caprid Dwarves

Dwarves with a horn growing from the center of their foreheads.
The horn is a real horn — cartilage-based, grows through
life, can be lost and regrows slowly. Young Caprid dwarves
with their first horn are called **baas kul**; adults with
full horns are **kulli**.

The Caprid dwarves live in **Firestead** — a geothermal
city under the Firelands, carved from and built on volcanic
basalt. The city never cools. Visitors from outside consider
it punishing. The Caprids consider our climate punishing.

**Religion:** The Caprid dwarves worship Have as the primary
creator-deity. Their priests are also Magma Song druids —
the two traditions are the same position. A priest who cannot
use Magma Song at Rank 2 or above is not accepted into the
hierarchy.

**The abolished clans:** The Caprid dwarves fought a civil
war before the Demon Flood. Several sub-clans were formally
abolished by a judgment of the whole community. Being of
abolished clan lineage is a permanent stigma. Abolished
descendants are not slaves — they exist and participate in
society — but they cannot hold priest positions, inherit
real property, or marry into living clans without special
dispensation. Some have taken clan tattoos from living
clans who accepted them.

**Products:** Witherbeam was built by Caprid craftspeople.
Dense Water is a Caprid druid product. Their metalwork is
highly regarded.

## Aslene Orcs

Distinct from Ravenland orcs in cultural terms. They were
never enslaved by elves. They do not carry the specific
historical trauma of the elven period. Their ethics come
from Volitia rather than from centuries of resistance.

**Étosh:** The core concept — see `gods.md` for full
description. Killing is not inherently bad. Cruelty without
purpose is. The individual's choice is their own. Collective
guilt is rejected.

**Female warrior culture:** Orc women in Aslene are the
primary hunters and warriors. This is not a gender rule
imposed from outside — it emerged from Volitia practice
in which the Sisterhood of Viraga holds authority over
community ethics. Men participate in hunts and war but
the female role is primary.

The coming-of-age rite is a solo hunt. A young female orc
must complete a successful hunt and present it to the
Sisterhood before she is recognized as an adult. Male
orcs have a separate rite that involves the community
rather than individual action.

**Relationship with other kin:** Aslene orcs live in
mixed communities with Vasnians and Quard horse clan members.
They do not mix much with Galdanes. They have a respectful
and cautious relationship with Caprid dwarves — mutual
recognition of hard competence.

## Wolfkin

Called Ranghöge by themselves. Appear as large wolves that
walk upright. Not as socially isolated as Ravenland wolfkin —
the Bloodmarch wolfkin have integrated more fully into
certain communities.

**Umbras:** Holy warriors. Active specifically at dawn and
dusk as part of Nightwalker religious observance. They do
not work regular hours.

**Grargs:** Half-wolfkin, half-orc hybrids. Rare. They occur
when wolfkin and orc communities are in close enough contact.
Grargs are not outcast — they are unusual and sometimes
distrusted but not formally excluded from either community.
They tend to be large, aggressive, and deeply unhappy.

---

## Moon Elves

Eastern coastal elves of the original continent Anoma. A small
population exists in the Bloodmarch, typically in remote or
isolated locations.

**The moonstone:** Moon elves have a moonstone embedded in
their chest, not the ruby of standard (Ravenland) elves.
The stone glows faintly blue in darkness. Removing it
kills the elf.

**Mentalism:** Moon elves practice Mentalism. Their mastery
is incomplete — they are descended from the great moon elf
tradition of Anoma but separated from that heritage for
centuries.

**Disappearance:** A moon elf who decides to leave a scene
is almost impossible to stop. They vanish instantly and
cannot be tracked through standard means.

## Halflings and Goblins

The Penite — pilgrim communities from Alderland who crossed
Gander's Pass before it collapsed seeking a holy land as
described in Eor's scriptures. Their salt religion (see
`gods.md`) defines them culturally.

Standard halfling and goblin mechanics apply. Penite
communities are scattered across the southern and eastern
Bloodmarch. They are not fighters by tradition; they are
traders, farmers, and travelers.

---

## Ravenlanders and Alderlanders

Now entering through Shadowgate for the first time in three
centuries. Most Bloodmarch communities have not seen a
Ravenlander before and have nothing but old stories to go
on. Alderlanders from before the Gander's Pass collapse
were at least known; Ravenlanders are genuinely foreign.

Standard human and kin mechanics apply for Ravenlanders
who are playing characters from outside the region.
