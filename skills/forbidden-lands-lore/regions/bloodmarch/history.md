# Bloodmarch — History

**Region:** Aslene (the Bloodmarch)
**Source:** Erik Granström, Free League Publishing
**Campaign:** Legacy of Horn

---

## Chronology

### Age of Vasnians (Before the Horse Clans)

Aslene was first settled by the Vasnians — a people of mixed
kin backgrounds who arrived far earlier than the horse clans.
They built towns, farmed the black plains, and developed their
own culture. The Vasnians are not specifically aligned with
any single kin; their culture became its own thing.

The red elves predate even the Vasnians. They live inside
Crimson Forests and have done so for as long as anyone can
account for.

### Arrival of the Horse Clans (Pre-Dating Records)

Five horse clans crossed into Aslene from the Drawn Lands —
a place that appears in multiple cultural traditions as
an origin, always described as "the other side." Most horse
clan traditions do not treat the journey itself as historical.
It simply is the beginning.

Each clan arrived as a distinct people and occupied different
terrain. Relations between clans were violent at first; raids
and wars shaped territorial lines that still hold today.

The clans:

- **Houns** — occupiers of the western plains; linked to
  the Helm of Horn
- **Sabirians** — horsewomen of the grasslands; linked to
  Sella's Dragonboot
- **Caberians** — eastern craftspeople and traders; linked
  to Glasstooth
- **Galdanes** — northern/mountain clans; linked to
  Arrows of the Fire Wyrm
- **Quards** — southern pastoralists; linked to the
  Goblet Staff of Have

### Sella the Liberator

Sella was a Sabirian woman who unified the five horse clans
against a common enemy that is not named in surviving oral
tradition — only described as "the demon" or "the old dark."
She was the first to forge Horn's Astra — the five sacred
objects — or to distribute existing objects as symbols of
unified purpose. Traditions differ on whether she made them
or found them.

She is called "the Liberator" in all five clan traditions.
The Sabirians claim her as their ancestor. The other clans
do not dispute this but do not emphasize it.

Her true history is a campaign spoiler. See
`campaigns/bloodmarch.md`.

### The Demon Flood — 875 AS

Zygofer, the Lich, opened Shadowgate Pass and poured demon
troops into the Bloodmarch from Ravenland. The demon armies
carried Crimson Sickness. Entire clans were infected or
destroyed.

The Galdanes were struck first and hardest — their territory
sits nearest Shadowgate. Entire clans collapsed into Crimson
Sickness.

The Bloodmarch descended into catastrophic war. The details
of how the Demon Flood ended — specifically who made what
deal with the demon prince guarding Shadowgate — are a
campaign spoiler. See `campaigns/bloodmarch.md`.

The result was the sealing of Shadowgate Pass and three
hundred years of isolation.

### The Three Centuries of Isolation (875–1165 AS)

No one entered the Bloodmarch through Shadowgate. No one
left through it. The people rebuilt with what they had.

During this period:

- Crimson Forests expanded in the east. Some formerly
  populated areas became uninhabitable.
- The Caprid dwarves sealed many of their old surface routes.
- New towns built to replace those lost in the Demon Flood.
- The religion of Rubor and Kolor developed within the
  Galdane clan territories, eventually spreading inward.
- The Witherbeam was built by Caprid craftspeople — the
  approximate date is disputed.

Contact with Alderland continued through Gander's Pass
during the first century, then that too was cut off.
Whether the collapse of Gander's Pass was natural or
engineered is a campaign spoiler.

### Shadowgate Reopens — 1165 AS

After the events at Vond described in the Raven's Purge
campaign, Shadowgate Pass reopened. The demon guard was
removed. The Bloodmarch is now accessible from Ravenland.

Adventurers entering through Shadowgate find Taregyll —
a rough frontier town that sprang up when the pass first
loosened, populated by opportunists and refugees from both
sides.

The clans' reactions to the reopening range from cautious
welcome to hostility. The Galdanes, who suffered most from
the Demon Flood, are deeply suspicious of outsiders.
