# Orc Religions of the Ravenlands

This reference develops orc religion through abandonment by the elder kin, clan continuity, obedience and refusal, trial, knowledge, ancestry, and the authority of women. The clan histories and practices named in Book 02 are manuscript facts. The Unabandoned and detailed ceremonies are grounded setting inference.

---

## The Gods Who Left and the Kin Who Remained

Orcs possess no single public church. Elves and dwarves say Clay made them as
servants. Many orcs reject the story because it turns enslavement into creation
and obedience into nature. Their strongest religious memory is therefore an
absence: elder masters sent them against human invaders, withdrew, and left
them to hold a war already lost.

Some clans honor **the Unabandoned**, ancestors and living leaders who remained
when flight was possible. Their names are recited with the work, fighters, and
dependents they kept together. A glorious death that leaves the clan helpless
earns less honor than an ugly survival that carries others out.

Female clan authorities tend genealogies and living continuity even where male
rank dominates public ritual. Males duel for standing; females decide which
victories may become marriage, succession, alliance, or exile. Outsiders who
hear only the challenge songs miss the religious judgment that follows them.

**The Stone That Stayed** is a Roka rite. A monolith or smaller upright stone is
surrounded while commands from a past battle are recited. One order that should
have been disobeyed is named with the others. The lesson is uncomfortable for a
clan built on obedience: leaders must be obeyed, and leaders who spend the clan
without purpose become traitors to those who stayed.

Urhur ceremony borrows freely from human and elven courts. Once repeated, an
invented rite can become binding because people organized gifts, rank, and
marriage around it. Their priests care less whether a custom is old than
whether the emperor has performed it publicly and others have relied upon it.

Isir trials with vipers and wasps treat poison endured as kinship with a hostile
world. The trial proves body and will but also consumes many candidates.
Survivors carry religious authority in their altered saliva and split tongues.
A leader who tests only followers and never risks poison loses standing.

Viraga rites use fox tokens, holy plants, secrecy, and the hunt. Their deepest
dogma is that the kin must gain knowledge the former masters denied them. This
can justify theft, abduction, and sacrifice. It also makes destruction of useful
knowledge within the clan an anathema greater than lying to male rulers.

Across clans, grave offenses include selling orcs into elder-kin service,
abandoning dependents during retreat, using surrender law to enslave someone
whose defeat was arranged by treachery, and giving an outsider the concealed
roads by which females and young escape war. Clans disagree over whether fear
itself is shameful or only action ruled by fear.

## Sources and Authority

Orc religion survives in clan practice, challenge formula, women's genealogies,
camp law, initiation scars, burial places, and the commands people still argue
about after the commander is dead. No central priesthood can declare one creed.
An outsider asking for the orc god may be asking the wrong question.

Religious authority is divided. Public leaders judge courage, obedience,
tribute, and war. Female authorities judge descent, adoption, marriage, hidden
routes, stores, and whether a victory left a clan capable of another winter.
Trial survivors carry bodily proof. Elders remember which splendid command
nearly ended the clan.

The division creates checks and hypocrisies. A male chief may proclaim an heir
whom women refuse to place in the genealogy. A Viraga may save the kin through
knowledge gained by kidnapping someone else's craftsperson. A Roka general may
obey a disastrous order because admitting doubt before battle would fracture
the line.

## Creation, Service, and the Elder Kin

Elves and dwarves say Clay made orcs to serve them. Many orcs hear a master's
claim hidden inside the creation story: we made you, therefore your freedom is
theft. Some reject Clay entirely. Others answer that a maker who abandons a
living work loses authority over it. A few clans retain Clay rites but name the
first free orc, rather than Clay, as the founder of their moral world.

The old alliance against humans remains part of orc memory. So does the elder
withdrawal. Religious arguments turn on the exact moment service ended. Did
the command fail when the commanders fled, when supplies ceased, when the war's
purpose changed, or only when living orcs refused it? Different answers support
different leaders now.

The Unabandoned are named with acts, not praise: held the ford until the young
crossed; carried seed instead of a second weapon; returned for the wounded;
refused an order that spent the last hunters. A name without an act does not
enter the recital.

## Daily Observance

At first food, one portion is assigned before warriors eat. Depending on clan,
it belongs to the watch, young, pregnant, injured, absent scouts, or whoever
kept the camp during the raid. The allocation states what the group believes
strength is for.

Weapons are named by obligation more often than by glory. A spear may be “the
road-keeper” because it guarded a retreat. Taking such a weapon without taking
its obligation is theft even when the owner is dead.

Commands are repeated back in altered words. Human observers mistake this for
poor discipline. The repetition proves that the hearer understood purpose as
well as sound. Roka officers formalize it; Isir leaders often punish it; Drifter
bands survive by insisting upon it.

Women keep the routes by which children, wounded people, stores, and knowledge
leave a failing camp. These paths are seldom sung publicly. Markers may be a
fox hair under bark, three stones below a root, or a cut made where a mounted
warrior will not see it.

## Birth, Naming, and Adoption

A child receives a household name before a clan name. The first recognizes who
feeds and guards the infant. The second follows public acceptance and can be
delayed when parentage, captivity, or politics are disputed.

Names taken from enemies are controversial. Some Urhur praise the theft of
prestige. Roka teachers object that a name detached from its act teaches
boasting. Viraga may give a child two names, one fit for a foreign settlement
and one kept inside the clan.

Adoption is common enough to need law. A rescued or captured child can become
kin through feeding, defense, and placement in a genealogy. The rite cannot
erase the circumstances of capture. Some clans keep a witness from the child's
first people; others make ignorance part of belonging. That difference decides
whether adoption repairs war or preserves its theft.

## Trial and Adult Standing

Adult standing requires proof that the person can carry risk without handing
all of it to weaker kin. Combat is one proof. Keeping watch while sick, guiding
dependents through bad ground, holding stores against a popular raider, or
admitting a failed scout report can also qualify.

At **The Weight Behind**, a candidate walks first carrying weapons, then returns
carrying a child, injured person, water vessel, or clan record. The second load
is judged more closely. A person who can advance but cannot bring anything back
is useful to war and dangerous to continuity.

Isir poison trials make survival visible and alter the body. Their religious
authority is real because the risk is real. It becomes fraudulent when leaders
choose candidates they expect to die, withhold known remedies, or exempt their
own descendants.

## Partnership, Fertility, and Women's Judgment

Partnership joins escape routes and obligations before property. Each partner
names who must be warned if the union breaks, which dependents remain protected,
and what knowledge may not follow a departing spouse. A public duel cannot
settle these terms.

Fertility carries clan weight but does not exhaust female standing. Barren orcs
often join Viraga, where separation from ordinary descent can produce wider
authority. Calling this mere compensation misses the political fact: a Viraga
may know the skills, weaknesses, and marriage secrets of several clans.

Female judgment after a male contest decides whether victory changes succession
or only reputation. The loser may retain household authority. The winner may
gain a weapon but no marriage claim. Outsiders who reward the loudest fighter
can discover that no one follows him after the feast.

## Death and the Last Command

Orc funerals distinguish the body from the command that outlived it. Weapons,
food, teeth, stones, or fox tokens vary by clan. The difficult work is naming
which orders remain binding.

At **The Last Command**, followers repeat one order from the dead leader. They
then state whether it ended with death, passes to a successor, or must never be
given again. A successor who refuses the hearing inherits rank without the
dead leader's religious standing.

Bodies lost in retreat are not automatically dishonored. A marker can be raised
at the last place the person was known to protect another. Recovering remains
matters less than recovering the truth of who was abandoned.

## Clan Traditions

### Urhur

Urhur ceremony makes rule visible through feast, costume, title, and public
invention. Emperor Hroka's newest rite can become binding once gifts and
marriages rely on it. The religious question is reliance, not age. This makes
Urhur adaptable and vulnerable to a ruler who changes custom faster than people
can settle its costs.

### Roka

Roka religion joins obedience to the duty of worthy command. Monoliths mark
orders expected to outlast a speaker. The Stone That Stayed includes one order
that should have been refused so discipline does not become worship of waste.

### Isir

Isir faith sacralizes survival in a hostile body and world. Venom, split tongue,
swarm, and pain create a caste whose proof cannot be borrowed. Their danger is
making every question of wisdom answerable through endurance.

### Viraga

Viraga treat denied knowledge as stolen inheritance. Fox tokens mark indirect
paths. Holy plants loosen speech before the hunt, while practical knowledge
captured from other kin enters guarded teaching. The rite can preserve the
orcs or turn teachers into prey after their usefulness ends.

### Drifters

Drifters carry fragments of clan practice without a fixed genealogy. They may
keep the Unabandoned by adding anyone who chose the band when departure was
easy. Clan priests call this theft. Drifters answer that staying created the
claim.

## Captivity, Surrender, and Enslavement

Because orcs were treated as a servant kin, captivity carries religious weight.
A prisoner may owe labor, ransom, exchange, or a bounded term. Turning capture
into hereditary ownership repeats the elder claim most clans condemn.

Practice often violates doctrine. Orc raiders take slaves. Viraga abduct
teachers. Isir bands kill surrendering enemies. The uncomfortable truth is not
that orcs forgot enslavement. It is that a people can name an old injury clearly
and reproduce it when power changes hands.

A captive seeking standing should be given a term that can be completed and a
witness who survives completion. Without both, service is slavery with ritual
words around it.

## Magic, Demons, and Altered Bodies

Roka hate magic and demons because they remember battlefield consequences.
Viraga pursue useful knowledge wherever it lies. Urhur rulers collect spectacle
and specialists. One orc camp may burn a sorcerer while another bargains for
the same spell before winter.

Altered bodies are judged through cause and obligation. An Isir's venom follows
accepted trial. A Misgrown limb imposed by a human master records assault. A
demonic gift accepted to save the clan may earn honor and permanent suspicion.
Appearance alone cannot settle the account.

## Fault, Repair, and Anathema

An orc who fails a duty can repair standing through replacement labor, public
admission, protection of those endangered, return of stolen clan knowledge, or
a dangerous service chosen by the injured group. Trial without repair proves
only that the offender can suffer.

Acts beyond ordinary repair include selling kin into permanent service,
revealing refuge roads for personal safety, arranging defeat to claim surrender
rights, destroying knowledge needed by the clan, and giving an order meant to
kill followers merely to remove rivals.

The worst leaders make followers bear an ordeal that the leader names holy and
will not share. Orc religion contains enough memory of abandonment to recognize
the pattern. Recognition does not guarantee resistance.

## Evidence at the Table

Show orc belief through a command repeated back with one crucial word changed,
a fox hair beneath bark, a genealogy no male chief may touch, an upright stone
carrying a condemned order, or a feast portion assigned to someone absent.

Useful disputes concern who remained, who was carried out, whether a command
still binds, who may teach captured knowledge, and when service becomes the
slavery every clan claims to hate.
