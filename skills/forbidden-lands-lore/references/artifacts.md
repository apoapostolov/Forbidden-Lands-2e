# Artifacts and Named Relics

Every named artifact in the canon. For each: the **myth as told**,
the **truth behind the myth**, the **mechanical hint** (lore-side
only — for full mechanics see `02-gamemasters-guide/07-artifacts.md`),
and the **current location or holder** where known.

The lore-rule for artifacts in this setting:

> **Every named artifact carries a curse, a contested provenance,
> or a binding that was load-bearing somewhere. The artifact is
> the question, not the answer.**

## Arrow of the Fire Wyrm

**Myth:** A young Aslene rider, riding alone across the desert,
heard the cry of a chained beast in the **Mountain of Tongues**.
Inside, **Erinya the great fire wyrm** was bound by a sorcerer
who used her flame to forge weapons. The rider broke the chain.
Erinya wept three drops of fire onto three arrowheads of black
glass; whoever bears one will always strike fire when needed.

**Truth:** Erinya is real and is a servant of **Horn**. The
sorcerer who bound her is the same Aslene tradition the
**Zertorme** cult at Amber's Peak descends from. Three such
arrows are known; one is in **Quard hands**, one is at
**Amber's Peak**, the third is rumored buried with a Galdane
chief in a **Margelda** cairn.

**Mechanical hint:** Each arrow burns once. The arrow is consumed.
The flame leaps from the arrowhead and continues to burn whatever
it strikes for several rounds.

## Asina

**Myth:** A young woman fled the demon-lord she had been promised
to and sailed for the islands. Aboard ship she carried only a knife
of strange iron given by her mother. The captain caught her hiding
and threw her overboard. The knife she dropped struck the deck
upright; the captain pulled it free and his hand crisped to bone.
The knife was **Asina** — the smuggled fang of a worm-god. She
drowned. The captain died. The knife remained.

**Truth:** Asina is the worked tooth of an ancient **Wyrm-aspect
serpent**, illegally taken from the Congregation of the Serpent
by the woman's mother, who was a **Psychopomp** of Vivírula's line
disgraced for theft. The knife was ritual property; its theft is
an open wound in the Congregation. Anyone presenting Asina to a
Psychopomp risks immediate seizure and execution.

**Mechanical hint:** Cuts demons as if their armor were not there.
Stings the wielder if used dishonorably.

**Location:** Surfaces and disappears. Last credibly seen with a
fence in **Lumra**.

## Barkhyde

**Myth:** A trapper in **Wilderwood** felled an Ent in a winter
of starvation, took the bark for clothing, and woke the next
morning with the bark grown to his skin. He could not be cut.
He could not be burned. He could not be embraced. His wife and
children left him. He starved to death because he could not feel
his own hunger. The bark fell from his bones and a hunter found
it and cut a coat from it.

**Truth:** Killing an Ent is a sin against Clay. The bark
remembers and remembers and remembers. Wearing it works as armor
because the bark refuses to allow harm to come to anything; it
also refuses to allow love or warmth to come.

**Mechanical hint:** Excellent armor. Whoever wears it long enough
loses something each season they cannot get back.

**Location:** Coat is held by a hermit in the **Dankwood** —
known to the **Golden Bough**, who consider returning it to soil
their problem to solve.

## Carskenfoot's Boots

**Myth:** **Aras Carskenfoot** of Belderand was the greatest stone
singer of his era — five hundred years dead. His feet sweated so
badly he died of foot-rot at thirty. His apprentice cured the
boots in lime and salt and gave them as a wedding gift to a
travelling singer. The boots were stolen from his cairn three
times and recovered three times.

**Truth:** Aras's gift was in his **bones**, not his feet. The
boots are an ordinary pair of well-made dwarven walking boots,
preserved well, with the stink of his death still faintly in the
seams. Stone singers across the Forbidden Lands compete to
possess them as a relic; the actual boots are no help to anyone.

**Mechanical hint:** They walk well. They smell badly. They are
worth too much in the wrong company.

**Location:** Belderand has them in a **glass case** in the
stone singers' temple. Two thefts last decade; both relics
recovered turned out to be fakes.

## Clay's Rosary

**Myth:** Old as the seas. The maiden **Thousand-Heart** loved
the sea-behemoth **Gargal** and was tricked by the dolphin
**Puld**. Her lover **Adembre** turned to red shoreline algae in
his grief. Her tears salted the sea. She gave Clay a rosary of
beads from the eyes of every creature she had loved, and Clay
gave it back to the world to keep the weather honest.

**Truth:** Clay-druids of the **Order of Maidens** keep the
rosary in cycles — when the rosary is in any one Maiden's hands
too long, the weather around that Maiden becomes erratic and
violent. The Maidens rotate the keepership monthly to spread the
strain.

**Mechanical hint:** Manipulates weather: each bead is a prayer
for one weather effect. Each prayer ages the praying druid.

**Location:** Currently held by **Maiden Lodavre** at
**Maidenholm**.

## Feroxa's Claws

**Myth:** **Feroxa** was the daughter of **Rust** and **Heme**.
She refused both their gifts and was struck down for impiety;
her body broke into the **hematite** of the world. Where the
hematite is forged into iron and the iron into a clawed gauntlet,
Feroxa's hand returns. The gauntlet hunts what its wearer hates
most.

**Truth:** **Teramalda** the Death Knight wears Feroxa's Claws
on her left hand. She has worn them for over three hundred years.
The Claws hunt the wearer's enemies; the wearer cannot put them
down once worn. Garmar Four-Beard's mistake was the cooking-alive
that woke them; before that the Claws were dormant in Teramalda's
inherited armor.

**Mechanical hint:** Acts of vengeance with the Claws are
mechanically devastating; ordinary acts cost the wearer.

**Location:** On Teramalda's left hand. She moves through the
**western woods** between Vond and Belderand.

## Ivelde

**Myth:** A drinking horn that never empties. The original drinker,
named **Ivelde**, drank from it for nineteen days and his liver
burst.

**Truth:** Whatever liquid is poured in continues to flow out for
nine days. **Empty it of stale beer and pour fresh; the stale
will come out for nine more days first.** Most often filled with
holy water by Maidens, with strong ale by drifters, and with blood
by the Vampyrs in two recorded cases.

**Mechanical hint:** Inexhaustible source of any one liquid for a
period after refill.

**Location:** A **Stridehome** brewer keeps it on a shelf with a
wooden stopper.

## Menkaura's Tooth

**Myth:** **Menkaura** was the giant spider queen of **Dankwood**.
The Ents broke her queendom; her carapace was carried to
**Stridehome**. Her largest fang was filed into a sword and given
to the war-leader's son. **Cuts everything.**

**Truth:** Cuts almost everything; eight things known to resist
the cut, and they are written on a copper tag tied to the hilt:
**Ent-bark; lava-iron from Horn's mountain; the breast-ruby of an
Elf still living; the scales of a great dragon; demon-mog;
purified moonstone; Huge's-Hearth gold; and the carapace of an
Insectoid queen.** Other than these, the sword cuts.

**Mechanical hint:** A sword that ignores armor or hardness for
most targets. Bound to the spider's grudge — strikes against ents,
elves, and dragons grow weaker by use.

**Location:** Held by **the war-leader's grandson's grandson** at
**Stridehome**, who has never drawn it.

## Phantom Daggers

**Myth:** Three daggers forged from the bones of the demon
**Throat-of-Oaths** by the **Order of the Silent**. The daggers
choose their wielder; the wielder cannot speak after being
chosen until the contract is fulfilled.

**Truth:** Tools of the Silent. Three exist; two are in the
hands of active assassins; one was lost in **The Hollows** during
a contract that went sideways.

**Mechanical hint:** Daggers throw themselves to a marked target
and return. The wielder cannot speak until the marked target is
dead.

**Location:** Two unknown. One — said to be in the silt under
the **bridge of The Hollows**.

## Queen Agatha's Twin Tablets

**Myth:** **Queen Agatha** of an ancient line wrote two tablets
to bind her two warring sons. Whoever holds one tablet feels what
the holder of the other feels and hears what they hear. The sons
killed each other anyway.

**Truth:** The tablets work as described. Most often used by
spies. The two original sons are buried in opposite cairns; the
**Reapenters** are searching for the tablets to coordinate their
hunts.

**Mechanical hint:** Pair of telepathic stones; one-way listening
each way; range very long.

**Location:** Both tablets in the **Library of Farhaven**, sealed
in lead under Psychopomp Aspis's authority.

## Scarnesbane

**Myth:** The hammer **Huge** sent to **Oramund** to slay
**Scarne**. After the slaying, **King Totela Goldbeard** of the
**Crombe** clan used the hammer to carve **Totela's Head** from a
mountain in his own likeness. Huge punished his vanity with
beard-rot and cast the hammer out into the world.

**Truth:** Scarne was not slain — she is imprisoned beneath
**Stonegarden**. Whether the hammer ever struck her at all is
disputed. The hammer is real; whoever wields it can wake Scarne
in her chains, which is why no dwarf is allowed to retrieve it.

**Mechanical hint:** Hammer of war and stonebreaking; touches
to certain stones at **Stonegarden** are catastrophic.

**Location:** Lost. Last reliably reported in a **Crombe** raid
on a Galdane camp two centuries ago.

## Nightwalker's Hourglass

**Myth:** A **bat-faced messenger** of the **Nightwalker** brought
an hourglass to a dying king and said _"every grain that falls
returns; turn it twice and you have time to undo what you cannot
bear; turn it three times and you have lost the time you would
have used to die."_ The king turned it three times and lived for
two hundred years and could not die. Eventually his bones
crumbled around an upright skeleton that turned the glass forever.

**Truth:** Two hourglasses are known. One is held by an **Aslene
oneiromancer**; the other was in **Wailer's Hold** when it fell
and is presumed buried.

**Mechanical hint:** Reverses time briefly for the holder. Each
use takes irreversible years from the holder.

**Location:** One in **Aslene** with the oneiromancer; one
unknown.

## Tezaur

**Myth:** A staff carved from the **first Yew that grew on the
grave of a giant**. The Nightwalker's gift to a forgotten druid;
later passed to a **Watcher** who passed it to a **Wolfkin** who
passed it back to a **Melder** who hid it.

**Truth:** Tezaur opens locks of any kind — wooden, iron,
verbal, magical. Each opening leaves a small twig-shaped scar
on the staff. When the staff has no smooth wood left, it crumbles
to dust.

**Mechanical hint:** Universal key. Slowly disintegrates with use.

**Location:** Hidden in the **Dankwood** by a **Melder** who is
the only one who remembers where.

## Tvedra's Twin Rings

**Myth:** **Tvedra** the witch and her lover **Hinrek of Falender**
wore matching rings. Whatever wound Hinrek took, Tvedra took also,
sharing his pain. He survived three battles he should not have.
She died in a fourth, of cumulative wounds, in her bed in Falender
the morning of the battle.

**Truth:** Whoever wears the second ring takes half the wounds
inflicted on the first ring's wearer. The first ring's wearer
takes half. Neither feels the other's pain — both feel the same
fraction of the wound. Wearing both rings on the same hand was
attempted twice; both attempts ended in the wearer being torn
in half from internal stresses neither ring nor body could
explain.

**Mechanical hint:** Damage-sharing. Coordinated use can save a
life; uncoordinated use kills both.

**Location:** Ring of Hinrek is in **Falender** ruins. Ring of
Tvedra is buried with her in a Falender churchyard the **Rust
Brothers** have searched twice.

## Voller's Helmet

**Myth:** **Voller** was a Quard rider who entered a single
combat against a **dread raptor** without his helmet on a bet,
and won, and put the helmet on his horse for the parade home.
The horse was suddenly able to speak. Voller dropped dead the
next morning.

**Truth:** Whatever wears the helmet can speak whatever language
the wearer's master most wants to hear. The horse, who had no
master, spoke in the voice of Voller's dead father. Voller's
heart stopped.

**Mechanical hint:** Compulsion-helmet. Wearer cannot lie to the
person they most love or fear.

**Location:** Held by a **Galdane** chief in **Margelda** who
keeps it on his shrine and refuses to wear it.

## Wail's Horn

**Myth:** **Wail** the wind goddess lost her breath in a fight
with the ocean; a great toothed shark tore her free; she rewarded
the shark with the breath of the air, and the shark became the
first walrus. The horn of the walrus is **Wail's Horn**, which
sounds the goddess's voice across miles. **Hollerbellows**, a
dwarven bard of the Second Alder Wars, blew the horn from the
cliff above an Alderlander army and the army was thrown from
the cliff into the sea. Hollerbellows choked to death on his own
song.

**Truth:** The horn kills what hears it within a half-mile in
the direction it points. Anyone within ten paces of the blower,
including the blower, is killed by the same blow. The horn does
not distinguish friend from enemy. It has been blown three times
in recorded history; each time the blower died.

**Mechanical hint:** Area weapon of immense power that includes
the user.

**Location:** Held in a **Belderranian** vault under three locks.
The dwarves know they cannot use it without dying.

## Well of Tears

**Myth:** A well in a forgotten village whose water grants
**memory of what one has forgotten**. Drinkers retrieve a memory
they had lost; the memory remains and the drinker forgets
something else of equal weight.

**Truth:** The well exists and is in a village whose name has
been forgotten because the entire village drank from it. The
village is somewhere east of **Wilderwood**.

**Mechanical hint:** Trade one memory for another. The trade is
unguided.

**Location:** A well, in a village, whose location is forgotten.

## Wyrm's Key

**Myth:** A serpent-shaped key forged in the temple of the
**Wyrm**. Opens any temple door, any reliquary, any tomb that
honors the Wyrm. Stolen by a young Frailer scholar in **520 AS**;
the **Congregation of the Serpent** has hunted it ever since.

**Truth:** The key opens what its myth says, and also opens any
door in **Farhaven** library — including the doors no one has
opened in nine generations. The Congregation does not want it
returned; they want it destroyed. Most Wyrm doors that have
remained closed in the library have remained closed for reasons
the present Congregation does not fully remember.

**Mechanical hint:** Universal key for one religion's spaces, plus
a few they would rather you didn't enter.

**Location:** Currently in the **Reapenters'** possession;
**Father Geno** carries it on a thong around his neck, hidden
under a black-feather cloak.

## Stanengist — the Hanging Stones Crown

**Myth:** It is said that the first six elves of the Forbidden
Lands — the ones who shaped the rivers, planted the forests, and
gave every creature its nature — after completing their work, had
their heart-rubies gathered in a crown of dwarven gold. The crown
bore six sockets; six stones were set. The elves rested inside
and kept watch. Kings wore the crown for generations, and the
land held together. Then, before humans arrived, the thief
**Merigall** stole three of the six rubies and the land has
been divided ever since. Whoever reassembles the six stones in
the crown and places it on their head will, according to legend,
gain the power to rally all kin and rule the Forbidden Lands.

**Truth:** The crown exists. Multiple independent traditions —
Redrunner dispatches, Canide clan records, and a sealed document
in the Library of Farhaven — confirm both the six original elves
and the theft. The full history of the stones is a matter of
serious contest. The crown's powers beyond the legend are not
publicly known.

_Full provenance and powers contested. See `references/campaigns/ravens_purge.md`._

**Mechanical hint:** A crown of bright dwarven metal with six
ruby sockets; three stones set and three empty in the version
most commonly described. The metal is ancient dwarf gold worked
with a hardening alloy that gives it the strength of steel;
the design is worked as winding greenery, with the six ruby
sockets arranged as fruits: five in a horizontal row, one
elevated above the center. Only a skilled jeweler can safely
set or remove the stones without damaging socket or ruby;
attempts by others invariably damage one or both.

**Location:** Unknown. Current location is the central question
of the Raven's Purge campaign.

## Maligarn — the Giant Slayer

**Myth:** The shieldmaiden **Viridia** fought the bloodthirsty
giant **Scrome**, who had devoured whole villages. She killed
him in a gorge with the sword Maligarn but died of wounds
shortly after. Her companions had her emerald heart placed in
the crown Stanengist in her honor. The emerald later left the
crown and was set into the sword's crossguard, where Viridia
now watches from the blade. The sword has been lost ever since,
passing through many hands and ending in none.

**Truth:** Viridia was real. Whether her death came from wounds
or the encounter with Scrome itself is debated. The emerald in
the sword is of elven origin — how a ruby becomes an emerald
is a question the scholarly traditions of Stonegarden and
Farhaven disagree on. The sword itself has been sighted in
multiple locations over the centuries, never held for long.

_Full provenance and powers contested. See `references/campaigns/ravens_purge.md`._

**Mechanical hint:** A longsword with a copper-twined grip and
a gold crossguard bearing a single large emerald. Old accounts
note the emerald glows faintly green in the dark. The blade is
silver-white, the alloy unknown to any living smith; it holds
an edge that has never required sharpening. Those who have
wielded it describe it as moving almost of its own accord —
as though the sword has a preferred angle of attack and angles
toward it.

**Location:** Lost. Last credible rumor places it in an abandoned
dwarven mine complex in the western ranges.

## Nekhaka — the Scepter of Kings

**Myth:** When **Algared**, first and wisest king of the
Algarian line in Alderland, died, he asked that his ruby heart
be set in his shepherd's crook of alderwood so that he might
continue to counsel his descendants in dreams. The scepter
passed through the Algarian dynasty for generations. When
**King Algarod** was destroyed in the Fourth Alder Wars by
Zygofer's demonic hordes, the scepter was lost somewhere in
the Ravenlands. Its location has never been confirmed.

**Truth:** Whether Algared was human, half-elf, or something
else is disputed even within Alderland scholarship. His
influence over the Algarian dynasty through the scepter is
documented in Raven Church records. The scepter's loss during
the Demon Flood is historically accepted.

_Full provenance and powers contested. See `references/campaigns/ravens_purge.md`._

**Mechanical hint:** A shepherd's crook of dark alderwood, the
wood still supple despite centuries. The crown is carved in
the shape of a temple roof — the curve carved rather than bent,
with elder pendants hanging from carved cross-pegs, and the
ruby set as the roof's eye at the apex. An iron spiral at the
base allows the scepter to stand upright without support. In
Algarian tradition, the shape expresses 'the king's strength
and vigilance in his role as the people's protector.' Accounts
note it hums faintly in the hands of those with a blood claim
to Alderland's old line.

**Location:** Unknown. Lost in the Ravenlands during the Demon
Flood of 874 AS.

## Blood Star (Hemella) — the Cloak Clasp

**Myth:** The elven princess **Iridne**, forbidden by her father
to be with the orc chieftain **Horena**, chose death over exile
from her love. Her companions had her ruby heart set into a round
copper clasp bearing a golden tree motif — the Blood Star —
and delivered it secretly to Horena. Her father discovered this
and sent ships to reclaim the ruby; the clasp was lost at sea in
the battle that followed. Those who have carried it since say the
elf inside appears in their dreams, counselling patience and
understanding.

**Truth:** Iridne's ruby is real and confirmed by multiple
traditions. Whether the legend of the orc lover is accurate
history or a later romanticization is debated. The clasp itself
is described identically in documents from human, orc, and
elven sources.

_Full provenance and powers contested. See `references/campaigns/ravens_purge.md`._

**Mechanical hint:** A round copper-base clasp with a golden
tree motif and an elven ruby at center. The elf within
sometimes speaks — or is said to — in the dreams of those who
carry it.

**Location:** Among the eastern orc clans. **Empress Soria** of
the Urhur has been seen wearing it, a gift from Emperor Hroka.

## Scarnesclaw

**Myth:** A claw taken from the dragon **Scarne** while she lay
in chains. The thief was found the next morning with no wounds
and no face. The claw was filed into a blade at some point by
someone who did not survive the process. The blade cuts
anything — no armor holds it. It has been in druidic hands for
as long as any living person can remember.

**Truth:** Whether Scarne was alive or dead when the claw was
taken is unknown. The knife is real and is now confirmed in
the possession of **Kritre the Caller**, high druid of the
**Pelagia** sanctuary. Kritre will not discuss it.

**Mechanical hint:** A blade of curved dark horn material,
darkened nearly to black. Treats all armor as if it were not
there.

**Location:** Carried by **Kritre the Caller** at **Pelagia**.
