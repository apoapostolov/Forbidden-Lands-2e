# Bitter Reach — Gods and Religion

**Region:** The Bitter Reach
**Source:** Magnus Seter, Free League Publishing
**Campaign:** Wake of the Winter King

---

## Overview

The Bitter Reach has no unified religion. It has a
dominant philosophy: survive or die. In such conditions
faith takes practical forms.

The gods of Ravenland travel north with settlers.
Ravenland immigrants whisper to the Nightwalker at
sea — "May the Nightwalker avert his gaze from you!"
is a common farewell. Once on land in the Bitter
Reach, most give up expecting any god to listen.

## The Winter Elves and Cosmic Forces

Ferenblaud's elves had something closer to a religion
than any other kin in the region. They studied the
moon, sun, and stars and believed that cosmic energies
from heavenly bodies flowed into the world and could
be mastered. Their highest goal was to establish contact
with beings from other dimensions — entities beyond
the gods of mortal kin.

Some of these experiments succeeded. What broke through
was not divine. The ruins beneath the ice still carry
traces of those entities. Scholars who dig too deep
will find evidence that something vast answered
Ferenblaud's summons and that it is possibly still
present in some form beneath the glacier.

## Ice Affinity and Orc Spirituality

Orc druids — especially the Frostwind Clan — hold the
deepest surviving spiritual tradition in the Bitter
Reach. Their connection to snow, ice, and cold is
both magical and philosophical. Orc elders say: *the
ice is alive, and it is the most dangerous beast in
the Bitter Reach*.

Ice Affinity is not prayer. It is attunement. The
druid does not call upon a higher power — they listen
to what the ice says and apply it.

## Champion Oaths

The Champion profession swears an oath to a higher
power. That power can be a god, a demon, a person,
or a place. The Bitter Reach does not specify which
gods are worshipped — champions sworn to a location
(a particular ruin, a mountain pass, a settlement)
are as common as champions sworn to any deity.

This practical framing — "what is the oath sworn to,
not what does theology say" — reflects the Bitter
Reach's approach to all transcendent questions.

## Temples

Winter elf ruins include what appear to be temples,
though they were not dedicated to any god of Ravenland.
The Stone Ravens were created specifically to guard
these sites. What was worshipped in these temples
is unclear from any surviving text. The imagery
involves the night sky, monstrous entities, and
geometric star patterns.
