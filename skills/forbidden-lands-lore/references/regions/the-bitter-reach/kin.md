# Bitter Reach — Kin

**Region:** The Bitter Reach
**Source:** Magnus Seter, Free League Publishing
**Campaign:** Wake of the Winter King

---

## Humans

### Ailanders

The majority of humans in the Bitter Reach. Fled from
oppression or poverty in Alderland or Ravenland. Those
who have lived here for generations think of themselves
as natives and are met with some respect by orcs and
wolfkin. Newcomers are treated as tenderfoots.

Most live in Northfall or in squalid fishing villages.
They fish for cod and krill, hunt seals and Pikebeasts,
or go inland for reindeer and Nanuik. Most newcomers
attempt treasure hunting. Many die within months.

Ailanders speak only when there is need. Brief sentences.
No pleasantries.

### Alderlanders

At campaign start, a handful of Alderlanders operate
covertly in the Bitter Reach as vanguard agents of the
king of Alderland. They pose as peddlers and prospectors
while gathering intelligence and preparing Northfall for
the arrival of the Alderlander army.

They live apart in a small enclave within Northfall,
grudgingly mixing with Ailanders. More talkative than
Ailanders — they want information and will engage
travelers to get it. Easily identified by their garish
southern clothes, completely unsuited to the climate.

The running joke among Ailander natives: an Alderlander
never dresses for the weather, only for the king. They
arrive in fabrics from Alderlander merchants — bright,
thin, and visibly unsuited to cold. The joke is made at
their expense until a severe winter kills them.

### The South-farers

People who generations ago traveled south from the
Bitter Reach to Ravenland. The first were orcs from
Fallowmoor who grew tired of the harsh climate. Later,
some humans followed. Small enclaves survive on the
northern coast of Far Vivend and Margelda, speaking
the Bitter Reach dialect. They are likely the source
of the first treasure rumors that reached Ravenland.

## Elves

### Summer Elves (Redrunners)

A small group of Redrunners dispatched south-to-north
by Blaudewedd through the Stillmist. Led by the druid
Marmena. Their mission is to locate and protect the
remaining four Seals. They don't share this mission
with outsiders.

Marmena is a tall elf with flame-colored hair. She wears blue clothes
embroidered with silver stars and carries a staff and an elven scimitar.
She has made camp near the base of the Morma Glacier, close to the
river Ised, and watches treasure hunters departing Northfall to judge
who poses a risk to the Seals.

She is contemptuous of most other kin. Earning her trust takes time
and demonstrated restraint. She is openly hostile to anyone who has
already broken a Seal.

The Redrunners hide in ruins outside the settlements,
watching treasure hunters who may be heading for the
Seals. They will first try to lure adventurers away
from dangerous locations. If that fails, they attempt
persuasion. Violence is a last resort.

A few other elves — some Unruly (see Gamemaster's Guide
p. 54) and scattered Redrunners — also live among
humans in the Bitter Reach or wander the wastes alone.
They are hostile to orc clans in the Barren Mountains.

### Winter Elves

Ferenblaud's followers, frozen for three thousand years.
Related to the elves of Ravenland but rejected the
teachings of the Wanderer and Clay in favor of studying
the weave of creation itself.

Their society was highly evolved: the elves lived in
luxury while slaves — primarily orcs — led miserable
lives. The winter elves served as generals, sorcerers,
and special forces. Orcs provided the shock troops.

As the Seals are broken, the curse loosens and winter
elves begin to awaken. Their mental state is close to
the undead at first — disoriented, filled with rage,
driven by a lust for revenge. They seek to reclaim their
former greatness and enslave all other kin in Ferenblaud's
name. Most who survive Ferenblaud's fall are his most
powerful followers: Fighters and Sorcerers.

Physically: tall and proud, but harrowed. Skin pale and
withered from three millennia frozen. Wear ancient armor
and weapons from before the curse, relying on magic to
protect against the cold.

### Half-elves

The few half-elves in the Bitter Reach are typically
Frailers from Alderland, skilled navigators who steer
ships through the northern storms. Long lives and
experience at sea make them invaluable. Most live
aboard ships anchored along the coast, ready to depart
at need.

## Dwarves

Dwarves know the history of the winter elves better
than most. Legends of Rodenvale and its cruel ruler
are known to most clans, and they have taken it upon
themselves to protect Ravenland from whatever stirs
in the cold north.

### The Gatekeepers (Crombe Clan)

Live in the Southern Range near the entrance to the
Winding Ways. No one — including the Gatekeepers
themselves — remembers when they arrived. Few remember
the path through the Winding Ways. They guard the gates
and will be first to face Alabastor's Misgrown army
when it forces through. When that happens they retreat
to their mountain stronghold and send warning to the
Pathkeepers.

### The Pathkeepers (Canide Clan)

Patrol forgotten paths in the Southern Heights. Their
mission is to stop all who try to cross the mountains
between Ravenland and the Bitter Reach. When the
Misgrown build their camp by the gate to the Winding
Ways, the Pathkeepers attempt to contain them and
discover their intentions.

### The Forgethralls

The most hidden of all kin in the Bitter Reach. They
are descended from dwarves enslaved by Ferenblaud as
weaponsmiths, forced to forge weapons for the winter
elven armies. When Ferenblaud's kingdom fell, most
Forgethralls died in the chaos. Some survived and built
their lives in the ancient forges beneath the ice where
they once toiled.

The Forgethralls know the passages beneath the ice
better than anyone. They use those tunnels to avoid
all contact with other kin, including other dwarves in
the Southern Heights. They have not yet been discovered
by treasure hunters or adventurers.

## Orcs

Three clans live in the Bitter Reach. The orcs are
believed to have been created as a slave race by
Ferenblaud's sorcery and were the primary labor force
and troops of Rodenvale. After the curse fell, orcs
survived the calamity in the Barren Hills and swore
never again to serve another kin.

Today they live as hunters. Some trade meat and furs
with other kin in Fallowmoor. A few work as scouts and
pathfinders for treasure hunters. All orc elders know
what would happen if Ferenblaud reawakens. This makes
them hostile to treasure hunters who risk breaking
the curse.

The three clans maintain a fragile peace based on
shared distrust of the human settlers in Fallowmoor.

### Bearskull Clan

Mainly in the northern Bitter Reach, where the Nanuik
roam. The only orcs who venture onto the ocean.
Scouts and pathfinders for adventurers exploring
elven ruins often come from this clan.

### Wolfhowl Clan

Southern Barren Hills. Hunt reindeer. Sell meat and
hides to settlements on the coast.

### Frostwind Clan

Most mysterious of the three. Their druids are said
to have a magical connection to Ferenblaud's winter
elves. Some may know Elemental Magic — a secret kept
even from other orcs. Live high in the Barren Hills
and across the Morma Glacier. Hostile to settlers.
Led by Hoyra, the most powerful shaman of any clan.

Hoyra wears hooded skin clothes and a nanuik fur cloak. She carries
the black staff Frenne — a magical artifact from before the curse —
and wears bone amulets and protective cords. Her gaze is direct and
steady. She radiates authority without effort.

She has done what no orc leader managed before her: united all three
clans to guard against treasure hunters who risk breaking the Seals.
She mistrusts all outsiders and regards newcomers as ignorant children
handling objects they cannot understand.

**Cultural note:** Orc druids, especially those of
the Frostwind Clan, hold the greatest knowledge of
old legends in the Bitter Reach. They will share
this knowledge only with those who have earned
genuine trust — not with treasure hunters who came
to plunder elven ruins.

## Wolfkin

One tribe lives in the Bitter Reach. Their ancestors
crossed the Southern Range from Ravenland centuries
ago on paths now long forgotten. Today led by Akelea,
descendant of Haunani who first led the crossing.
About a hundred individuals, based in Wolvenhome,
hunting the Barren Hills.

Wolfkin live in harmony with cold and ice and embrace
the harsh climate. They associate freely with wolves
and Dire Wolves as extended family. They mostly keep
to themselves. Perpetual feud with the orcs. Humans
regard them as bandits or beasts, so wolfkin rarely
enter Northfall — and never alone.

Their trade contacts are limited to a handful of
peddlers who make the effort to seek out Wolvenhome.
Wolfkin who do enter human settlements tend to move
quickly, say little, and leave before trouble finds
them — because it usually does.

## Halflings

Almost none in the Bitter Reach. Most halflings don't
tolerate the cold. The odd adventurous one arrives
with southern ships and usually perishes or leaves
quickly. Those born here live as lesser members of
goblin communities. Due to the cold, halfling births
are rare.

## Goblins

A small goblin clan lives in Fallowmoor near the Morma
Glacier. Feud with the wolfkin over hunting grounds.
Enemies of the Canide dwarves. Occasionally raid traders.
Despite this, some goblins hire out as pathfinders to
treasure hunters. Their fate is closely tied to the
halflings here, as in Ravenland.

## Other Kin

Whiners, saurians, and ogres — known in Ravenland —
have not been seen in the Bitter Reach settlements.
Whether they are absent from this far north or have
simply avoided detection is not known.

## Key Players

The following individuals shape events across the Bitter
Reach. Most are known by reputation throughout the region.

### Ingmarg

The dominant merchant of the Silver Coast. Came to the
Bitter Reach as a peddler and built a small empire
through decades of shrewd dealing. His stronghold in
Northfall is the most prosperous building in the
settlement.

Large and heavy-set, always in expensive furs, rings of
amber, whale bone, silver, and gold on every finger.
Jovial in manner, ruthless in dealings. He is the only
buyer in the Bitter Reach who pays real coin for what
comes out of the elven ruins — never at a fair price.

He tolerates the Alderlander army's arrival with visible
reluctance. He is privately looking for a way to reassert
control over Bitter Reach trade. See `places.md` for his
stronghold in Northfall.

### Baraggor

Commander of the Alderlander expeditionary force. Arrived
with four warships — Wyrm's Fist, Wyrm's Blade, Wyrm's
Shield, and Wyrm's Hope — carrying over a thousand
soldiers. Her standing orders are to claim the Bitter
Reach for the king of Alderland.

A grim soldier with a deep scar from forehead to jaw and
a slight limp from an old wound. Always armored, always
ready. Wears a purple mantle with a blood-red plume when
she disembarks. Spends nights aboard Wyrm's Fist; visits
Northfall to negotiate with local power brokers.

Her soldiers operate aggressively. She expects results and
does not wait. See `places.md` for Wyrm's Fist.

### Alabastor

General of the Misgrown army that broke through the
Winding Ways from the south. Commands from a stone tower
in the war camp in the foothills of the Southern Range.

Thin and ghoul-shaped, with deep red glowing eyes,
festering wounds, and bloody footprints wherever he walks.
Three artifacts are fused permanently to his body: an
executioner's hood sewn from an assassin's skin, armor of
bones that deflects arrows, and a banner that speaks with
the voices of the dead.

He does not parley. His scouts range widely into the Bitter
Reach. His objective is to conquer and enslave all kin he
encounters.

### Bele

King of the Ice Giants. First created by Blaudewedd. More
than five meters tall, shaped from ice and stone, arms
like logs, hands like boulders. Eyes shine white. Voice a
deep rumble. Speaks slowly — behind the slowness sits a
sharp mind and three thousand years of knowledge of the
Bitter Reach.

Lives at the Palace of the Ice Giants, sitting on a throne
of crystal. He has not left in years. His advisors Bor and
Rym counsel him in opposite directions about the Ice
Giants' continued purpose. The demon Wurda works to
destabilize his court.

Those who seek audience with the Ice Giants eventually deal
with Bele. See `places.md` for the Palace of the Ice Giants.

### Wurda

A demon who operates under three guises: an old human
woman, a black warg with enormous jaws, and the shape
of the last creature it killed. Few in the Bitter Reach know that
Wurda is a demon. Most encounter her as the old woman —
an itinerant figure carrying scrolls of ancient text,
offering legends, rumors, and directions to elven ruins.

She frequents Northfall, the Field of Swords, and
occasionally the Palace of the Ice Giants. The information
she provides is always accurate. Treasure hunters trust
her precisely because she has never given bad directions.

What she never mentions: every ruin she points toward
contains a Seal.
