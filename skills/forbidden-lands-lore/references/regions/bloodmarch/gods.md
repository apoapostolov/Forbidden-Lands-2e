# Bloodmarch — Gods and Religion

**Region:** Aslene (the Bloodmarch)
**Source:** Erik Granström, Free League Publishing
**Campaign:** Legacy of Horn

---

## Horn

Volcano god and father of the five horse clans. A god of
construction, creation, and power. Horn is an active volcano
in the center of the Bloodmarch — the god and the mountain
share a name.

All five clans revere Horn as the origin point of humanity
(or at least of their humanity). His priests are called
Horn priests. The central temple, Ashenstead, stands on the
slopes of the volcano where the Firelands begin. He is depicted
as a massive man with a head of ash and a spine of burning
stone.

**Holy fire:** No separate holy fire tradition. Horn's priests
use the Firelands themselves as sacred ground. Ceremonies
typically involve exposure to volcanic heat.

**Caprid connection:** Caprid dwarves claim Have created the
world but Horn shaped it — they treat Horn as a junior partner
to Have in the creation myth. Ordinary horse clan worshippers
reverse this hierarchy.

---

## Have

Goddess of the horse clan women. Also called "the Blue Goddess"
by Ravenlanders and Alderlanders who have heard of her. She is
the goddess of war, love, fertility, learning, and the holy
blue fire. Her worshippers include the Pyrolytes.

Have's holy fire burns blue in the temple of Ashenstead. The
fire is genuinely sacred — demons are harmed by it and Crimson
Sickness can be cured with Blue Tar heated on it. Her high
priestess has the title Warmistress. Priests are called
Warmothers and Warfathers regardless of gender.

Ashenstead serves as a shared temple for both Horn and Have.
The two cults are not in conflict — their congregations are
largely the same people. Men lead Horn's rituals; women lead
Have's rituals.

**Sacred substance:** Blue Tar. Produced by holy blue pines
and associated with Have through origin stories about her tears.

---

## The Pyrolytes

Demon-hunting warrior cult of Have. Founded after the Demon
Flood; their explicit purpose is to fight demon corruption.

Pyrolytes carry Blue Tar at all times and are trained to
identify Crimson Sickness at early stages. They are regarded
as heroes across all five clans — even the Galdanes who
distrust outsiders respect Pyrolytes.

They do not answer to the Warmistress. They have their own
command structure and their own holy sites. They sometimes
conflict with secular horse clan authority when their
anti-demon obligations cross into political territory.

## Volitia

Aslene orc religion. A pantheistic system that treats the
entire world as alive and interconnected. No temples; no
formal clergy. Practice is woven through daily life.

**Klei:** The sky entity that created the physical world.
Not worshipped directly — acknowledged as the source.

**Étosh:** The state of balance the orcs seek to maintain
between self and world. Étosh is simultaneously a spiritual
condition, a social obligation, and a warrior ideal. Killing
has no moral weight in itself — it becomes significant
only if it disrupts Étosh in the orc community. The decision
to kill always belongs to the individual; collective guilt is
not a Volitia concept.

Orc females in particular carry the social weight of Étosh
monitoring. The Sisterhood of Viraga is the formal institution
for this.

---

## The Sisterhood of Viraga

Female orc order that adjudicates matters of Étosh. The
Sisterhood is not a government but its judgments are binding.
It organizes the rite of passage by which young female orcs
become full adults — the first solo hunt and its presentation
to the Sisterhood.

Aslene orcs who violate Étosh judgments are outcast. This is
more severe than it sounds — outcast status means not just
social exclusion but formal removal from all Volitia
protections.

## Nightwalker (Ranghöge)

Wolfkin god. Patron of transitions: dawn and dusk, birth and
death, arrival and departure. Depicted as a wolf standing on
two legs with one eye and carrying a spear. The Umbras —
wolfkin holy warriors — are active specifically at dawn and
dusk, the liminal times.

Ranghöge does not demand conquest or expansion. He demands
presence and attention at transitions. A wolfkin who fails
to observe the dawn after a night of darkness is not cursed
— they simply become slightly less themselves over time.

---

## Order of Egression

Ranghöge warrior cult. The Egression use Blood Magic —
a school of magic derived from the rituals of transition
that mark Ranghöge worship. Blood is spilled at crossings:
rivers, borders, doorways. The blood is the fee.

Blood Magic is not taught outside the Order. Outsiders
who ask too many questions about the mechanics are typically
directed to a wolf.

---

## Eor

Moon goddess of the halflings and goblins — specifically
the Penite, the pilgrims who came to the Bloodmarch seeking
a holy land.

Salt is sacred to Eor. Penite gatherings always include a
salt ceremony. A hospitality tradition has grown up around
this: any stranger who is offered salt at a Penite table
is under guest-right for the duration of the meal. Refusing
the salt is a deliberate insult.

Eor does not appear in horse clan religion, Volitia, or
Caprid theology. The Penite do not try to convert — they
are pilgrims, not missionaries.

## Rubor and Kolor

A manufactured religion originating within the Galdane
territories. Red (Rubor) versus all other colors (Kolor).
Red is the color of horn, blood, the Firelands, and the
volcano god. All other colors are impure.

In practice Rubor-Kolor functions as a political cult that
provides justification for Galdane aggression against other
clans and against outsiders. Non-red colors in clothing
or ornamentation are marked as heresy. Several communities
in the northern Bloodmarch have been converted by force.

The full institutional origin of this religion — specifically
who founded it and for what purpose — is a campaign spoiler.
See `references/campaigns/bloodmarch.md`.

## Rust Church

Not native to the Bloodmarch but present in significant
numbers. The Rust Church and its warrior brotherhood the
Ironbrows arrived through Shadowgate in the early period
before the Demon Flood — or via Gander's Pass from Alderland.
Their history in the Bloodmarch is patchy.

The Ironbrows practice Magnetism, an iron-based magic
tradition tied to their faith. They are combat-trained and
disciplined and function as a mercenary force when needed.

They are not connected to the Pyrolytes and regard them
as rivals.

---

## Wail

Goddess of the Drawn Lands, worshipped by the Sabirian
horse clan. The Wailing plains take their name from her.

The Sabirians believe wingsteeds are gifts from Wail, sent
as atonement for her abandonment of the horse clans when
they most needed her support during Sella's campaign. This
tradition shapes Sabirian treatment of wingsteeds as holy
animals.

Wail is paired with Flow in the myths of the Drawn Lands.
Both are gods of passage; both are associated with the
west road that is now blocked by the Kreysel hurricane.
Details involving Wail's role in the true history of the
Bloodmarch are a campaign spoiler. See `references/campaigns/bloodmarch.md`.

## Congregation of the Serpent

Alderlander religious organization that worships Wyrm.
Politically rival to the Rust Church in Alderland and
now competing in the Bloodmarch. They have 60 agents
and operatives in the Bloodmarch under the command of
**Poansa Moulter**.

Headquartered in Brodelmarg, the Congregation uses the
Alderlander prophet **Fangler** as their Bloodmarch mouthpiece.
Fangler preaches that Wyrm will be reborn in the Bloodmarch.

**Ad Vipera:** The Congregation's secret intelligence unit.
Motto: *Ad vipera, ad versa.* They believe the end of the
world is close. Priority target: the Rust Church. Their
sixty agents in the Bloodmarch include ten elites of
the Sisterhood of the Black Mamba.

**Sisterhood of the Black Mamba:** Elite sub-unit within
Ad Vipera. All members are immune to all poisons and can
shapeshift into a black mamba. Poansa Moulter leads this
grouping personally.

## Meat

The god of the Seventh (worm people). Called "The Crawling
Land." The Seventh believe their people are engaged in
a multigenerational transformation: at the twelfth
generation, all that exists will be Meat — the god's
living body. "Beyond the Becoming there will be only Meat,
and all living things will be part of her."

The worm people do not speak directly of their religion;
this information is pieced together from their behavior,
from what they have written in the flesh of their hosts,
and from what those hosts remember.

Vaerefor van Reiben has been known to argue to the Seventh
that the Crimson Forest is a precursor to Meat, recruiting
them as unwilling allies.

---

## Tincturus

The god of the drakewyrms. Described as a divine giant
rainbow. Drakewyrms are not sapient in the human sense but
they will fight to the death to defend any rainbow that
appears near them, which they experience as a manifestation
of their god.

---

## Flow

Sister of Wail. Goddess of flowing freshwater. She is paired
with Wail in all Drawn Lands tradition — the two sisters
together represent the full journey of water from rain to
river to ocean.

The Watch of the Sisters was dedicated jointly to Wail and
Flow when it was built as a friendship outpost between the
Drawn Lands and Aslene. After the curse, Flow's role became
secondary to Wail's in local tradition, though the priestesses
maintained both faiths.

The Cauldron of Flow is a sacred spring in the Watch complex
that runs when the Metrochrone is running. It is currently dry.

---

## Oneira

The sleeping goddess of existence, worshipped through the
Dreamstress at Oxengelder.

The Dreamstress teaches that Oneira is dreaming the entire
world into existence — all life, all history, all time is
the content of her dream. The Dreamstress claims to be
Oneira's prophet, the one being whose dreaming is perfectly
attuned to the goddess's visions.

Oneiromancy — the practice taught at Oxengelder — is the
act of participating in Oneira's dream rather than merely
existing inside it. Advanced oneiromancers can reshape
small parts of the dream (and therefore reality) through
practice.

Who or what Oneira actually is beyond the Dreamstress's
doctrine is not established in public tradition. The
theological claims remain unverified. Oneiromancy as a
functional magic school is real regardless of the doctrine
behind it.
