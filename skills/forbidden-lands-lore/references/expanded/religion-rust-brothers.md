# Religion of the Rust Brothers

**Category:** Ravenlands-specific doctrine, worship, rite, and anathema under
the Rust and Heme church.

This reference holds the church's sacramental iron, corrupted theology, daily
worship, ceremonies, sin, penance, and protected religious knowledge. Ranks,
recruitment, household occupation, inquisition, selective terror, and the
trained character of the order are in [Culture of the Rust
Brothers](culture-rust-brothers.md).

**Content note:** This document discusses human sacrifice, bodily alteration,
religious persecution, and coercive correction. Render these subjects through
consequence and witness. Graphic detail is seldom needed.

## Authority and Disclosure

### Four Levels of Knowledge

Keep the following levels separate in description and play.

- **Observable fact:** Rust Brothers wear iron and cultivate rust on its visible
  surfaces. They carry coal-filled chain weapons, collect tribute, and punish
  heresy. Demon-tainted objects or remains pass into their custody.
- **Settlement belief:** Brothers are born under demon signs and receive spouses
  in the Mist. They cannot be surprised. Iron lets them smell a lie.
- **Church claim:** Rust chooses bodies already touched by the enemy because
  only marked flesh can approach corruption without being deceived by it.
- **GM information:** The order searches battle sites and demon lairs for the
  matter left by demonic manifestation, injury, or death. It carries
  this matter to secure houses and then to its central authorities. The
  authorities examine and store it. They use what they learn while denying that
  knowledge to everyone else.

The last point should appear to players as a pattern before it appears as an
answer. A Brother arrives after a demon is killed. The corpse is stripped of
one substance but left with its teeth and hide. A sealed ceramic flask is
wrapped in wet wool and entered in a small wax tablet. A witness who asks what
was taken is questioned about where the witness learned to ask.

### Canon, Inference, and Local Variation

The Rust Brothers' tribute, forced conversion, and persecution are manuscript
facts. The Misgrown priesthood and Iron Guard belong to the same layer, as do
coal-filled chain weapons and the use of rusty iron against demons. Their
detailed routines of investigation and settlement rule are setting inferences.
They follow from those facts and from credible methods of premodern coercive
government. Treat a named office, rite, or punishment as local unless the
manuscript states otherwise.

Do not make every priory identical. One prior rules through hostages, another
through debt records, a third through the only armed riders within two days'
travel. The institution remains recognizable because its doctrine and incentives
are shared.

## Iron Worn as Doctrine

### Sacramental Rust Is Maintained Rust

Iron corroded through becomes unreliable. Mail rings thin, rivets loosen,
and plate pits at the places that must turn a blow. An experienced Rust armorer
does not confuse decay with devotion. Bearing surfaces, edges, buckles, and the
inside of mail are cleaned and greased. Outer plates, pendants, masks, and the
heads of ritual weapons are allowed to redden or are deliberately browned.

A poor village imitator may wear a genuinely ruined mail shirt. An Iron Guard
veteran wears armor that looks diseased and still closes properly at every
strap. The order has made corrosion into a controlled finish. Even rot must
obey.

Visible rust leaves marks. Red dust gathers in beard stubble and collar folds.
Rain cuts orange lines down yellow cloth. The smell is wet iron, old grease,
and coal smoke. A Brother who sleeps in a household leaves grit on the bedding
and a stain where the breastplate rested against the wall.

### Weight Is Part of the Performance

Rust clergy favor more iron than ordinary travel requires. Chains, plates,
idols, and weighted vestments bruise the shoulders and shorten the day's march.
The burden displays endurance and access to metal. In a poor settlement, the
iron worn by six Brothers may exceed the village's stock of plowshares and axes.

The burden also separates the wearer from common work. A Brother cannot easily
stoop under a low byre roof or join a harvest line in ceremonial iron. The body
becomes slow, loud, and difficult to approach. Dependence on servants then
appears as rank.

Tall or gaunt members intensify the effect. The order gives them high crests,
long masks, narrow shoulder plates, and chains that exaggerate each step. Their
bodies become church property arranged to be remembered.

### The Great Censer

The characteristic weapon is a perforated iron ball or cage on a long chain,
large enough to require the shoulders and hips rather than the wrist. Coals burn
inside it. Resin, bitter herbs, hair, or damp offerings may be cast through the
openings according to the rite. When swung, the weapon trails smoke and sheds
sparks.

It is part flail, part meteor hammer, and part religious implement. In close
quarters it is dangerous to everyone nearby. The bearer needs space, practiced
footwork, and companions who know when to step back. A miss can break a doorpost,
wrap the chain around a beam, or scatter coals across a thatched floor.

That risk is useful in public punishment. The crowd must move when the bearer
moves. No one can pretend the rite is symbolic once a coal has burned through
their sleeve. The censer makes doctrine occupy a circle of ground.

Some censers are made for ritual and intimidation rather than battle. Others
have a thick cage, a short gripping chain near the handle, and a head heavy
enough to break bone through a padded coat. The GM should decide which kind is
present before combat rather than granting every censer every advantage.

> **The smith's test:** A captured Iron Guard censer looked ruined under its
> red scale. The village smith cleaned one link with a file. The metal beneath
> was sound and the rivet newly closed. She stopped filing. Neglect would have
> been cheaper. Someone maintained the weapon carefully enough to make it look
> abandoned.

## The Corrupted Theology of Rust and Heme

### The Nine Hammer Articles

The church teaches nine propositions as the **Hammer Articles**. Villagers may
know them as short responses. Clergy learn the authorized interpretations and
the cases in which a superior can suspend an article.

1. **The gifts were iron and wood.** The raven and serpent were bearers, not
   gods. To worship the animal rather than the substance is to mistake a cup
   for what it carries.
2. **Rust protects by rule.** Iron is tool, weapon, boundary, and command.
   Protection without dominion is weakness wearing a good name.
3. **Heme gives growth and rot together.** Every birth carries disease, every
   harvest produces waste, and every household requires culling.
4. **Humanity is ore.** No person is born finished. Submission to right
   authority is the furnace through which a usable human being is made.
5. **The prophet bears what others cannot.** Zytera is taught as the living
   prophet who accepts demonic danger so that lesser humans may remain beneath
   it.
6. **Tribute closes the bargain.** Food, silver, labor, and sacrifice purchase
   divine and worldly protection. Misfortune after refusal proves the bargain
   was left open.
7. **Knowledge descends by fitness.** A lower rank is protected by ignorance.
   Taking knowledge above one's station is theft from the whole people.
8. **Corruption can be bridled by the chosen.** Contact that condemns a villager
   may consecrate a superior who received permission and survived the rite.
9. **Heresy is slag.** Raven worship, refusal of correction, and rival claims
   over demonic truth must be removed before they weaken sound material.

The articles form a closed system. A good harvest proves tribute was accepted.
A failed harvest proves more tribute was required. A high priest's alteration
proves selection. The same alteration in a farmer proves contamination.

### The Short Catechism

Public instruction uses question and response because books are scarce and the
church distrusts uncontrolled copies. The exact words vary, but the sequence is
stable enough for a patrol to test a village.

> **What was given?** Iron to command. Wood to increase.
>
> **What enters iron?** Rust, and Rust makes it known.
>
> **What enters wood?** Heme, in fruit and canker.
>
> **What are we?** Ore under the hammer.
>
> **Who bears the hidden weight?** The chosen, above us.
>
> **What keeps the gate?** Tribute given before refusal.

An incorrect word matters less than hesitation. Priests listen for households
that answer too softly, children who learned an older version, and people who
look toward one another before naming Zytera.

### The Gods Are Deliberately Crude

Rust idols are made from iron allowed to redden. Heme figurines are cut from
wood with few strokes. Fine detail insults the gods because perfection is said
to exist in substance, not in a human carver's image.

Clergy also gain control over acceptable craft. A beautiful household
figure can be condemned as pride or concealed Raven worship. A crude official
idol cannot be criticized for poor workmanship because roughness proves
orthodoxy.

Sacred damage differs from neglect. Rust may thicken on the visible face of an
idol, but a cracked support is replaced. A wooden Heme figure may split with the
grain, but rot that threatens the shrine is cut away in ceremony. The church
decides which decay is revelation and which is a servant's failure.

### Rust and Heme Are a Marriage of Rule

Rust is named husband and protector. Heme is wife, growth, disease, hunt, and
sacrifice. Their marriage makes force and reproduction one order. Rust claims
the right to direct what Heme produces. Heme supplies the living bodies, crops,
and herds on which Rust's protection depends.

The doctrine supports current patriarchy, but the cult's history resists a
simple division. Ferrale Teramalda led armies. Sisters preserve household
knowledge without which priests cannot identify disease or suspicious births.
Local teaching bends around these facts. A male prior says Heme acts through
Rust. A senior Sister says iron without wood has nothing to protect and no fuel
for its forge.

The disagreement is permitted while both speakers accept hierarchy, tribute,
and the church's right to decide. It becomes heresy only when used to deny an
order.

### Humanity as Material

Rust doctrine compares the uninitiated to raw ore. Discipline is the furnace,
pain the hammer, and confession the removal of scale. Heretics are slag. The
comparison removes moral standing from the person being worked.

Ore does not consent to the forge. Slag has no claim on the smith. Once the
comparison is accepted, coercion becomes proof of the church's office. A person
who breaks under torture was impure. A person who endures required a stronger
hammer.

The doctrine cannot be disproved by its victims because every response confirms
it. Submission proves that correction worked. Resistance proves that more work
is needed. Death proves that little usable iron was present.

### Heme Makes Life Suspect

Heme joins wood, fertility, blood, growth, disease, and sacrifice. Under this
teaching, life is productive and treacherous. Crops feed people and harbor rot.
Bodies bear children and breed corruption. Forests shelter prey and conceal
things that should be hunted.

Rust gives the church its language of force. Heme gives it permission to enter
the household. Birth, illness, marriage, menstruation, animal breeding, and
burial can all become matters for inspection. A church that governs both iron
and flesh can call every private condition a public danger.

The Sisters of Heme therefore need not be gentler counterparts to the Brothers.
Their authority may be quieter and more intimate. A Sister who knows which
woman miscarried, which child has a strange tooth, and which cow calved wrong
possesses an inquisition's raw material.

### Ignorance as Protection

The church teaches that demons punish populations that learn how to oppose them.
A known weakness invites retaliation. A copied instruction may condemn every
settlement through which it passes.
Humanity survives, in this account, by remaining beneath demonic notice and
placing dangerous knowledge in the hands of people already marked to bear it.

This belief gives information control the name of mercy. The Brothers confiscate
a demonologist's notes because no farmer should know what makes a demon bleed.
They burn a witness's account because a useful truth might travel farther than
the protection needed to survive it. They demand obedience because ignorant
servants are less threatening to demons than informed enemies.

The reasoning is corrupted even when some danger behind it is real. It assumes
ordinary people are expendable dependents, grants the church permanent secrecy,
and makes every failure an argument for greater control. The Brothers continue
to seek the very knowledge whose possession they punish.

### Their Contradiction Is an Institution

Rust Brothers claim that knowledge of demons endangers humanity. They travel
widely to gather that knowledge. They claim demonic contact corrupts. Their
leaders display alteration as authority. They claim sacrifice preserves the
people. The sacrificed are always drawn from those denied a voice in the choice.

The contradictions define rank instead of weakening the institution. What is
sin in a villager becomes burden in a priest and necessity in a superior. The
right to survive hypocrisy is one of the church's privileges.

An individual Brother may believe every word. Sincerity does not redeem the
practice. A sincere torturer still tightens the cord, and the doctrine has been
constructed so that the victim cannot appeal to the stated purpose of the act.

## Faith in Daily Practice

### The Faithful Are Kept, Not Ordained

Most people under Rust rule are neither Brothers nor Sisters. Church records
call compliant households **kept households**: counted, tithed, available for
inspection, and eligible for protection. Villagers may use the phrase sincerely
or with the bitterness of livestock entered on a tally.

A kept household owes tribute, attendance at major rites, notice of demon signs,
and obedience to a lawful church demand. In return it can request judgment,
armed help, removal of dangerous dead, recognition of marriage, or relief from
some local obligation. Delivery is never guaranteed. The request itself confirms
the church's jurisdiction.

Faithful status belongs to the household more than the solitary person. One
member's heresy can stain the rest. One member's service can purchase lenience.
Kin pressure then performs the work of enforcement.

### The Household Shrine

A compliant home keeps a thumb-sized Heme figure near stored food and a piece
of rusted iron above the principal door. The objects are cheap enough that
poverty does not excuse their absence. Their placement tells an inspector who
controls the stores and which entrance the household acknowledges as public.

The iron declares that the household stands within Rust's claim. No one expects
the object to protect by itself. The wooden figure is checked for deliberate
detail, hidden hollows, and foreign marks. A carved face is dangerous. A hollow
back is worse because paper or herbs can be concealed inside it.

Many households keep older practices beneath these objects. Water is still
poured for Flow. A Raven feather lies under the roof ridge. The Heme figure may
stand over a stone that belonged to Clay. Public orthodoxy covers a household
religion accumulated over generations.

### Waking the Iron

At first work, a devout household touches the door iron before opening the
house. The person responsible for tools checks one edge, hinge, or buckle and
sets right any neglect. The prayer is practical: "Rust hold what the hand has
made."

Priests teach that sound maintenance honors Rust because power requires usable
iron. Sacramental rust belongs on the sign, outer face, or ritual object. Rust
on a plow edge is waste. This distinction lets clergy condemn a farmer for a
bad tool while wearing red scale across ceremonial plate.

At a priory, Waking the Iron becomes inspection. Missing keys, weak links, damp
mail, and low oil are discovered before prayer ends. The superior who leads it
learns which subordinate has been careless.

### Feeding Heme

Before the main household meal, a small portion of broth, mash, or blood from a
fresh slaughter is touched to the wooden figure and then given to an animal,
the compost heap, or the fire. Heme receives food by returning it to growth,
rot, or appetite.

The portion grows during illness, pregnancy, or livestock trouble. A Sister of
Heme may prescribe what is offered and who must abstain. The practice gives her
authority over stores precisely when the household can least afford waste.

Priories collect the best version of the offering on festival days. Households
keep the smaller daily act. The difference teaches rank through portion size.

### Speech, Oaths, and Confession

The faithful swear over iron and wood together. The oath-taker grips an iron
ring while a witness holds a bare branch or Heme figure. Breaking the oath is
said to corrupt both tool and issue: work fails, children sicken, animals cast
their young.

Confession is less concerned with private thought than concealed relation.
Priests ask whom the person fed, what they carried, which sign they failed to
report, and who taught the forbidden words. An inward doubt matters when it
produces an unreported act or rival loyalty.

People learn to confess a manageable fault before inspection uncovers a larger
one. A small admission lets the priest demonstrate correction. It also creates
a record that can be used later.

### Work Is Moral Evidence

Straight rows, counted stores, clean tool edges, and prompt tribute are praised
as worked character. Rot in a granary, a broken hinge, or an animal left to
suffer can be named Heme's warning and the household's failure.

Material competence therefore carries religious safety. A skilled farmer can
appear orthodox through order. A sick, exhausted, or impoverished household
looks morally suspect because its maintenance fails first.

The confusion profits the church. It can call poverty impurity, seize the
failing holding, and give it to a more productive adherent. The new harvest then
proves the judgment.

### The Devout Body

Lay adherents wear a rust-colored thread, an iron ring, or a wooden bead. Full
iron display belongs to clergy and soldiers. A farmer who copies a Ferrale's
chain invites punishment for assuming weight above their station.

Fasting is used before judgment, ordination, and major collection. The church
calls the hollow face clear of Heme's excess. Labor continues. A fast that costs
the priory work is shortened for the useful and lengthened for the expendable.

Scars gained in church service may be rubbed with rust pigment during festivals.
An accidental wound receives no such honor unless a priest adopts its story.

## Rituals and Ceremonies

### The Censer Circuit

The common public rite begins with space. Villagers clear a circle around the
shrine or meeting house. The censer-bearer tests the chain cold, counts the links,
and walks the boundary once before coals are added. Everyone sees how far the
weapon can reach.

The lit censer is swung at the four approaches while a priest names what must
remain outside: unquiet dead, unauthorized spirits, Raven deceit, or disease.
Smoke passes over doors, grain, weapons, and assembled people. Sparks that land
on clothing are endured unless they catch flame.

The circuit blesses and threatens with the same movement. It marks the church's
claim to the enclosed ground. Anyone standing outside has made a visible choice.

### Binding an Iron Guard

Iron Guard induction is called **Taking the Closed Chain**. The candidate stands
inside a chain laid on the ground while armor and weapons are placed beyond it.
The chain-leader names the commands that outrank kinship, village debt, and
personal oath. The candidate steps across only after accepting them.

Each piece of equipment is then handed inward. The order may supply a helm, a
priory a shield, and a tithed village the mail or mount. The new guard therefore
wears the obligations of several institutions while swearing that only the Rust
Lord commands the whole.

The weapon's edge remains bright. Rust is rubbed into the guard, pommel, shield
boss, or outer plate. A superior inspects the distinction. Piety that ruins a
working edge fails the rite.

A mounted guard leads the animal through censer smoke and makes it cross the
chain. Fused or altered mounts are presented as creatures already brought under
Rust's dominion. If the animal refuses, the candidate must master it before the
assembly or accept service on foot.

Promotion to chain-leader adds one heavy link to the command chain. On removal
from office the link is struck open. Losing a unit's censer, abandoning its dead
without recovering church iron, or breaking formation to pursue private revenge
can stain the whole chain.

### The Great Measure

Harvest tribute is received in ceremony rather than warehouse silence. The
Measure-Bearer displays the priory weights, tests them against a stone or iron
standard, and calls households in the order set by the previous year's standing.

Each offering is separated into seed, food, transport loss, and church share.
The church's arithmetic is spoken aloud. A disputed measure becomes an inquiry
into the household rather than the scale.

Silver is struck on the rim of an iron bowl so clipped or mixed pieces sound
wrong. Human tribute is named last. The person or household offered must stand
beside the measured goods, making the equivalence visible without the priest
stating it.

The rite ends only after arrears are assigned. A debt may be carried into the
next harvest, converted into labor, secured by a child, or attached to land.
The Great Measure turns worship into an enforceable account.

> **The light sack:** At Dren Vale, a widow's grain came short by the weight of
> one boot. The Measure-Bearer removed his boot and placed it on the other pan.
> The balance still did not rise. He took her eldest daughter into priory service
> and returned the grain for seed. The village remembered the returned sack as
> mercy.

### The Joining of Iron and Wood

Marriages recognized by the church are made before a rusted ring and a forked
wooden branch. The partners place their household gifts beneath them: tools,
cloth, livestock cords, keys, or bowls. A Sister inspects the living exchange.
A Brother records who commands the new household's tribute.

The rite teaches the marriage of Rust and Heme through unequal duties. One
partner is named keeper of boundary and debt. The other keeps stores,
birth, illness, and continuity. Local custom and material competence influence
who receives which role, though male priests usually press the first toward men
and the second toward women.

Both touch the same iron ring. The church can later claim jurisdiction over
property, children, and separation because it witnessed the joining. A marriage
made outside the rite may be tolerated until inheritance or accusation makes
recognition valuable.

### Finding the Mark

A birth under strong Rust rule is reported before the next sunset. A Sister of
Heme examines mother, child, cord, and afterbirth for ordinary danger and signs
the church can claim. The examination is medically useful and politically
unsafe.

An unusual feature is shown to a second witness before it can be concealed.
The Sister decides whether to name it injury, disease, Heme's variation, or a
mark of selection. That decision may protect the child, condemn the household,
or place the child under future priory claim.

The child is laid briefly beside dull iron and unfinished wood. A household
promises to report later change. No permanent church name is given until the
child survives the dangerous first days.

### The Last Weighing

At death, the body is washed and examined for change. An iron piece is placed
at the feet and a wooden piece at the breast. The first marks the boundary the
dead must not cross. The second acknowledges that flesh returns through rot,
root, or feeding earth.

The officiant asks whether the dead settled tribute, kept forbidden knowledge,
or showed signs of restless return. Kin answer before witnesses. A disputed debt
can attach to burial, inheritance, or the treatment of the corpse.

The church may permit ordinary burial, require a guarded grave, or remove the
body for service. Families understand the last option. They still must answer
the questions that make it sound lawful.

### The Burning of the Copy

Confiscated writing is destroyed in public only after an examiner has inspected
it in private. The priest reads or paraphrases one condemned passage, places the
page in the censer, and requires the former owner to state that ignorance has
been restored.

The ceremony hides appropriation inside destruction. A copied page may already
be sealed for the priory. A memorist may stand among the attendants. Villagers
see the dangerous words burn and assume the church has surrendered them too.

Ash from the page is mixed with ordinary hearth ash and scattered where it
cannot be recovered. The book's clasps, leather, and blank leaves become church
property.

### Correction and Re-entry

A lesser offender who accepts penance can be returned to the kept community.
The person stands outside the censer circuit holding the object connected to
the offense. After confession and payment, the object is broken, burned, or
claimed by the church.

The priest opens the circle and draws the person inside by an iron chain held
loosely between them. The offender then eats from a household bowl supplied by
someone who agrees to act as surety.

Re-entry does not erase the record. It gives neighbors permission to trade,
marry, and work with the offender while making the surety answer for recurrence.

### The Marriage in Red Breath

Senior ordination reenacts the marriage of Rust and Heme at the boundary between
human flesh and demonic power. Public teaching calls it a burden accepted for
the people. Settlement rumor calls it marriage to a demon in the Mist.

The candidate fasts, names a human attachment to be subordinated, and enters a
smoke-filled inner place with selected officiants. A living or preserved source
of alteration is brought under restraint. The rite seeks a bodily sign that can
be interpreted as union.

Lower clergy hear the chain, the censer, and whatever voice answers from inside.
The procedure remains hidden from them. At dawn they receive the candidate or
prepare a sealed account of failure. Both outcomes are preached as acceptance
of weight.

At the cult's theological center, demonic intimacy is forbidden to villagers,
cultivated in leaders, and renamed sacrifice when it produces authority.

### The Church Year

Rust and Heme observance attaches itself to the Ravenlands work calendar. A
priory need not control every festival. It must control the moments when stores,
bodies, and obligations are easiest to count.

| Festival Boundary | Rust and Heme Observance |
| --- | --- |
| **Awakening Day** | Tools are inspected, seed stores opened, and the first Heme offering placed in the compost or furrow. Priests record which households lack seed and therefore may need church debt. |
| **Springturn** | Boundaries are walked with cold censers. Broken fences and disputed fields are judged before animals move to wider grazing. |
| **Midsummer** | The Joining of Iron and Wood is celebrated with marriages, animal blessing, and public recitation of the Hammer Articles. Marked recruits are displayed to possible sponsors. |
| **Harvest Day** | The Great Measure begins. Tribute, arrears, and service obligations are fixed while grain can still be counted. |
| **Rotday** | Spoiled stores, diseased animals, suspect books, and unresolved accusations are brought out for culling. The church names destruction as Heme's necessary cutting. |
| **Midwinter** | The dead are counted, restless cases reviewed, and the priory's protection roll recited. A major sacrifice may close debts the church declares too dangerous to carry into the next cycle. |

The calendar is an instrument of memory. A household may forget the year of an
old punishment but remember that it happened before the Rotday culling when the
black cow was killed.

### Human Sacrifice

The church teaches that food, silver, and human life occupy one tribute order.
Human sacrifice is reserved for severe arrears, major protection rites, senior
ordination, or a claimed need to appease powers beyond the settlement.

Selection follows the same administrative logic as punishment. Captives,
condemned heretics, debt hostages, unwanted dependents, or people designated by
lot may be offered. Rank determines whose life can be substituted with silver,
service, or another body.

The ceremony forces the community to name the death as payment. Households may
be required to contribute fuel, restraint, transport, or witness. The victim's
property is entered against the obligation, so even the accounting denies that
a murder occurred outside lawful exchange.

Avoid spectacle for its own sake. The horror lies in the measured grain beside
the bound person, the corrected ledger, and the planting that resumes the next
morning.

## Sin, Penance, and Anathema

### Four Degrees of Offense

Rust judgment asks whether a person can be worked further and whether continued
membership benefits the church. Offenses are divided by remedy rather than by
private guilt.

| Degree | Meaning | Usual Response |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Fault** | Neglect, missed observance, careless speech, or small arrears that do not challenge authority. | Restitution, extra labor, repeated instruction, public admission. |
| **Stain** | Concealment, repeated fault, contact with forbidden practice, or conduct that spreads doubt. | Formal penance, surety, confiscation, bodily punishment, temporary exclusion. |
| **Heresy** | A rival teaching or deliberate refusal of church judgment. | Inquisition, forced recantation, seizure of dependents or property, possible execution. |
| **Anathema** | A person or act declared outside the protection owed to usable humanity. | Total forfeiture, denial of ordinary rites, authorized killing, or removal for sacrifice or hidden service. |

The same act can move between degrees according to rank and usefulness. A
villager who opens a sealed vessel commits heresy. An examiner who opens it
without permission bears a stain. A Ferrale who ordered the opening may call it
necessary burden.

### Anathemas of Substance

The following acts attack the cult's claim that iron and wood are divine gifts:

- carving a detailed face or animal form into an official image of Rust or Heme
- deliberately polishing all rust from a consecrated idol or rank chain
- allowing sacred iron to fail through neglect, then concealing the failure
- burning a Heme grove, figure, or offering outside authorized culling
- using a Rust sigil as common scrap, payment, cookware, or a privy fitting
- forging false rank links or wearing the chain of an office not granted

Armorers and shrine-keepers receive exceptions because maintenance requires
cleaning, repair, and replacement. The exception belongs to office. A farmer
who performs the same act without permission can be condemned for imitation.

### Anathemas of Knowledge

The church treats unauthorized knowledge as stolen weight. Grave offenses
include:

- hiding a demonological text, diagram, specimen, or witnessed weakness
- copying a condemned page before surrendering it
- teaching a lower rank what a superior ordered sealed
- opening a church vessel, letter, archive, or reliquary without office
- destroying dangerous remains before the church can examine them
- asking publicly why a confiscated object was carried away rather than burned

Ignorance does not always excuse possession. The owner was obligated to report
what they did not understand. This lets the church punish curiosity and silence
with the same charge.

### Anathemas of Allegiance

Raven worship is the cult's central religious enemy. Sheltering a Raven Sister,
carrying her message, concealing her medicine, or refusing to name her route can
be treated as heresy or anathema. Open priesthood receives the hardest sentence.

The Congregation of the Serpent is despised but tolerated. Its clergy become
anathema when they challenge Rust jurisdiction, protect Raven worshippers, or
claim authority over seized knowledge. Mere private adherence is often taxed
rather than destroyed.

Other grave acts include refusal of tribute after judgment, aiding a condemned
person's flight, breaking a church-imposed surety, freeing a dead servant claimed
by the order, and denying Zytera's prophetic standing before witnesses.

### Anathemas within the Order

Ordained members can commit offenses unavailable to laypeople:

- revealing that a public burning followed private copying
- placing loyalty to household, lover, or former village above a sealed command
- altering a tribute record for personal gain without sharing the benefit upward
- performing a melding, sacrifice, or major sentence without sufficient rank
- abandoning an Iron Guard chain, sacred weapon, or sealed burden to save oneself
- claiming a revelation that bypasses the Rust Lord's authority
- exposing a superior's contact with demonic power as the same corruption
  punished in common people

The last offense strikes the institution's governing distinction. It is often
prosecuted as rebellion even when the statement is factually exact.

### Penance Must Leave Evidence

Private remorse has little value to the church. Penance must repay a loss,
demonstrate submission, create a witness, or make the corrected person easier to
govern.

| Penance | Practice and Function |
| --- | --- |
| **Bearing Weight** | Iron is added to a yoke, belt, or chain during work. The burden displays correction and reduces the person's ability to flee. |
| **The Dull Fast** | The penitent eats unsalted grain or thin broth while continuing assigned labor. Rank decides duration and exemption. |
| **Making Good the Measure** | The offender replaces tribute, repairs property, or works until a named debt is met. A surety keeps the tally. |
| **Standing Outside the Smoke** | The offender attends worship beyond the censer circuit and receives no shared bowl. Social exclusion is made visible without ending useful labor. |
| **The Open Account** | The penitent publicly names contacts, routes, possessions, and debts. Information becomes the offering. |
| **Road Penance** | The offender carries messages, supplies, or dangerous objects between priories. Survival can restore standing; death closes the account. |
| **Taking Scale** | A rank link, patch of hair, garment hem, or strip of skin is removed before witnesses. The mark prevents the correction from becoming private history. |

Penance is often assigned to households. One person bears weight while another
supplies food. A child may be pledged as surety. The offender's return then
depends on relatives continuing the sentence.

### Absolution Is Conditional

The church declares the account settled and the person usable again, never clean
in an inward sense. A prior can reopen the case if new information changes the
measure.

Written absolution names witnesses, payment, and surety. Where writing is rare,
the corrected person carries a broken link bound with red thread. Another priory
may honor it, question it, or claim the issuing priest lacked rank.

An absolved person remains dependent on the institution that punished them.

### Declaring Anathema

Anathema is pronounced before the kept households whenever possible. The priest
names the protection withdrawn: burial, lawful trade, oath standing, shelter,
and appeal to church judgment. Property and dependents are reassigned before the
person is removed or killed.

Anyone may then harm the condemned without owing the church compensation, though
local kin may still pursue vengeance. The Iron Guard prefers a controlled death
because feud wastes resources. A Ferrale can instead claim the person for
interrogation, sacrifice, or labor hidden from the settlement.

The condemned person's name may remain in the tribute book with a bar of rust
pigment through it. Erasure would suggest the church had lost something. The bar
shows that authority acted.

> **The unburied wife:** A woman condemned for sheltering a Raven Sister was
> killed at the gate and denied the burial ground. Her husband paid the next
> tribute with her loom weights. The Measure-Bearer accepted them but entered
> his household as half a hearth. For the next four winters, every marriage
> offer to his daughters began with that fraction.
