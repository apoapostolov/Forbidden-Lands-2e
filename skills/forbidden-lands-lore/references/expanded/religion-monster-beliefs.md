# Monster Beliefs of the Ravenlands

This reference groups the poorly documented beliefs of saurians, ogres, whiners, and Misgrown. It distinguishes observed conduct and manuscript fact from outsider claim and GM inference. These peoples do not share a religion; they share the danger of being described by hunters, captors, priests, and scholars who profit from misunderstanding them.

---

## Saurians: the Marsh Presences

Outsiders know that saurians worship beings sighted from barges in the Gargan
Marshes. They do not know whether these are gods, ancestors, great animals,
living places, or offices held by different bodies. Do not settle the question
merely to complete a pantheon.

Observable practice centers on water level, communal feasting ponds, dams,
crocodiles, winter fat, and the strict peace of trade platforms. Saurian
religion distinguishes water where outsiders may stand under protection from
water where anything alive may be prey. The difference is marked with posts,
smell, cut reeds, and spoken warning, not an abstract border.

At a **Bank Closing**, saurians inspect a dam before winter. Each family presses
mud into one weak place and leaves a scent mark. A collapse later reveals which
section failed and who had accepted it. The rite joins engineering, territory,
and accountability.

During a **Deep Feeding**, carcasses are allowed to ferment in a chosen pond
until the group can tear and swallow them. What outsiders experience as stench
and decay is controlled food preparation, feast, and return to marsh life. A
priest or elder decides when the food is ready. Premature eating insults the
group and risks sickness.

Crocodiles occupy an uncertain sacred position: trained burden-bearers,
dangerous kin-neighbors, and famine food. Killing one when other food remains
may require replacement, public feeding of its keepers, or surrender of metal.
No one who has only watched from a boat knows which remedy applies.

Likely anathemas include violence on a marked trade platform, breaching a dam
during cold season, hiding disease in a feast pond, wasting metal received for
communal use, and speaking as if one had understood a marsh presence seen only
at the surface. Treat these as local inference until a saurian community shows
otherwise.

## Ogres: Thunder, Mark, and Chosen Kin

Religion matters little to many ogres in the institutional sense. No common
priesthood records a creed. Their thunder gatherings, body marks, violent music,
and chosen attachments nevertheless carry sacred force.

When thunder begins, ogres howl, strike wood and metal, and search for a rhythm
loud enough to enter the storm. The practice recognizes a world greater than
speech and small law. An ogre who cannot join may be ill, grieving, or outside
the group's chosen bond.

Brands, stretched ears, and weighted noses turn endurance into visible history.
Marks can show standing, loss, adoption, boast, or punishment. Outsiders who
assume every mark means the same thing may insult an ogre by praising a sign of
disgrace.

Ogre adoption can be sincere and possessive at once. A chosen companion is fed,
defended, marked, and prevented from leaving. Some ogres understand departure
as proof the bond was false from the beginning. Others hold a **Last Noise**, in
which the departing person strikes one object and is allowed to walk while the
sound remains audible.

Common taboos are local: breaking another's status weights without challenge,
interrupting a thunder rhythm for mockery, claiming a mark never endured, or
killing an adopted person before witnesses have declared the bond broken. These
limits do not make ogre treatment of captives safe.

## Whiners: Religion behind the Hollow

Almost everything outsiders say about whiner belief is compromised by hunting,
caging, and harvesting. A captive questioned by people who value its flesh has
no reason to reveal a shrine, queen, route, or true name.

What can be observed is collective defense of hollows, the recovery or exchange
of captive kin, stone chewed and remade through the body, and violent revenge
against harvesters. Druids claim deep colonies and an egg-laying queen. The
claim may be natural history, hostile analogy, or both.

One plausible religious principle is that body and shelter are continuous. A
whiner eats stone, changes it, and adds it to the hollow; other kin cut whiner
flesh and use it to change their own bodies. The first exchange is communal and
consensual. The second is the central violation.

Possible rites include sealing a dead whiner into fresh porous stone, carrying
a chip from a lost hollow until a new chamber is joined, and biting captors'
coins before returning them with living prisoners. Possible anathemas include
revealing a brood chamber, trading another whiner's flesh, abandoning a captive
whose location is known, and placing growth-gold beneath one's own skin for
private wealth. Keep all of these as GM inference until play establishes a
particular hollow's law.

## Misgrown and Religious Ownership of the Body

Misgrown are not one religious kin. They are humans altered through demonic
power, concentrated around authorities that benefit from the alteration. The
Rust church reads marked bodies as election when useful and contamination when
disobedient. Raven Sisters may see a patient and victim. Druids may see imposed
form with living claims. Villagers may see danger before personhood.

Misgrown religious life therefore begins with jurisdiction. Who may name the
body? Who may touch it, heal it, display it, recruit it, or decide that change
has become identity? A private rite may consist only of covering a feature the
church demands be shown, or giving an altered limb a household name rather than
a doctrinal one.

The gravest offense within such hidden fellowships is delivering another
Misgrown person to a priest in exchange for one's own acceptance. The gravest
danger is that refusal may cost every marked person in the settlement what
little protection they possess.
