# Elvenspring Culture of the Ravenlands

Elvenspring live between mortal urgency and elven memory. Households age at
different rates; gifts, land, teaching, and mediation can outlast one party to
the bargain. This reference covers descent, apprenticeship, partnership,
healing, property, divided jurisdiction, and duties reopened after the Blood
Mist. Their gods, orders, sacred places, calendars, memorials, and anathemas
remain in [Elvenspring Religions](religion-elvenspring.md).

---

## Duty within Mortal Time

Elvenspring worship Clay and the Shardmaiden, honor land gods, and often respect
Raven. Their faith must answer time. They live longer than humans and die far
sooner than elves. Every tradition offers them a different measure of when work
is finished.

An Elvenspring household may keep a three-part memorial: clay for what the dead
made, a feather for promises carried among humans, and a seed or green branch
for duties left to druids. No one part gives a complete account. Together they
state that mixed descent creates obligations rather than spiritual impurity.

The common anathema is abandonment disguised as patience. An Elvenspring who
delays a mortal need because an elf considers a decade brief is judged to have
chosen the convenience of the longer-lived. Frailers are accused of the reverse:
burning every inheritance for power within one life. Actual people rarely fit
either sermon cleanly.

## Household Practice

Elvenspring households keep objects at several rates of change. Damp clay
figures track current illness or work. Fired Maha tablets preserve statements
meant to survive the present keeper. Feathers record human-facing promises.
Seeds, grafts, and survey marks carry duties into another season.

The household checks these objects together at each seasonal turn. A promise
may remain while its clay figure is dissolved. A tree may outgrow the marker
that assigned its care. A tablet may still be legible after everyone affected
rejects its statement.

Mixed households often assign one person to short accounts and another to long
ones. The division can become gendered, hereditary, or exploitative. Whoever
keeps the long account can delay; whoever keeps the short one can manufacture
emergency.

## Birth, Naming, and Descent

Elvenspring bodies follow mortal birth. Their names often acknowledge several
relations: household, human line, elven teacher, druidic order, or place. No
single name proves which community will accept them.

At **The Three Touches**, a newborn touches clay, living leaf or seed, and an
object carrying a human promise. The adults name who will teach body care, land
duty, and public obligation. One person may accept several parts. The rite
makes gaps visible before crisis.

Descent from an elf can bring status, expectation, magic, and lifelong
comparison with someone who does not age at the same rate. A child praised as
the continuation of an elven parent may spend a mortal life waiting to become
old enough for authority.

Elvenspring descent is neither dilution nor spiritual bridge by nature. Those
descriptions assign a social task before the person consents. Some accept the
role. Others join Frailers, human courts, or remote circles to escape it.

## Apprenticeship and Adult Standing

Druidic apprenticeship teaches diagnosis before ceremony: soil, water, birth,
wound, animal behavior, weather, memory, and Maha. The apprentice must learn
which changes can be reversed within a human season and which outlive the
people who approved them.

At **The Short Work**, the candidate completes a useful task within one turn or
day. At **The Long Witness**, the same candidate accepts observation of a place
through a full year. Adult standing requires both. Quick competence without
continuity and patient observation without action fail in different directions.

A shard-bearing Maiden receives power and a debt to the Shardmaiden. Local
circles ask what ordinary work the power displaces. A warrior who hunts demons
still needs someone to maintain the path, feed dependents, and repair what the
hunt crosses.

## Partnership and Unequal Aging

Partnership with a human may last one shared old age. Partnership with an elf
may leave the Elvenspring visibly aging beside someone who can choose another
young body. Neither relation is treated as lesser, but each requires explicit
custody and inheritance.

At **The Two Winters**, partners exchange duties for one winter before making a
lasting household. Each learns the other's rate of planning. An elf partner
must name decisions that cannot wait a decade. A human partner must name which
obligations survive one harvest's hardship.

Elvenspring partners often designate a future witness younger than either.
Without one, the longer-lived partner can control the account after every other
witness dies.

## Relations with Humans, Elves, and Orcs

Raven worship gives Elvenspring a language of public duty and recognized oath.
Human churches may still treat them as useful outsiders rather than full
authorities. Wyrm institutions respect preservation and can mistake longevity
for consent to keep every dangerous record.

Elven religion offers origin, teaching, and long memory. It also offers
patronage that can last an Elvenspring lifetime without maturing into equality.
The respectful elf who says “later” may control more effectively than a human
who says “no.”

Orcs remember service imposed by elves and dwarves. An Elvenspring envoy who
speaks for elder kin inherits that suspicion. Claiming mixed descent as proof
of neutrality avoids the history rather than answering it.

## Healing, Birth, and Changed Bodies

Elvenspring healers combine plant craft, clean water, splint, stitch, prayer,
shifting magic, and household knowledge. No method receives trust merely because
it is natural. Poison grows. Infection is natural. Skill is shown in outcome and
in the risks named beforehand.

At **The Open Figure**, an unfired clay body receives marks for the known wound
and gaps for what remains uncertain. Patient, healer, and household witness can
add a mark. The figure is kept damp until treatment ends. Firing it without the
patient's consent turns a working record into a permanent story about their body.

Birth joins Clay's shaping to Raven witness and local women who know the actual
work. Orders sometimes claim a gifted child before the parent has recovered.
Village practice delays any religious placement until the person who gave birth
can sit, eat, and answer without a senior druid in the room.

Shifting magic creates hard consent. A dying patient may accept a form they would
refuse under less need. The healer records which changes were necessary, which
were chosen, and which can be reversed. Gratitude does not transfer authorship of
the surviving body.

Misgrown people expose the orders' limits. Maidens recognize hostile demonic
work and may still speak over the person living inside it. Village druids are
more likely to ask which limb hurts, what work remains possible, and whom the
marked person trusts near sleep.

## Mediation, Witness, and Divided Jurisdiction

Elvenspring are often asked to translate human urgency into elven hearing. The
role brings access and blame. If the council delays, the village calls the
mediator an elf. If the council acts quickly and errs, elves call them governed
by mortal panic.

At **The Two Accounts**, each party states the same dispute in the time of the
other. The elf must name what happens before one human winter. The human must
name what descendants inherit after the present need. The mediator then records
where each account remains unable to answer the other.

Witnesses are chosen for consequence as well as sight. A woodcutter who did not
see the old vow may know what the closed grove costs now. An elf who heard the
vow may not have visited since. A ruling that hears only origin gives memory
ownership of the present.

Elvenspring judges prefer remedies with several dates: immediate food or safety,
a seasonal repair, and a later inspection. This fits their religious measure and
creates work future keepers may refuse. Every judgment therefore names who may
end or alter it.

Mixed jurisdiction can protect the weak by giving them another authority to
approach. The same division can let every authority send them elsewhere. A
pregnant human at a druidic shrine, an orc apprentice under Raven oath, or a
Misgrown patient in a Maiden camp may discover that everyone claims principle
and no one supplies a bed.

## Property, Gifts, and the Work of Stewardship

Orders rarely call land property. They speak of custody, survey, protection, and
living claim. Mortal households notice that these words still decide who may cut
wood, graze animals, build, inherit, and remain.

At **Handing the Care**, the outgoing keeper gives the successor tools, records,
known dangers, and the names of people relying on the place. A title without
these burdens is ceremonial theft. The recipient may refuse after inspecting
the condition.

Gifts from elves can exceed mortal ability to repay. A rare seed, living tool,
or century of instruction may create dependence disguised as generosity. The
Elvenspring recipient asks what the giver expects before witnesses die.

Human coin is accepted where labor, medicine, travel, or supplies require it.
Golden Bough teachers may condemn payment while depending on village food. A
clear wage can protect both parties better than noble talk about shared purpose.

Shard, Maha tablet, healer's kit, survey, and sacred vessel belong to different
claims. An order may own the shard's custody, a village the vessel it supplied,
an apprentice the tools personally made, and a patient the record of their body.
Calling everything temple property erases those hands.

## The Blood Mist and the Reopening of Duties

The Blood Mist cut teachers from students, orders from village circles, and
elven parents from mortal descendants. Local druids acquired full authority
because distant correction could not arrive before dark. Some kept communities
alive. Some made preference into doctrine.

Long survey routes collapsed into short loops. Records continued at both ends
and described different forests under the same name. When the Mist lifted, each
order called its own line continuous. The trees did not settle the dispute.

At **Splicing the Years**, keepers place the separated records side by side.
Contradictory entries remain visible. People who performed the work during
isolation speak before the order that preserved the older title.

Elvenspring who crossed reopened roads often met elven relatives unchanged in
body and unchanged in expectation. The mortal descendant had inherited debts,
offices, and graves the elf still considered temporary. Reunion could resemble
an inspection.

The open land also strengthened Frailers. They offered quick power, messages,
and protection before formal orders rebuilt their reach. Condemning the bargain
afterward does not answer why desperate settlements found the Frailer first.
