# Wolfkin Religion of the Ravenlands

Heme the Huntress stands in pack law and forest. Blood and breeding bind the
pack through seasonal movement. Music orders the taking of prey, while
hunt-madness shadows the remembered protection of the Blood Mist. Canon
distinguishes the Huntress from the Rust Church. Wolfkin regression under
prolonged isolation is an established campaign truth. Detailed rites are
grounded setting inference.

---

## Heme as the Huntress

Wolfkin worship Heme as the Huntress, the unadulterated life they believe humans
abandoned with fur, pack, and forest. The Blood Mist was her breath. It allowed
passage beneath trees and punished exposure on open ground. The cult remembers
this as both favor and instruction: protection belongs to the right form of
life in the right place.

Huntress worship is not the Sisters of Heme in another costume. Wolfkin reject
the Rust church's settled measures, human hierarchy, and claim to own divine
wood. They may cooperate because both moved under the Mist or desire its return.
They do not agree on who serves whom.

Season, breeding, hunger, kill, and pack formation structure the faith. Blood
shows that life fed life. Narcotic mushrooms loosen ordinary restraint. Complex
howling and drum rhythms hold the group together while individuals enter altered
states. A gathering that sounds like chaos to humans can have exact parts,
entries, and prohibitions.

**Opening the Scent** begins a communal hunt. Hunters smell a token from the
prey, name who must be fed, and state any boundary the chase may not cross. A
kill beyond that boundary belongs to the offended pack or settlement unless a
new bargain is made.

**Giving the Throat** distributes a kill. The hunter who struck first does not
automatically eat first. Cubs, breeders, injured hunters, elders, guests, and
dominant animals receive portions according to pack law. A leader who feeds
only strength may win one winter and lose the pack by spring.

Training young with wounded prey is accepted among many packs as the honest
teaching of panic, pursuit, and clean killing. Other packs condemn it as making
prey suffer to flatter the teacher. The dispute is internal, not proof that one
side has become human.

Huntress anathemas include wasting a kill, caging a pack member as correction,
wearing wolf hide as mockery, poisoning prey so its body cannot be shared,
claiming a cub without pack acknowledgment, and betraying a forest refuge to
people who will clear it.

## Religious Authority

Wolfkin have ritual specialists and singers. Mushroom keepers and trail readers
hold narrower knowledge. Elders may judge all of them, but no office corresponds
neatly to a human priest. Authority follows the matter at hand. The hunter who
knows a herd speaks before pursuit. The keeper who dried the mushrooms controls
the dose. A nursing parent can overrule a hunt leader on the food needed by cubs.

Pack leaders claim broad authority because every activity touches safety. This
claim is strongest during movement and weakest after food has been divided. A
leader who turns temporary pursuit command into permanent ownership of mates,
cubs, and kills creates the conditions for challenge or fracture.

Huntress shrines are used routes, sleeping hollows, kill places, mushroom
grounds, and clearings where harmony can carry. A fixed idol can exist, but the
pack judges it by scent and use. An untouched image surrounded by no tracks is
an abandoned object.

## Forest, Mist, and Open Ground

The forest gave the Blood Mist a ceiling. Wolfkin moved beneath it while people
on open ground died. The Huntress is therefore remembered through cover,
interrupted sight, layered scent, and paths with more than one exit.

Open country remains spiritually and bodily dangerous. Some packs train their
young at widening clearings, returning to cover before panic takes the body.
Others forbid the attempt and call the plains the Furless Trap. An exile forced
onto open ground may experience the sentence as exposure before the Huntress,
not mere banishment.

The lifted Mist divided belief. Returners want the Huntress's breath restored
and accept alliance with Rust priests toward that end. Keepers say the old
protection was a season, not a command to cage the whole land in it. Outlaw
packs may praise open roads because roads make paid tracking possible.

## Daily Observance

Sleeping places are circled downwind before rest. The last wolfkin to complete
the circuit reports unfamiliar scent, broken cover, and the route kept open.
This is watch duty and prayer in one act.

Food is smelled before division. Illness, poison, fear, another pack, and bad
storage can all be present in meat. A leader who begins ceremonial speech
before this inspection is performing above the needs of the pack.

At departure, bedding and waste are scattered or buried according to the
pack's purpose. Concealment protects the route. A deliberate scent mark claims
return. Confusing the two can start a territorial dispute.

Moon, weather, and season affect movement and prey. They also shape breeding and
the distance over which sound carries. Wolfkin do not need a separate theology
for each. The Huntress is known in how living bodies meet those conditions.

## The Pack as Shelter of the Mind

Wolfkin personhood is kept in company. A name is called and answered. Food is
divided by another hand. Someone knows the sleeper's scent, corrects a mistaken
trail, hears a change in the howl, and remembers which quarry was forbidden.
These ordinary exchanges hold memory and judgment in place.

Prolonged solitude strips those supports away. Wolfkin call the resulting
collapse **hunt-madness**. They speak of it as a shameful terror and watch for
it whenever a hunter is missing too long or a pack dies around one survivor.
Only enemies mistake the condition for a return to wolfkin purity.

### Liminal Solitude

A wolfkin alone in the forest has too much world and no witness with whom to
divide it. Tracks cross without agreement about which matter. Every wind brings
scent that cannot be checked against another nose. The trees repeat beyond
count, and the path behind loses the remembered voices that made it a path home.

Open ground carries the old terror of exposure beneath the Blood Mist. Solitary
forest produces a related dread. A clearing may be narrow and still feel
without edge when no familiar scent answers from the trees. The canopy lifts
too high. Gaps between trunks open in every direction. Some wolfkin crouch
beneath brush for fear of that depth; others run until movement itself chooses
the direction.

Early signs are practical. The lone wolfkin lays out portions for absent pack
mates, repeats names to keep them attached to faces, sleeps in shorter intervals,
and follows tracks that would normally be dismissed. They may demand constant
speech from companions, tie a cord between sleeping places, or refuse to cross
a quiet clearing until someone answers a call from the other side.

A wolfkin who does these things is still trying to remain present. Mockery and
punishment worsen the danger. A pack responds with touch, known food, repeated
routes, call and answer, shared watch, and work that requires another member to
complete it. No one recovering from isolation sleeps beyond hearing.

### The Hunt without the Pack

As the collapse advances, names become scents and speech contracts to direction,
distance, wound, and quarry. The wolfkin can stalk, ambush, and remember terrain,
but cannot weigh the hunt against an oath, a child, tomorrow's food, or the life
of the quarry. Intelligence narrows around pursuit until nothing remains
outside it.

The hunt-mad choose a quarry and continue until it dies or they do. Pain,
weather, boundaries, traps, and the voices of former kin become information
about the chase. Killing and feeding bring a short stillness. A new scent soon
becomes the next quarry. Many die pursuing armed prey, crossing open ground, or
following an animal into water, fire, or stone.

A fully hunt-mad wolfkin does not return. Packs may catch an early descent and
restore the exchanges that preserve judgment. Once the last social recognition
is gone, a familiar voice can delay a strike but cannot call the person back.
The pack then faces a mercy hunt against a body whose habits, hiding places, and
childhood calls they know.

### Pack Death and Foster Claim

The sole survivor of a pack is in immediate danger even when unhurt. Nearby
packs are obliged to take the bereaved among them until a lasting bond is made.
The newcomer eats inside the circle, shares watch, sleeps against living scent,
and answers the foster pack's evening count. Refusal leaves a hunt-mad predator
for every settlement and trail nearby.

The duty is costly. A foster pack must feed someone whose grief may turn to
challenge, panic, or flight. Old feuds do not vanish. A survivor may wake among
the scent of former enemies and understand that these are the only people
keeping their name intact. Some packs demand service or a future marriage for
the shelter. Others accept the duty because one day they may leave a survivor
of their own.

At **Calling the Living Name**, each foster names one remembered act of the
survivor that was neither hunt nor kill. The survivor answers with one present
duty. The rite is repeated at dusk until the answers cease to sound borrowed.
It gives the pack evidence of recovery without pretending the dead have been
replaced.

Deliberate isolation is among the cruelest wolfkin punishments. Packs that use
it know what they are making. Human captors may discover the same method and
call the resulting frenzy proof that wolfkin were beasts all along.

## Birth, Cubs, and Pack Recognition

Birth belongs to a sheltered circle with food stored nearby and at least two
exit routes. The parent who gives birth need not raise alone. Pack recognition
determines who owes milk, warmth, carrying, and defense.

At **First Scent**, each adult who accepts responsibility breathes over a scrap
of bedding and touches nose or brow to the cub. Refusal can expose a disputed
parentage, an omen claim, a food crisis, or a planned departure. The rite does
not make resources appear. It makes refusal visible.

Cubs receive use-names early and deed-names later. A deed-name may record a
successful stalk, an embarrassing noise that saved the pack, a scar, or a
preference no elder managed to correct. Names are calls, and a call must remain
distinct at distance.

## Teaching the Hunt

Young wolfkin learn wind, silence, pursuit, restraint, and the moment a wounded
animal becomes dangerous. Packs disagree over wounded-prey training. Supporters
say panic cannot be taught through tracks alone. Opponents say a teacher who
prolongs pain to display control has confused instruction with appetite.

At **The Lost Trail**, an adolescent follows a deliberately broken scent and
must decide when evidence has ended. Returning with uncertainty can pass. An
invented track cannot. The rite values the hunter who says “gone” before the
pack wastes a day following pride.

Adult standing includes the right to scent-mark boundaries and speak in kill
distribution. A strong hunter who cannot stop at a declared boundary may remain
outside both rights.

## Pairing, Breeding, and Consent

Breeding seasons intensify rivalry, movement, and ritual. They do not erase
pack law. A pairing may require acknowledgment from the partners, existing
parents of cubs, and whoever keeps winter food. Packs that treat dominance as
blanket consent produce violence they later describe as nature.

At **Crossing Scent**, partners place sleeping cloth in each other's usual place
for one night. By morning both know what enemies, dependents, sickness, and
obligations travel with the bond. A partner may refuse after the exchange
without being accused of theft.

Some packs keep exclusive pairings. Others organize parenting more broadly.
The religious concern is whether every dependent has named protection and
whether a powerful hunter can abandon one litter for a newer bond without cost.

## Kill, Feast, and Distribution

The kill belongs first to the needs named at Opening the Scent. The striker
earns standing, not unrestricted ownership. Blood may mark brow, muzzle, tree,
or drum before butchery. The mark records the life taken and where its strength
goes.

Giving the Throat is followed by practical division. Organs spoil first. Hide
requires work. Bones, sinew, fat, and stomach contents have uses. Waste is
religious failure because it proves the hunt served excitement more than need.

Eating intelligent prey divides packs. Some distinguish no sacred boundary
between speaking and other meat. Others refuse kin flesh because remembered
words remain inside the eater. Packs that hunt humans may still observe guest
law toward a named traveler. Threshold, scent, and declared status carry the
contradiction.

## Ecstasy, Mushroom, and Music

Narcotic mushrooms are gathered by people who know species, ground, drying,
and dose. A ritual dose loosens ordinary restraint without releasing the keeper
from responsibility for what follows. Adulterated or excessive doses can turn
a gathering into mauling, seizure, or a night of false omens.

Howl and drum parts carry entry, challenge, pursuit, kill, grief, breeding,
warning, and return. A skilled group can hold several lines at once. The music
lets individuals lose ordinary speech without losing the pack's structure.

At **The Held Note**, one singer sustains the home tone while others enter and
leave the pattern. If the home tone fails, the gathering stops. Packs use the
rite to judge whether a leader can support attention without dominating every
voice.

## Death, Grief, and the Last Scent

The dead are smelled by those who knew them before washing or burning changes
the body. Each witness names the last reliable scent-place: sleeping hollow,
watch post, kill, lover, cub, or wound. These places guide memory and warn of
disease.

Burial, exposure, burning, and consumption vary. Frozen ground and pursuit may
make custom impossible. The essential act is **Breaking the Return**, in which
the dead person's usual path into camp is blocked or crossed with another
scent. For several nights sleepers learn that the missing approach will remain
empty.

A dead leader's marks are inspected. Boundaries no living member can hold are
released. Keeping them from pride invites challenge from neighbors and hunger
inside the pack.

## Exile, Rage, and Return

An exile loses access to sleeping places, shared kills, cub recognition, and
the pack's known routes. This is social and religious death. A werewolf raging
outside pack law may be driven away because no one can safely witness an oath
during the frenzy.

Exile also carries the risk of hunt-madness. Some packs distinguish removal
from territory from removal from company: an offender must travel with two
witnesses until another pack accepts custody. Others send the exile alone and
deny responsibility for what later comes out of the trees. Huntress singers
call the second practice a concealed execution.

Return requires more than submission. The exile must name what scent, food,
boundary, or body was violated and accept a practical limit. The injured
members decide whether proximity is possible. Some injuries leave no safe
route back.

Packs sometimes use cages borrowed from humans. Huntress teaching condemns the
practice because correction without movement, scent, or retreat destroys the
conditions under which wolfkin can regain control. A pack may still cage an
enemy overnight. Calling the captive no longer kin is the usual excuse.

## Huntress and Heme

Wolfkin call Heme the Huntress and recognize blood, wood, disease, fertility,
and death through living cycles. Rust doctrine turns wood into the obedient
wife of iron and places human priests above the material. Wolfkin reject both
claims.

Cooperation with Sisters of Heme can bring medicine, information, or passage
through Rust territory. It also gives the church access to pack routes and
breeding grounds. A Sister who calls a gathering primitive may still want its
knowledge of mushrooms. A pack that calls her prey may still use her knife.

The sharpest dispute concerns the Blood Mist. Rust wants control over its
return. Returner packs want its cover. Neither can assume the restored Mist
would obey the bargain or spare the same bodies twice.

## Sacred Time and Seasonal Packs

Wolfkin count sacred time by body and prey. The first lean litter, the week when
deer shed velvet, mushroom fruiting after warm rain, ice strong enough to carry
a hunter, and the night migratory birds cease calling all require different
work. A calendar kept without scent or tracks is only another human board.

At **Loosening the Winter Circle**, packs inspect every sleeper, store, boundary,
and remembered grievance before they divide for spring. People who carried one
another through hunger may discover that warmer weather restores an old feud.
The rite names which bonds survive the season of need.

The long summer gathering is held where several packs can hear without sharing
one sleeping ground. Songs cross the distance. Courtship, fosterage, disputed
cubs, refuge claims, and winter routes are answered through messengers moving
between the circles. Bringing every pack under one fire would make hospitality
look too much like submission.

Autumn's **First Empty Track** marks the departure of a herd or flock. Hunters
walk the abandoned route and collect dung, hair, feather, broken browse, and the
signs of predators following it. The ceremony teaches absence as evidence. A
young hunter who calls the animals gone without finding where they went has
named ignorance, not season.

Winter rites are quiet. Breath reveals location. Snow carries tracks farther
than scent. Pack prayer moves into maintenance of hides, paws, weapons, dried
food, and the sleeping ring. Hunger makes every distribution judgment religious.

## Pack Formation, Division, and Succession

A pack is made through repeated dependence. One hunt or mating does not create
it. People must share sleep, food, danger, young, and the right to interrupt
another member's anger before the bond earns recognition.

At **Making the Circle**, each prospective member places bedding at the edge of
the common ground. Over several nights the circle is tightened. Anyone may stop
the movement and name the scent, habit, enemy, or injury that prevents closer
sleep. The objection must be answered before bodies are pressed together.

Pack division is not automatically failure. Too many adults can exhaust prey,
turn every pairing into rivalry, and leave young under constant challenge. At
**Taking the Second Path**, the departing group receives fire, a route warning,
and portions from the last shared kill. Boundary and foster duties are stated
before the scents separate.

Succession begins when others stop answering the leader's first call. A leader
may yield hunt command while keeping standing in songs, foster decisions, or
refuge law. Human observers often mistake a single defeated challenge for a
change of every office.

At **The Voice behind the Voice**, a proposed leader gives route, danger, and
purpose. Another wolfkin repeats the call from beyond sight. If the pack follows
only when it hears the first person's throat, the order rests on dominance. If
the repeated information moves them, the command can survive the leader.

A pack that loses several adults may join another without surrendering its dead.
The smaller circle keeps one song, sleeping place, or distribution claim until
the first cub it raises to adult standing. Then the joined pack decides whether
the old name remains useful.

## Sickness, Injury, and Contagion

Scent makes sickness public early and stigma difficult to hide. Fever, infected
wound, spoiled digestion, fear, pregnancy, and demonic change can alter a known
person's smell before they speak. Pack members are expected to report the change
without claiming to know its cause.

At **The Separate Fire**, a sick member sleeps downwind but within hearing. Food
and water are delivered by the same few attendants. The distance protects the
pack without abandoning the person to silence. No patient is left beyond call
unless their illness makes answering dangerous.

Wound licking is limited by experience. Saliva cleans some dirt and carries
other sickness. Healers use boiled water, cloth, pressure, splints, mushrooms,
and human tools when useful. A singer who forbids a sound treatment because it
looks furless may be asked to keep the wound with prayer alone.

Rage complicates care. A wounded wolfkin may strike before recognizing touch.
Forked poles, nets, shields, and familiar bedding can restrain without a cage.
The people holding them must speak continuously. Silent restraint resembles
predation and can drive the patient deeper into it.

Demonic alteration is judged by relation. Does the person still answer names,
respect the feeding order, and recognize the boundary stated before a hunt? A
changed body may remain kin. A familiar body that no longer recognizes any
claim may be lost. Rust doctrine reverses this test whenever appearance benefits
the church.

## Refuge, Territory, and the Right to Pass

Territory holds several claims. A pack may own sleeping ground, share water,
guard a birthing hollow, reserve a mushroom patch, and permit another pack to
follow prey through the same trees. One scent mark cannot explain all these
rights.

At **Reading the Boundary**, neighboring packs walk opposite sides of a marked
route. Each renews only the signs it can still defend and names paths left open
for fire, flood, wounded prey, foster duty, or a sole survivor. A boundary with
no emergency crossing is a trap waiting for weather.

A refuge is held against more than enemies. Felling, smoke, repeated carts,
poisoned bait, loud mining, and too many strangers can break the layered cover
that made it safe. Compensation may require moving a road rather than paying
silver.

Guest scent is made by rubbing a visitor's cloth with ash, bedding, or gland
scent accepted by the pack. It grants passage to named places for a stated
time. Wearing the mark after protection ends is deception. Copying it to enter
a birthing hollow is anathema.

Wolfkin guides often refuse to name a refuge on a human map. They may lead the
same traveler there blindfolded in genuine trust. Humans call this contradiction
because they treat access and record as the same gift.

## Trade, Tools, and Furless Work

Wolfkin use iron, cloth, rope, pottery, salt, medicine, and other settled craft
while condemning the life that produces much of it. Packs live with the
contradiction through trade, theft, salvage, and the claim that a tool can be
taken without taking human ways.

A spearhead is judged by balance and edge. Praise goes to the smith only when a
continuing relation exists. Packs otherwise name the trader, raid, corpse, or
ruin from which it came. Human makers resent seeing their best work enter a song
as something found beside a road.

At **The Unfamiliar Hand**, a new tool is used first on a task that can fail
without killing anyone. A trap is set without a spring, a pot boiled with water,
or a metal hook loaded with stones. Testing before dependence is devotion to
living bodies.

Permanent stores trouble Huntress teaching because stored wealth attracts walls,
guards, and rank. Winter food remains necessary. Packs distinguish food intended
for named mouths from surplus kept to command strangers. Leaders blur the line
when they want tribute.

Coin has scent but no obvious use. Many packs punch it, string it, hammer it into
small plates, or trade it quickly. A mercenary who keeps silver may become the
pack's bridge to settlements or the person who starts measuring kin by wages.

## Relations with Other Kin and Their Gods

Goblins share night travel and bonds with riding wolves. Packs judge goblin
breeding as control over animals whose refusal should matter. Goblins answer
that a hungry wolf chooses the handler who feeds it and that Huntress sermons
often conceal ownership behind kinship words.

Elves and druids condemn wolfkin origin, hunting, altered states, and treatment
of prey. Wolfkin remember druids who protected a forest by excluding the pack
that knew its paths. A Golden Bough survey can still become common ground when
it records prey, felling, fire, and winter refuge rather than declaring the
forest untouched.

Raven guest law is useful because it makes a promise audible. Raven judgment
becomes hostile when a human settlement treats all forest beyond its fence as
unclaimed. Flow keepers can protect water and then deny that blood, wallow, and
carcass belong in living streams.

Orcs understand abandonment and the price of carrying dependents through a
retreat. They also enslave and raid. Wolfkin respect the orc who brings the
injured home and despise rank contests that spend food needed by the camp.

Halfling hospitality offers full bowls and rooms heavy with concealed fear. A
pack may trade safely for years without being invited past the front room. The
wolfkin nose knows who sleeps below the floor. Naming them can rescue a hidden
person or destroy the only shelter they have.

## After the Blood Mist

The Mist gave forest wolfkin movement denied to many enemies. Packs carried
messages, hunted close to trapped villages, and treated open ground as a wall.
Some elders remember those years as proof that the Huntress chose their form.

The same Mist killed wolfkin who followed prey beyond cover, trapped divided
packs on opposite sides of a clearing, and made foster duty impossible across
short distances. Cubs learned that the visible sky could consume a person.
Calling the whole age a blessing makes these dead into doctrinal inconvenience.

When the Mist lifted, the open ground remained in the body. Packs tested it by
placing singers at the forest edge, stretching scent lines across grass, and
crossing only while every traveler could hear a known call. Some routes took
years to become ordinary.

Returners seek the old cover and believe preparation can correct its cost.
Keepers ask who controls the border of the restored Mist and which pack will be
cut off when it settles differently. Outlaws value the open land because a
tracker can now sell skill beyond the trees.

At **The First Uncovered Sleep**, a pack camps where no canopy stands. Watches
overlap. Bedding is placed close enough to touch. No one is mocked for returning
to the tree line before dawn. Success gives a route, not a cure.

## Dream, Scent Memory, and False Quarry

Wolfkin dreams are judged after waking movement. The sleeper walks the camp
boundary, smells familiar people, drinks, and reports what remains. A dream that
vanishes under known scent receives no hunt.

At **The Waking Track**, another member lays a short path containing one true
sign and one misleading sign. The dreamer follows until they can name which is
which. The test prevents a vivid night image from becoming immediate command.

Mushrooms, fever, blood loss, solitude, and demonic influence can make a quarry
feel chosen. Pack law requires a second nose before pursuit across a boundary.
A leader who exempts their own visions has made appetite into revelation.

The dead may appear through remembered scent before image. Packs do not assume
every such dream is a visitor. Grief repeats bedding, wound, smoke, and last food
with great accuracy. The distinction matters less when the dream asks only for a
grave path repaired. It matters greatly when it names someone to kill.

Rust Sisters sometimes offer interpretation of Heme dreams. Their knowledge of
drug, disease, and blood can be useful. Their conclusion usually moves authority
toward the church. Packs let them examine the sleeper and keep them away from
the boundary decision.

## Strangers, Fostered Outsiders, and Pack Speech

Another kin can live with a pack without becoming wolfkin. Acceptance concerns
sleep, food, warning, boundary, and the right to be sought when missing. Body
does not need to change for these duties to become real.

At **Learning the Answer**, the outsider is given a call distinct from alarm,
prey, and challenge. They must answer from beyond sight and recognize the pack's
reply. A person who cannot howl may use whistle, horn, struck wood, or a spoken
name.

Outsiders rarely read scent well enough to know when a private warning has been
given. The pack must translate boundaries it expects the guest to obey. Punishing
an unsmelled violation preserves superiority rather than safety.

Humans fostered after childhood may value walls and solitary sleep. Forcing them
into the common ring can make pack care resemble captivity. A small sleeping
distance is permitted when sound and watch still connect it.

An outsider who leaves may retain foster claim for one winter, route, or stated
danger. Keeping it forever would let every former guest enter a birthing refuge.
Ending it requires a final food division and return of the guest scent.

## Judgment without a Court

Pack judgment happens where consequence can be smelled and shown. Spoiled meat,
broken cover, frightened cub, false boundary, and an absent member are brought
before speech whenever possible.

At **Walking the Harm**, offender and injured members follow the route of the
act. They stop where another choice remained possible. The pack then assigns
repair from that point rather than demanding pain at the final scene.

Challenge settles standing only when standing caused the dispute. It cannot
replace food, restore a refuge, heal a cub, or call back someone driven into
solitude. Strong leaders prefer challenge because they can win it once and avoid
a season of work.

Expulsion requires another pack, escorted route, or an explicit judgment that
the sentence risks hunt-madness. Naming the danger does not make the sentence
just. It prevents later claim that the pack merely asked the offender to leave.

Forgiveness is shown by resumed proximity. The repaired member receives food
inside the circle, a watch partner, or permission to answer the pack call. A
speech of pardon without these acts leaves the exclusion intact.

## Anathema and Repair

Repair begins with restoring what the act removed: food, safe route, shelter,
warning, cub care, or trustworthy boundary. Submission gestures matter only
when the practical loss is answered.

Anathemas place the pack itself at risk: poisoning shared prey, betraying a
forest refuge, killing under declared guest scent, claiming a cub without
recognition, wasting a kill during hunger, using ritual intoxication to conceal
planned violence, deliberately driving kin into solitude, abandoning the sole
survivor of a dead pack, praising hunt-madness as sacred purity, and refusing
the pack's call while secretly drawing danger toward it.

No rite can restore a cleared forest in a night. The offender may owe seasons
of patrol, planting, sabotage, or relocation. A leader who accepts one bloody
ordeal instead is choosing spectacle over repair.

## Evidence at the Table

At the table, show Huntress worship through a downwind circuit worn into the
leaves, portions laid aside before the hunter eats, a boundary marked for refuge
rather than ownership, or a complex howl that stops the instant one supporting
note fails. A lone traveler who asks companions to keep talking, a sleeping cord
tied to another wrist, or a foster pack reciting one non-hunting memory at dusk
shows what the pack protects.

A useful religious conflict concerns who receives food, whether the Mist should
return, which forest remains refuge, whether a cub has been acknowledged, or
whether a hunt crossed the boundary named before pursuit. The arrival of a sole
survivor asks a harder question: which pack will pay the cost of keeping a
person from becoming quarry and hunter at once?
