# Dwarven Culture of the Ravenlands

Dwarven households turn work into standing. Apprenticeship fixes a place in
clan and workshop; contracts decide whose hand repairs a failure. This reference
covers kinship, inheritance, law, outsiders, the Stonegarden bazaar, and the
works reopened after the Blood Mist. Huge, sacred work, reincarnation, rites,
and anathema remain in [Dwarven Religion](religion-dwarves.md).

---

## Bazaar of the Four Clans

The bazaar fills every public side gallery and permitted garden clearing through
all five days. Work from all four clans is exhibited, sold, exchanged, or used
to bargain for future labor. Masters display cut stone, tools, armor,
instruments, mechanisms, books, glass, pigments, jewelry, and repaired objects
whose old faults remain visible. Apprentices stand beside the work and answer
questions their masters pretend not to hear.

Stalls bear the mark of maker, clan, source of material, and responsible guild.
Anonymous sale is permitted only for scrap. A purchaser who later discovers a
concealed fault can bring the marked object to the next gathering and place it
on the first day's failure table.

Much of the trade is conducted through commissions and reciprocal access rather
than immediate silver. A Canide grants timber and surface passage for a share
of Meromannian ore. A Crombe lends a lock pattern in exchange for inspection of
a Belderranian gate. An elven buyer offers living resin that no dwarven guild
can produce. The worth of the bargain depends on whether the other party will
still answer at the next gathering.

The Canides test scales, assign places, keep fire lanes open, and judge claims
of copied marks until the clan courts sit. Hosting gives them knowledge of who
is short of iron, whose apprentices are leaving, and which foreign ruler is
buying hinges for more doors than their hall possesses. That knowledge pays
part of the festival's cost.

## Religious and Political Fault Lines

Belderranians want the annal to confirm royal sequence. Meromannians use it to
keep lost holds ahead of new works. Canides turn maintenance of Stonegarden into
a claim that exposed work deserves precedence. Crombes use the guest peace to
argue that custody must come before expansion. Every clan can quote Huge and
the last gathering against the others.

The games sharpen the dispute because competence becomes visible outside rank.
A young mason can defeat a king's master before four clans, then receive a
commission that keeps the result alive. A ruler can answer by electing that
mason as champion, buying the allegiance displayed against them. Refusing to
recognize a fair result is also entered in the annal.

Foreign invitations divide the clans. One faction sees an elven witness as
proof that dwarven terms can bind the surface. Another sees an outsider walking
the Stone Gardens while dwarves remain barred from elder layers beneath them.
Orc attendance forces a harder account. A polite table cannot settle the debt
of enslavement, though it can decide who repairs the road before winter.

After the fifth day, the four working tools remain beside Huge's bench until
their crews reclaim them. The new slate volume goes below to the library. Its
unfinished duties are read aloud when the clans return.

## Birth, Naming, and Aptitude

A newborn receives a temporary work-name derived from a tool, material, flaw,
or circumstance noticed at birth. The name makes no prophecy. It gives the
household something honest to say before aptitude can be known.

The lasting name is often confirmed after the child completes a useful task
that another person relies upon. Carrying water without spilling, sorting
sound nails from cracked ones, keeping a lamp trimmed, or remembering a warning
can suffice. Wealthy families stage elaborate demonstrations. Working crews
remember the child who earned a name by noticing smoke behind a wall.

Disability is interpreted through placement rather than purity. Huge may have
reshaped a returning soul for another task, but no dwarf can know the task in
advance. This belief can protect a child from exposure and also burden the
child with demands to prove a hidden use. The humane ruling is practical: feed,
teach, observe, and let competence disclose itself. Cruel halls call neglect a
test from Huge.

## Apprenticeship and Adult Standing

An apprenticeship joins teacher, learner, household, and future users of the
craft. The learner owes labor and attention. The teacher owes food, tools,
correction, and the limits of dangerous methods. A master who teaches a cut but
conceals the load it cannot bear has given half a lesson and owns part of every
later failure.

At **Taking the Measure**, the apprentice measures one finished work, one
damaged work, and one planned work. The master asks where each will fail first.
The answer may be wrong. Refusal to answer is worse, because a craftsperson who
will not name uncertainty cannot be trusted with hidden structure.

Adult religious standing comes from accepting maintenance. Construction earns
praise; maintenance proves duty after praise has ended. A dwarf who makes a
bridge and will not return for the spring inspection remains spiritually junior
to the keeper who clears its drains each year.

## Household, Partnership, and Inheritance

Dwarven partnership rites join stores, tools, debts, and maintenance duties
before they join names. Each partner presents one sound object and names who
may use it. Shared tools are marked only after both households state what
happens if the partnership ends.

The marriage or household contract includes unfinished work. A surviving
partner may inherit a commission together with its promised delivery, spoiled
material, and compensation owed for delay. Romantic language does not erase
the account. Nor does the account exhaust affection. A worn tool kept after its
task is done may say more than a carved memorial.

Inheritance follows competence, clan claim, blood, adoption, debt, and the
needs of the work. These seldom point to one heir. A child may inherit a name
and no forge; an apprentice may inherit the forge and its obligations; a clan
may reclaim the ore; a widow may hold every customer agreement. The religious
hearing asks which division leaves the least false work behind.

## Law, Contract, and the Inspection of Power

Dwarven contracts name material, measure, delivery, maintenance, acceptable
fault, witness, and the condition that closes the work. Affection and honor may
support the promise. They do not replace the section that tells a widow who must
repair the wall.

At **Reading against the Grain**, a contract is read by someone expected to
oppose it: a rival guild, future maintainer, downstream household, or clan that
must cross the structure. The reader looks for a load assigned to a person who
never consented.

Kings command large works and can hide political desire inside Huge's plan. A
road to a royal mine may be preached as World Expansion while village drains
collapse. The crews maintaining both are the first religious opposition. Their
refusal stops matter from moving.

Guild secrecy protects dangerous craft and guild price. The two motives are
often mixed. At **The Sealed Limit**, a master withholding a method must disclose
what kind of failure the missing knowledge prevents. The listener receives no
technique, but can challenge a secret whose only named danger is competition.

A judgment is considered unfinished until an inspector can find it in the work.
Compensation paid without a repair mark settles property and leaves future hands
ignorant. A public sentence without supplied material is a speech beside a crack.

## Outsiders, Adoption, and Work under Huge

Huge made dwarves for the World Expansion. Whether another kin can enter that
calling remains disputed. Most halls let outsiders work, fewer let them witness
measure, and fewer still let them dedicate a foundation.

At **Taking a Foreign Tool**, a non-dwarf presents one completed work and one
failure. Dwarven judges inspect both without asking which god the worker named.
Acceptance grants standing within that craft and site, not a claim of dwarven
rebirth.

Human and ogre children raised in dwarven halls expose the limits of reincarnation
doctrine. A gifted child may be called a returning dwarf when the claim secures
labor or inheritance. The same hall may call an injured child foreign when care
becomes expensive.

Orc work carries the old history of service. A contract cannot be called free
when the orc's only alternative is hunger inside dwarven territory. Some guilds
require an orc witness and a stated release date. Others preserve slavery through
apprenticeship terms no one expects to end.

Elven makers approach form through living change rather than fixed measure. Joint
work requires agreement about what may grow. A dwarven support that allows no
root movement and an elven planting that lifts every course are both refusals to
share maintenance.

## The Blood Mist and the Interrupted Expansion

The Blood Mist isolated surface works from holds, quarries, guild courts, and
the crews named to maintain them. Canides carried duties no underground ruler
could inspect. Meromannians lost roads to already lost works. Crombe custody
hardened because every sealed gate could hide death after dusk.

At **Reopening the Account**, a returning clan does not begin with title. It
inspects who cleared drains, replaced stone, guarded tools, buried workers, and
fed the keepers during isolation. Earlier ownership is then read beside performed
maintenance.

Many useless foundations gained new uses. Humans built on them. Wolfkin marked
refuge beside them. Roots split and stabilized different courses. A dwarf may
call the alteration damage and still need the current inhabitants to explain why
the wall remains standing.

The Mist also interrupted reincarnation claims. Children born in isolated halls
were identified without distant singers or clan records. When the roads opened,
several adults discovered that another hold had assigned the same returning
ancestor to someone else. Political office could not pass to both.

At **Joining the Broken Shift**, crews from both sides of a reopened route place
their last separate repair marks beside the first joint one. The older plan is
read aloud. Any part no longer useful remains in the record rather than being
obeyed out of embarrassment.
