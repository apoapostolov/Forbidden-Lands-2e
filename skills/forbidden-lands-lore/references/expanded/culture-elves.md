# Elven Culture of the Ravenlands

Elven bodies can be remade while their rubies carry memory forward. Birth,
partnership, custody, teaching, and council therefore follow timescales that
shorter-lived kin cannot share. This reference covers those institutions and the
differences among elven traditions. Gods, ruby personhood, sacred materials,
restoration, religious councils, and anathema remain in [Elven
Religions](religion-elves.md).

---

## Birth, Growth, and the First Stable Form

Elven beginning does not resemble human birth closely enough for human kinship
terms to govern it without adjustment. A young ruby develops self, memory, and
the capacity to sustain flesh. Teachers supply form before the young elf can
maintain it alone.

The First Reflection recognizes a stable chosen body for one season. During
that season companions learn gait, resting habits, preferred touch, scent,
voice, and the way the young elf responds to pain. This knowledge later helps
distinguish restoration from imitation or possession.

Teachers can impose taste while claiming guidance. Stillelves favor forms that
fit long forest life. Redrunners value speed and inconspicuous travel. Unruly
teachers may praise dramatic difference. The young elf's choice is never made
outside culture. Changeable flesh still carries culture's influence.

At **Leaving the Teacher's Shape**, the young elf alters one feature the teacher
selected. The change may be small. Its purpose is to establish that instruction
did not create ownership.

## Partnership, Descent, and Custody

Elven partnership concerns shared memory, bodies made together, places tended,
and custody during disembodiment. A partner may hold another's ruby after bodily
death, which makes affection a form of physical power.

At **The Open Wrapping**, partners state who may unwrap, move, restore, or delay
restoration of each ruby. Alternate custodians are named because both partners
may lose bodies in the same event. The agreement is renewed after any long
separation or chosen change of form.

Descent follows teaching, shared ruby origin, created bodies, memory, and chosen
relation more than blood. An older elf may call several younger elves children
without claiming ownership. Humans who translate every such term as biological
descent produce false inheritance and scandal.

Love across kin confronts different time. A human or Elvenspring partner ages
while the elf can restore youth. Some elves change their bodies to age alongside
the partner. Others keep one form and become living evidence of the difference.
Neither practice guarantees fidelity.

## Elven Traditions

### Stillelves

Stillelves treat restraint, long observation, and withdrawal as protections
against decisions made at mortal speed. Their Ent elders join person to place
for centuries. The danger is allowing patience to become authority over every
shorter life nearby.

### The Unruly

Unruly elves favor bodily change, encounter, art, and refusal of Stillelf
restraint. Some read the Red Wanderer as a command to keep moving through form
and relation. Their rites protect experimentation, but spectacle can make
another person's body into material.

### Ents and the Golden Bough

Ents embody the Long Rooting. Golden Bough druids treat forest health across
generations as religious evidence. Rooted wisdom has a physical weakness:
someone else controls news, travel, and which mortal petition reaches the tree.

### Melders

Melders alter living shape through Clay's craft. They face the sharpest version
of the consent problem. A successful form can survive while the person who
received it rejects the purpose imposed upon them.

### Watchers

Watchers make vigilance an inherited office. They observe borders, ruins,
demons, and human expansion. A watch kept after its object changed becomes a
ritual without information. Watchers periodically state what evidence would end
the duty.

### Redrunners

Redrunners recover rubies, relics, and knowledge through movement. Their Red
Runs keep the kin connected. Militancy can turn recovery into confiscation when
the ruby's current custodian has a claim the runner refuses to hear.

## Council, Testimony, and Decisions That Take Too Long

Elven council begins when people with relevant memory, craft, custody, and
consequence agree to remain in one place. There is no fixed number. Gathering
the perfect witnesses can delay judgment until the question has changed.

At **The Short Hearing**, a mortal claimant may demand that the council decide
what must happen before the next harvest, birth, winter, or military movement.
The judgment is provisional but binding for that interval. Older elves dislike
the procedure because urgency can harden error. Elvenspring defend it because
delay can harden power more quietly.

Memory testimony is given with the body in which the event was experienced when
possible. An elf restored in another form names the gap. Smell, height, hearing,
and bodily danger change what a witness noticed. The memory remains evidence,
with edges set by the changed senses.

At **The Contrary Walk**, a council visits the place with a guide who opposes
the expected judgment. The guide chooses the route and what evidence receives
attention. Councillors need not agree afterward. Refusal to walk is entered as
part of the decision.

An elf can abstain for decades. Property, custody, and vulnerable bodies cannot.
When no consensus forms, the people maintaining the disputed thing gain authority
by continued work. Philosophical patience may transfer a shrine, forest, or ruby
chamber to its keepers without any vote.

## Teaching, Adoption, and Outsiders inside Elven Practice

Elven teaching is long, intimate, and easy to confuse with ownership. A teacher
may remember every form a student wore and still fail to notice that the student
has outgrown the relation.

At **Breaking the Lesson**, the student rejects one instruction before witnesses
and demonstrates the alternative. The teacher must name the danger rather than
invoke age. Failure may prove the old method sound. It may also prove that
permission to disagree was only ceremonial.

Humans, orcs, dwarves, and wolfkin can enter particular practices without
becoming elven. A human may keep a survey route. An orc may witness the return
of a ruby taken from an elder fort. A dwarf may build a chamber whose stone does
not claim ownership of what it holds. These relations remain political.

Adoption by an elf provides teaching, shelter, and a witness who may outlive
every earlier relation. It can also freeze the adopted person's childhood. At
**Naming the Mortal Measure**, the adoptee states which kin's law governs adult
standing, partnership, property, and burial. The elven teacher cannot postpone
those transitions by saying the student remains young.

Knowledge given to outsiders is judged by capacity and consequence. Belial's
story teaches dwarves that disclosure itself may betray. Elves more often ask
whether the recipient can care for what follows. Both traditions are tempted to
use responsibility as a reason never to share power.
