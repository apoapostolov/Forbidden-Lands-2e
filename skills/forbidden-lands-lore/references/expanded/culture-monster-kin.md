# Monster-Kin Cultures of the Ravenlands

Saurians, ogres, whiners, and Misgrown share no ancestry or social order.
They share poor documentation by outsiders. This reference keeps observable
household, work, trade, kinship, and authority apart from hunter claim and
scholarly analogy. Sacred beings, death doctrine, conversion, and the limits of
religious knowledge remain in [Monster Beliefs](religion-monster-beliefs.md).

---

## Saurian Culture

### Authority and the Long Harangue

Authority follows the matter. Dam keepers speak on water and winter shelter.
Feast keepers judge fermentation. Crocodile handlers control approach to their
animals. Trade speakers remember prices, insults, and the exact protection
declared at each platform.

Saurian speech sounds rambling to outsiders because a public statement locates
itself in water and season. It names witnesses, prior use, and exceptions before
the demand. Cutting the account short can change a temporary passage into a
permanent right.

At **The Repeated Bank**, another speaker repeats a judgment from a different
place on the platform. The second voice changes any word that depends on where
the first speaker stood. If the ruling no longer works from both banks, it is a
claim of position rather than common water.

No central priest rules the marshes. A keeper may gain influence by knowing
several channels and presences. That knowledge remains suspect when used to
command a family whose winter pond the keeper has never entered.

### Household, Eggs, and Young

Saurian households form around warm water and stored fat. Feeding ponds and
platforms require labor to keep channels open. Human ideas of one house under one
roof fit poorly. Several sleeping hollows may share one dam and one winter duty.

At **Warming the Clutch**, adults bring rotting plant matter, mud, and watched
heat to the egg place. Each contribution is pressed into a separate section.
Later failure can be traced without turning every lost egg into a divine sign.

Young are taught the difference between protected water and prey water before
they can understand a trade speech. A marked post is touched, smelled, and
circled from the safe side. Crossing it in play is corrected at once. Outsiders
who move the marker for convenience teach a lethal lesson.

Recognition follows feeding and protection more strongly than proven descent.
An adult who guarded a clutch through flood may hold claims equal to a parent.
Stealing an egg attacks a person and the whole labor that maintained its heat.

Cold kills young quickly. A ritual cannot substitute for dry cover, stored food,
and adults carrying heat between water and nest. Saurian religion places these
acts before any interpretation of unusual markings on the shell.

### Food, Fermentation, and Distribution

Saurians cannot chew. Bodies and roots must be torn small or softened; so must
fish. Fermentation cooks by time and water while the household guards the food
through its decay. A feast pond is a kitchen whose walls move.

At **Opening the Pond**, the feast keeper first tests smell and surface. Gas and
insect life reveal the deeper condition; several pieces are tested for firmness.
A portion is removed for the sick and young before the strongest enter.
Outsiders invited to watch are placed upwind and told exactly where not to
step.

Waste carries a different meaning in a marsh. Bone, hide, offal, and spoiled
material feed water or become tools. The offense lies in concentrating rot where
it poisons a sleeping hollow, trade platform, or future clutch.

Eating speaking kin is possible under prey law. A trader under platform peace is
not prey. A trespasser beyond the marked water may be. Saurian communities differ
over captives who understand neither marker nor warning; some escort them out,
others say ignorance did not make the water less occupied.

Crocodile meat is divided first among handlers and animals that shared its work.
Famine may end the animal's protected status. Killing it early because a hunter
prefers the flesh turns a communal reserve into private appetite.

### Trade Peace and Metal

Trade platforms are built above water no party may hunt across while peace holds.
Posts, reed cuts, smell marks, and spoken terms define the protected approach.
The platform is inspected after every flood.

At **Laying down the Eel Spear**, traders place weapons with points toward their
own water. A knife for sale remains wrapped until a saurian handler opens it.
Drawing a second weapon to demonstrate quality can empty the platform before an
apology is translated.

Metal is valued for edge and durability, not coinage. A bent spearhead may be
worth more than silver because it can be ground into hooks. Gold unable to cut,
fasten, or weight a net has little claim on food.

Purple shell, fish, frog, and bread root leave the marsh through specific
families. Payment to the platform speaker does not settle the harvester's claim.
Unscrupulous traders exploit the difference, then call the later demand double
payment.

Violence under peace requires repair beyond blood-price. The platform must be
cleaned, damaged posts replaced, every party warned, and a new speaker found if
the former one can no longer guarantee approach. Until then, trade moves to a
worse bank and everyone pays.

## Ogre Culture

### Marks, Weight, and the Public Body

Brands and stretched flesh are records that cannot be left at home. The person
carrying one may have chosen pain, accepted punishment, survived capture, or been
marked while unable to refuse.

At **Naming the Iron**, the wearer explains a new weight before it is attached.
Witnesses lift it and hear the claim. A status weight that another person cannot
verify may still be worn, but it earns no place in common distribution.

Large ears and nose can show endurance and standing. Heavy ornament also tears
skin, impedes work, and catches in a fight. Older ogres whose flesh no longer
supports great weights may pass them to a chosen companion. Taking them before
the wearer releases the claim is stripping a living history.

Punishment brands are contested. Some groups require the offender to carry the
mark openly. Others hold that a scar imposed by an enemy records the enemy's act,
not the captive's guilt. Outsiders buying or praising marked skin usually cannot
tell the difference.

An ogre may cut away a brand, losing flesh and the story attached to it. Former
companions can treat the act as release, confession, or theft from witnesses who
shared the event.

### Chosen Kin, Captivity, and Possessive Care

Ogres have little broad kin loyalty. A chosen relation can therefore bear more
weight than ancestry. The companion is fed first, defended fiercely, and included
in thunder rites. They may also be watched, marked, or injured to prevent escape.

At **Sharing the Roof Stone**, an ogre and chosen companion each move one heavy
object into the other's sleeping place. The exchange makes mutual access visible.
If only the smaller companion carries weight, adoption has become service.

Some ogres cut a captive's tendons and call the resulting dependence proof of
kinship. Care after deliberate injury does not undo the act. It creates a closed
world in which the captor can point to every meal as evidence of love.

The Last Noise offers a rare bounded departure. The leaving person strikes an
object and may walk while the sound remains audible. A clever companion chooses
a bell, hollow cliff, or long metal sheet. An ogre who muffles the sound after
agreeing to the rite has attacked the witness as well as the departing person.

Children of mixed human and dwarven descent may be raised in halls rather than
among ogres. An ogre who later meets such kin can claim recognition that the
other person does not share. Body alone does not create chosen relation, though
many ogres find this refusal incomprehensible.

### Food, Gift, and Solitary Property

An ogre household may contain one person and still keep strict claims. The club,
drinking vessel, storm object, stored meat, and adopted person's bedding occupy
known places. Moving them can be challenge or invasion.

At **The Heavy Gift**, a giver offers something the recipient must physically
carry away. The weight proves no moral truth. It shows whether the giver
considered the receiver's body and journey. A millstone given to a human guest
may be mockery disguised as generosity.

Feasts happen when hunting, theft, trade, or thunder draws several ogres together.
Food is divided by contribution, challenge, affection, and the loud demand of
whoever reaches first. A chosen dependent may receive more than a visiting ogre,
which outsiders mistake for kindness until they see what departure costs.

Beer is wager, payment, and storm fuel. An intoxicated promise may still bind if
the others changed behavior around it. Some groups require the promise repeated
after sleep. Others consider sober denial cowardice.

## Whiner Culture

### Authority, Queens, and the Limits of Outsider Analogy

Druids report egg-laying queens and colony structure. The observation may be
accurate. “Queen” may still import human rule into a body whose work concerns
birth rather than command.

Authority can be observed in who receives food, which call changes collective
movement, who decides a captive exchange, and whose body is guarded during
flight. Outsiders should record these acts before naming monarch, priest, or
brood mother.

At **The Many Answer**, a warning passes through walls by bite, scrape, thump,
and voice. No individual needs the whole message. A hunter who captures the
first caller has not silenced the hollow.

Some large individuals may lay eggs while others feed, tend, carry, or defend.
This division can create sacred dependence and coercion. Protecting a
reproductive body does not prove that body rules or consents to every use made
of it.

Whiners questioned under threat may confirm whatever colony tale keeps captors
away from the real chamber. Contradictory accounts are evidence of danger, not
inferior reason.

### Flesh, Healing, and the Harvesting Violation

Whiner flesh heals and fattens other bodies. This property makes every whiner a
walking resource in the eyes of hunters, physicians, orc buyers, and wounded
adventurers. Religious life forms under the knowledge that a stranger's need can
become a justification for dismemberment.

Within a hollow, small voluntary cuttings may treat kin. If so, the practice
does not authorize outsiders to harvest. Consent inside a community of shared
risk cannot be purchased from a cage keeper.

At **Counting the Returned Flesh**, a rescued captive is examined without blame.
Missing tissue, scars, weight, infection, and the names or smells of other cages
are recorded. The first work is food and wound care. Questions wait until the
person can survive the answers.

Weapons used by harvesters may be bitten, fouled, broken, or built into latrines.
The treatment strips them of healing authority. A surgeon's clean knife and a
butcher's clean knife can be the same object in different hands.

Using flesh cut from a corpse remains disputed. One hollow may permit it if the
dead person released the body. Another may see every use by outsiders as keeping
the harvest alive. A trader who claims corpse source must still explain custody.

### Gold, Growth, and Dangerous Wealth

Gold placed beneath whiner skin grows heavier. Outsiders read the body as mine,
mint, or miracle. Captors insert gold and wait while the person bears pain,
infection, confinement, and the risk of being cut again.

Within a hollow, growth-gold could supply ransom, trade, or tools impossible to
make from stone. Its use would also create a class of bodies expected to carry
communal wealth. Treat the question as contested rather than assuming rejection
or easy acceptance.

At **Biting the Price**, whiners test a coin offered in exchange for a captive.
They may return the marked gold with the rescued person. The bite identifies the
payment if hunters later claim no bargain occurred.

Private insertion of communal growth-gold is a likely anathema because it makes
shared danger into hidden wealth. Forced insertion by hollow leaders would be
the same violation with a religious speech around it.

Gold that has grown inside a dead whiner carries a body claim. Melting it can
erase the marks by which the hollow might prove where it came from. Traders who
pay less for bitten gold profit from that erasure.

### Captivity, Recovery, and War

Every known cage creates a duty. Immediate attack may kill the captives. Delay
may cost more flesh. Whiner war therefore turns on information, exchange, traps,
and the price a hollow can force hunters to pay without exposing its chambers.

At **Holding the Empty Place**, the rescued person's sleeping hollow remains
open during captivity. Food is placed there and then reassigned. The act keeps
the missing person inside distribution without pretending they have eaten.

Captive exchange can include kidnapped travelers who had no part in harvesting.
Whiners may torture or trade them because larger kin recognize pain only when it
reaches their own people. The logic is understandable and does not make the
victim responsible.

A traveler who cuts cages open and leaves cannot claim the duty complete. Weak,
wounded whiners need food, shade, a route, and protection from the hunters who
own the land around the cage. Rescue without removal can make punishment arrive
sooner.

Whiners may maintain people outside the hollow who trade warnings, medicine, or
metal. Such intermediaries are targets for both harvesters and whiners who regard
any contact as betrayal.

## Misgrown Culture

### Naming, Display, and the Right to Cover

Rust doctrine gives altered features titles: sign, blessing, warning, use,
punishment, proof. The named person may use a household word instead. A horn can
be “left brow,” a scaled hand “the cold one,” and a second mouth “the eater.” The
plain name returns daily ownership to people who wash, feed, bind, or live with
the body.

At **The Chosen Showing**, a Misgrown person states which features may be seen,
touched, described, or covered in a particular setting. Healers can request
access. Priests can demand it. Only the person can turn either act into ordinary
consent when refusal is possible.

Church processions display marked bodies as proof of divine selection. The same
feature is hidden when it shows failed control, pain, or an origin the order
cannot explain. A person praised in armor may be locked away when infection
reveals the cost beneath it.

Covering can protect from stare, cold, sun, work injury, or doctrinal claim. It
can also worsen heat and wounds. Tailors, leatherworkers, and healers who know
altered bodies become important religious allies without holding priesthood.

### Hidden Fellowships and Mutual Care

Misgrown fellowships form through medicine, safe sleep, clothing, food that fits
an altered mouth, and warnings about recruiters. Shared form is not required.
The person with bone growth may have little bodily knowledge useful to someone
whose skin weeps resin.

At **Keeping the Night Body**, two people sleep in shifts when an alteration
changes during rest, draws predators, speaks, or acts without full waking control.
The watcher records what occurred and wakes the sleeper before making a religious
judgment.

Scarce care gives the carer power. The only person willing to clean a hidden
wound may demand secrecy or loyalty. They may demand sex, coin, or obedience.
Fellowship law treats such demands as taking payment from a door no one else
will open.

Medicines are shared with origin attached. Rust remedy and druidic salve are not
equivalent merely because both help. Whiner flesh, demonic secretion, and
ordinary boiled cloth also remain distinct. A recipient needs enough truth to
choose the danger.

Marks can make ordinary work impossible and other work uniquely easy. A second
arm may lift, a claw may cut, night eyes may guide. Fellowship teachers resist
the church's claim that usefulness proves the alteration was given for that
labor.

### Birth, Descent, and Children under Suspicion

An altered parent does not know what a child will inherit. Rust priests use the
uncertainty to claim supervision of pairing, pregnancy, birth, and the newborn.
Villagers may demand separation before any sign appears.

At **Naming before the Mark**, trusted households give the infant an ordinary
name before priest or examiner records the body. The sequence establishes a
person whom later alteration affects. It prevents the first visible difference
from becoming the whole name.

Parents may hide harmless traits to keep a child outside church custody. Hiding
a dangerous need can kill the child or caregiver. They make that decision under
pressure from institutions that punish either disclosure or concealment.

Some Misgrown cannot bear children or reproduce through unfamiliar processes.
Rust doctrine may call infertility an offering and seize fertile people for
breeding. Hidden fellowships build kin through adoption, apprenticeship, shared
escape, and long care.

A child whose body resembles a known demon can become evidence in another
person's trial. The resemblance proves no allegiance. It does reveal which
authorities have studied the demon closely enough to recognize it.

### Work, Service, and the Theology of Use

Rust teaches that iron and wood receive form through proper force. Misgrown
bodies are assigned tasks that fit doctrine and anatomy: guard, laborer, tracker,
executioner, vessel, omen, breeder. Refusal is described as material resisting
its maker.

At **Refusing the Use**, a fellowship records one task the person will not
perform merely because their body can. The refusal may cost food or shelter.
Another member witnesses it so later coercion cannot be rewritten as vocation.

Settlements outside Rust rule also exploit altered capacity. A village that
hides a Misgrown laborer from priests may expect unpaid night work in exchange.
Protection becomes ownership by smaller steps.

Wages and tools need adaptation. A clawed hand may destroy a standard grip. A
large body needs more food. Unusual sight can make bright work painful. Paying
the same coin for unequal cost is often praised as treating everyone alike.

Some people embrace a task tied to form and gain pride, office, and skill. The
wrong lies in making capacity into compulsory purpose, not in allowing the body
to become part of chosen craft.

### Escape, Conversion, and Returning to a Settlement

Leaving Rust service requires food, clothing, medicine, route, and a place whose
people can withstand church inquiry. Removing armor may expose tissue never
allowed to bear weather. The first free night can be physically more dangerous
than the last guarded one.

At **Taking Back the Name**, the escapee chooses which church name, rank, scar,
or title companions may continue to use. Witnesses record coercive acts without
making confession the price of shelter.

Conversion to another faith changes who may touch, bury, marry, employ, and
interpret the body. It cannot make the church forget a useful servant. A Raven
oath or Clay rite needs defenders able to enforce the new jurisdiction.

Return to a home settlement may fail even when relatives remain. The person who
left had one body and household role. The returning body may need space, food,
care, or secrecy the family cannot supply. Affection does not widen a doorway.

Some return as armed church agents and later defect. Their victims may refuse
fellowship. Rescue from the institution does not settle harms committed within
it.
