# Culture of the Rust Brothers

The Rust Brothers turn rank, bodily alteration, record keeping, and public
punishment into a government that can travel. This reference covers recruitment,
advancement, household entry, inquisition, control of demonic knowledge,
selective terror, and the habits trained inside the order. Doctrine, worship,
rituals, sin, and anathema remain in [Religion of the Rust
Brothers](religion-rust-brothers.md).

The order described here is evil without qualification. Its members may speak
truthfully about demons. They may protect a road or spare a village. Each useful
act becomes part of a system that denies ordinary people knowledge. It claims
judgment for the initiated and removes the right to refuse. Understanding that
system should make the Rust Brothers more disturbing, never secretly innocent.

**Content note:** This document discusses torture and execution. It also covers
bodily alteration, child recruitment, persecution, and coercive control. Render
these subjects through consequence and witness. Graphic detail is seldom needed.

## The Shape of the Fear

### They Announce Themselves Before They Arrive

A Rust procession is heard first. Mail shifts under plate. Iron pendants knock
against breastbones. A censer chain drags when its bearer wants it to drag. The
sound gives a village time to hide a book, send a child into the reeds, or decide
which household will answer the gate.

This warning is deliberate. Sudden raiders catch people unprepared. Rulers want
preparation directed toward submission. By the time the Iron Guard reaches the
palisade, the headwoman has put on her good cloak, the tribute sacks stand in
the yard, and every absence has become visible.

The Brothers therefore occupy time before they occupy space. A report that they
were seen at a ford two days away changes sleep, work, and speech. Hidden grain
is moved at night. Old quarrels are reopened because someone must have informed.
A Raven charm that hung safely for twenty years is burned by its owner.

### Their Restraint Is Administrative

Rust Brothers do not normally destroy the settlements they mean to govern. A
burned village pays no grain, shelters no garrison, produces no recruits, and
cannot be made to witness the next correction. Their preferred cruelty leaves
the mill working and the households afraid.

They select a person, a family, or a narrow circle around an offense. The rest
are ordered to watch. Punishment teaches the price of disobedience while survival
teaches that obedience still has a price worth paying. This is more durable than
indiscriminate slaughter because every survivor is made to calculate.

Their mercy is consequently another instrument. A prior may spare five accused
people after executing one, then require the five to thank Rust for discernment.
The spared learn that their lives came from the same hand that chose the dead.

### The Terror Is Uneven on Purpose

Predictable taxation produces resentment. Completely random killing produces
flight or revolt. Rust rule mixes a known demand with uncertain accusation.
Villagers know how much grain is owed, but they cannot know whether an old song,
a strange birthmark, or a missing traveler will be named heresy this season.

The uncertainty makes people inspect one another. The order needs fewer guards
when households silence their own kin. A settlement under mature Rust rule may
look orderly from the road. The gate is repaired, the tribute weighed, and no
one speaks until the elder has spoken.

> **The empty place:** At a western village feast, seven bowls were laid for
> eight adults. When a traveler asked about the missing bowl, the host's wife
> pressed her thumb into the table until the nail whitened. Her brother had
> spoken against the human tithe in spring. The Rust Brothers took only him.
> His field was divided among the households that testified.

## The Marked and the Chosen

### Unusual Bodies Are Recruited as Evidence

The church seeks bodies it can interpret as signs. Its recruiters record:

- unusual height or a gaunt face
- fused fingers or an extra digit
- extensive birthmarks or asymmetry
- unfamiliar eyes
- visible misgrowth

Some recruits were born this way. Others bear scars from disease or hunger.
Some are Misgrown or have been altered through the church's alliance with
demonic powers.

Rust teaching erases these distinctions. Every marked body is said to have been
found first by the enemy and claimed afterward by Rust. The person is offered
status at the price of agreeing that their body belongs to the doctrine.

The bargain cuts twice. A stigmatized person receives food, weapons, and rank,
then learns to enforce the system that made ordinary life unsafe for them. The
recruit is taught that villagers would have
mocked, exposed, or killed them without the church. Sometimes this is true.
The church makes sure it remains true.

### The Order Cultivates Strangeness

Visible alteration is not required. Ordinary recruits learn stillness and
restricted speech. They hold eye contact and refuse to acknowledge discomfort.
Food may be limited before public rites so faces appear hollow. Hair is shaved
to expose scars. Masks conceal some differences and imply worse ones.

The order manufactures a family resemblance without requiring one kind of body.
Villagers remember that the Brothers stood too straight, blinked too little,
and did not warm their hands at the fire. Training produces these behaviors.
Their effect does not depend on supernatural cause.

Avoid calling every Rust Brother a psychopath. The order selects for callousness
and disciplines away visible hesitation. Some members enjoy suffering. Others
perform it as work, fear their superiors, or have learned to place doctrine
between the hand and what the hand does. Administrative cruelty can be colder
than frenzy.

### Born Under Signs

Settlements say a true Rust Brother is known at birth. The signs vary: rust in
the wash water, iron-colored afterbirth, a tooth already cut, no cry until a
blade is laid beside the child. Such stories allow any unusual birth to be
claimed later as prophecy.

The church seldom corrects these accounts. A prior who recruits an adolescent
may tell the household that Rust marked the child before the parents knew what
they were looking at. Refusal then becomes resistance to a divine claim rather
than a family decision.

Children marked by rumor may be treated with fearful courtesy, driven from
games, watched around animals, or reported to a priory. The order profits even
when it never recruits them. The village has learned to police difference in
Rust's name.

### Marriage in the Mist

A common belief holds that senior Brothers and Sisters of Heme enter the Mist
to marry demons. No village agrees on what returns. Some say the spouse walks
unseen at the priest's left side. Others say the marriage enters the body as an
extra eye, a new limb, or a voice heard during sleep.

The church uses the rumor without settling it. Marriage makes its intimacy with
demons sound lawful and sacramental. The enemy becomes an affinal power: feared,
consulted, and bound by terms that common people are forbidden to know.

The name of marriage lets a church that claims to defend humanity accept demonic
alteration among its highest ranks. The altered priest is presented as
one who entered danger on behalf of lesser people. The actual relationship is
closer to collaboration, acquisition, and dependence.

## The Church and Its Ranks

### One Church, Several Bodies

The Rust and Heme Church joins four institutions without making them one: a
priesthood, an armed order, a tribute system, and a chain of guarded knowledge.
A Rust Brother may command a village rite but have no authority over an Iron
Guard patrol. A Sister of Heme may overrule both during a difficult birth, then
be made to stand behind them at the public feast.

Rank answers who may teach, judge, and receive secrets. Office answers what work
the person performs today. Command answers who can use force. The Rust Lord
keeps the three chains joined by appointing superiors, moving guards between
priories, and reserving certain knowledge to Alderstone.

The Blood Mist weakened uniform administration. Titles survived on bodies and
objects. Rank could be read from the number of iron links, the color of a hem,
a mask, or the right to hold a censer by its inner chain. Pronunciation and
local duties vary. A rank recognized at Vond may be called by another name in a
forest priory, but the iron signs are read the same way.

### The Vowed Ranks

Only **Ferrale** and **Rust Lord** are established manuscript titles. The lower
ranks below are a setting-facing structure for cult design. A remote priory may
combine, rename, or omit one without changing the order's general shape.

| Rank | Standing and Work | Visible Sign | Knowledge Allowed |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Offered** | A postulant surrendered by kin, sentenced into service, purchased from debt, or admitted by petition. Performs kitchen, stable, cleaning, and carrying work under watch. | Undyed cloth and an unclosed iron ring on a cord. | Household prayers, obedience formulas, names of local superiors. |
| **Scaled** | An initiated novice. Keeps the shrine, accompanies collections, tends censers, copies tallies, and learns to question without offering an opinion. | One closed rusty link at the throat; shaved crown or exposed brow. | Public doctrine, local offenses, tribute measures, common signs of demon taint. |
| **Brother or Sister** | A full member of the order. May preach, witness oaths, conduct ordinary rites, collect tribute, and hold a church office. | Rust-brown mantle over red or yellow cloth; iron sigil worn against the skin. | Priory rules, names of informants within the assigned district, approved spells and rites. |
| **Tempered** | A senior priest trusted to judge lesser clergy, lead inquiries, and carry sealed orders between houses. Many command a circuit of villages. | Three joined links fixed to the mantle; a narrow iron mask during judgment. | Regional reports, restricted handling procedures, records of prior sentences and confiscations. |
| **Ferrale** | High priest and keeper of an inner authority. A Ferrale may ordain, appoint priors, authorize melding, order a major sacrifice, and judge heresy among the ordained. Many are Misgrown. | A closed mask or displayed alteration framed in iron; chain worn across the chest rather than at the throat. | Inner doctrine, selected demonic studies, correspondence with the highest church authorities. |
| **Rust Lord** | Supreme head of the order. Kartorda holds the title in 1165 AS. The Rust Lord commands the Iron Guard and decides which Ferrales receive the church's most dangerous knowledge. | No fixed sign limits the office. The step from Offered to Scaled is common. Full ordination is scarce because a
Brother draws food, iron, and protection from the priory for life. A poor house
may keep useful novices at the second rank for years. Their labor is cheaper and
their deaths require less explanation.

Ferrale is both rank and claim. A senior priest can be addressed as Ferrale by
one district and denied the title by another until Alderstone confirms it. Such
disputes are settled through possession of seals, escorts, archives, or the
body of the rival. Theology follows the available evidence.

### Rank Marks Can Be Taken Away

Demotion is public. A link is opened with cold chisels, never cut cleanly. A
mantle is shortened at the hem. The iron sigil may be turned against the skin
so its points rub during work. The former rank remains visible as damage.

A stripped Brother is seldom returned to ordinary life. They know names,
routes, and household weaknesses. The order assigns them to a labor house,
an Iron Guard baggage train, or a duty from which return is unlikely.

The church sometimes displays a demoted cleric during village collections. The
lesson is directed inward. Villagers see that even the hand holding the chain
can be placed beneath it.

### Offices of the Priory

Offices belong to work rather than holiness. One person may hold two in a small
house. Large priories separate them so that no subordinate controls grain,
records, and armed access at once.

| Office | Work and Authority |
| --- | --- |
| **Shrine-Keeper** | Maintains idols, coals, cloth, offerings, and the list of household observances. Often a Scaled novice or trusted lay adherent. |
| **Measure-Bearer** | Keeps weights, tribute tallies, arrears, and grants of confiscated property. Cannot forgive a debt without a superior witness. |
| **Censer-Bearer** | Carries the great censer in procession, punishment, and defense. The office requires strength and drilled spacing, not priestly rank. |
| **Purger** | Examines restless dead, tainted sites, and accused bodies. Decides what can be burned locally and what must be removed. |
| **Examiner** | Questions witnesses, seizes texts, compares reports, and prepares sealed summaries for higher ranks. An examiner knows how to recognize information they are forbidden to understand fully. |
| **Prior** | Governs one religious house and its attached villages. Assigns stores, approves local sentences, and negotiates with Iron Guard commanders. |
| **Corpse Herder** | Handles dangerous dead, altered beasts, and unstable servants with chains, poles, cages, and trained routines. The work is technical and kept apart from public worship. |

The Measure-Bearer and Examiner often become enemies. The first sees people as
debts and productive households. The second sees the same people as sources and
concealed associations. A household useful to one office may be expendable to
the other.

The prior is expected to reconcile the dispute. A weak prior hides behind a
Ferrale's old instruction. A capable one chooses which record will reach the
Ferrale.

### The Iron Guard

The Iron Guard answers directly to the Rust Lord, though distance forces local
commanders to bargain with priories for fodder, smithing, shelter, and guides.
Its ranks are military and should remain plain: guard, chain-leader, captain,
and warden. A warden holds a fortress, a road, or a large punitive command.

An ordinary priest cannot command a Guard knight merely by invoking Rust. The
priest can deny rites, report disobedience, or withhold local stores. The knight
can refuse an unsafe order and wait for written authority. Villagers caught
between them learn quickly which dispute is real and which is performed to
extract another payment.

Iron Guard induction is separate from priestly ordination. A knight swears to
the Rust Lord, the order, and the protection of humankind as the church defines
it. The oath makes obedience to command a religious duty while leaving ordinary
people outside the protected category whenever the church names them corrupt.

Chain-leaders command the smallest useful unit: enough guards to hold a gate,
separate a household, and protect a censer-bearer. Captains coordinate several
such units. Wardens can authorize punitive movement within their charge, but a
major sacrifice or clerical execution still requires priestly judgment.

### The Sisters of Heme

The Sisters use the same formal ladder from Offered to Ferrale, but their daily
authority follows different routes. They attend births, inspect disease, oversee
animal culling, supervise marriage exchanges, and know which households depend
on hidden care. This gives them information the public priesthood cannot gather
from tribute lists.

The present church is patriarchal and makes the Sisters less visible at temples.
It cannot erase Ferrale Teramalda from its own history. Senior men resolve the
contradiction by calling her a singular vessel chosen before ordinary rules.
Senior Sisters cite her whenever a local Brother attempts to deny powers that
their house has exercised for generations.

Some Sisters accept the bargain. Private household authority can be broader
than a place in procession. Others seek Ferrale rank, patronize marked girls,
or accumulate birth and illness records until a prior cannot govern without
them.

Heme offices have local names such as hunt-mother, blood-keeper, or grove-wife.
These are offices, not universal ranks. A hunt-mother may be a full Sister with
great local power and no authority outside her valley.

## Formation and Advancement

### How a Recruit Enters

Petition, family offering, punishment, and seizure all bring recruits to the
order. Voluntary petitioners often come hungry or landless. Some bear a stigma.
Others want authority. Family offerings settle debts or seek protection for a
child whose body has drawn dangerous attention. A sentence into service turns
punishment into labor.

The recruit's motive is recorded by the superior who benefits from it. A hungry
adolescent becomes "called by Heme." A captured bookkeeper becomes "ore found
beneath false writing." The new account belongs to the church and follows the
recruit longer than the old name.

Every recruit needs a sponsor. The sponsor supplies clothing and answers for
early failures. In return, the recruit becomes a dependent and informant, then
later an ally within the priory. Rank is therefore never only
personal merit. It is a chain of people who expect repayment.

### The Time of Offering

An Offered recruit sleeps near the work they are assigned. Stable workers sleep
above animals. Kitchen workers sleep beside the grain door. The arrangement
makes theft difficult and teaches that rest is another duty allotted by rank.

The unclosed iron ring at the recruit's throat can be removed without breaking
it. This means the church has not yet decided the person is worth keeping. A
superior may send the Offered home in disgrace, transfer them with a debt note,
or close the ring and claim them permanently.

Instruction begins with measures, names, and response formulas. The Offered
learns how much grain fills the priory scoop, which bell calls each rank, and
who may open which door. Doctrine enters through work before anyone explains
the gods.

### Closing the First Link

Initiation as Scaled is called **the Closing of the Link**. The sponsor places
an open iron ring around a stone mandrel. The recruit names one attachment the
church may use: household, village, former faith, lover, teacher, or hidden
shame. A smith or censer-bearer closes the ring with a hammer while the sponsor
repeats the name.

The ring is then hung at the throat. Rust scraped from a church idol is pressed
into the recruit's damp palms. The hands remain open until the rite ends. Any
attempt to wipe them clean is refusal.

The named attachment becomes a handle. A family may be favored to reward
obedience, inspected to test loyalty, or punished if the novice flees. The rite
does not sever the old bond. It gives the church a handle on it.

### What Novices Learn

Scaled novices memorize doctrine through paired statements and physical work.
They clean the inner face of armor while preserving rust outside. They separate
sound grain from rot under a Sister's eye. They copy a tribute mark without
being told the name behind it.

They also learn controlled ignorance. A novice must recognize a sealed vessel,
an unauthorized diagram, and the residue of a forbidden rite. They must summon
a superior without opening, reading, or tasting what they found. Curiosity is
treated as theft from rank.

Questioning practice begins with ordinary discrepancies. A novice counts bowls,
boots, animals, and bedding. Only after they can describe a household without
accusation are they taught which differences can be made into charges.

### The Old Mist-Walk

From the tenth century until 1160 AS, entry into full Brotherhood was proved by
walking into the Blood Mist at night while singing and swinging a rusty censer.
Some candidates returned. Many did not. Survival was declared judgment by Heme,
though the church never understood why belief and familiarity protected some
travelers.

The rite gave the order a selection method no village could imitate. It also
made every full Brother a person who had passed beyond the settlement's nightly
boundary and returned. Rank entered the room before the priest spoke.

The lifting of the Mist removed this proof. Older clergy call themselves the
**Mist-Tempered** and may deny equal standing to anyone ordained after 1160.
Their bodies are aging, so the distinction grows more valuable each year.

### Ordination after the Mist

No replacement has equal authority. Most houses combine an **Empty Walk** with
a **House of Red Breath**. The candidate crosses unfamiliar ground alone after
dark, then spends the next night inside a closed room filled with censer smoke,
heat, recited accusations, and the sound of chains moving beyond the wall.

The candidate returns carrying an object placed at the far marker and repeats
whatever the superior told them never to reveal. Success proves obedience and
endurance. It cannot reproduce the old danger, which is why the ceremony grows
harsher whenever its legitimacy is questioned.

Some Ferrales seek surviving pockets of Blood Mist or expose candidates to
captured demonic vapors. Others reject such rites as wasteful tampering. A new
Brother's ordination therefore reveals a patron's theology and appetite for
risk.

Full ordination ends with the **Bearing of the Censer**. The candidate carries
a lit censer around the assembled house, keeping the chain taut while each
member adds a pinch of material: resin from the shrine, chaff from tribute,
hair from a punished heretic, or shavings from maintained armor. The candidate
then receives the iron sigil against bare skin.

### Tempering and Promotion

A Brother becomes Tempered through appointment. Age alone grants nothing. The
candidate must complete four tests:

1. Complete a circuit of villages.
2. Reconcile two sets of records.
3. Lead a rite.
4. Deliver a sealed object without violating its handling instructions.

One test concerns doctrine. The others show whether the church can trust the
person with routes and discrepancies.

Promotion requires a superior's sponsorship and a vacancy or new source of
income. A priory cannot create another senior mouth without another tithe,
endowment, confiscated field, or useful office. Expansion of rank follows
expansion of extraction.

The public test is called **Taking the Weight**. Iron pieces representing the
candidate's future duties are hung from a yoke: keys, tally weights, a short
chain, and the sealed case of an examiner. The candidate stands while accusations
against their service are read. A superior removes each piece only after the
candidate answers for it.

### Ferrale Ordination

Ferrale ordination is not public. It includes a melding or bodily alteration
often presented as marriage with a power beyond the human world. The candidate
enters an inner room or concealed place with a senior officiant. What returns
must bear a change that the church can name as acceptance.

The change may be displayed, framed by a mask, or concealed under vestments.
Its practical worth matters. An added eye is assigned to examination. A changed
hand handles what lower ranks cannot. A useless or painful alteration is praised
as a heavier burden and hidden from anyone who might call it failure.

Settlement stories say the Ferrale takes a demon spouse in the Mist. The church
lets the story stand because marriage makes collaboration sound governed. Inner
records are colder: date and witnesses; material used and physical result;
whether the candidate survived.

Ferrales ordained after the Mist compete fiercely over authentic sites, old
instructions, and access to altered beings. A candidate may spend years gathering
the required knowledge only to have a patron die before the rite.

### Advancement Rewards the Useful Wound

The order promotes competence, obedience, and the ability to make personal pain
useful to authority. A former outcast who can recruit other marked people is
valuable. A clerk who can expose hidden grain is valuable. A zealot who kills
the village's only smith without permission is a liability.

Cruelty earns rank when it produces tribute, information, or submission. Cruelty
that destroys resources is punished as self-indulgence. The distinction lets
senior clergy condemn excess while preserving the system that ordered it.

## The Church Inside the Household

### Care Opens the Door

The Sisters of Heme know what to do at births and wounds. They recognize fever,
spoiled milk, and the signs of a failing body. Much of this knowledge is sound.
A Sister may save a mother whom no one else can help, set the child's breathing
right, and remain until both can drink. Before leaving, she also counts the
household beds and asks who shares them. She examines every birthmark and enters
the child's future obligations.

Refusal has a price. A household that bars the Sister keeps its secrets
and loses the best healer within a day's walk. One that admits her receives
care under inspection. Rust rule does not need to invent every dependence. It
places a church witness inside the dependencies that already exist.

> **The second name:** Sister Orsa stopped the bleeding after a hard birth and
> slept beside the hearth for two nights. Before leaving, she wrote the child's
> household name in the mother's book and another name in her own. Twelve years
> later, the priory used the second name when it came to collect a novice.

### Every House Must Find a Fault

Some priories require a household reckoning before Midwinter. Each hearth must
name one fault committed under its roof: hidden grain, neglected worship,
quarreling during a fast, an unreported stranger, or words spoken against the
church. A household that reports nothing is charged with concealment. A serious
offense draws inquiry. The safest offering is therefore a small true shame.

Families learn to preserve suitable faults. A broken cup may go unmentioned
until the reckoning. A child may be taught to confess stealing an onion so the
priest need not ask why an aunt sleeps in the loft. The practice trains people
to furnish their own accusations and makes innocence sound rehearsed.

Children are questioned apart because they know routines without knowing which
parts are dangerous. The examiner asks who eats after dark, which door is
opened before dawn, and why the good blanket was washed twice. The questions
sound domestic. Their answers can empty a house.

### Kin Must Assist in Correction

The order often appoints a relative as **mercy-keeper** during confinement. The
keeper carries water, cleans the prisoner, reports speech, and decides when to
ask the guard for rest. Refusal abandons the prisoner to strangers. Cooperation
makes the relative part of the procedure and gives the church another witness.

If the prisoner weakens too quickly, the keeper is accused of neglect. If the
prisoner resists, the keeper is accused of indulgence. A spouse can preserve a
life only by helping the church continue the sentence.

Execution deepens the claim. Kin may be ordered to provide a clean shirt,
binding cord, cart, fuel, or burial cloth. These are ordinary duties toward the
dead, redirected before death to make the sentence resemble household care.

### The Church Rewrites Last Words

A condemned person is given a short recantation to learn. It names the church
as protector, calls the offense a voluntary error, and thanks the witnesses for
remaining obedient. The words are repeated until the prisoner can say them
without prompting. Failure delays water, burial, or the release of property.

Afterward, children and close kin may be required to repeat the same words at
Rotday or Midwinter. The church thereby takes custody of memory. A family may
remember the condemned person's true speech at home, but the public version is
the sentence written by the killers.

At one western gate, a dead woman's son recited her recantation for nine years.
On the tenth he spoke her real last sentence. The village elder struck him
before the visiting Brother had time to turn his head.

### Rescue Becomes Possession

The Rust Brothers offer sanctuary to children and adults whom settlements fear
for unusual bodies, seizures, voices, scars, or suspected demonic signs. The
offer can save a person from exposure or a frightened household. It also gives
the church a recruit who has been taught that ordinary kin wanted them dead.

The rescued person is renamed, separated from letters and visitors, and shown
the old accusation whenever loyalty falters. A yearly family meeting may be
permitted under supervision. The novice arrives fed, armed, and composed. The
family must thank the order before speaking. If the novice denounces the
household, advancement follows. If the novice embraces a parent without leave,
the next visit is canceled.

Sincere defenders emerge from this practice. They remember a real
danger and credit the institution that turned the danger into lifelong debt.

### The Dead Continue to Owe

Burial accounts can outlive the person named in them. A body removed as a
servant remains attached to its former household for tribute purposes. The kin
may owe winter cloth, lamp oil, a replacement tool, or payment for the guard
that keeps their dead relative under control. Refusal is preached as abandonment
of the dead.

A family can petition for release and ordinary burial. The priory sets a price
based on the servant's remaining usefulness. The sum is usually reachable only
if neighbors contribute, which makes private grief a public test of loyalty.

> **Grandmother's shoes:** The dead woman had no need of shoes, but her name
> remained on the priory roll as a road servant. Each Fallturn her daughters
> delivered a pair. The third year they sent two left shoes. No one in the
> village admitted knowing why.

### Hospitality Is an Inspection

When clergy claim lodging, the highest-ranking visitor takes the household's
best sleeping place. A junior sleeps near the stores. A novice is assigned to
the children or servants and listens after the lamps go out. The hosts must
remove outer plates, brush rust from the floor, dry wet padding, and account for
anything found in a seam or boot.

The arrangement crosses ordinary household boundaries while remaining close
enough to hospitality that resistance looks shameful. By morning the visitors
know who commands the hearth, who flinches at doctrine, which child asks
questions, and whether the grain chest sounds hollow.

The Brothers leave orange stains on linen and offer no payment. Some households
keep the stained cloth for inspections. Others cut it into strips and hide one
strip in every wall, so no single search can remove the memory.

## Selective Terror

### The Punished Person Is Chosen for an Audience

Rust punishment begins with the witnesses. The order asks whose suffering will
be remembered, whose fall will divide the village, and whose property can reward
cooperation. A poor outcast may be easy to seize but teach little. The miller's
son, a respected midwife, or the elder's oath-brother can govern many people by
being broken in public.

Selection need not follow guilt. It follows usefulness. The accused may have
committed an offense, concealed another person's offense, or merely occupy the
right place in the settlement's web of dependence.

The charge is often narrow enough to sound credible. One person withheld a
page. Another gave water to a Raven Sister. A household failed to report a birth
sign. A hunter kept tissue from a demon kill. A child repeated a protective
charm. Such charges make neighbors search their memories for lesser versions of
the same act.

### Torture Produces Submission, Not Truth

The inquisition uses torture to obtain names, objects, public recantation, and
an account that validates church authority. Accurate information is useful but
not required. Pain makes a person search for whatever answer will stop the next
act. Experienced interrogators know this and compare statements against local
rivalries, physical evidence, and prior reports.

They also exploit false accusation. If a victim names an enemy, that enmity
becomes evidence about both households. If the victim names a friend, the bond
can be tested. If no one is named, the refusal becomes a sermon about hidden
networks.

The order may stop before permanent injury when it wants a functioning informant.
It may choose a visible injury when it wants a walking warning. It executes when
continued life would let the victim become a center of resistance or contradict
the sentence too openly.

Do not render such scenes as contests of villainous invention. Show the stool
carried from the meeting house, the witness list, the water bucket no one is
allowed to lift, and the work left undone while every adult attends. Procedure
makes the scene worse.

### Execution Is a Settlement Obligation

A public execution forces participation. Villagers build the platform, provide
fuel, dig the grave, stand in household order, or repeat the response after the
priest. Refusal creates the next accused. Compliance distributes responsibility
until no survivor can claim clean hands.

The body may be denied ordinary burial, displayed beside the gate, or returned
to kin under conditions. Each option governs the living. A displayed body marks
the boundary of speech. A conditional burial makes the family enforce silence
to keep the grave undisturbed.

Afterward, the church reallocates something: land, grain, office, a tool, the
right to collect a toll. Material benefit attaches neighbors to the verdict.
The people who gain may hate the Brothers and still defend the new arrangement.

### The Village Must Survive the Lesson

Punitive killing has a limit set by extraction. Too many deaths reduce planting,
leave animals untended, and create fugitives with nothing left to lose. A capable
prior knows which household performs which work and does not execute the only
millwright before harvest unless a replacement arrives with the Guard.

Apparent mercy follows the same calculation without softening it. A Brother may
release a healer because three children have fever. The healer understands that
her skill, rather than her person, was spared.

> **The corrected measure:** In Harrow Fen, the tithe was short by two sacks.
> The prior did not burn the granary. He had the elected measurer's hand bound
> to the weighing beam until the village named where the grain had gone. At
> dusk the prior accepted one name, took one man, and sealed the granary again.
> Planting began on time. For twelve years afterward, the village delivered an
> extra sack.

## The Inquisition in a Settlement

### Before Arrival

The order gathers knowledge through tollkeepers and peddlers, as well as reports
from minor clergy. Disappointed heirs bring household secrets.
People previously spared bring names. Informants report unusual births,
forbidden visitors, and demon sightings. They also report missing tribute, old
books, and households that have acquired unexplained silver.

The report need not be formal. A Brother remembers who offered news at the last
market. A priory servant marks a door sign on a tally stick. A captured traveler
buys release with the name of a literate widow.

Fear begins when the village learns that private facts have traveled farther
than villagers can travel themselves.

### Entry and Inventory

The Iron Guard secures gate, shrine, well, and meeting house. Clergy summon the
elders and demand the household count. Tribute is weighed before accusations
begin. This makes ordinary governance and extraordinary punishment appear to
be one continuous duty.

The Brothers inspect objects before opinions. They look for recent ash in a
hearth, replaced floorboards, sealed jars, unfamiliar ink, wounds on livestock,
rust disturbed on a shrine, and households that cleaned too thoroughly. A
competent inquisition is frightening because it notices work.

They ask the same practical question of several people and compare the small
differences. Who carried water? Which path did the goat herd use? When was the
south field last plowed? A hidden visitor alters ordinary routines before the
visitor appears in testimony.

### Accusation and Separation

The accused is separated from household witnesses. The church then assigns
different meanings to silence. A spouse's silence becomes complicity. A child's
silence becomes parental corruption. An elder's silence becomes a rival claim
to authority.

The order may promise that one confession will spare the household. It usually
defines confession broadly enough to require another name. The accused cannot
complete the demand without extending the inquiry.

### Sentence and Record

The sentence names the offense, the correction, and the debt now owed by the
survivors. A spoken sentence is repeated by witnesses. A written one is copied
for the priory and may be nailed to the meeting house even where few can read.
The unread document still shows that authority remembers.

Records make old fear portable after the Mist. A new patrol can enforce a debt
imposed by a dead prior. A village that survived through local compromise now
faces an institution able to compare its story with another village's account.

### Departure

The Brothers leave a demand that can be inspected later: an empty chair at the
council, a new keeper of the shrine, a child pledged to service, a sealed chest,
or a household made responsible for the next tithe. The village performs the
punishment after the soldiers leave.

The church spends a few days and one victim. The settlement supplies the
remaining years. That is the economy of selective terror.

## The Monopoly on Demonic Knowledge

### The Traveling Search

Rust parties preach and collect tribute on the road. Their search has other
objects:

- demon tracks and altered animals
- failed summonings and old laboratories
- battle remains
- witnesses and texts

They ask who touched the corpse first and what changed afterward. They measure
what common people are forbidden to examine.

These parties may include a priest, armed escorts, a scribe or memorist, and a
handler equipped for dangerous remains. The church presents the work as removal
of contamination. That description is incomplete rather than wholly false.

At a kill site, they may burn clothing, scour tools, and forbid burial until
their examination ends. They remove the matter that held or carried the demon's
form in sealed non-metal vessels, packed so a leak will be noticed. Everything
is entered by source, place, witness, and condition.

Do not name the substance in player-facing description. Let its handling teach
that it matters.

### Appropriation Before Destruction

The Brothers do not burn every dangerous text immediately. A prior first asks
whether the church possesses the information. A page absent from church holdings is copied, carried
away, or committed to trained memory. A duplicate may be destroyed in public.

The order of operations exposes the monopoly. The sin lies in possession by the
wrong person, not in the knowledge itself. A book condemned as a threat to
humanity may reappear behind an iron-bound library door with its owner's name
scraped away.

Objects are treated the same way. A weapon proven useful against a demon becomes
a relic of Rust. An ingredient becomes a sealed tithe. A witness becomes a
servant, prisoner, or corpse according to whether their knowledge can be
contained.

### Ignorance Has a Chain of Rank

Most Rust Brothers are also kept ignorant. A village priest knows what to seize
but not why it matters. A traveling examiner knows safe handling but not the
whole use. A superior receives reports from several routes and can compare them.
Knowledge rises through the church while commands descend.

Leaders gain protection from rivals. Subordinates gain deniability about the
work they help perform. The Brother who sealed the flask can say truthfully that
he never saw it opened.
The guard who burned the notes can say he never read them. Compartmentalization
becomes a religious virtue.

### The Hidden Bargain

The church teaches that demons will destroy humanity if their enemies circulate
too much knowledge of them. It therefore concentrates demon study among people
already bound to demonic power and protected by armed secrecy.

The arrangement creates dependence on the danger it claims to contain. The more
the order learns, the more dangerous uncontrolled witnesses become. The more
residue it gathers, the more it needs secure routes and compliant settlements.
Every discovery produces a reason for wider authority.

Collection brings a Rust party quickly after a demon's death. A village asking
for protection may wait. Collection is central work. Protection is the price
used to justify it.

## What Their Rule Does to People

### Fear Enters Ordinary Maintenance

Most days under Rust rule are spent grinding grain, mending fences, courting,
quarreling, and laughing. Terror lies in which ordinary acts have acquired a
second meaning.

A missing cup may show that a fugitive was fed. Extra lamp oil may imply secret
reading. A child's question about a scar may repeat forbidden knowledge. A
newly sharpened spade may be a tool, a hidden weapon, or evidence that someone
dug after curfew.

Every task must be completed and made to look innocent. The second labor leaves
no repaired fence and fills no grain bin.

### The Church Colonizes Anticipation

The most governed village is one in which no Brother is present. Adults lower
their voices when iron rings against stone. Children learn which color cloth
means they must come indoors. The headman keeps a clean tribute ledger beside
a private tally of what the tribute cost.

People rehearse answers before patrols arrive. A household agrees who found the
stranger and who refused shelter. The lie may protect someone, but the rehearsal
also teaches everyone to imagine the church's questions.

### Selective Violence Divides Memory

Indiscriminate attack gives a settlement a common enemy. Selective punishment
creates beneficiaries, informants, doubters, and people who believe the victim
was chosen for a reason. No household remembers the event from the same place.

One family lost a parent. Another received the field. A third testified under
threat. A fourth hid and now resents the praise given to braver people. The
church can return years later and govern through those differences.

### Bodily Difference Becomes Evidence

Because the priesthood displays marked and altered bodies, villagers may read
unusual bodies as signs of secret allegiance or demonic claim. This places
people born different, injured survivors, and those changed by illness in
danger before they have acted.

Some households hide a child's hand or face. Others offer the child to the
church before neighbors can accuse them. A person rejected by a village may
enter Rust service and later return with authority over those who rejected them.
The order has converted prejudice into recruitment.

### Common Forms of Settlement Response

- **Exact compliance:** tribute is delivered early and every rite performed.
  The village seeks safety by becoming uninteresting.
- **Distributed concealment:** no single household knows the whole hiding place,
  route, or forbidden text. Torture of one person cannot expose everything.
- **Sacrificial accusation:** a weak household or disliked stranger is blamed
  to protect the settlement's stronger network.
- **Double service:** the same people provision the Iron Guard and warn Raven
  Sisters, hoping neither side learns the full account.
- **Flight in increments:** one adolescent leaves as a marriage partner, then
  an aunt follows, then tools disappear. The household escapes without declaring
  itself fugitive.

Each response spends someone else's safety or trust. The church arranged that
price long before a Brother next enters the gate.

> **The coal bell:** The people of Lornstead used a cracked pot as an alarm.
> One strike meant fire, two meant wolves, and a slow uneven ringing meant iron
> on the east road. After the Mist lifted, a child rang the last pattern as a
> joke. His mother struck him hard enough to split his lip, then sat beside the
> pot until dark. No patrol came.

## The Inner Character of the Order

### Stoicism Is Trained and Displayed

Rust discipline treats visible need as unworked material. Recruits learn to
stand under weight, eat after superiors, keep a hand near heat, and receive pain
without asking an observer for pity. The exercise produces endurance and a
public contempt for ordinary limits.

Discipline redirects fear upward. A Brother must fear doctrinal failure, demonic
retaliation, and superiors more than cold, hunger, or the suffering of a
villager.

The famous stillness is trained in this way. The face remains quiet because
expression can be reported. Zeal appears in what the person will do while
quiet.

### Hatred Is Made into Rank

Many recruits enter after exclusion, humiliation, hunger, or bodily stigma.
The church names these experiences proof that common people are weak, treacherous,
and unworthy of knowledge. Personal injury is enlarged into contempt for the
class of people from which the recruit came.

Advancement rewards the contempt. The Brother who still identifies with a
victim hesitates. The one who calls the victim ore demonstrates formation.
Cruelty becomes evidence that the recruit has escaped common softness.

The order preserves old humiliation as a wound. It supplies uniform, weapon,
and doctrine so the bearer can inflict it downward.

### Zeal and Calculation Coexist

Rust Brothers can be fanatical without being impulsive. They count sacks before
they preach sacrifice. They map kinship before selecting an accused. They know
how many guards a village can feed and how long a public rite can interrupt the
harvest.

Their competence makes them more dangerous. A frothing zealot may be avoided.
A quiet examiner who understands grain storage can remain for twenty years.

Some members enjoy the power openly. Others describe every cruelty as necessity.
The institution has work for both. Sadism supplies energy. Procedure supplies
permission.

### Misunderstood Does Not Mean Redeemable

Outsiders often misunderstand the Rust Brothers by imagining simple motives.
Raiding and fear both matter, but neither explains the order. Its theology makes
human submission the proper shape of survival and gives that submission a
permanent government.

Their concern about demonic retaliation can be genuine. Their protection can
work. Neither fact changes what they have chosen: preserve humanity as an
ignorant laboring population, reserve dangerous power to themselves, and punish
anyone who claims an equal right to know or decide.

An individual may defect, refuse an order, or save a victim. Such acts oppose
the institution. They do not reveal a hidden goodness within its design.

## Rendering the Rust Brothers in Play

### Present Consequence Before Explanation

Open with one thing the adventurers can touch or hear:

- orange dust on a guest bed
- a meeting house emptied during planting
- a shrine figure cut open in a search for pages
- a child who knows the tribute weights by heart

Let doctrine arrive through the choices that evidence forces.

Do not begin with a lecture about Rust theology. A Brother calls a prisoner
"ore" while checking whether the prisoner's hands can still work. The word then
has a use and a cost.

### Keep Them Competent

Rust Brothers understand roads, tribute, village divisions, and the handling of
demonic danger. The Iron Guard knows formation and punitive logistics. A defeat
should come from resistance, divided authority, a false report, material limits,
or adventurer action rather than foolishness.

Competence does not require omniscience. The order's secrecy creates blind spots.
Low ranks do not know why an object matters. Informants settle grudges. Torture
produces bad information. A superior may conceal a discovery from another
superior.

### Use Fear Through Choice

Give the settlement something it can lose and something it has already
compromised. Useful situations include:

- The Brothers demand the person who first cut open a demon corpse. Four people
  did so, but the village can survive the loss of only one.
- A confiscated notebook has been copied before it is burned. The copy contains
  a true defense against one demon and a mistake that will kill whoever trusts
  it.
- An unusually tall child has been promised to a priory. The parents now claim
  the promise saved the child from a village killing.
- An Iron Guard examiner protected the settlement from a real infestation and
  stayed to build an informant network around the gratitude.
- A Brother offers to spare an accused Raven worshipper if the adventurers
  surrender a sealed vessel taken from a demon site. He will keep the bargain.

The evil should not depend on the Brothers lying about every fact. A kept bargain
can bind more tightly than a broken one.

### Six Signs of Mature Rust Rule

| D6 | What the Adventurers Notice |
| ---: | --- |
| 1 | The shrine owns the village's only reliable weights. Tribute and justice use the same measure. |
| 2 | A good field lies untilled because accepting it would affirm the execution that made it vacant. |
| 3 | Children stop a counting song before its final line. The missing words name a Raven Sister. |
| 4 | The smith keeps two sets of tools: bright working iron and rusted pieces displayed during inspection. |
| 5 | No household has a complete book. Pages are divided among roof thatch, grain jars, and coffin boards. |
| 6 | A sealed ceramic flask hangs in an iron cage above the shrine door. No villager admits knowing who brought it. |

### Questions for the GM

Before placing Rust Brothers in a settlement, answer:

1. What does the church extract here besides tribute?
2. Whom did it punish last, and who gained from that punishment?
3. Which useful service makes resistance harder?
4. What forbidden knowledge or demonic remnant is being sought?
5. Which local person understands the church's procedure best?
6. What will the Brothers preserve because destroying it would weaken their
   control?

The answers should produce a situation rather than a scheduled scene. The Rust
Brothers arrive with aims, constraints, information, and a practiced method.
The settlement has its own compromises. Adventurers can expose, bargain, steal,
rescue, destroy, or leave. Whatever they choose, the village must still plant.
