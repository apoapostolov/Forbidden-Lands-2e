# Places, Villages, and Adventure Sites

This file is the canonical lore for **named geography** and the
**village-as-system** — the social, religious, and economic
shape of inhabited places. Mechanics for the systems referenced
here (Need, Heat, governance, encounters, factions) are in the
manuscript; this file holds the lore-side anchors.

## The Village as System (lore-side)

A Forbidden Lands village is described, when described well, by
seven components:

1. **A working food source** — field, herd, fishery, foraged
   forest, fungus harvest, eel weir, bread root from sedge,
   honey, grain.
2. **A water source** — well, stream, cistern, spring, river.
3. **A defense** — palisade, stone wall, cliff, marsh, bog,
   rock formation, Ent-shepherded grove.
4. **A shrine or shrines** — Wyrm, Raven, Rust, Heme, Huge,
   Clay, Wail, Flow, Horn, Nightwalker, Eor, or local nature
   spirit. Most villages keep two or three actively.
5. **A burial place** — separate, walled or fenced, watched.
   Walkers (confused dead) are tolerated; mirrors are kept away
   from them.
6. **A governing authority** — clan elder, communal speaker,
   noble, oath-lord, Rust Brother priory, sacred site
   custodian, merchant council.
7. **A grudge structure** — three to five households or factions
   in standing tension; the Heat between them rises and falls
   on event.

Need (food, salt, iron, coin, news, an heir, a body counted) and
Heat (insult, theft, exposure, humiliation) are the two pressure
gauges that drive village events. A village in **adequate Need
and rising Heat** is the standard scene-state.

## The Hollows — the Master Reference

The Hollows is the canonical novella-detailed village. It is the
template for what "village authenticity" means in this manuscript.

### Origin

Built on the battlefield and burial cairn of **King Algarod**,
who fell here in the **Fourth Alder Wars** in **874 AS**. The
ground is bone and steel for forty paces down. The bridge across
the central stream sits on a foundation of skulls; villagers
know this and crack a hard joke about it.

### Layout

A small bridge divides the village. North of the bridge is the
**old quarter** — Mrs. Pollmor's **Dead Man's Hand Inn**, the
shrine of Heme, the burial wall, two long-houses, a smith.
South of the bridge is the **new quarter** — Yawim the dwarf
brewmaster's **Three Skulls Tavern**, the well, the carpenter,
the goat pen, the storehouse. The bridge belongs to neither.

### Factions

- **Mrs. Pollmor's faction** — old families, old grudges,
  Heme-worship, traditional courtesies. Pollmor herself is a
  widow of the Mist years who buried two husbands here. The
  **Dead Man's Hand Inn** keeps a corner table reserved for the
  village's restless dead — beer poured every evening, a chair
  kept empty. **Old Hettar**, a confused walker, sits at the table
  some evenings and listens.
- **Yawim's faction** — younger families, dwarven and
  half-dwarven traders, new arrivals, the merchants who have
  begun moving since the Mist lifted. Yawim brews a strong dark
  beer the **Belderranian** dwarves trade for upriver. He paid
  to widen the bridge two years ago and has not been forgiven
  for it.
- **The Wyrm Temple** — a small Congregation of the Serpent
  shrine on the rise above the village, tended by **Old Sister
  Vellora**, who has been here since before the Mist lifted.

### Practices

- The **Spring ritual** is letting the well overflow — Flow's
  rite. The water runs through the old quarter and washes the
  burial wall; it is considered a benediction by both factions.
- **Walkers** are integrated into evening custom. The Inn's
  reserved corner is one example; the smithy keeps a second
  hammer that Old Hettar used in life.
- The **village's third shrine** is the unmarked one at the
  bridge foundation — for King Algarod and his dead. No
  religion claims it; everyone leaves something there at
  Rotday.

### Pressures

- A **Phantom Dagger** (see artifacts) was lost in the silt
  under the bridge during a **Silent Order** contract that
  went sideways. The Silent know. They have not yet sent
  someone.
- Yawim is suspected of **secretly recovering Old Hettar's
  hammer** from the smithy and selling it to a Belderranian
  for the right amount of silver. He didn't, but the rumor is
  doing the work the truth could not.
- A **Reapenter** scout passed through the area last month;
  Sister Vellora warned Mrs. Pollmor; Mrs. Pollmor has not yet
  warned Yawim's side.

### Why The Hollows is the Template

Three factions, three shrines, two pressures from outside, one
shared ritual. The dead are integrated, not banished. The
authority is divided and aging. New money is moving into old
ground. The party can take any side or none. **The party will
not "save" the Hollows — the Hollows will outlast the party.**

## Settlement Creation Notes (lore-side)

Use the manuscript's village-generation tables for randomness;
use this section for **what kinds of villages exist as recurring
texture**:

- **Walled stronghold-village** — usually around a noble tower
  or a Rust priory. Heat is high; food is taxed; the **Iron
  Guard** stops by quarterly.
- **Cliffside hamlet** — small population; eel-weir or fishery
  for food; defense is the cliff itself. Often Ailander.
- **Bog village** — built on a slight rise in marshland; bread
  root and frog and eel; Saurian trade nearby. Often kept by
  a **Sister of Heme** or a **Maiden Druid**.
- **Forest stead** — a single extended family or two on a
  clearing; food is hunting and a small field; defense is the
  forest itself. Often near an Ent-grove the families know
  not to disturb.
- **Mountain mining village** — Belder or Crombe dwarves with
  some humans; food is traded for ore; defense is the slope
  and the dwarven walls.
- **Crossroads inn-village** — two or three buildings around
  an old shrine where two roads meet; food is what travelers
  bring; news is the actual currency.
- **Refugee Galdane camp** — tent-poles and waggons; nomadic;
  Heat is constant against any settled neighbor; food is herds.
- **Ailander hidden village** — barely visible from a road; a
  Raven Sister within; food is mixed; defense is concealment
  and old ward-stones.

## Heat and Need (lore-side)

These two pressures are not a mechanical bookkeeping device.
They are how the manuscript tells you that something is going
to happen.

- **Need** rises with bad weather, raids, taxes, levy, plague,
  failed harvest, lost livestock, lost news, lost children,
  lost old people gone in winter.
- **Heat** rises with insult, theft, exposure of a secret
  (drunk speech, a found object, an overheard word), broken
  oath, a stranger pointed-at, a faction triumph, a faction
  humiliation, a death no one will own.

A village at **Adequate Need + Rising Heat** is the default
scene-state. A village at **Bare Need + High Heat** is on the
edge of internal violence and will likely scapegoat the next
stranger to arrive.

## Named Geography Master Index

The Forbidden Lands proper are bounded by the **Iron Lock** to
the south, the **Bloodmarch** and **Aslene** beyond Shadowgate
Pass to the southwest, the **Eastern Sea** to the east, the
**Icefang Mountains** and **Dragaland** to the north, and the
deep west woods of **Dankwood** and **Wilderwood** to the
west.

### Western Ravenland (Alderlander and Misgrown lands)

- **Alderstone** — Zytera's seat. The largest fortified town
  in the western lands. **Wardens of the Rust Lord** based here.
  Misgrown gather around the city. Iron Guard mustering ground.
- **Vond** — fell to demons in the Demon Flood; partly reclaimed.
  The **Vond Wall** raised in 620 AS by the Aslenes against
  southern incursion. Now a contested ruin.
- **Falender** — burned 869 AS in the Demon Flood. The **Dreamer
  in Falender** lived and worked here. Now a ruin where wolfshadows
  and nightwargs are sometimes seen. Site of Hinrek's grave and
  Tvedra's ring.
- **Lumra** — fell 820 AS. Once a major Meromannian dwarven hold;
  Garmar Four-Beard cooked Teramalda over embers here.
- **Wailer's Hold** — fell 865 AS to demons and Alderlanders.
  **Tormund Halfhand** is gathering an army to retake it.
- **Weatherstone** — a noble's tower with a small village.

### Central Ravenland

- **The Hollows** — see above.
- **Solecrest** — small Alderlander village with a Heme shrine.
- **Vivend** — Golden Bough druidic site at the edge of
  **Dankwood**.
- **Margelda** — region of plains and broken ground; **Galdane**
  refugees settled here with **Zertorme**'s permission.
- **Moldena** — old town on the trade route, partly dwarven.
- **Arina Forest** — vast central forest. Roka and Isir orcs in
  the south; Urhur orcs further east; Insectoid colony rumored.
- **Clay's Eye** — a sacred lake.
- **Glethra** — gold mines, seized by the Iron League in 800 AS.
- **Shadowgate Pass** — the only mountain pass to Aslene; gate
  of the Quards' and Galdanes' arrival; tear in reality through
  which Gatekeepers strayed.

### Eastern Ravenland

- **Iron Lock** — the great wall built 878 AS by the Alderlanders
  to seal the south. **The Silent** stand guard.
- **Stonegarden** — Canide dwarven hold on a sheer plateau, ruled
  by **King Karonax** and **Queen Sulma**. Scarne is imprisoned
  beneath. Site of a secret Canide-Urhur diplomatic channel.
- **Belderand** — under-mountain dwarven city, **Belderranian**
  capital, ruled by **King Turik**. Main temple of the stone
  singers. Wail's Horn vaulted here.
- **Feulenmark** (the **Clan Land**) — the orc homeland; rocky
  moors and forest. **Ogre** clans on its edges.
- **Maidenholm** — seat of the **Order of Maidens**, and of the
  **Shardmaiden**.
- **Farhaven** — coastal town with the **Library of Farhaven**;
  **Aspis** the Psychopomp tends it. Queen Agatha's Twin Tablets
  sealed here in lead.
- **Amber's Peak** — **Zertorme**'s seat, hailed by Quards as
  Horn's chosen savior; pilgrim Quards camp at its foot.

### Northern Ravenland

- **Lake Varda / Laak Varda** — bread root harvest; saurian
  trade. Sometimes a Heme festival.
- **The Blush** — salty river-delta to the Eastern Sea;
  Saurian trade station; mussel gatherers and the eloquent
  mire drake who advises them.
- **Belifar** — a coastal village.
- **Brodelmarg** — small town; trade.
- **Yendra** — fishing town on the Eastern Sea.
- **Gargan Marshes** — the Saurian heartland; trade only at the
  southern rim platform-stations; everywhere else is hunting
  ground.
- **Stridehome** — village near old **Dankwood**, holds
  **Menkaura's Tooth** in the war-leader's family.
- **Stillmist** — the elven sanctuary; cannot be found by looking;
  **Rosenstaad** lies within it.
- **Rosenstaad** — the elven temple where dead Ents' rubies are
  mounted; also where shattered elves can be remade.
- **Pernty** — small Ailander village.
- **Grindbone** — bone-dust trade settlement.
- **Freygrove** — sacred Heme grove with a small village beside it.
- **Wilderwood** — vast western forest, less explored than Dankwood.
  The Well of Tears is somewhere east of here.
- **Mount Ora** — peak in the central range.
- **Byard** — village; **Varleyman of Byard** rode a drakewyrm.
- **Dankwood** — vast forest of the Stillelves and Golden Bough;
  Menkaura's old queendom; many Ent-groves.
- **Entwater** — river bordering Dankwood.

### Far North and Beyond

- **Icefang Mountains** — northern boundary; very few settlements.
- **Scarda** — coastal village; **Scarda's Teeth** shrine kept
  for seven generations after the pirate Scarda's three-serpent
  duel.
- **Eastern Sea** — coastal trade route; storms and sea-serpents.

### Far Southwest

- **Bloodmarch** — Aslene rider clans' lands. Dread raptors.
  The Quards and Galdanes both came through here.
- **Dragaland** — distant land of dragons; possible source of
  the Swarming Death.
- **Mount Bilica** — the mountain the Ailanders crossed in 550
  AS to reach Ravenland.

### South of the Iron Lock

- **Alderland** — the orthodox Raven-Church kingdom; only
  approached through the Iron Lock and only with permission.

## How to Use Geography in Prose

- Specify direction by **land-feature**, not by compass: "down
  the Entwater road," "behind the Heme grove," "the second
  hill east of the bridge."
- Use **distance in days of walking** with named overnights, not
  miles: "three days, sleeping at Stridehome, then the Saurian
  platform on the Blush, then home."
- Use named festivals for time of arrival: "they came in at
  Rotday and stayed for Midwinter."
- Reference the village system components when introducing a
  new village in prose — at minimum **food, defense, shrine,
  authority**, in the first 200 words.
