# Setting Reference: Ravenland and the Human Peoples

This document collects confirmed setting facts about the Forbidden Lands world,
focusing on geography, the three human cultures, and the physical and cultural
details that are useful when writing manuscript-facing prose.

Sources: corebook `02-your-adventurer.md`, `01-front-matter.md`, community
canon from the Fria Ligan official forums, and designer commentary from
Erik Granström.

---

## The World

**Ravenland** is the setting. It is a former kingdom — now a broken territory —
that was isolated for roughly three hundred years (ten human generations) by
the Blood Mist, a red supernatural haze that rose from the ground at night
and devoured travellers. The Mist has only recently lifted. The land is not
recovered. Roads have been swallowed. Bridges are rotted. Settlements are
inward-turned and suspicious. Ruins outnumber inhabited villages.

The game takes place in the immediate aftermath of isolation. The Forbidden
Lands are not the dark past — they are the present that the past left behind.

**Religion:** Three active factions claim spiritual authority.

- **Raven Church** — traditional faith of Ravenland, centred on the Raven God.
- **The Congregration** — originally set Zygofer on his destructive path; tried
  to reel him in. Organized, morally compromised.
- **The Rust Brothers** — aligned with Zygofer wholeheartedly; his former
  servants, still active.

**Currency:** Silver coins. There are no silver mines in Ravenland. All coins
are old Alder War remnants, worn and clipped. Copper also comes from Alderland.
Gold from local deposits exists but is treated with suspicion.

## The Three Human Peoples

Humans are not native to Ravenland. They arrived during the period of the Alder
Wars and never left — first because the wars closed the roads, then because the
Blood Mist sealed the land. Three distinct human cultures entered Ravenland from
different directions and for different reasons. After ten generations in shared
isolation, they are mixed and intermarried, but observable traits persist.

The corebook says: _"Some of your ancestors came with the armies from Alderland
who invaded Ravenland against the will of the Raven God. Or they sought
sanctuary from the war, as refugees and settlers. Your ancestors came walking
from Alderland across the mountains in the south or riding from Aslene in the
west."_

The three peoples are: **Alderlander**, **Aslene**, and **Ailander**.

---

### 1. Alderlander

**Origin:** Alderland — a kingdom to the south of Ravenland, beyond the
Southern Range mountains. Entrance via mountain passes (long march on foot).

**How they came:** As soldiers and colonizers. Alderland sent armies to invade
Ravenland. Many soldiers settled after the war; some came as camp followers and
never returned. Alderlanders are the most numerous human group in Ravenland.

**Culture:** Hierarchical, feudal, organized. Written contracts, formal oaths,
military chain of command. More intensive agriculture. Closer to a Germanic/
Saxon Dark Ages analogue. They built the most organized settlements, and the
existing legal conventions (courts, posting boards, contract law) trace largely
to Alderlander practice.

**Half-elves:** The Alderlander strain of half-elves are called **Frailers** —
descendants of elven hostages left with humans in Alderland during the Shift.
Long-lived, slender, pointed ears. No Elvenspring concept of duty to nature.
Tend toward ambition and acquisition.

> **Physical type** _(author-proposed: Icelandic/Swedish | Apostol-approved)_
> Tall, lean-to-hardy build. Pale complexion — the ash-white of northern
> winters. Hair fair: straw, flaxen, ash-blond, some mid-brown; worn long and
> dressed simply. Men bearded or close-cropped. Eyes pale — grey, ice-blue,
> blue-grey. Spare in expression, slow to gesture. Built for cold and long
> sustained work. The face of someone who endures quietly and does not
> advertise it.
>
> _Real-world anchor: Icelandic / Swedish. Hardy, stoic northern people. Feudal, hierarchical, written-contract culture._

---

### 2. Aslene

**Origin:** Aslene — a territory to the west of Ravenland.

**How they came:** Riding. The Aslene are a horse people. They entered
Ravenland from the west, mostly as nomadic or semi-nomadic groups. Some settled
in Ravenland; others (the **Galdenes**, a named sub-group) remained more
nomadic.

**Culture:** Steppe-based, Parthian/Scythian in practice (confirmed by designer
Erik Granström, who described the Aslene as a mix of Parthian and Scythian).
Community analogue: "Vikings and Mongols from the Dark Ages." Horse care,
light equipment, comfort with distance and weather. Less hierarchical than
Alderlanders, more comfortable with mixed-kin company. Settled Aslenes
assimilate faster; nomadic Aslenes (the **Galdenes**) retain the old ways.

> **Physical type** _(author-proposed: Parthian/Scythian-Mongol steppe | Apostol-approved: Arabic/Mongol mix, proper Mongol features, light-skinned)_
> Lean, medium height. Light complexion — not pale Nordic, not deep-olive
> Mediterranean; the lighter end of inner-Asian steppe coloring, like
> Mongolian or Kazakh people who live north of the desert belts. Features
> distinctly Mongolic: wide cheekbones, flat nasal bridge, epicanthic fold or
> light variant of it, strong jaw. Hair black to very dark brown, straight and
> coarse-textured, worn tied back or loose. Eyes dark brown, narrow-set,
> slightly tilted. Frame compact and functional — horseman's build, low center
> of gravity, strong through the thighs and core. Layered clothing, practical
> for weather shifts. Nothing in the face that is soft or ornamental.
>
> _Real-world anchor: Arabic/Mongol mix on the lighter-skinned, northern
> steppe type. Mongolian and Turkic inner-Asian — not Arabic in features,
> but with that light inner-Asian warmth in the skin, not desert-dark._

---

### 3. Ailander

**Origin:** Unclear homeland — not south (Alderland) and not west (Aslene).
Community consensus places them in **eastern Ravenland**. They came as
refugees, not as fighting forces.

**How they came:** Fled. They sought sanctuary from some conflict, possibly
related to the Alder Wars. They arrived with less and built less. They are
the smallest of the three groups.

**Culture:** Slavic agrarian analogue _(Apostol-approved)_. Sparsely settled,
pastoral — farming and herding. More egalitarian than Alderlanders — fewer
written contracts, more direct obligation and kinship ties. Before the Blood
Mist they had more sustained contact with elves, Elvenspring, and dwarves
than the other human groups. Their magic traditions are older and less
structured than Alderlander practice. They split from Alderlanders centuries
earlier over religious differences.

> **Physical type** _(author-proposed: Atlantic Celtic / Western Islander | Apostol-approved: Slavic — discards islander analogue entirely)_
> Short to medium, stocky and practical-framed. Pale complexion. Hair lighter
> than expected — straw-blond, light brown, sometimes near-white in early
> childhood. Eyes blue, grey, or pale watery hazel. Wide through the
> shoulders, thick-wristed — farmer's and herder's build, made for sustained
> low-effort work across long ground. Unhurried in movement. Plain wool
> clothing in earth tones, nothing decorative. The look of people who have
> never put ornament on a thing they use daily.
>
> _Real-world anchor: Slavic. Shorter, lighter-haired, peasant-agrarian
> stock. Farmers and herders. Not Atlantic Celtic or Western Islander._

**Culture note:** The smallest human group. Refugees, not conquerors. More
egalitarian than Alderlanders. Long contact with elves and dwarves. Older,
less organized magic traditions.

## Non-Human Kin Notes (for vignette use)

These are the eight kin. Physical and cultural tells for each:

| Kin          | Key Physical Tells                           | Cultural Tells                                                                                                 |
| ------------ | -------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Human**    | Variable — see three peoples above           | Key attr: Empathy. Adaptive.                                                                                   |
| **Elf**      | Tall, graceful, long-lived                   | Withdrawn, ancient grievances, forest-bound                                                                    |
| **Half-Elf** | Sharp bones, pale elven eyes in a human face | Ambitious, impatient, often magical. Two types: Frailer (from Alderland) and Elvenspring (from Ravenland).     |
| **Dwarf**    | Short, broad, barrel-chested                 | Stone-workers, craftsmen, deeply religious (world-builders for god Huge). True Grit: endure without complaint. |
| **Halfling** | Plump, short                                 | Pastoral, social, passive-aggressive; secretly contain wildness                                                |
| **Wolfkin**  | Lupine features, fur, fang                   | Hunter-culture, cold contempt for soft kin, pack loyalty above all                                             |
| **Orc**      | Very large, powerful                         | Ex-slave culture with deep grievance; fierce and free; feared on sight                                         |
| **Goblin**   | Small, quick, nocturnal features             | Night-runners, wild, anti-civilized; secretly kin to halflings                                                 |

### Dwarf Cultural Details

- God: **Huge** — gave dwarves the task of building the world.
- Stone Song magic is dwarf-native.
- Former owners of orcs as servants in exchange for land — a deal they regret.
- Deeper dwarves (underground culture) distrust surface-runners.
- **True Grit**: the kin talent — spend Willpower to ignore a critical injury for
  one round. Manifests in character as stoicism, silence about damage, refusal
  to acknowledge limitation until they fall.

### Half-Elf Cultural Details

- Two distinct sub-kin within the same mechanical kin:
  - **Frailers**: Alderlander heritage. Greedy for knowledge and power. No
    nature-duty ethic. Long-lived, slender, slightly pointed ears. Often sorcerers.
  - **Elvenspring**: Ravenland heritage. Slightly more aware of duty. More
    likely to have druid training.
- Psychic Power: the kin talent — spend Willpower to reroll Insight, Manipulation,
  or Scouting. This is a mental hyper-awareness — not ESP but exceptional
  attunement to subtext, emotional current, and environmental reading.

## Writing Rules for Human Nationality

When using nationality as a physical or cultural signal in prose, the rule is:
**show one thing, never label it.**

Good:

> He had the broad flat vowels of the Alderlander uplands and the look of a man
> who had spent his life arguing about grain counts.

> She sat in the saddle the way an Aslene sits — like the horse was the thing
> that was borrowed from her, not the other way around.

> The boy's hair was the dark red of a wet heather slope, and he had that
> particular Ailander stillness, the one that looks like patience but
> is actually just suspicion gone quiet.

Bad (too on-the-nose):

> He was from Alderland.

Bad (AI-tell):

> Her distinctive Aslene riding style was evident to anyone who looked closely.

One physical detail per character, at most. Do not label the culture by name in
fiction prose. Let the reader assemble it or let it remain background texture.

## Magic System Notes (for vignette use)

Fifteen magic paths exist. Two are sorcerer-only:
**Blood Magic** and **Mentalism** are the most politically charged.

| Path            | Notes                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                |
| --------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Blood Magic** | Binds through blood — Blood Oath creates magically enforced contracts. Blood Tap harvests power from wounds. Firewalker ignites the caster's blood for fire attacks. Immolate sets enemies alight. Combat-applicable, brutal, expensive (Willpower). |
| **Mentalism**   | "Native to the Bloodmarches." Truth Sense, Geas (compelled obedience), Confusion, Amnesia, Break Mind, Mind Seed (plant a false personality). The most overtly political path.                                                                       |
| **Awareness**   | Trail reading, True Path (navigation), True Sight, remote sensing. Nature-magic adjacent.                                                                                                                                                            |
| **Symbolism**   | Ward magic — protective signs that repel or alert.                                                                                                                                                                                                   |
| **Death Magic** | Communion with the dead, corpse animation, death-sensing.                                                                                                                                                                                            |
| **Healing**     | Wound closure, disease treatment, resurrection.                                                                                                                                                                                                      |
| **Stone Song**  | Dwarf-native. Earth attunement, stone communication, tremor sensing.                                                                                                                                                                                 |

For Ash (half-elf sorcerer, Path of Blood): her wards are Blood Oath bindings.
Her wound-sealing is a blood-command effect, not a healer's craft. This reads
visibly different from a druid's healing — there is a word spoken, a cost paid,
and the blood obeys.
