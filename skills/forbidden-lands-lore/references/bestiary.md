# Bestiary Creatures and Monster Lore

This file is the canonical lore for every named creature in the
Forbidden Lands 2E manuscripts (the Gamemaster's Guide bestiary
and the Book of Beasts). It records origin myths, ecology,
cultural integration, named individuals, folk warnings, and
encounter shapes.

For statblocks see the source chapters. For encounter design
mandate (the seven shapes; resources tied to physiology), see
`skills/forbidden-lands-bestiary/SKILL.md`. For the wider tonal
rules and anti-trope list, see `tone.md`.

The lore-rule for monsters in this setting:

> **A monster is not a fight. A monster is a contract that the
> village has been keeping, and the contract is now breaking.**

## Abyss Worm

Burrowing worm that swallows people from below. Invaded after the
Demon Flood. Old wives' warning: do not stamp; vibration draws it.
Young worms can be lured into spear traps with drumming. Adult
worms' campsites are protected by pouring lamp oil on the ground —
they hate it.

**Worm saliva** eats through 3 points of Armor Rating per hit;
**jaw stones** grind any dried ingredient to powder.

**Cultural anchor — the Seeded Field:** an Alderlander stead has
fed through two winters when the valley starved because an Abyss
Worm under its field breaks the ground the plow cannot. Each
spring at first plowing, the worm takes one villager — by old lot,
usually someone past work.

## Amoeba

Ancient amorphous mass of scum and shadow. Floats with merged
remains of gods, demigods, creatures, beasts. **Dwarven
tradition** holds amoebas are waste from Huge's forge; an
alternative belief calls them residue of demonic experiment.
Old soldiers say only **bright torment** — fire, light — drives
them back. Sorcerers use **amoeba bile** for transformation work.

## Amphibian

Created by the water goddess **Flow** to guard her sacred springs.
Secretive, reclusive, but fierce against contamination. Hate
demons (whose mog muddies the water). Their **eggs** are a
delicacy (five silver per egg) sought by coastal fishermen and
saurians. Live in vast underwater kingdoms.

## Basilisk

The **Wyrm**'s appointed king of all reptiles, corrupted into an
abomination. Endowed with the divine power to give and take life
by breath, to create and recreate by gaze. Used those powers for
evil and is now considered a vile anomaly. Bound by **cockerel's
egg** coated in toad poison (Potency 7) — destroy the egg and the
basilisk dies. **Alchemists** seek basilisk eggs for "lead-to-gold"
transmutation work. Hunters know that crushing the wrong stone in
a basilisk's lair has ended many a long pursuit.

## Bloodling

Demonic mist-creature of the Blood Mist years. Now rare, found in
mist-pockets. Mythologized as the **vaporized blood of Skuld, the
murdered goddess of revenge**, or as **Heme's breath**. Hurt by
**copper blades only**; iron and steel pass through. Drawn by
quarrels and red haze; fled by torch-flame and the bite of old
copper.

## Bog Man

Restless dead of bog-sacrifice. Throughout history, slaves,
prisoners, and "other unfortunate souls" were buried alive in
wetlands as offerings. Bog men are greedy and drawn to treasure
and magical items thrown into the depths. Marsh wives swear by
torches and old prayers against them.

## Demon

Any creature foreign to the world, summoned by spell or arrived
through a **nexus** — a gate between worlds. Demons are held
together by **mog**, a living substance from the demonic world
**Churm og**. The worst demons are old survivors of the Demon
Flood or spawn of Zygofer's laboratories, **and many still answer
to bargains made in blood.**

A demon is rarely a monster to fight. It is a contract someone
signed, with a beard now three fingers long.

## Death Knight

A very powerful undead with intact will and sense of self.
Regularly devours the heart, liver, brain, or other organs of
humans to keep its strength. Lesser undead obey it. The first
Death Knight was **Teramalda**, made when **Garmar Four-Beard**
cooked her over embers in her own armor in 821 AS. The Rust
Brothers know how to elevate ghouls into Death Knights.

## Dragon

Small, bestial-minded creatures in the Forbidden Lands today,
though legends speak of intelligent giant dragons. All descended
from **Scarne**, the original dragon mother. **Crombe dwarves
claim they slew her**; in fact, she is imprisoned far below
**Stonegarden** in an elastic metallic net that has been weakening
for 1500 years. She is highly intelligent, speaks all languages,
is utterly humorless, and remembers every slight ever done to her
with perfect fidelity. She is furious at the Crombe dwarves. If
freed, her stated intent is to demolish **Dragon's Tooth** mountain,
drop **Scarnesbane** in the ocean so no one can ever use it against
her, and establish herself as dragon matron in the northern
mountains to breed a new generation. The dwelvers below Stonegarden
are debating whether to release her; the net is weakening whether
they act or not. **Drakewyrms** are the smaller cousins \u2014
glittering scales, vain, hungry, can be flattered, worship the
**rainbow as Tincturus** and leave offerings of bone and shiny
things where colors touch the ground. The wizard **Varleyman of
Byard** rode one as a mount.

Gaps open between shed scales \u2014 a careful scout can guide a hard
shot. Dwarven clans remember dragon blood and bone in old songs;
some greater dragons may keep grudges older than human kingdoms.

## Dread Raptor

Created by **Horn** to be the eternal enemy of horses, who bear
flaming manes from the same fire god. **All Bloodmarch rider
clans** test their young by sending them to a dread raptor's
nest. Bringing back feathers earns the right to carry bow and
arrow and earns the rider's name.

## Ent

The first living things **Clay** made. So powerful they threatened
to destroy the rest of creation; Clay rooted vegetation
afterwards. Ents remain as forest shepherds, revered by elves and
Elvenspring. Speak the elven tongue. Some are Stillelves who fused
their ruby with an oak or ash. The death rite: a procession of
five walking trees carrying a sixth toward the Stillmist; the dead
Ent's ruby is mounted at Rosenstaad.

Ents judge; they do not always forgive. Aggressive Ents are
extremely dangerous adversaries.

## Gatekeeper

Cosmic guardians of empty spaces and free flow of life between
worlds. Strayed into the Forbidden Lands, drawn by Zygofer's
demonology and the tears in **Shadowgate Pass**. Test passers
through telepathic challenges with riddles of suns, moons, runes,
shadows, and geometric figures. **Druidic knowledge** grants +1
to resisting their challenges.

## Ghost

Wraiths or ghosts of the incorporeal dead, pinned to where they
died by an unfinished thing or strong emotion. Only the **holy
purging** of a Raven Sister, or answering the wrong, lays them.
Fire and old rites drive a ghost back for a season, never longer.
Ghosts may possess statues, doors, bells, or tools where memory
has hardened.

For walking dead in villages, see Undead below.

## Giant

Said once to have roamed Ravenland in great multitude. The
**Nightwalker** slew most in their sleep during the **Night of
Cadavers** at the Raven god's request, to make room for smaller
creatures. Survivors are one of the original kin and live very
long lives. **Single weak spot on the top of the head** —
skalds sing of the climb and the blow struck downward.

## Giant Specter

Ancient titans, possibly never alive in the conventional sense.
Possibly the dark side of eternal life — dead twins of immortal
elves. Or the Red Wanderer's tormenting spirits, sent to keep
humans in check. Strange affinity with elves; sometimes they speak
ancient but understandable human tongue and can hint at lost
elven artifacts.

## Giant Spider

Spawn of the **Nightwalker**, possibly his first. Spinning his
dreams. Sworn enemies of demons and Misgrown. Spinning toward
the great battle for creation. Tunneling labyrinths may include
a **portal to the old gods**. Ancient females are telepathic and
read minds to locate victims. **Temples of the Spider God** —
overgrown ruins with the Nightwalker's spider-mark — are real.

The historical battle of **Menkaura**, the spider queen who
ruled **Dankwood between Entwater and the Crombe river**, ended
when twenty war Ents broke her queendom. Menkaura's carapace was
brought to **Stridehome** and made into jewelry, weapons, and
**Menkaura's Tooth**.

## Giant Squid

Cave-dwelling. Larvae press through cracks for years until they
find space to grow. Adults secrete pheromones that attract more
squid; colonies result. Camouflage on rock walls; exude black ink
that darkens water. **Hammer or club to the bulb between the arms
ends a squid faster than any blade.**

## Gray Bear

Hunter that stalks chosen targets without haste. **Demonic origin
suspected:** during the winter of **Feulenmark** after the Demon
Flood, hunters tracking what looked like a flayed ogre found a
Gray Bear's den; only two brothers survived. Sightings have
increased since.

## Greater Golem

Dwarven binding-craft. The forehead rune means **traitor** in old
dwarvish; carries the soul within stone; marks the place where the
binding can be undone. The first golem was **Belial the
Loose-Tongued**, mastersmith of Huge who carried the secret of
steel to elves and humans. He stands eternal guard outside the
divine forge.

Used now by Alderlander lords to protect mausoleums. Sites: tombs
of the **Gordian Empire** (from the Divided Years), entries to
dwarven crypts along the **sunken lane**.

## Gryphon

**Zygofer's experimental winged cavalry** for Iron Guards. So
territorial that only three or four of one family can be kept
together — more and they fight to the death. Hunt anything; love
**horse meat**, smell it from far off, lose their wits in
excitement. Can be lured into traps with horseflesh.

## Harpies

Created by **Zygofer and the Rust Brothers from captured Raven
Sisters** — corrupted through torture and magic, fused with bird-of-prey
bodies (the raven was too weak to support their weight) and joined
to **mog**. Other villages call them cursed mothers. Weak in open
combat; prefer betrayal; flee when bloodied.

## Hydra

A **demonological experiment escaped from Zygofer's laboratories**.
Bifurcates after acquiring six or more heads. A druid swore she
saw a five-necked hydra slaughter one of its own heads in
**Arina Forest** and split into two three-headed hydras.

**Lye, fire, red-hot iron** — anything that scorches a stump
closed will hold it. Without it, every cut neck gives two and the
beast may someday divide.

## Imp

Winged pests that feed on arcane discharge. Drain sorcerers'
power. **Cold-worked iron** turns them back; **certain old prayers**
repel them. **Nicodemus of Fire and Flame**, House Winterbane's
arch mage, subdued one with a **horseshoe** and bound it as a
familiar and chess opponent. Spell over-charges (three or more 1s)
attract D3 imps automatically.

## Insectoids

Giant insect-like humanoids that arrived in the last 300 years —
Demon Flood arrivals or normal insects possessed by demons. Hive
intelligence increases with colony size. **The elf provisioner
Mazmer** had philosophical conversations with an Insectoid queen
in her chamber. Mazmer's method (smear oneself with worker innards
to mask the smell) was tried by at least two adventurers who
were never seen again.

Kill the queen to root out a colony. If a second queen is born,
the colony swarms — half wanders off to nest elsewhere.

## Iron Dragon

The **antithesis of forging and engineering**. A primordial force
of decay, gnawing at the world's roots, eroding bedrock with
treacherous snake-holes. Every dwarf considers it a sworn enemy.
Magnetically attracted to dwarven societies; **dwelvers may control
them** to attack dwarven holds. The **Meromannian** clan produces
specialized **dragon slayers** with battle-axe, iron chain, and
anchor. Long neck respiratory organs are the vulnerable point;
spear-work beats hammer.

## Mara

A nightmare creature with no physical form. Sometimes appears as
a demonic grinning cat, sometimes a shriveled boar-bristled
corpse — **the sleeper recognizes the face of his nemesis** in
its features. **Aslene dream sorcerers (oneiromancers)** can
summon maras; the summoned mara always bears the dream sorcerer's
features. Old village wives keep a candle and charm by the bed.

## Manticore

Demonologically merged from **snakes, lions, humans, sharks, and
porcupines**. **Cubs have been observed** — alarmingly. Their
**barbs** are key ingredient in a paralysis salve.

## Minotaur

Origin: the young hermit **Zygofer**, in the demon-haunted
wilderness, brought a cow for milking. Possessed by demonic lust
and rage, he conceived the first Minotaurs on her. They burst
fetal-membraned to the woods and bred their own kin. **Some
minotaur bloodlines still gather near places of old sorcery.**

## Mire Drake

A **vile creature, more cunning and vicious than the most
black-hearted sea serpent**. Worshipped as a god and oracle by
local people: the **mussel gatherers of the Blush** seek the
counsel of an eloquent mire drake known for prophecies on weather,
childbirth, war, and mussel plague. Some mire drakes orchestrate
oracle cults, extracting child sacrifice in exchange for
prophecy.

## Mummy

Restless lords of ages past, made by **ancient burial rites**
that turned their bodies into preserved predators thirsty for
**salts and juices** of the living. Bound by death magic to
their burial chambers; do not follow far from their bones. Open
flame is historically effective. Lord-mummies retain courtly
hierarchies — embalmed servants attend a princess.

## Nature Spirit

The old gods inhabit landscape: earth, mud, marshes, pastures,
fields, waters, bridges, paths, trails, crossroads. Govern fate
at the crossroads moments. **Sevenfold vengeance** for betrayal;
**seven years of need** for forgotten sacrifice. Sites: cairns,
gates, field idols of woven straw, crossroads with crowns of
woven twigs and broken antlers. Offerings: blood for pest-protection;
shiny things; coins; rings.

## Nightwarg

Result of the **Dreamer in Falender**'s working with the
**Nightwalker**: a predatory darkness woven against the demon
hordes that became its own evil. Made of condensed darkness; only
appear at night; eyes shine like pale full moons; cold breath;
leave no tracks even in fresh snow. Their bite **freezes flesh
to ice — not even a drop of blood falls from the victim**. See
also Wolfshadow.

## Possessor

Sheets of demonic Blood Mist that survived the lifting by
inhabiting host bodies. Scholars call them **vanths** — the
mythological huntresses of the night who, in the Nightwalker's
name, invade bodies and turn them into beasts of the red mist.
Same vulnerabilities as the Mist itself.

## Rat King

Servant of the demon lord **Pox Yersinia**. Carries a **bone
flute carved from the demon goddess's cadaver** that commands
rats, fleas, carrion birds; brings the **Blue Plague** to villages.
Druids and healers are the rat king's particular dread.

## Rock Troll

Trolls who have partially turned to stone over eons. **Woodland
druids mistakenly revere them as spirits of the living rock** —
they are not. They are colossal cruel, ravenous, irritable filth
with cracked stone hearts. **Dwarves desire their stone hearts and
the precious metals within their bodies**, and stories say greedy
dwarves **lure each other into rock troll territories** to feed
the beasts for future slaughter and mining. Strange carvings in
rocks and trees mark their ranges — claw-marks or troll-runes
addressed to underworld powers.

## Sea Serpent

Punctures hulls and snatches crew during repair. **Tail blood
maddens it** — its genital glands are in the tail. The pirate
**Scarda** cut three sea serpents in the tail and they ate each
other until only a giant ring of teeth remained on the water;
this is the source of the **giant sea urchins of the Eastern
Sea** and the shrine called **Scarda's Teeth**, kept by a
village for seven generations on a bay shore.

## Shapeshifter

Slimy gelatinous creature. Mimicry of intelligent speech is
clumsy; that is the tell. Trail of **mottled gray slime**.
**King Algavard II** married one — the night before the wedding
the beast killed his bride **Transmagulda** and stood in for
her. The court sorcerer found slimy marks in her chamber.

## Skolopendra

Underground predator of abyss worms, basilisks, and drakewyrms.
Folk names: **nightcrawler, poison hog, underbeast**. Lays eggs
inside ample-bodied hosts. Hunts at the water's edge of rivers
and shallow streams; lairs in maze-like burrows in the banks.

## Strangling Vine

**Escaped experiment from a demonological laboratory.** Hides
among ordinary greenery; some uproot and walk slowly, leaving
false safe glades. Central trunk is buried; **violet flowers**
in constant bloom are its sensory organs. Drags prey to the
trunk, which extends suction-feet to dissolve and absorb.

## Swarming Death

Vast deadly cloud carried by the **western wind** over mountains
and woods. No mind, no consciousness, only hunger. Possible
origin: faraway **Dragaland**; kin to Insectoids; or emanations
from beyond the demon gate at Shadowgate Pass. **Fire is the
weapon** — collective consciousness fears what it cannot share.

## Troll

Born underground; gluttonous; surface in search of food. Eat
anything chewable: fresh innards, foul carrion no fox would
touch, farm refuse, latrine. Use simple weapons and tools; grunt
to communicate; lack self-preservation. Sensitive to sunlight;
**fully susceptible to healing magic**. **Vicious dwarven rumor:
dwarves lure trolls to human villages with music because certain
rare minerals only form in trolls who have eaten human flesh.**

## Tupilaq

Necromantic assassin-construct of the **Order of the Silent**.
Raised from merged corpses and carcasses; hates itself; cannot
rest until the target is wiped out — not just killed, but every
trace erased. Their deaths yield a life-giving elixir.

## Twisted Ent

When the Demon Flood swept the forests, mog tangled with the roots
of trees. Some Ents despaired so completely that **their rubies
shriveled into black lumps containing demonic larvae**, and their
crowns became tentacles. The **Rust Brothers** use Twisted Ents
in sieges as a secret weapon. **Beautiful music** halts a Twisted
Ent — fragments of its pre-corruption life return through it.

## Undead — Walkers, Skeletons, Ghouls

A natural phenomenon. The dead "tend to keep walking about in a
state of confusion before finding their final rest" and are
treated with respectful courtesy. Most decompose to skeletons over
time. **Some become ghouls who draw power from other bodies.**

The **Rust Brothers' practice** of weaponizing the restless dead
is widely disliked.

Burial-ground rules: speak gently as to a child; never show them
their mirror image (it can make them aggressive); they are
attracted to shiny things; firmly dissuade them from returning to
the village.

## Undead Dragon

**"The winged lord of restless death"** in royal chronicles of
the Alder Wars. Reigns over mass graves and corpse pits. Holds
court with skeletons and ghouls; entertains with poetry and
music. Life essence bound in dwindling **bone marrow stores**;
feeds on the marrow of fresh undead.

## Vampyr

Believe themselves **mankind's firstborn**, charged by **Rust**
to keep humanity's god-given supremacy through blood. Worship
Rust; **see Zytera as a false prophet** and the Rust Brothers as
the corrupted ones. Need blood daily. Mass-hypnotize the
weak-minded; control up to D10 humanoids.

## Water Troll

Cousin of the rock troll, lurks in seaweed at water's edge.
Cunning and devious. Marks territory with **scratch-carvings or
troll-runes** addressed to underworld powers. Preserves victims
in crevices to age the flesh to its preferred consistency.

## Whiner

See `kin.md`. Bestiary classification reflects how
hunters treat them — as creatures, not as kin — but the lore
remembers their humanoid intelligence and grief.

## Will-o'-the-Wisp

**Ghosts of druids, Raven Sisters, and other holy figures burned
to death** for their religious work. Trauma transformed them into
vengeful spirits. Appear as huge floating skulls surrounded by
flame. Bound to a **charred tree, abandoned cauldron, or
woodpile** — the execution site. Lure victims into death traps in
swamps. **Burning the dwelling-place permanently banishes them**;
otherwise they return each evening.

## Wolfshadow

The **Dreamer in Falender** sought the **Nightwalker**'s help to
weave a predatory darkness against demons. The result was the
first wolfshadows and nightwargs. Some say the Dreamer himself
was the first wolfshadow. Wolfshadow possesses humanoids and over
years transforms them into **nightwargs** — wolflike humanoids of
condensed darkness. The transformation can be broken on the night
of a **full moon** by a critical injury inflicted with a weapon
**consecrated to the Nightwalker**.

## Wyvern

Probable immigrant species; predatory lizard. Lives in crags near
wetlands. Attacks in flocks; tears prey apart and carries pieces
home. **Barbed-chain weapons** snag in the leathery wings. Bite is
toothed and infectious. **Energy-laden blubber under the skin**
keeps them warm in winter; tailors sew warm cloaks from the
hide and the blubber is flavorful and nutritious. **Get them to
the ground** — they are clumsy and almost defenseless on land.

## Krasjika

A dragon mother referenced in the opening of the Book of Beasts.
**"The spawns' mother Krasjika has already rotted, but refuses
to leave this world out of love for her offspring and human
flesh."** Encountered by **Shieldmaiden Armanda** in legend.

## Raven's Purge Creatures

The following entries originate from the Raven's Purge source.
Statblocks are in `campaigns/ravens_purge.md`; these entries hold
the lore and ecology.

## Calydone

A fire-breathing boar — demonic animal produced through
**Brinhelda**'s fire essences and Zertorme's experimental program
at Amber's Peak. Released onto the surrounding plains as a field
weapon and ecological weapon simultaneously. Not domesticated;
not controllable. Larger and longer-legged than a natural boar.

## Dwelver

Pale, chalk-white, roughly halfling-sized. The ancient ancestors
of the dwarves. Live 1000 meters and more underground in the
elder layers beneath Stonegarden. Can partially or completely
soften their skeletons to press through cracks one inch wide.
Cannot speak at surface level (air too thin for their vocal
anatomy); speak fluently at 200m depth or below. Cannot see the
sun or touch above-ground land without harm.

Master Stone Singers; metallurgists beyond any living craftsman.
Can teach the **skeleton-softening** technique to above-ground
Stone Singers who earn their trust. Possibly able to control
iron dragons; this is uncertain even to dwarven scholars.

On encounter in the depths: cold, clammy skin; apparent age
indeterminate between forty and four hundred years. Below two
hundred meters they speak continuously — running commentary on
everything present, every opinion aired, every observation made.
Above ground they produce no sounds at all; the anatomy will not
allow it. They regard the surface dwarves with contemptuous
familiarity, like a grandparent who has been proved right about
everything and expects acknowledgment. Their sense of humor is
ecentric to the degree of being untranslatable. Can fashion tin
rope ladders: treated with diffundants, the ladder hardens to
steel strength for half an hour. The treatment is repeatable.

## Erosia

A demonic entity assembled from cobbled human parts fused with
mog from Churmog. First impression: a flesh-colored, shapeless
toad the size of a horse. On close inspection the individual
components retain their own beauty; the whole does not reflect
the parts. **If one can look past the big picture, it is possible
to find a certain beauty and grace in the individual parts.**

Personality adjusts to match the visitor. Always friendly, always
inviting. Will not defend itself if attacked — responds instead
by citing poetry about the beautiful mortality of life. Masters
several musical instruments and other arts. Merigall uses Erosia
as a host and receptionist in the pavilion.

## Marga

Zygofer and his wife Martea's demonified daughter. She was a
girl who absorbed mog before she was ready; the result is a
massive flesh-mass that fills the deepest chamber of the
**Stoneloom Mines**. Her avatar \u2014 the only part of her that
retains her original appearance \u2014 is a girl of eleven years,
brown-haired, sullen, with disturbed and willful intelligence.
The avatar is her heart. **Marga can only be killed by impaling
her avatar's heart.** Destroying the mass does nothing
permanently. She has the personality of a child who learned
the world will give her whatever she devours, and has been
devouring for a long time.

The Saurians trapped in the mines worship her as the **Goddess
of the Mines**; she treats them as pets and sometimes gives them
prey she has caught to feed on. She still plays. Her idea of
an introduction is 'You're it!' Her lullabies: *Sister, brother,
how we wax. Mother, father sharpens the axe.* Above all other
things, she wants a playmate her own age. This is a worse
problem than it sounds.

## Martea

Zygofer's wife. She followed his path too closely and was
absorbed by an Abyss Worm she could not control. Now fused with
it — her disfigured face emerges from a slit at the front of
the worm's body. Additional slits along the body hold the faces
of creatures she has 'saved' — all of them terrified, all still
screaming for help.

Martea carries a lock of Zygofer's hair against her face and
addresses it as her husband. Between encounters involving blood,
she is rational and maternal. When she smells blood, the worm's
nature takes over and she attacks without exception. She
considers herself a good mother. She is in the Stoneloom Mines.

## Ore-Weller

Mobile mineral fusiform living in the deep structures below
Stonegarden. Devours raw materials and reshapes itself from
them. Not a living being in any organic sense; no organs of
any kind. Immune to ordinary weapons — every hit with a
metallic weapon degrades the weapon by 1. Can only be destroyed
by complete dispersal: sufficient explosive force to scatter
the mass prevents reformation; isolated fragments do not
reconstitute. Dissolves metals in humanoid tissue with acid
secretions. Distracted reliably by gold coins — its preferred
material; will stop to absorb them, ignoring other targets.

## Pyrotaur

Humanoid with a bull's head; fire-immune; breathes fire.
Produced by Zertorme through a program using fire essences
from the demon **Brinhelda**. Semi-intelligent; completely
loyal to Zertorme. Released onto the plains near Amber's Peak
alongside calydones.
## Scarne

A female dragon, imprisoned in the deep structures below
Stonegarden for 1500 years in a net that is now weakening.
The Crombe dwarves built the Dragon's Tooth fortress along the
stone-clad ribs of her body before she was imprisoned, mistaking
her for dead. She knows this.

Completely devoid of humor. Interprets every joke and attempt
at irony as a literal statement; has not developed the capacity
to model otherwise. Remembers every perceived slight without
exception, across centuries. The Crombe dwarves have accumulated
a significant account. Post-release plans, finalized over 1500
years: demolish Dragon's Tooth, drop Scarnesbane in the ocean
far from any land, travel north of the Forbidden Lands to become
matron dragon to her younger kin.
## Stone Titan

Twelve-meter black obsidian giant. No higher intelligence. Four
are sealed in obsidian alcoves at **Pelagia**. Wakened by the
hammer **Scarnesbane** or by the artifact **Scarnesclaw**
(which scratches them awake). Controlled by **Maha** sign-tablets;
without instructions they wander randomly. Stop after 60 days
of activation or when encountering a mountain barrier. Per druid
legend, the stone titans came from the sea with the first humans.
They are the druids' final deterrent.

## Swinehound

A demon-tainted crossbreed \u2014 part dog, part unknown \u2014 sold
by Rust Brothers as guardians and war-hounds. Fed on human
flesh in the kennels; they will not accept animal meat once
trained. The **Demonic Bite** passes infection: Virulence 5.
The bite does not heal normally until the demonic element is
purged.
