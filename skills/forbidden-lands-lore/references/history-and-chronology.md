# History and Chronology

The current year is **1165 AS** (After the Shift). The Blood Mist
lifted in **1160 AS**, five years ago. This is the baseline "now"
for any manuscript prose unless explicitly stated otherwise.

## The Calendar

Eight phases of the year, each roughly six weeks:

| # | Phase | Season-feel |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Springrise | Thaw, mud, lambing |
| 2 | Springwane | Late spring, planting |
| 3 | Summerrise | Long days, early warmth |
| 4 | Summerwane | Hay, dust, drought-risk |
| 5 | Fallrise | Harvest |
| 6 | Fallwane | Slaughter, salting |
| 7 | Winterrise | First snow, fires lit |
| 8 | Winterwane | Hunger, ice, wolves |

**Festivals:** Awakening Day (start of Springrise), Lushday
(midsummer green), Harvest Day (Fallrise), Rotday (Fallwane,
when the dead are remembered), Midwinter, Midsummer. **Springturn**
and **Fallturn** mark equinoxes. Most villages keep at least
Awakening, Harvest, Midwinter, and Rotday.

Use these names in prose. Never write "spring" or "autumn"
generically.

## The Chronology

Years count from the Shift (the human arrival in Ravenland)
1,165 years ago. Key events:

### The Mythic Past (before 0 AS)

- The god **Clay** creates living things; the Ents are the first,
  and so powerful Clay must root the rest of vegetation as a
  precaution. Ents remain as forest shepherds.
- **Scarne**, the dragon mother, rules in Ravenland. The dwarven
  commander **Oramund**, with the hammer **Scarnesbane** (legend:
  sent by Huge), defeats her. The truth is uglier: Scarne was
  not killed but imprisoned beneath what is now **Stonegarden**.
- The **Nightwalker** slays most of the giants in their sleep
  during the **Night of Cadavers** at the Raven god's request.
- The dwarves of the **Crombe** clan settle on Dragon's Tooth at
  Scarnehall, raised from the dragon's stone-clad ribs.

### The Shift and Settlement (0 AS onward)

- **0 AS:** Humans (the **Frailers' ancestors** and the original
  Alderlander stock) arrive across the ocean from a homeland
  destroyed by giant earthworms. The **Raven god** leads them.
  They become rivals to the native elves, dwarves, and orcs.
- The **Raven god** divides the land between the kin to keep
  the peace. The dwarves and elves take hostages from the humans;
  these hostages' descendants become the **Elvenspring**
  (half-elves on the elf side) and the **ogres** (offspring of
  humans and dwarves). The humans take elven hostages also; their
  descendants in Alderland become the **Frailers**.
- Tensions never ease. Conflict between humans and the older kin
  becomes the chronic state of the world.

### The Ailander Exodus (around 510–540 AS)

- The **Ailanders**, persecuted in Alderland for their worship
  of the **Raven god** under the priesthood of the **Raven
  Sisters**, flee north into Ravenland.
- They cross over **Mount Bilica** in **550 AS** and settle in
  the lands that will later be called the Forbidden Lands.

### The Aslenes Arrive (around 610 AS)

- The **Quard** clan from Aslene, fleeing harsh clan wars, comes
  through **Shadowgate Pass** with horses bred from the magma of
  **Horn**'s volcano. They are riders, hierarchical, devout to
  the fire-god Horn. They mate with the Ailanders over generations.
- **620 AS:** The **Vond Wall** is raised by the Aslenes against
  further incursions from the south.

### The Iron League and the Long Conflict (around 800 AS)

- **800 AS:** The **Iron League** forms; **Teramalda** invades.
  Teramalda's living armor is the truth behind the legend of
  **Feroxa's Claws** — see `artifacts-and-named-relics.md`.
- **820–821 AS:** **Lumra** falls. The dwarven lord **Garmar
  Four-Beard** has the woman who would become the wandering
  armor cooked over embers in her own steel. Her armor wakes;
  she becomes the first **Death Knight**.
- The **Glethra** gold mines are seized.
- **833 AS:** **Etarik Hammerhand**, ambassador from the
  **Meromannians** to **Alderstone**, delivers his line: _"Your
  place is not here, everyone hates you, and you do not even
  like each other."_

### The Alder Wars and the Demon Flood (860s–880s AS)

- **865 AS:** **Wailer's Hold**, the Meromannian dwarves' mining
  city, falls to demons and Alderlanders and is abandoned.
- **869 AS:** **Falender** is burned. The **Dreamer in Falender**
  — hermit worshipper of the Nightwalker — sought to weave a
  predatory darkness against the demon hordes; the result was
  the first **wolfshadows** and **nightwargs**, and the Dreamer
  himself may have been the first wolfshadow.
- **874 AS:** **King Algarod**, the **Demon Flood**. King Algarod
  falls in the Fourth Alder Wars. The ground that becomes **The
  Hollows** is his battlefield and burial cairn.
- **875 AS:** The western parts of Aslene are razed by Zygofer's
  demonic hordes. The **Galdane** clan flees east into Margelda
  and is given land by **Zertorme**.
- **878 AS:** The **Iron Lock** is built across the southern
  passes by the Alderlanders. **The Silent** stand guard on it.
  The land north of the Lock becomes _the Forbidden Lands_.
- **880 AS:** **Zytera** rises to power. He breaks elven rubies
  for his demonic experiments.
- **883 AS:** The **Rust Brothers** are founded.

### The Blood Mist (around 900–1160 AS)

- The Blood Mist descends over the whole land. For roughly
  ten generations, no traveler crosses it safely. Villages
  hunker down behind walls; news does not pass; trade dies.
- **Bloodlings** (demonic mist-creatures, sometimes said to be
  the vaporized blood of the murdered goddess Skuld, or Heme's
  breath) hunt during the mist.
- The **Wolfkin** travel safely through the Mist in forests; they
  call it _the breath of the Huntress_. The Rust Brothers are
  also untouched. Both groups would prefer the Mist to return.

### The Mist Lifts (1160 AS)

- The Mist lifts because the demon **Merigall** sings a song of
  homesickness to the bloodlings, drawing them back through the
  nexus. The land is open again. Roads are bones; old claims
  return; old grudges return; refugees move; news moves.
- **The party of any campaign exists in the five years since.**

### Now (1165 AS)

- **Tormund Halfhand** stirs the Meromannian dwarves toward
  reclaiming Wailer's Hold by force.
- **Emperor Hroka the First and the Greatest** of the **Urhur**
  orc clan rules from an abandoned elven city, calls himself
  emperor of the orcs, and has gifted his wife **Soria** the
  elven ruby **Iridne** — currently inhabited by the elf within
  it, who appears in her dreams.
- **General Archa** of the **Roka** orcs is the most respected
  orc in the Forbidden Lands and worries about his own sons.
- **Eldag the Ravager** of the **Isir** orcs raids dwarven lands.
- **Zytera** holds **Alderstone** and the western lands; Misgrown
  cluster around him. The **Iron Guard** ranges from his priories.
- **Zertorme** at **Amber's Peak** is hailed by Quard prophets as
  Horn's chosen savior; pilgrim Quards flock to him.
- **Zygofer**, ancient Frailer sorcerer, broods in his lair. The
  half-elf **Therania** plots; the demon **Merigall** moves
  freely; the demon lord **Krasylla** is named in fear.
- **King Turik** of **Belder** keeps Belderand at arm's length;
  his four heirs — **Rigalda Beak**, the stone-singer **Berwyld**,
  **Gandeman Stone**, and the dreamer **Trondeval** — wait.
- **King Karonax** and his queen **Sulma the Iron Bitch** rule
  the **Canide** dwarves at **Stonegarden**.
- **King Tademir Stonefriend** of the **Crombe** dwarves keeps
  **Scarnehall** on Dragon's Tooth.
- The **Reapenter** sect (leader **Badalar the Butcher**, with
  the elf **Father Geno**) hunts surviving humans across the land.
- The **Rust Lord Kartorda** leads the Misgrown high priests.

## How to Use This Chronology in Prose

- Reference dates only when an old object, an old person, or an
  old grievance forces the date into the scene. Most prose should
  reference _the Mist years_, _before the wars_, _the Demon Flood_,
  not numerical AS.
- "The Alder Wars" is a generic label; specify _Fourth Alder
  Wars_ if you mean Algarod and the Demon Flood, _Second Alder
  Wars_ if you mean Hollerbellows and Wail's Horn.
- Living memory of the Mist is the rule, not the exception. Older
  villagers remember the Mist as their childhood weather. Younger
  villagers are the first generation born after.
- Most named historical figures above are **still alive** in 1165
  AS. Elves live thousands of years; dwarves live centuries; even
  Death Knights and undead artifacts persist. A scene in a Crombe
  hall may include an elder who fought at Wailer's Hold.
