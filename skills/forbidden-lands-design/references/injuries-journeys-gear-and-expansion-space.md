## Critical Injuries As A Design Category

Critical injuries are where the game reveals whether it truly values consequence over simple defeat.

They are not just punishment tables.
They are campaign-shaping conversion rules that turn:

- scene loss
- bad risk decisions
- or accumulated attrition

into lasting narrative and mechanical cost.

### Table structure in the current manuscript

Observed row counts:

| Table | Rows |
| ----- | ---- |
| Slash Wounds | 19 |
| Blunt Force | 17 |
| Stab Wounds | 17 |
| Burn | 27 |
| Horror | 36 |

Immediate reading:

- `Burn` and `Horror` have much broader consequence space than the physical weapon tables
- the manuscript already treats these as qualitatively different forms of catastrophe

### Design jobs of critical injuries

Critical injuries currently serve five jobs:

1. preserve lethality without making every defeat immediate death
2. make damage type matter after the fight
3. create care burden and time loss
4. produce lingering impairment
5. create retirement or death thresholds in the harshest cases

### Severity bands

Even without formal labels, the tables function in four bands.

#### Band I: transient setback

Characteristics:

- short healing window
- limited action restriction
- mostly tactical inconvenience

Examples:

- social penalty injuries
- weapon-grip penalties
- temporary running penalties

#### Band II: serious temporary impairment

Characteristics:

- multiple days of impact
- reliable loss of some tactical options
- still clearly playable

#### Band III: campaign-shaping injury

Characteristics:

- major healing time
- strong limitation
- changes how the character is played for a meaningful stretch

#### Band IV: terminal or retirement-default result

Characteristics:

- lethal timer
- persistent agency collapse
- catastrophic identity change
- practical end of adventuring in many campaigns

### Why the injury system works

The current structure is good because it separates:

- “you lost the scene”
- from “you lost the character”

That gives the campaign texture.

The player can survive and still pay dearly.

### Main design risks in injury work

#### False playability

An injury is presented as technically survivable, but in practice leaves the character without useful agency.

This is not automatically bad.
It becomes bad when the text pretends otherwise.

#### Overcompression by healing exceptions

If too many talents, spells, or recovery rules erase injury time or severity, the whole injury system becomes cosmetic.

#### Over-modern naming or categorization

If injuries become too clinical or too abstract, they lose manuscript fit.
If they become too vague, they lose table precision.

### Best design use of critical injuries

Good new injury design should:

- tell the truth about playability
- connect penalties to existing rules
- distinguish damage types honestly
- preserve both fear and survival

## Journeys As A Pressure Engine

Journeys are not filler between encounters.
They are the game's largest sustained attrition machine.

### Main pressure levers in journeys

1. Quarter Day allocation
2. terrain friction
3. darkness and weather
4. forage and hunt uncertainty
5. mishaps
6. camp quality
7. interrupted rest
8. conditions

### Quarter Day economy

The strongest feature of the journey system is that time is divided into actionable units.

That forces tradeoffs between:

- moving
- scouting
- foraging
- hunting
- surveying
- making camp
- resting
- sleeping

This is good design because “time spent staying alive” is still play.

### Why journeys feel tense

The journey chapter pressures the party in three simultaneous currencies:

- time
- resources
- condition risk

That means even a successful expedition can arrive depleted.

### Positive design features

#### Every support action costs movement

Forage, hunt, survey, and camp-making all compete with direct progress.

This prevents logistics from becoming free upside.

#### Mishaps are not uniform damage packets

Journey mishaps hit:

- time
- gear
- cold exposure
- disease
- position
- morale

That creates more varied expedition stories than plain HP loss.

#### Camp quality matters

Camp is a keystone mechanic because it affects:

- recovery
- concealment
- comfort
- exposure to further mishaps

### Structural weaknesses to watch

#### Rule sprawl by optional modules

Journeys already carry many optional and expanded subrules.
New additions should deepen decisions, not add more disconnected tables.

#### Hidden GM burden

The chapter has many moving parts.
If a new rule adds another check without replacing anything, the chapter may become too heavy to run cleanly.

### Best expansion directions for journeys

- stronger camp roles
- travel formation and burden tradeoffs
- better mundane field medicine during travel
- logistical choices tied to weather, not just penalties
- clearer risk/reward travel stances

## Gear As A Design System

Gear is more than a shopping list.
It is the material layer of the game's problem-solving logic.

### Major gear categories visible in the chapter

1. carrying and containment gear
2. expedition trade goods
3. weapons
4. shields and armor
5. clothing and protection
6. transport
7. tools
8. raw materials
9. animals and buildings
10. profession-crafted items
11. artifacts
12. inventor items

Observed structural fact:

- the chapter has `108` section headings at level 3 or 4

This is one of the broadest design spaces in the book.

### Design jobs of gear

Gear currently does four major things:

1. raises success odds
2. gates actions or professions
3. converts money and materials into reliability
4. externalizes preparedness into visible objects

### Strong gear patterns already present

#### Tool gating

Some work is impossible or sharply penalized without proper tools.

This is good because:

- it protects profession niches
- it makes preparation tangible

#### Resource-die gear

Items like arrows, torches, and makeshift tools use depletion models instead of static permanence.

This is one of the cleanest ways the manuscript models usage pressure.

#### Material differentiation

Advanced crafting sections distinguish materials by:

- bonus
- difficulty
- weight
- durability
- climate implications

This adds design depth without inventing a second combat engine.

### Gear-system tensions

The best gear rules in this manuscript sit between:

- practical usefulness
- scarcity
- specialist access
- repair burden

If gear becomes too available, talents lose niche.
If it becomes too rare, preparation loses meaning because access becomes arbitrary.

### Missing or underdeveloped gear niches

The chapter is broad, but some areas can still deepen cleanly:

- mundane field surgery and splinting tools
- camp maintenance kits
- hauling and load-distribution tools
- weatherproofing supplies
- low-tier social or trade presentation gear
- clearer expedition-grade versus settlement-grade craft gear

## Underdeveloped Design Spaces

These are the most promising spaces for deepening play without betraying the engine.

### 1. Logistics under duress

Current strength:

- encumbrance and travel already matter

Gap:

- limited structured choices for load redistribution, haul aids, pack discipline, and emergency abandonment decisions

### 2. Mundane field medicine

Current strength:

- healing exists
- injuries matter

Gap:

- there is room between “bandages” and “magic” for more grounded, travel-usable medical procedure and equipment

### 3. Camp discipline

Current strength:

- making camp matters

Gap:

- camp is mostly one roll plus mishaps
- there is room for camp posture choices such as concealment, warmth, speed, dryness, watchfulness, and comfort

### 4. Social leverage through equipment and preparation

Current strength:

- some clothing and perfume matter

Gap:

- the game could support more low-key social preparation tools without turning into a social-combat minigame

### 5. Hirelings and work delegation

Current strength:

- strongholds imply labor

Gap:

- expedition labor outside the stronghold remains underformalized

### 6. Non-magical weather adaptation

Current strength:

- cold and shelter matter

Gap:

- there is room for better non-magical choices around insulation, drying, waterproofing, and heat management

## Expansion Principles

These are the rules for adding depth without causing bloat.

### Principle 1: expand through decisions, not extra bookkeeping

A good addition gives the player a sharper choice.
It should not merely add another maintenance roll.

### Principle 2: preserve existing niches

New rules should not make:

- healing talents
- camp talents
- crafting talents
- specialist tools

feel redundant.

### Principle 3: keep the pressure stack intact

New material should not erase:

- time pressure
- resource pressure
- injury pressure
- travel pressure

unless it is expensive enough to matter.

### Principle 4: prefer extensions of current grammar

Good additions usually use:

- Quarter Days
- resource dice
- existing skill checks
- existing conditions
- existing gear bonus structure

rather than new abstract tracks.

### Principle 5: deepen the middle, not only the extremes

The game already has:

- trivial tasks
- catastrophic outcomes

The best expansions often improve the middle:

- hard travel choices
- partial readiness
- temporary mitigation
- expensive safety

### Recommended near-term expansion directions

If the goal is depth with manuscript fit, prioritize:

1. field medicine and injury stabilization gear
2. camp posture and weatherproofing choices
3. logistical support gear for hauling and shelter
4. clearer expedition versus workshop tool distinctions
5. non-magical survival tradeoffs that cost time, weight, or materials
