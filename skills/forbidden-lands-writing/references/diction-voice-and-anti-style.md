## Lexical Tendencies

The manuscript prefers words with physical consequence.

Sample chapter signals from the corpus:

- `08-journeys.md` is saturated with `cold`, `weather`, `fire`, and terrain-material terms
- `10-gear.md` is saturated with `iron`, `fire`, `stone`, `grip`, and `hand`
- `02-your-adventurer.md` and `05-combat-and-damage.md` retain blood, fear, hand, and bodily phrasing even in explanatory contexts

This confirms the underlying diction pattern:

- the book thinks in body, weather, material, labor, and danger

## Verb Preferences

The manuscript prefers verbs that imply physical action or concrete change.

Prefer verbs like:

- cut
- drag
- bind
- haul
- split
- twist
- break
- bruise
- climb
- carry

Avoid abstract explanatory verbs when a concrete one exists.

Prefer:

- “break the line”

over:

- “disrupt the situation”

Prefer:

- “the chill stiffens your body”

over:

- “the cold has a negative effect”

## Modifier Density

The manuscript is not adjective-free, but it is selective.

Typical successful pattern:

- one strong noun and one useful modifier

Typical failed pattern:

- stacked mood adjectives
- generic fantasy intensifiers
- decorative adverbs

### Practical rule

If a phrase has:

- two adjectives
- one abstract noun
- one weak verb

it can almost always be improved by replacing the whole cluster with one sharper noun or verb.

## Punctuation Restraint

The manuscript prefers plain punctuation over performance punctuation.

Good habits:

- colon for definition
- list for comparison
- em dash only when it earns a turn
- parentheses sparingly

Bad habits:

- excessive parenthetical explanation
- dramatic punctuation as mood substitute
- semicolon chains where two sentences would be clearer

## Anti-Style Warnings

### AI-generic fantasy

Symptoms:

- shadow, whisper, ancient power, grim destiny, darkness-without-object

Fix:

- replace mood fog with body, tool, terrain, weather, cost

### Modern design-blog voice

Symptoms:

- “basically”
- “in other words”
- “this means that”
- tutorial filler

Fix:

- define once
- state consequence
- stop

### Clinical modernity

Symptoms:

- institutional language
- over-medicalized naming
- therapy-speak

Fix:

- keep anatomy if needed
- rename toward harsher, older, materially grounded diction

### Proposal leakage

Symptoms:

- “this version”
- “the intent is”
- “the goal here is”

Fix:

- if it belongs in the manuscript, write as if it always belonged there

### Overdecorated epic prose

Symptoms:

- inflated grandeur
- too many adjectives
- myth without tactile grounding

Fix:

- cut to one image
- tether it to action or object

## Contrastive Revision Rules

When revising, ask:

1. Can this noun become more concrete?
2. Can this verb become more physical?
3. Can one adjective disappear?
4. Can this sentence end on weight instead of abstraction?
5. Is this line explaining itself instead of doing its job?
