## Purpose

This file provides before-and-after pairs drawn from actual drafts and revisions in this repository. Each pair shows an AI draft or weak draft alongside the revised version, with notes on what changed and why.

Read these examples before drafting. They teach the revision instinct faster than any set of rules.

## How To Use These Examples

1. Read the "before" version first. Note what feels wrong.
2. Read the "after" version. Note what changed.
3. Read the change notes. Confirm whether your instinct matched.
4. When revising your own draft, look for the same patterns.

The examples cover all six registers: fiction opener, flavor exposition, rules, mixed bridge, item description, and example scene.

## Example 1: Gear Rule Rewrite

**Register:** Item description / rules hybrid

### Before (AI draft)

> Makeshift Tools represent a versatile collection of basic implements that enable adventurers to perform a wide range of practical tasks in the field. This comprehensive kit includes various items such as shovels, picks, and hammers, providing the necessary tools for construction, demolition, and general utility work during expeditions.

### After (manuscript voice)

> A short shovel, a hand pick, a hammer, a pry-iron, a few wedges, nails, cord, scraps of leather, a knife, a flask of oil. Enough to dig, split, or lever most things. None of it is fine, and none of it is meant to last.

### Change notes

- **Copula avoidance removed.** "Represent a versatile collection" → cut entirely. The kit is named by its contents, not described through a proxy verb.
- **Participial chain removed.** "Enabling adventurers to perform" → "Enough to dig, split, or lever." Function stated as flat fact.
- **Promotional qualifier removed.** "Comprehensive kit" → no evaluation at all.
- **Abstract nouns replaced with concrete inventory.** "Various items such as" → the actual items, every one of them.
- **End-weight established.** "None of it is meant to last." The final sentence lands on a hard physical truth.

## Example 2: Injury Naming

**Register:** Rules / consequence

### Before (clinical generic)

> Abdominal Trauma — The character suffers severe damage to the midsection, resulting in significant pain and potential organ compromise.

### After (manuscript voice)

> Gut-Ripped — Something inside tore open. You double over, and what comes out of your mouth is mostly blood. Ceaseless pain. Unless treated within a day, infection sets in.

### Change notes

- **Clinical naming replaced.** "Abdominal Trauma" → "Gut-Ripped." The manuscript names injuries the way soldiers and survivors would name them.
- **Passive description replaced with second person.** "The character suffers" → "You double over." Places the reader inside the injury.
- **Abstract consequence replaced with bodily image.** "Potential organ compromise" → "What comes out of your mouth is mostly blood."
- **Timer introduced.** "Unless treated within a day" adds game pressure through consequence.

## Example 3: Proposal Tone Cleanup

**Register:** Tone shift from design-document to manuscript

### Before (proposal leakage)

> As identified during the ranged weapons balance review, the current talent structure creates a measurable power gap between melee and ranged archetypes. This adjustment addresses the observed disparity by introducing a scaling mechanism that rewards investment without creating dominant strategies.

### After (manuscript voice)

> A skilled archer can pin targets behind cover, force repositioning, or drop a threat before it closes. Each rank extends what you can do at range without replacing what melee fighters do in the press.

### Change notes

- **Design language removed.** "Balance review," "talent structure," "power gap," "archetypes," "scaling mechanism," "dominant strategies" — all cut.
- **Player-facing action introduced.** "Pin targets," "force repositioning," "drop a threat" — the reader sees what the talent does in play.
- **Comparative balance preserved.** "Without replacing what melee fighters do in the press" — the balance concern is stated in world terms, not design terms.

## Example 4: Fiction Opening

**Register:** Fiction opener / epigraph

### Before (AI dark fantasy)

> Darkness descended upon the ancient lands as the forces of evil gathered their strength. A sense of dread permeated the forgotten halls where once great kingdoms had flourished. The time of reckoning was at hand.

### After (manuscript voice)

> The messenger had been dead three days by the time they found him. His armor was Alderlander — good steel, but the straps had been cut, not unbuckled. Whoever killed him wanted the message, not the metal.

### Change notes

- **Abstract atmosphere eliminated.** "Darkness descended," "forces of evil," "sense of dread," "time of reckoning" — all mood fog with no physical anchor.
- **Specific body replaces abstraction.** A dead messenger with cut armor straps. The reader can see it.
- **Deduction creates tension.** "Wanted the message, not the metal" — the reader is now thinking about who killed him and why. Tension through specificity, not through declared dread.
- **Cultural grounding.** "Alderlander" names a kin. "Good steel, but the straps had been cut, not unbuckled" tells you about the quality of the armor and the method of the killer in one clause.

## Example 5: Spell Description

**Register:** Mixed rules and flavor

### Before (AI magic description)

> This powerful spell allows the caster to channel the raw, untamed energies of the earth itself, enabling them to reshape stone and rock according to their will. The effects can be truly awe-inspiring, as the very foundations of the world respond to the sorcerer's command.

### After (manuscript voice)

> You press both hands flat against the stone and push your will into it. The rock answers sluggishly — it shifts, cracks, and re-forms, but never quietly, and never exactly as you intended. Spend 2 WP. The stone moves up to three meters in a direction of your choice, but roll for the spell — a failure means it moves where the stone wants to go.

### Change notes

- **Inflated significance eliminated.** "Raw, untamed energies," "truly awe-inspiring," "very foundations of the world" — mood inflation with no mechanical content.
- **Participial chain removed.** "Enabling them to reshape..." → "The rock answers sluggishly."
- **Sensory grounding added.** Hands flat against stone. The rock cracks and re-forms. Sound and texture.
- **Game consequence integrated.** The flavor leads directly into the WP cost and the roll. A failure has a specific consequence: the stone moves where it wants.
- **Character of the magic established.** "Never quietly, and never exactly as you intended" — this is the personality of stone magic. It is reluctant, loud, and approximate.

## Example 6: Cultural Flavor

**Register:** Flavor exposition

### Before (AI cultural description)

> The Alderlanders are a proud and resilient people with a rich cultural heritage that spans generations. Their traditions reflect a deep connection to the land, and their social structures have evolved to meet the challenges of life in a harsh and unforgiving wilderness.

### After (manuscript voice)

> Alderlanders farm. They clear forest, drain marsh, and build on what they take. When the land stops giving, they move and clear more. They do not ask the forest's permission. This is why the elves hate them.

### Change notes

- **Promotional cultural framing eliminated.** "Proud and resilient," "rich cultural heritage," "deep connection to the land" — these could describe any fantasy culture. They are decorative blankets.
- **Specific actions replace traits.** "Farm, clear, drain, build, move, clear." The culture is shown through what the people do, not through what the narrator says about them.
- **Conflict introduced.** "This is why the elves hate them." One sentence that explains an entire political relationship.
- **Voice shift.** "They do not ask the forest's permission." This is the manuscript's voice — matter-of-fact, slightly dark, grounded in the power dynamic between people and land.

## Example 7: Mixed Bridge

**Register:** Mixed flavor and rules

### Before (AI flavor-then-rules split)

> The wilds of the Forbidden Lands are a treacherous and unforgiving expanse, where danger lurks behind every tree and death awaits the unwary traveler.
>
> Movement across the hexagonal map is governed by the Journey rules. Each hex represents one quarter-day of travel under normal conditions.

### After (manuscript voice)

> The land between villages is not empty. It is dense with forest, bog, ruin, and worse. You cross it one quarter-day hex at a time, and every hex has a chance of weather, mishap, or encounter. Plan your supplies before you leave, because the wilds do not provide on request.

### Change notes

- **Flavor-rules split eliminated.** The before version has one paragraph of lore and one paragraph of clinical rules. The after version holds both in every sentence.
- **Abstract danger replaced with inventory.** "Danger lurks" → "forest, bog, ruin, and worse."
- **Rules embedded in world description.** "One quarter-day hex at a time" is the rule. "Weather, mishap, or encounter" is both flavor and procedure.
- **Player-facing advice integrated.** "Plan your supplies" is practical guidance delivered in the same breath as the world description.

## Example 8: Talent Description

**Register:** Rules with implicit flavor

### Before (AI talent description)

> Pathfinder is an incredibly useful talent that significantly enhances the character's ability to navigate through the wilderness. This remarkable skill allows adventurers to find optimal routes, avoid dangerous terrain, and ultimately arrive at their destinations more efficiently, making it an invaluable asset for any party venturing into the wilds.

### After (manuscript voice)

> You read trails the way a literate man reads a posted notice. Broken branches, animal paths, dry creek beds — all of it tells you which way is passable and which way ends in a cliff or a bog. When you Lead the Way on a journey, you may reroll one die on the Pathfinding roll.

### Change notes

- **Promotional language eliminated.** "Incredibly useful," "significantly enhances," "remarkable skill," "invaluable asset" — all cut.
- **Character grounding added.** "You read trails the way a literate man reads a posted notice" — one simile that establishes who you are.
- **Material details.** "Broken branches, animal paths, dry creek beds" — the reader sees what the pathfinder sees.
- **Rules stated cleanly.** "Reroll one die on the Pathfinding roll" — the mechanical benefit is one sentence.

## Example 9: Journey Passage

**Register:** Mixed bridge / procedural

### Before (AI journey narration)

> As your party embarks on their epic journey across the vast and treacherous wilderness, they will encounter numerous challenges that test their resolve, resourcefulness, and determination. The journey system provides a comprehensive framework for simulating the dangers and rewards of overland travel.

### After (manuscript voice)

> Each day of travel is divided into four quarter-days. You spend two of them walking. The others you spend making camp, sleeping, or foraging. If you push into a third quarter-day of walking, you do not rest — mark one point of exhaustion for each character who walks.

### Change notes

- **Epic framing eliminated.** "Epic journey," "vast and treacherous," "tests their resolve" — promotional summary.
- **Design-document voice eliminated.** "Comprehensive framework for simulating" — the manuscript never describes its own systems.
- **Flat procedural prose.** The manuscript voice for journey rules is the most stripped-down register in the book. It lists what happens, when, and what it costs.
- **Consequence stated plainly.** "Mark one point of exhaustion" — the reader knows the price immediately.

## Example 10: Item Distinction

**Register:** Item description with comparative distinction

### Before (AI item description)

> The longsword is a versatile and formidable weapon that has served warriors throughout the ages. Its elegant design balances reach with maneuverability, making it an excellent choice for both offensive and defensive combat scenarios. The weapon's enduring popularity speaks to its effectiveness in a wide range of tactical situations.

### After (manuscript voice)

> A straight blade, an arm's length and a half, fitted for one hand or two. Heavier than a short sword but faster than a two-hander. The grip is long enough to choke up for close work or extend for a full swing. Most fighters who can afford one carry it.

### Change notes

- **Promotional language eliminated.** "Versatile and formidable," "elegant design," "excellent choice," "enduring popularity" — catalog copy.
- **Material specificity added.** "Straight blade, arm's length and a half, fitted for one hand or two" — the reader sees the object and knows its size.
- **Comparative distinction added.** "Heavier than a short sword but faster than a two-hander" — positions the weapon against its neighbors.
- **Practical detail.** "Choke up for close work or extend for a full swing" — written by someone who has held a sword.
- **Cultural fact.** "Most fighters who can afford one carry it" — tells you about economy and military culture in one sentence.

## Pattern Summary

Across all ten examples, the same revisions recur:

1. **Cut promotional and inflated language.** The manuscript does not sell. It states.
2. **Replace abstract nouns with concrete inventories.** Name the objects. List the materials. Show the body.
3. **Fuse flavor and rules.** Every flavor sentence should point toward a rule. Every rule sentence should carry a trace of the world.
4. **State consequences plainly.** What happens? What does it cost? When does it fail?
5. **End on weight.** The final word or phrase of a sentence should be the thing that matters most.
6. **Cut the last sentence.** In seven of ten examples, the AI draft's final sentence is a summary or uplift that adds nothing. Cut it.
7. **Replace synonyms with game terms.** If the game calls it WP, call it WP. Every time.
8. **Write from inside the world.** "You press your hands against the stone" not "the caster channels energy." The reader is the character.
