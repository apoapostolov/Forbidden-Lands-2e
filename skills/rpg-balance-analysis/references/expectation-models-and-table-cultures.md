## Player Expectation Model

Players usually evaluate a rule through multiple overlapping expectations.

### Fantasy payoff

Expectation:

- “This lets me be who I imagined.”

Risk:

- if the fantasy is strong but the practical payoff is weak, the player feels tricked

### Competence payoff

Expectation:

- “Investment should make me reliably better.”

Risk:

- if specialization remains too unreliable, investment feels fake

### Fair-risk payoff

Expectation:

- “If I take serious risk, the upside should matter.”

Risk:

- rules that punish risk without giving meaningful return teach learned caution

### Legibility payoff

Expectation:

- “I should know what I am buying before I commit.”

Risk:

- hidden costs and edge cases create mistrust

### Dignity payoff

Expectation:

- “Even if I fail, I should feel like a real participant rather than a fool.”

Risk:

- humiliating or misleading failure states damage attachment to the game

## GM Expectation Model

GMs often care less about raw power than about what the rule demands of them.

### Adjudication expectation

- can this be run without frequent improvisational repair

### Pressure expectation

- does the rule preserve the intended difficulty, scarcity, and campaign friction

### Ensemble expectation

- does it let the whole table act, or does it create one-player procedural dominance

### Tone expectation

- does the rule support the game's promise, or undermine it

### Maintenance expectation

- can I keep this rule in mind for months of play without hidden debt piling up

## Spotlight Economics

Spotlight is a balance resource.

Some rules grant:

- more actions
- more narration rights
- more scene framing power
- more mandatory GM attention
- more rescue demands from other players

Those are spotlight transfers, even if no number on the page says so.

### Spotlight concentration types

#### Procedure concentration

One player resolves more rolls, more follow-up decisions, or more chained effects than others.

#### Fiction concentration

One rule makes one player the universal answer to scouting, healing, mobility, or social entry.

#### Emergency concentration

One player becomes the constant rescuer or damage sponge, making scenes orbit around them.

### Healthy spotlight

Rules can create brief spotlight peaks if:

- the role is clear
- the cost is real
- others still have meaningful involvement

### Unhealthy spotlight

Rules become unhealthy when they repeatedly:

- monopolize decisions
- demand other players orbit around them
- turn support characters into batteries or spectators

## Table-Culture Scenarios

Rules do not land the same way at every table.

### Cautious table

Typical pattern:

- risk aversion
- preference for planning
- conservative spend behavior

Balance implication:

- high-risk rules may underperform because the table will not lean into them

### Reckless table

Typical pattern:

- high push rate
- frequent gambles
- more failure tolerance

Balance implication:

- refund loops and burst rules may become much stronger here than at cautious tables

### Optimization-minded table

Typical pattern:

- fast discovery of dominant lines
- rules text read as optimization problem

Balance implication:

- vague balancing assumptions are quickly exposed

### Narrative-first table

Typical pattern:

- strong taste for dramatic payoff
- less tolerance for sterile efficiency

Balance implication:

- mathematically fine but emotionally flat rules can still fail badly

### Hard-GM table

Typical pattern:

- strict consequences
- low rescue behavior

Balance implication:

- punitive or misleading rules become much harsher than average

### Soft-GM table

Typical pattern:

- frequent hidden correction
- reluctance to let edge cases fully bite

Balance implication:

- brittle rules may appear balanced only because the GM is secretly repairing them

## Balance Interpretation Rules

### Rule 1

A rule that only works at one table culture is fragile.

### Rule 2

If a rule is safe only under constant soft correction, it is not truly balanced.

### Rule 3

If a rule only feels good at reckless tables but becomes dead weight elsewhere, its design is too narrow.

### Rule 4

If optimization-minded tables can break a rule using obvious text, the flaw is real even if casual tables miss it.
