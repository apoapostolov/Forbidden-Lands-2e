## Action-Economy Balance

Action economy is usually the strongest hidden balance currency in an RPG.

### Why it matters

Changing a rule from:

- slow to fast
- fast to free
- once-per-round to repeated
- reactive to proactive without cost

often matters more than adding a numeric bonus.

### Action-economy value categories

#### Extra action

Very strong because it expands possibility space directly.

#### Action compression

Two jobs in one action.
Usually stronger than a flat die bonus.

#### Reaction access

Can be strong or oppressive depending on frequency and trigger clarity.

#### Setup reduction

Reducing readying, load, draw, or stance cost can quietly multiply a build's output.

### Balance warning

If a rule grants extra actions and also improves reliability, it should be treated as a premium-risk design element.

## Game-Theory Taxonomy

### Dominant strategy

One line is better in most situations.

### Degenerate loop

The rule feeds itself or creates a self-sustaining exploit.

### False choice

Several options appear different, but one is objectively superior once understood.

### Kingmaking incentive

The best move is to decide who among others wins rather than improving your own position meaningfully.

### Anti-party incentive

The individual optimum harms group play while the rules still reward it.

### Information asymmetry trap

The best use depends on knowledge many players will not have, creating hidden unfairness.

## Synergy Analysis

Synergy should be evaluated on three levels:

### 1. Raw power synergy

Two rules create much higher output together than alone.

### 2. Perception synergy

The combo makes one player feel like the obvious or necessary solution to too many problems.

### 3. Complexity synergy

The combo creates too many chained exceptions, counters, or adjudication burdens.

### Synergy questions

For any combo, ask:

1. what output rises
2. what cost disappears
3. what pressure channel collapses
4. what table burden increases

## Campaign-Scale Balance

Scene balance is not enough.

RPGs run over many sessions, which means:

- small efficiencies compound
- repeated bypasses hollow out challenge
- recovery and replenishment matter more than burst output alone

### Campaign-scale model

Test a rule across these axes:

- frequency of activation
- repeat cost
- replenishment speed
- pressure removed from the campaign
- niche erosion over time

### Common campaign failures

#### Pacing collapse

The campaign's survival or resource spine stops mattering.

#### Niche erosion

One tool increasingly replaces talents, gear, or social roles that used to matter.

#### Challenge inflation spiral

The GM must escalate threats to compensate, which punishes the rest of the table.

## Repeated-Use Tests

Apply these tests:

### Repetition test

Does the rule remain fair if used at every opportunity?

### Replacement test

Does it obsolete an old choice rather than complement it?

### Attrition test

Does it bypass a cost that the campaign assumes will stay meaningful?

### Escalation test

Would the GM feel pressure to counterbuild around it?
