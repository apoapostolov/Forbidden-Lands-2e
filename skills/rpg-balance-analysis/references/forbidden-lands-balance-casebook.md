It is grounded in the repo's own manuscript and in the completed `forbidden-lands-design` skill corpus.

## Why Forbidden Lands Is A Strong Balance Case

Forbidden Lands is useful because it is not balanced around equal comfort.
It is balanced around a harsh but legible pressure stack.

That means rules should be tested not by “is everyone equally strong” but by:

- does risk still buy power
- does survival still cost something
- does preparation still matter
- does the GM still have to run a world rather than babysit a loophole

## Case Study: Willpower Loop

### Mathematical layer

Willpower is generated through pushed Base Dice.

That ties power to danger.

### Psychological layer

Players feel clever when they turn desperation into fuel.

### Balance risk

If WP is made easier to get, cheaper to spend, or more likely to refund, too many separate systems become simultaneously stronger.

### Lesson

This is mathematically fair and experientially satisfying because the cost is visible and real.

## Case Study: Critical Injuries

### Mathematical layer

Critical injuries turn defeat into time loss, capability loss, and sometimes death.

### Psychological layer

They make survival feel costly and meaningful.

### Balance risk

If a result is technically survivable but functionally unplayable, the text must say so.
Otherwise the rule becomes psychologically dishonest.

### Lesson

A harsh rule can feel good when it tells the truth about consequence.

## Case Study: Journeys And Attrition

### Mathematical layer

Travel consumes time, resources, and safety margins.

### Psychological layer

The wilderness feels like an adversary rather than scenery.

### Balance risk

Too many easy bypasses to food, shelter, warmth, or tempo will hollow out the expedition loop.

### Lesson

Attrition systems are healthy when they create meaningful logistical choices, not just maintenance chores.

## Case Study: Talents And Action Compression

### Mathematical layer

Extra actions and free reactions often outperform plain die bonuses.

### Psychological layer

They feel exciting because they grant control, tempo, and dramatic competence.

### Balance risk

These effects can quietly monopolize spotlight and pressure the GM into encounter correction.

### Lesson

A rule can feel great and still be dangerous at campaign scale.

## Case Study: Magic Volatility

### Mathematical layer

Spells are reliable in effect but risky in mishap exposure.

### Psychological layer

This produces real temptation instead of clean optimization.

### Balance risk

If safety and discount effects stack too far, magic stops being unstable leverage and becomes universal problem-solving.

### Lesson

High-output systems stay healthy when volatility remains structurally meaningful.

## Case Study: Gear Scarcity

### Mathematical layer

Gear improves reliability, access, and protection.

### Psychological layer

Preparedness feels earned because it is material, visible, and scarce.

### Balance risk

If specialist access is too easy, talents and settlement functions lose purpose.

### Lesson

Scarcity is part of balance, not flavor garnish.

## Balance Lessons

Forbidden Lands repeatedly demonstrates these truths:

- a harsh rule can be healthy if it is legible and meaningful
- mathematically fair can still feel bad if the expectation promise is wrong
- action economy and recovery compression are premium balance levers
- pressure bypass is often more dangerous than raw power
- good balance in this game is about preserving the pressure stack, not equalizing comfort
