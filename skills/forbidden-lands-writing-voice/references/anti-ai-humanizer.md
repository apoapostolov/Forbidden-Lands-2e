# Anti-AI Humanizer

This document is the master catalog of AI writing patterns that
appear when a language model drafts prose. It covers 43 named
patterns organized into seven failure layers. Each pattern includes:

- What the pattern is
- An AI example (what the AI writes)
- The human fix (what a writer writes instead)
- The Forbidden Lands application (how it manifests specifically in
  this manuscript and what the correct replacement looks like)
- The author model (which author in the tradition never does this)

After the catalog: the 7-layer failure taxonomy, the diagnostic
checklist, and the revision protocol.

---

## The Seven Failure Layers

AI text fails in seven stacked layers. Surface passes (spelling,
grammar, basic coherence) will not catch them. Only targeted
inspection does.

**Layer 1 — Vocabulary Tells.** The AI defaults to a prestige
vocabulary that sounds literary but carries no specific meaning.
"Ancient," "shrouded," "pulsing," "primal," "timeless,"
"vibrant," "rich," "complex," "mysterious." These words are
noise. They describe nothing. They suppress the specific noun.

**Layer 2 — Sentence-Structure Tells.** The AI has learned
certain rhetorical patterns from large corpora of published text
and produces them automatically. Em-dash clusters, rule-of-three,
negative-then-positive clarification, copula avoidance, elegant
variation. These patterns feel smooth; they read as generated.

**Layer 3 — Paragraph-Structure Tells.** The AI writes every
paragraph the same way: topic sentence, elaboration, smooth
landing. Real writers vary paragraph shape. Real paragraphs do
not always end on resolution. The AI's paragraphs are structurally
interchangeable. A manuscript's paragraphs are not.

**Layer 4 — Point-of-View and Interiority Tells.** The AI
announces emotions rather than rendering them. It inserts
sympathy for the reader. It gives characters analytical
self-knowledge they would not have. It explains motivation.

**Layer 5 — Dialogue Tells.** The AI's characters speak in
complete grammatical sentences, explain backstory to each other,
and are tagged with emotion elaborations. Real dialogue is
incomplete, oblique, and specific to the speaker.

**Layer 6 — World-Building Tells.** The AI provides generic
atmosphere and over-complete information. It frames everything
as adventure-ready. Religion, monsters, and factions become
decorative.

**Layer 7 — Structural and Editorial Tells.** The AI wraps
scenes with sentimental resolutions, thematic restatements, and
editorial closings. Real scenes end on weight, not consolation.

---

## The 43 Patterns

### Layer 1: Vocabulary Tells

---

**Pattern 1 — Significance Inflation**

The AI treats everything as important and signals this with
significance vocabulary: "ancient," "primal," "sacred," "legendary,"
"immense," "vast," "profound," "deep." The effect is that nothing
is important because importance is constant.

AI: *The ancient forest stretched before them, vast and primordial,
filled with the deep mystery of ages past.*

Human fix: Name what is specifically there. "The birch stand ran
for three hundred paces before the first pine. After that, dark
all the way to the ridge."

Forbidden Lands application: "The old stone shrine" → "a flat
stone with a Raven-shape scratched into it, old enough that
rain had smoothed the beak to a line." The specific observation
replaces the adjective.

Author model: Le Guin. She never tells you something is ancient.
She describes what time has done to a specific object.

---

**Pattern 2 — Fantasy-Register Drift**

The AI, tasked with fantasy prose, defaults to a generic fantasy
vocabulary: "arcane," "mystical," "eldritch," "shadowy," "blade
of destiny," "the dark lord," "chosen one," "the ancient power
stirred." This is not the register of this manuscript.

AI: *The eldritch runes pulsed with arcane energy as the dark
sorcerer raised his mystical staff.*

Human fix: Use the specific vocabulary of the world. Spells have
named paths. Sorcerers have specific motives and specific tools.
Magic costs Willpower and leaves marks.

Forbidden Lands application: "dark sorcerer" → "Zygofer, who
smelled of copper and had not slept in four years." The generic
becomes specific.

Author model: Sapkowski. His magic users are specific people
with specific practices. Yennefer is not a "dark sorceress." She
is a woman who twisted her spine during an transformation and
still feels the vertebra shift when she breathes deep.

---

**Pattern 3 — Corporate and Motivational Register**

The AI picks up vocabulary from business writing and
self-improvement content: "meaningful," "impactful," "engage
with," "drive toward," "empower," "leverage," "optimize."
Also its softer cousins: "deeply," "truly," "really," "genuinely,"
"incredibly." These words carry no information.

AI: *The village had been deeply impacted by the Blood Mist, with
truly meaningful cultural adaptations that allowed its people to
meaningfully engage with their new reality.*

Human fix: Replace every adverb of intensity with the specific
fact it was inflating. Replace "was impacted by" with what
actually changed.

Forbidden Lands application: "deeply impacted" → "The village
had not had a priest since 1103. The shrine to the Raven was
a door from a burned house, propped against a pine." The
adverb is gone. The specific fact does the work.

Author model: Cornwell. He uses no motivational vocabulary.
Ever.

---

**Pattern 4 — Modern Psychological Vocabulary**

The AI has characters think in concepts from modern psychology:
"trauma," "attachment issues," "boundaries," "processing grief,"
"toxic," "gaslighting," "triggered," "validate," "closure."
Characters in medieval survival fantasy do not think this way.

AI: *She recognized that his behavior was toxic and that she
needed to set clear boundaries to protect her mental health.*

Human fix: Describe what she observes and what she does. No
vocabulary that postdates 1950.

Forbidden Lands application: "She needed closure" → "She wanted
to see the house burned. That was the feeling. She did not name
it." The thought stays in period-appropriate form.

Author model: Cornwell. His characters do not analyze
themselves. They act, remember, and regret, and Cornwell never
supplies the psychological vocabulary for what they're
experiencing.

---

**Pattern 5 — Promotional Language**

The AI learned from marketing text. It describes fictional places
and cultures the way a brochure describes a resort: "vibrant
community," "rich culture," "diverse tapestry of peoples,"
"thriving marketplace," "a place where traditions run deep."

AI: *Hollowford was a vibrant community with a rich tapestry
of cultural traditions and a thriving marketplace.*

Human fix: Name three specific things about the village and
let the reader assemble the "vibrant community" themselves.
"Hollowford had a decent mill, a well that didn't go dry until
Rotday, and a feud between the Dressers and the Karns that
had been running since Algarod's third son was buried without
his sword."

Forbidden Lands application: Every time "vibrant" or "rich"
or "thriving" appears, delete it and replace with the specific
thing it was gesturing at.

Author model: Parker. He describes exactly what a thing is
made of and what it costs. Nothing is "rich." Everything has
a price.

---

**Pattern 6 — Abstract Noun Preference**

The AI prefers abstract nouns to concrete ones: "community,"
"society," "culture," "power," "meaning," "truth," "beauty,"
"sorrow," "hope." These words are containers with nothing
in them. A good passage fills the container with a specific
object.

AI: *The sense of community among the villagers provided hope
and meaning in the face of the sorrow that pervaded their lives.*

Human fix: "The miller's wife left bread at the door of any
house that had lost someone. The Karns left bread at the
miller's door when they found the miller's wife had no grain
to spare. That was the arrangement."

Forbidden Lands application: Replace "community" with the
specific exchange. Replace "sorrow" with the specific loss.
Replace "hope" with the specific act.

Author model: Le Guin. She is the master of making specific
exchange carry the weight of abstract claim.

---

### Layer 2: Sentence-Structure Tells

---

**Pattern 7 — Em-Dash Cluster**

The AI uses em-dashes as a default rhythm marker, inserting
them to create drama, parenthetical elaboration, or abrupt
transitions. A passage with more than one em-dash per page
has an AI tell.

AI: *The road ahead — once well-traveled — now lay empty, the
silence broken only by wind — an ominous sign of what awaited.*

Human fix: Rebuild the sentence. Use a period, a comma,
or rearrange the clause order so the em-dash is unnecessary.

Forbidden Lands application: In this manuscript, the em-dash
is used rarely and only when a thought interrupts another
thought mid-execution. "He raised the axe — she was already
through the door." Never as atmosphere decoration.

Author model: Cornwell. His prose almost never uses em-dashes.
He uses short sentences. The abruptness is in the period, not
the dash.

---

**Pattern 8 — Rule-of-Three**

The AI groups things in threes because rhetoric taught it to.
"Cold, dark, and silent." "She was tired, hungry, and afraid."
"The village needed grain, iron, and hope." Three is the AI's
resting count.

AI: *The night was cold, dark, and full of terrible sounds.*

Human fix: Pick one. Make it specific. "The cold got into his
boot through the left heel-seam where the stitching had gone
in the river."

Forbidden Lands application: Two related facts are usually
better than three. One specific fact is usually better than
two. The rule-of-three creates a list-feel that distances
the reader from the physical.

Author model: Cook. Croaker picks one detail and moves on.
He does not exhaust the subject.

---

**Pattern 9 — Negative-Then-Positive Clarification**

This is one of the most reliable AI tells in existence. Two
sentences, first a negation, then the correction. "It is not
X. It is Y." This pattern appears constantly in generated text
because LLMs learned it from explanatory writing.

AI: *It was not a fortress. It was a promise.*

Human fix: Collapse. "It was a promise, not a fortress." Or
rewrite entirely: "The wall was thatch. The only weapon in it
was an old bill-hook with a cracked handle."

Forbidden Lands application: Every occurrence of "It is not
X. It is Y." must be collapsed or cut. The pattern is
disqualifying.

Author model: Every author in this tradition avoids it. None
of them structure clarification through negation-correction
pairs.

---

**Pattern 10 — Copula Avoidance**

The AI avoids the verb "is" and replaces it with elaborate
substitute constructions: "serves as," "functions as," "acts
as," "operates as," "stands as," "represents," "symbolizes."
These substitutes lengthen sentences and carry no additional
meaning.

AI: *The well serves as the social center of the village,
functioning as a gathering point that represents the communal
heart of its people.*

Human fix: "The well. Three families drew from it before
dawn to avoid the queue." The "is" is implicit. The action
is specific.

Forbidden Lands application: "serves as" → cut, rebuild.
Every instance.

Author model: Parker. He writes "the well is here. It
is fourteen feet deep. It tastes of iron." Clean declarative.

---

**Pattern 11 — Elegant Variation**

The AI cycles synonyms to avoid repeating a word, believing
variety signals craft. "The warrior drew his sword... the
fighter raised his blade... the soldier's weapon caught the
light." In technical writing, game writing, and manuscript
prose, repetition is cleaner than variation when a specific
word is correct.

AI: *The Willpower spent by the mage... the sorcerer's inner
reserves... his mental fortitude... the caster's psychic
energy...*

Human fix: Willpower. Every time.

Forbidden Lands application: Game terms especially. "Willpower"
is the word. "Push Roll" is the phrase. "Hit Points" is not.
Elegant variation around mechanical terms creates confusion and
sounds generated.

Author model: All of them. Sapkowski says "Witcher" not "monster
hunter" or "mutant" or "professional" in the same paragraph.

---

**Pattern 12 — Hedging Stack**

The AI hedges its claims with "perhaps," "somewhat," "in some
ways," "could be said to," "might be considered," "arguably,"
"in a sense," "to some extent." Each hedge, individually, seems
like careful qualification. In combination they produce a voice
that commits to nothing.

AI: *Perhaps the village could be said to have been in some ways
a somewhat hostile environment, arguably as a result of the
somewhat traumatic events that had arguably shaped its
character.*

Human fix: Commit. "The village was hostile. Two families had
been at war since the year the iron-collector came through.
Everyone had taken a side."

Forbidden Lands application: Hedging is a modern academic
voice. It does not belong in manuscript prose. Any hedge
that is not a character's deliberate understatement should
be deleted.

Author model: Cornwell. He never hedges. His narrators make
declarative claims and stand behind them.

---

**Pattern 13 — Gerund Opening**

The AI opens paragraphs and chapters with gerund phrases:
"Being the kind of man who...," "Having spent years in...,"
"Knowing that the danger was real...," "Feeling the weight
of the decision..." This creates a static, backward-facing
opening instead of a dynamic forward-moving one.

AI: *Having lived through the Blood Mist, the old woman
understood what the villagers could not.*

Human fix: Start with what she does or sees. "The old woman
milked the goat in the dark. She had done it before the light
for forty years, first because of the Blood Mist and now
because the habit had outlived its reason."

Forbidden Lands application: Every paragraph that begins
with a gerund phrase needs to be rewritten to begin on action
or observation.

Author model: Gwynne. His paragraphs open on physical action,
almost without exception.

---

**Pattern 14 — Balanced Compound Conclusion**

The AI ends paragraphs and scenes with balanced compound
conclusions: "...and so the village endured, as it always
had, through fear and hope alike." The balance is the tell.
Real prose endings land asymmetrically — on a weight, not
a balance.

AI: *The sun set behind the hills, marking the end of one
chapter and the beginning of another, as it had for all the
generations before.*

Human fix: End on a specific detail. "The sun set. The
miller closed his shutters. It smelled like rain."

Forbidden Lands application: Any scene ending that feels
"complete" in an editorial sense has probably been balanced
by AI. Break the symmetry.

Author model: Cook. His chapter endings are abrupt, often
on logistics or a single observed fact. They do not resolve.

---

**Pattern 15 — Trailing Qualifier**

Sentences that end with qualifiers: "...as best she could,"
"...for the most part," "...to a degree," "...in some
measure," "...where possible." These qualifiers land at the
end of the sentence where the weight should be. They drain
the weight out.

AI: *She kept the village together through the dark winter,
as best she could.*

Human fix: End on the concrete fact. "She rationed the
salt grain herself. Half a handful per family per week,
which was not enough, and she knew it."

Forbidden Lands application: Cut every trailing qualifier
and rebuild the sentence to end on the specific fact.

Author model: Abercrombie. His sentences end on the hard
noun or the hard consequence. The qualifier, if any, comes
earlier.

---

**Pattern 16 — The "Not Just X But Y" Construction**

The AI uses "not just X, but Y" to signal escalation or
nuance. "Not just a warrior, but a poet." "Not just a ruler,
but a mother." "Not just survival, but meaning." This pattern
is a written-language rhetorical device that sounds analytical
and distant.

AI: *This was not just a village, but a testament to human
resilience.*

Human fix: "The village had been here for three hundred years.
The oldest house was in the ground to its eaves." Let the
fact carry the weight the AI was trying to signal.

Forbidden Lands application: Cut. Every instance.

Author model: Le Guin. She never uses this structure. She
presents the thing and trusts the reader to locate its meaning.

---

### Layer 3: Paragraph-Structure Tells

---

**Pattern 17 — The Topic-Sentence Paragraph**

Every AI paragraph starts with a topic sentence that
announces what the paragraph will prove, followed by
elaboration, followed by a landing. This is essay structure.
Narrative paragraphs do not work this way. The topic sentence
is the essay's skeleton showing through the skin.

AI: *The village economy was centered on grain farming.
Families maintained their own plots and contributed a portion
to the communal granary. This system ensured collective
survival during harsh winters.*

Human fix: Disassemble. "The granary was behind the Karn
house, which was a source of the trouble — Arthe Karn
collected the tithes and his family ate first. Everyone
knew it. No one said it, not when the Rust Brother was in
town."

Forbidden Lands application: Manuscript paragraphs should
not have visible structure. They should feel like thought
or observation. The topic sentence should be buried or
absent.

Author model: Sapkowski. His paragraphs arrive through
perception — what the character senses, then what they
understand, then what they do. Never topic-first.

---

**Pattern 18 — The Continuity Bridge Sentence**

The AI connects paragraphs with sentences that exist only to
link: "In addition to this...," "Furthermore...," "Building
on what has been established...," "With this in mind...,"
"As mentioned previously...," "Against this backdrop..." These
are transition scaffolds. Real prose does not need them.

AI: *Against this backdrop of isolation and fear, the village
had developed its own customs. Furthermore, these customs
had taken on a religious significance over time.*

Human fix: Cut both sentence-openers. Run the paragraphs
together or separate them with a line break. Let the reader
feel the connection.

Forbidden Lands application: If two adjacent paragraphs
require a bridge sentence to connect them, the paragraphs
are probably in the wrong order. Fix the order, not the
connection.

Author model: Cook. The Annals voice has no bridges. It
just starts the next entry.

---

**Pattern 19 — The Thematic Restatement Ending**

The AI ends scenes and chapters by summarizing their theme:
"And so she learned that survival was not just about food
and shelter, but about the bonds between people." "The lesson
was clear: in this world, trust was the most valuable thing."
This is the essay conclusion landing in fiction.

AI: *As the sun set over the village, he understood that
some things were worth fighting for. The bonds of community
had kept them alive through the darkest of times.*

Human fix: End on the physical scene. Let the theme
disappear into the image. "The sun was gone. He stood at
the gate with the bar in his hands and listened to the
dogs settle."

Forbidden Lands application: Any sentence that contains
"the lesson was," "he/she/they understood that," or a
thematic summary should be cut and replaced with the
specific physical closure.

Author model: Kay. His chapters often end on a single
sentence of image or fact, and the emotional meaning
arrives in the gap between the image and the preceding
narrative. He does not supply the meaning.

---

**Pattern 20 — The Even-Rhythm Paragraph**

The AI writes paragraphs where all sentences have similar
lengths. The rhythm is smooth and undifferentiated. Real
prose varies consciously: short sentences for impact, long
sentences for accumulation, fragments for shock.

AI: *The village sat on a hill above the river. It had been
there for many generations. The people were hardy and
resilient. They had survived many hardships. They would
survive this one too.*

Human fix: Break the rhythm. Vary the length. "The village
had been on the hill since before the Shift, which meant
before the roads, before the churches, before anyone wrote
it down. Hard people. Short sentences. Yes."

Forbidden Lands application: Read any draft paragraph aloud.
If all the sentences take the same amount of time to say,
the rhythm is wrong.

Author model: Abercrombie. His rhythm varies sentence-by-
sentence, and the variation is purposeful — long sentence
of context, short sentence of fact, shorter sentence of
consequence.

---

**Pattern 21 — The Signposting Sentence**

The AI signals importance: "This is crucial to understand,"
"What follows is significant," "Pay attention to this," "It
is important to note," "It is worth mentioning," "The key
point is." These are lecture-register phrases. They do not
belong in prose.

AI: *It is worth noting that the Rust Brothers had a
significant presence in this region, which is important
to understanding the political dynamics at play.*

Human fix: Just say the fact. "The Rust Brothers had a
priory at Stonefall. They collected from six villages on
the eastern loop and had done so since before the Mist."

Forbidden Lands application: Every signposting sentence
is a trust failure. It means the writer doubts the reader
will notice what is important. The fix is to make the
important thing concrete enough that no announcement
is needed.

Author model: Parker. He trusts the reader completely. He
supplies the specific fact without announcing its importance.

---

**Pattern 22 — Backstory Dump on First Mention**

When the AI introduces a character, place, or artifact, it
immediately provides full backstory in an expository block.
Real writing introduces the thing and supplies backstory
gradually, through action, through memory, through other
characters' reactions.

AI: *Vorkan was a veteran soldier who had fought in the
wars of the Iron League before the Blood Mist came, when
he had lost his brother at the Siege of Lumra and had
turned to drink, eventually finding himself here in this
small village, bitter and broken.*

Human fix: "Vorkan was sitting in the corner with a cup
he did not refill. He had a scar along his jaw that he
touched when he was listening." The backstory emerges
in fragments over three scenes.

Forbidden Lands application: This is especially damaging
in NPC introductions. A good NPC introduction in this
manuscript is one specific gesture. Everything else
arrives later.

Author model: Cook. Croaker never introduces a character
with backstory. He introduces them by what they do right
now. The history arrives through repetition.

---

### Layer 4: Point-of-View and Interiority Tells

---

**Pattern 23 — Emotion Announcement**

The AI states emotions directly: "she felt fear," "he was
overcome with grief," "a wave of sadness washed over her,"
"joy surged through his chest," "anger simmered beneath
the surface." These announcements tell the reader what to
feel and preempt the physical experience that creates feeling.

AI: *A wave of terror washed over her as she felt pure,
primal fear grip her heart.*

Human fix: Describe the physical. "Her mouth went dry. She
put the back of her hand against her teeth and held it there
for a moment before she could speak."

Forbidden Lands application: Banned in narrative prose,
acceptable in internal monologue if the character is
named and specific. "She was afraid. Good. Afraid was
sensible." — This is acceptable because it is the
character's thought, not the narrator's announcement.

Author model: Cornwell. His characters never feel emotions.
They notice things about their own bodies.

---

**Pattern 24 — The Sympathetic Narrator Insert**

The AI, trained to be helpful and reassuring, inserts
comforting meta-narrative comments: "Though she didn't
know it yet, things were about to change," "Fate had
other plans," "Little did he know that these small
decisions would shape the rest of his life." These
are author intrusions that infantilize the reader.

AI: *She left the village that morning with no idea that
she would never see it again. Little did she know the
journey would change her forever.*

Human fix: Remove. The scene does not know the future.
If you want foreshadowing, use a specific observed
detail that carries ominous weight, not a narrator
announcement.

Forbidden Lands application: Kay is the one author in
the tradition who uses omniscient foreshadowing — but
he does it as a literary technique with full consciousness,
not as a comfort-insert. The distinction: Kay's
"it would be the last time" is melancholy, weighted,
deliberate. The AI version is a spoiler presented
as narrative reward.

Author model: Sapkowski. His narrator knows less than
the reader suspects. He withholds.

---

**Pattern 25 — Over-Explained Motivation**

The AI explains why every character does everything. Real
prose leaves most motivation unexplained, or embeds it
in physical behavior. Readers do not need to be told why
a character acts; they need to see the act in enough
specific detail to infer the why.

AI: *She agreed to help the stranger because she knew
that in a world as cruel as this one, helping others
was the only way to maintain one's own humanity, and
also because she hoped he might bring news from the
outside world.*

Human fix: "She agreed. The stranger had boots that
cost more than her house, which meant he had come
from somewhere with a market."

Forbidden Lands application: Motivation is delivered
through observation. What the character notices tells
you what they want.

Author model: Abercrombie. His characters act and the
motive is assembled by the reader from the pattern
of their acts.

---

**Pattern 26 — Analytical Self-Awareness in First Person**

The AI gives first-person narrators analytical access
to their own psychology that no real person has: "I
recognized that my fear was really a form of grief,"
"I understood, in that moment, that I had been using
my anger to avoid confronting my loss." Medieval
survival characters do not think this way.

AI: *I realized then that my hostility toward the
stranger was really a projection of my own feelings
of inadequacy and isolation.*

Human fix: "I didn't like him. I couldn't have said
why. He sat at the wrong end of the table and that
was enough."

Forbidden Lands application: First-person POV characters
in this manuscript are pragmatists. They observe, they
price, they survive. They do not analyze their interior
life.

Author model: Parker. His first-person narrators are
experts at their craft and catastrophically bad at
self-knowledge. The gap between their competence and
their blindness is the source of his dark comedy.

---

**Pattern 27 — The Memory Flashback That Explains**

The AI triggers memory flashbacks at moments of
emotional pressure specifically to explain the emotional
pressure. The flashback is an information delivery
mechanism disguised as literary technique.

AI: *As she looked at the burned house, she was
suddenly transported back to the day her father had
first brought her here, telling her that this land
was their birthright...*

Human fix: Let the present scene carry its own weight.
If backstory is necessary, insert one specific
sensory detail from the past without explanation.
"The door had been blue. She noticed she remembered
that."

Forbidden Lands application: Memory in this manuscript
is involuntary and fragmentary, not expository. It
interrupts rather than explains.

Author model: Gwynne. His characters carry their
history in physical sensation, not in clear memory-
narratives.

---

### Layer 5: Dialogue Tells

---

**Pattern 28 — The Expressive Dialogue Tag**

The AI adds emotional elaboration to dialogue tags:
"he said, his voice heavy with the weight of years,"
"she replied, her eyes filled with unspoken sorrow,"
"he answered, his words carrying an undeniable
authority." Real attribution is almost always "said"
or "asked" or nothing.

AI: *"We cannot stay," she whispered, her voice
trembling with barely contained grief.*

Human fix: *"We can't stay," she said.* If the
grief is real, the words carry it.

Forbidden Lands application: Attribution in this
manuscript uses "said" and occasionally "asked."
Action beats replace tags where possible. "She
looked at the door. 'We can't stay.'"

Author model: Cook. The Black Company uses "said"
or nothing. The context carries the tone.

---

**Pattern 29 — Dialogue That Explains Backstory**

The AI's characters explain backstory to each other
in dialogue, using conversations as exposition
vehicles. Two people who have lived through the same
events do not explain those events to each other.
This is the "as you know, Bob" error.

AI: *"As you know, the Blood Mist came during the
reign of the Iron League, beginning around nine
hundred years ago, when the cursed wizard Zytera
first—" "Yes, I know all this," she interrupted.*

Human fix: Never. Characters discuss only what they
do not both know. Exposition enters through a
character who actually does not know something.

Forbidden Lands application: A traveler asking about
local conditions is the legitimate vehicle for
exposition. A local explaining local history to
another local is always wrong.

Author model: Le Guin. Her characters discuss what
they are doing and where they are going. The world
is embedded in those practical conversations.

---

**Pattern 30 — Perfect Grammar in All Speech**

The AI's characters speak in complete grammatical
sentences with correct syntax, no incomplete thoughts,
no interruptions, no trailing off. Real speech is
incomplete, irregular, and specific to the speaker's
register.

AI: *"I believe that the situation at the northern
farms represents a serious threat that we should
address immediately," said the elder.*

Human fix: *"The north farms," the elder said.
"Somebody needs to go."*

Forbidden Lands application: Ailander characters
speak with different cadence than Alderlander
characters. A Rust Brother speaks differently
than a Raven Sister. The grammar differences
are not dialect jokes; they are register tells.

Author model: Sapkowski. His dialogue is heavily
accented by character — the Witcher's terse
ironics, the sorceress's precise technical speech,
the peasant's half-sentences. No two characters
share the same sentence pattern.

---

**Pattern 31 — Dialogue Tags Used for Emotion**

The AI upgrades dialogue verbs to carry emotion:
"she laughed," "he groaned," "she hissed," "he
barked," "she scoffed," "he stammered." These
verbs try to insert emotion through attribution
rather than through the words spoken.

AI: *"You know nothing about real danger," she
scoffed.*

Human fix: *"You know nothing about real danger,"
she said.* Or use an action beat: "She set down
the cup. 'You know nothing about real danger.'"

Forbidden Lands application: Permitted rarely:
"he laughed" when the laugh is unexpected or
significant. Banned when used to insert emotion
the dialogue does not carry.

Author model: Abercrombie. He uses "said" almost
exclusively, but his dialogue is so precisely
written that the emotion is always present in
the word choice.

---

**Pattern 32 — The Character Who Summarizes for the Reader**

The AI invents moments where a character explains
the situation in terms that serve the reader's
understanding rather than any realistic motivation:
"So let me make sure I understand — the monster
has been here for generations, and the village
has been paying tribute, and now that payment
is disrupted...?" This is a reading-comprehension
check, not dialogue.

AI: *"So what you're saying is that the Rust Brothers
have cut off the road, the village is running out
of food, and unless we act now, everyone will die
by Winterwane?"*

Human fix: Let the reader assemble. Use dialogue
for what characters say to each other, not for
narrative recaps.

Forbidden Lands application: If a scene requires
a recap-dialogue to be comprehensible, the scene
has too many moving parts. Reduce the complexity,
do not recap it.

Author model: Parker. His characters never recap
for the reader. They talk at cross-purposes, assume
shared knowledge, and leave the reader to keep up.

---

### Layer 6: World-Building Tells

---

**Pattern 33 — The Generic Atmospheric Opener**

The AI opens scenes, chapters, and paragraphs with
weather or atmosphere that is not anchored to a
specific body in a specific place: "The forest was
dark and silent," "A cold wind blew across the
barren land," "The night hung heavy over the
village," "Shadows gathered as the sun set."

AI: *A chill wind swept across the frozen landscape
as the last light of day faded into darkness.*

Human fix: Anchor to a specific body. "Mira
pulled her cloak tight at the throat and turned
her face from the wind." The same cold is present
but now it has a specific person experiencing it.

Forbidden Lands application: Every atmospheric
opener must be linked to a character who is
physically there. The weather does not exist
independently.

Author model: Gwynne. His openings are always
"Corban woke" or "Kastell walked." The world
arrives through the body.

---

**Pattern 34 — Over-Complete Information**

The AI is trained to be comprehensive. Given a
prompt about a village, it describes every aspect
of the village. Real prose withholds. The things
it does not say are as important as the things
it says. A great scene is a selection, not an
inventory.

AI: *The village had forty-three houses, two wells,
a shrine to both the Raven and the Wyrm, a
palisade of sharpened pine stakes that was in need
of repair, a mill on the river, three families with
hereditary rights to the best land, and a longstanding
dispute over the inheritance of the Fenn property.*

Human fix: Pick three. Let the others be discovered
or implied. "The palisade needed work. You could
see it from the road. The miller didn't look at
visitors."

Forbidden Lands application: A village introduction
should not answer every question about the village.
It should raise them.

Author model: Cook. Croaker describes only what
is relevant to the current problem. Everything
else is not in the Annals.

---

**Pattern 35 — Religion-as-Flavor**

The AI treats religion as world-dressing. A shrine
is mentioned to establish atmosphere. A prayer is
said to signal character devotion. A ritual is
described as a "tradition" or a "custom" without
economic or political weight. Real religion in
this manuscript is daily practice, political
pressure, and economic obligation.

AI: *The villagers maintained their ancient traditions,
gathering at the Raven shrine each evening to
sing the old songs and remember those who had been
lost to the Blood Mist.*

Human fix: Make the religion specific and
transactional. "The Raven Sister passed through
every Harvest Day to collect the tithe and check
the burial records. The villagers paid. The shrine
stayed clean. It was an arrangement."

Forbidden Lands application: Every religious
reference must carry a specific obligation, a
specific authority, or a specific daily practice.
No religion-as-flavor.

Author model: Le Guin. In Earthsea, religion
is craft, custom, and daily practice. No one
gathers to sing old songs. They do specific things
at specific times for specific reasons that are
embedded in how the world works.

---

**Pattern 36 — Monster-as-Obstacle**

The AI frames monsters as obstacles to be
overcome: the challenge to defeat, the threat
to neutralize, the enemy to kill. This is
the D&D inheritance. In the Forbidden Lands
tradition, monsters are contracts.

AI: *The players must confront the monster
that has been terrorizing the village. Only
by defeating it can they earn the villagers'
trust.*

Human fix: The monster has been here longer
than the current generation. Something is
paying it to stay away from the village.
The something is not paid anymore. The scene
is about who makes the next payment, not
who kills the monster.

Forbidden Lands application: Every monster
encounter must pass this test: what does
the monster want, and what has it been
promised? If it has been promised nothing,
the encounter design is wrong.

Author model: Sapkowski. His monsters are
ecological and contractual. Geralt collects
the facts before he reaches for the sword.
Sometimes he never reaches for it.

---

**Pattern 37 — Village-as-Set**

The AI describes villages as environments
designed for adventurers: "a place where heroes
can find quests," "a stop on the road," "the
locals are wary of strangers but can be won
over," "there are rumors of strange happenings
in the forest nearby." The village is designed
to serve the adventurers, not to exist on its
own terms.

AI: *The village of Greystone serves as an
excellent starting point for adventures,
with several rumors the PCs can follow up
on and locals who may offer them work.*

Human fix: The village has nothing to do
with the adventurers. It has its own crisis,
its own grudge, its own winter, its own
priest problem. The adventurers land in the
middle of something that was already happening.

Forbidden Lands application: See the Village-
as-System model in
`skills/forbidden-lands-lore/references/places.md`.

Author model: Le Guin. Her villages are
complete economies that exist independently
of any visitor. The visitor enters them and
is changed. The village continues.

---

**Pattern 38 — Quest-Hook Framing**

The AI frames every problem as an invitation:
"The villagers desperately need someone to...,"
"The party might choose to investigate...,"
"This presents an opportunity for the heroes
to..." The passive opportunity-framing is
D&D adventure-module language.

AI: *The villagers desperately need someone
to deal with the creature that has been
killing their livestock. This could be
a good opportunity for the party to
earn some coin.*

Human fix: "The Karn family's goat pen
was breached again. Arthe Karn's daughter
came to the inn at midnight and said it
took her father's dog and left feathers."
The problem is concrete. No one invites
anyone to solve it.

Forbidden Lands application: Every encounter
seed should present a situation, not a
mission. The party decides whether to engage
and how. The world does not present itself
as completable content.

Author model: Sapkowski. Geralt is not
invited. He arrives and the village is
already in crisis. He has to negotiate
his involvement.

---

### Layer 7: Structural and Editorial Tells

---

**Pattern 39 — The Sentimental Resolution**

The AI ends difficult scenes with a small
warm moment that restores emotional equilibrium:
after violence, a child laughs; after loss,
someone puts a hand on a shoulder; after
hardship, the fire is warm and the ale is
good. The warmth is not earned. It is
consoling.

AI: *Despite everything they had lost, the
survivors gathered around the fire that
night and found comfort in each other's
company. They would endure.*

Human fix: Let the cold stay cold. "The
fire smoked. Mira sat apart from the
others. Nobody said anything about Torvald."

Forbidden Lands application: Joy in this
manuscript is specific, small, and earned
through specific action. It is not narrative
consolation. See the Swedish tone section
in
`skills/forbidden-lands-lore/references/tone.md`.

Author model: Cook. The Company does not
have warming endings. The fire goes out.
The watch starts.

---

**Pattern 40 — The Editorial Narrative Close**

The AI closes scenes and chapters with
editorial sentences that step back from the
fiction: "And so began the strangest chapter
of their lives," "The night held its secrets
a little longer," "History would remember
this day," "Little by little, the world was
changing." These are editorial voices, not
narrative voices.

AI: *And so it was that the party left the
village behind, their hearts heavy with
what they had witnessed, stepping into an
uncertain future.*

Human fix: End on the specific physical.
"They left before dawn. The gate creaked.
No one came to close it behind them."

Forbidden Lands application: The editorial
close is perhaps the single most common
AI tell in this genre. Cut every instance.

Author model: Kay. Even his chapter endings,
which often carry weight, end on image, not
editorial summary. "She turned away. The
harbor was still. The ship was gone."

---

**Pattern 41 — Every Scene Opening With Weather**

The AI, trained on a corpus of fantasy fiction
where weather-openers are common, defaults to
weather as the first sentence of any new scene.
"The day was cold," "Rain fell on the village,"
"A grey sky hung low over the hills." By itself,
weather is not wrong. As a default — every
scene, every chapter — it reads generated.

AI: *The morning was grey and cold, with a wind
that smelled of rain. It was the kind of weather
that made people want to stay inside.*

Human fix: Vary the scene opener: sometimes a
body in motion, sometimes a sound, sometimes a
smell, sometimes a spoken line, sometimes the
middle of an action already in progress.

Forbidden Lands application: Weather is
authoritative in this manuscript but it must
be specific and physically inhabited. "Grey
and cold" is nothing. "The snow had crusted
overnight. She could hear Mira testing the
crust with her boot before she put her weight
on it."

Author model: Cornwell. He varies his scene
openers constantly. Weather, body, dialogue,
action, observation — he does not default.

---

**Pattern 42 — The Parallel Beginning-and-Ending Echo**

The AI constructs formal symmetry by echoing
the chapter or scene's opening image at its
close: scene opens with a candle, closes with
the candle going out; opens with a door, closes
with a door. The symmetry is detectable and
signals composition-by-formula.

AI: *The candle burned when she entered, its
steady flame a comfort in the dark... As she
left, she paused to blow out the candle,
leaving only darkness behind.*

Human fix: End somewhere different from where
you started. The scene changed. The ending
should carry that change, not mirror the opening.

Forbidden Lands application: The closing image
should follow from what happened in the scene,
not from the opening image. Symmetry is for
architecture. Scenes are time.

Author model: Abercrombie. His chapters end
in unpredictable places. The last line of a
chapter is often a non-sequitur that is actually
everything.

---

**Pattern 43 — The Lesson Stated Directly**

The AI states the moral of a scene at its
close: "She had learned that trust could not
be easily regained," "He now understood that
power was not the same as strength," "They
had discovered that survival sometimes meant
making impossible choices." This is the
English-class essay structure — state the
thesis at the end.

AI: *As he walked away from the village, he
finally understood that some prices were
too high, even for survival.*

Human fix: Cut the final sentence. The
preceding scene already provided the
material. Trust the reader to assemble
the conclusion.

Forbidden Lands application: The lesson must
never be stated. It must be felt. If the scene
does not create the feeling without the lesson-
sentence, the problem is in the scene, not
in the ending.

Author model: Le Guin. She is the master of
the unstated lesson. Ged's silence at the end
of *The Farthest Shore* teaches more than any
statement could.

---

## Author-Specific Evasion Techniques

Each author in the tradition avoids the 43
patterns through specific techniques:

**Sapkowski evades** by grounding every scene
in the physical and by structuring around price.
Every encounter has a specific cost. When the
cost is specific, significance inflation becomes
impossible. Sapkowski also uses dry wit to
undercut sentiment before it can form.

**Kay evades** by using omniscience as a
technique, not a default. He knows more than
his characters; he uses this sparingly and
always for melancholy weight, never consolation.
He refuses the lesson-sentence because his
chapters carry too much historical specificity
for any one lesson.

**Parker evades** by technical specificity.
You cannot inflate the significance of a
forge-weld if you describe it correctly.
Technical procedure is a natural AI-tell
repellent because it requires specific knowledge.
Parker also uses dark comedy to kill sentiment
before it lands.

**Le Guin evades** by commitment to the
anthropological voice. She describes what
people do before she names what it means.
She trusts the description so completely
that the editorial voice never appears.

**Cornwell evades** by physical immediacy.
His narrator is always in his body, always
noting the weather in terms of its specific
effect on specific body parts. Weather is
not atmosphere; it is logistics. The logistics
voice destroys the atmospheric-opener pattern.

**Gwynne evades** by oath-structure. His
narrative spine is always the oath: who gave
it, who broke it, at what cost. This commitment
to a single structural spine leaves no room
for the AI's parallel-structures and balanced-
conclusions.

## The Diagnostic Checklist

Run this checklist on every draft before
submitting or integrating.

### Vocabulary Pass

- [ ] Delete every occurrence of: ancient, primal,
      arcane, mystical, eldritch, vibrant, rich,
      tapestry, deeply, truly, meaningfully, profound,
      timeless, legendary, vast
- [ ] Cut every "serves as," "functions as," "acts as"
- [ ] Cut every promotional adjective (thriving,
      flourishing, incredible, remarkable)
- [ ] Replace every modern psychological term with
      observable behavior

### Sentence-Structure Pass

- [ ] Count em-dashes: more than one per page needs review
- [ ] Search for three-element lists: reduce to one or two
- [ ] Search for "It is not X. It is Y.": collapse every
      instance
- [ ] Search for "not just X, but Y": cut every instance
- [ ] Search for "perhaps," "somewhat," "arguably":
      cut or commit
- [ ] Search for trailing qualifiers at sentence endings

### Paragraph-Structure Pass

- [ ] Check every paragraph: does it begin with a topic
      sentence? If so, disassemble.
- [ ] Check transitions between paragraphs: remove any
      bridge sentence
- [ ] Check paragraph rhythm: are sentence lengths varied?
- [ ] Check every paragraph ending: does it balance or land?
- [ ] Check for signposting sentences: cut every instance

### POV and Interiority Pass

- [ ] Search for emotion words (fear, grief, joy, sorrow):
      replace with physical description
- [ ] Search for "she/he/they felt": replace or reframe
- [ ] Search for "little did he know," "though she didn't
      know it yet": cut entirely
- [ ] Check motivation: is it explained or observed?
- [ ] Check first-person narrators for self-analysis:
      replace with observation

### Dialogue Pass

- [ ] Check dialogue tags: replace every emotional tag
      with "said" or an action beat
- [ ] Check dialogue for backstory delivery: cut
- [ ] Check dialogue for perfect grammar: add incompleteness
- [ ] Check for recap-dialogues: cut and reduce complexity

### World-Building Pass

- [ ] Check scene openers: is every opener atmospheric?
      Vary.
- [ ] Check for over-complete information: select, do not
      inventory
- [ ] Check religious references: are they economic and
      practical?
- [ ] Check monster framing: is it obstacle-language or
      contract-language?
- [ ] Check village framing: is it adventure-set language
      or economy language?

### Structural Pass

- [ ] Check scene and chapter endings: do they balance/
      console or land?
- [ ] Check for editorial closings: cut every instance
- [ ] Check for lesson-statements: cut every instance
- [ ] Check for beginning-ending symmetry: break it

## The Revision Protocol

Apply in this order:

**Step 1 — Strip the vocabulary layer.** Find and delete
every word in the significance-inflation category. Find and
replace every "serves as" and "functions as." This pass
takes ten minutes on a typical draft and immediately
raises the quality by removing the most visible layer.

**Step 2 — Break the sentence symmetry.** Read the draft
aloud. Find the even-rhythm paragraphs. Deliberately vary
the sentence length in each paragraph: one sentence should
be short (under 10 words), one should be long (20-30
words), and neither should end on an abstract noun.

**Step 3 — Collapse the clarification patterns.** Search
for all instances of "It is not X. It is Y." and collapse
them. Search for "not just X, but Y" and cut them.

**Step 4 — Ground the emotion.** Find every emotion
announcement and replace it with the physical experience
the character is having.

**Step 5 — Fix the dialogue.** Change every non-"said"
tag to "said" or an action beat. Find every backstory-
delivery conversation and cut or rewrite.

**Step 6 — Audit the endings.** Every scene ending
that consoles, balances, or restates the theme must
be rewritten to end on a specific physical image or
fact.

**Step 7 — Apply the author lens.** Name the author
whose register this scene should match. Re-read three
paragraphs of that author. Return to the draft and
apply their specific technique.

The draft is ready when a careful reader cannot
identify the passage as generated. That is the
only standard.

### Verb Preferences

**Physical verbs — use these:**

cut, drag, bind, haul, split, twist, break, climb, carry, cling,

**Rules-prose verbs — use these:**

roll, spend, gain, lose, take, suffer, push, mark, choose, count,

succeed, fail, attempt, activate, trigger

**Inflated verbs — replace these:**

- "serves as" → "is"
- "functions as" → "is"
- "stands as" → "is"
- "represents" → "is" (unless genuinely metaphorical)
- "boasts" → "has"
- "features" → "has"
- "offers" → "gives" or "has"
- "enables" → "lets"
- "fosters" → cut the sentence and say what actually happens
- "underscores" → cut or replace with plain statement
- "highlights" → cut or replace with plain statement
- "leverages" → "uses"
- "utilizes" → "uses"
- "facilitates" → "helps" or restructure
- "encompasses" → "includes"
- "embodies" → "is"
- "navigates" → "crosses" or "enters" or the physical action
- "delves into" → "explores" or cut entirely

### Modifier Discipline

**Adjective rule:** One adjective per noun maximum unless stacking
physical observations. "A short broad-bladed axe" works — both
words describe what you see. "A powerful ancient devastating weapon"
is three adjectives doing one job.

**Earned vs. decorative:** An adjective earns its place if removing
it changes what the reader pictures. "An iron mace" — removing
"iron" changes the image. "A deadly mace" — removing "deadly"
changes nothing; maces are deadly by nature.

**Adverb rule:** The manuscript uses almost no adverbs. Replace
the adverb-verb pair with a stronger verb: "walked slowly" →
"crept" or "shuffled."

**The AI modifier problem:** AI drafts over-modify because the
model has learned that qualified statements are safe. "The
relatively dangerous somewhat unpredictable mildly corrosive..."
State the fact. If it is dangerous, say dangerous.

---

### Punctuation Restraint

**Commas:** Use for lists, for separating clauses, and for
conditional openers. Do not sprinkle for rhythm.

**Em dashes:** One per paragraph at most. AI drafts produce
2–4 per paragraph, often nested. This is one of the most
reliable AI tells in genre fiction. Replace with full stops,
commas, or restructured sentences.

**Semicolons:** The manuscript avoids them. Use only when
connecting two independent clauses that share a tight logical
link and a period would create false separation.

**Exclamation marks:** Only in dialogue. Never in rules or
flavor text.

**Ellipses:** Only in dialogue to indicate trailing speech.
Never in rules or flavor prose.

**Colons:** Used freely for introducing lists and definition
structures. Workhorse punctuation, not dramatic.

---

### Forbidden Lands-Specific Anti-Patterns

These are failure modes specific to Forbidden Lands draft text,
not general AI tells.

**Fantasy fog:** "Shadow and sorrow haunt the ancient stones
of the Forbidden Lands, where destiny intertwines with despair
beneath a blood-red sky." Not one concrete noun refers to
something touchable. Fix: replace mood nouns with material
nouns. Cold rain on a mail shirt is atmospheric. "Sorrow
haunting stones" teaches nothing.

**Therapeutic framing:** "Through their journey, the adventurers
will discover deeper truths about themselves." The manuscript
is not therapy. Characters bleed, starve, and die. Fix: cut
the sentence. If growth needs showing, show it through changed
capabilities.

**Epic inflation:** "An awe-inspiring display of raw, unbridled
magical power surged through the ancient halls." The manuscript
describes magic through specific sensory images: eyes turned
grey as granite, stone rising from a cliff. Fix: replace
inflation with one specific image.

**Design-document voice:** "This talent is designed to synergize
with the combat system's action economy." The manuscript never
acknowledges that it is designed. Fix: state the rule as a fact
about the character and world, not the system.

**Proposal leakage:** "As proposed in the balance review, this
adjustment addresses the observed power gap." Fix: remove all
design rationale. Once promoted to the manuscript, the rule
stands alone.

### Contrastive Revision Tests

Apply these five tests to any draft before finalizing.

**1. The replacement test:** Would the original authors have
written this sentence, or would they have written something
shorter, harder, and more specific? If shorter-harder-specific,
rewrite.

**2. The placement test:** Imagine the draft placed inside the
manuscript between two existing paragraphs. Would the reader
feel a voice shift? If yes, the draft has imported a foreign
register.

**3. The deletion test:** Can you remove this sentence and lose
nothing? If the remaining text says the same thing, the sentence
was filler. Cut it.

**4. The specificity test:** Does this sentence contain at least
one concrete noun — something with weight, temperature, texture,
or edges? If purely abstract, it needs an anchor.

**5. The ending test:** Read only the last five words of each
sentence. Concrete nouns, hard verbs, or material facts? Or
trailing qualifiers, soft abstractions, mood-word fog? Sentence
endings are where AI writing leaks most visibly. Fix endings
first.
