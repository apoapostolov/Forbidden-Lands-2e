## GEAR

> _"Like any girl ever let you close!"_ Molder said from the fire.

The following tables cover various kinds of goods and services available in the Forbidden Lands.

### SUPPLY

Some goods and services are rare and the adventurers can't expect to find everything they're looking for in any little village. Each item lists a Supply rating, which indicates the probability that the adventurers will find the item in a typical village.

**COMMON:** The item is generally available in decent quantities.

**UNCOMMON:** Roll a D6. If the result is 4 or higher, one D6 units of the item are available. A new roll can be made each week.

**RARE:** Roll a D6. The item is available on a 6 only, and only one item is available. A new roll can be made once every week.

**EXTREMELY RARE:** Roll a D66. The item is available on 65-66 only, and only one item is available. A new roll can be made once every week.

### ECONOMY AND PRICES

Markets in the Ravenlands run like early medieval exchanges: local, personal, and shaped by scarcity more than coin.

- **Gold is ceremonial:** Gold prices mark prestige and patronage, not daily trade. In villages, gold-priced items require a sponsor, a special order, or a rare caravan; if purchased locally, treat the item as Extremely Rare and add 1D6 weeks lead time.
- **Silver and barter dominate:** Use silver and copper for daily goods. If a price feels too high for a village, allow barter at face value or substitute a service obligation.
- **Scarcity inflation:** If an item is Rare or Extremely Rare and actually found, increase the price by 50% to 200% based on local need.
- **Specialist access:** Items that require advanced talents (smith, bowyer, builder) may be unavailable outside towns or strongholds even if the supply roll succeeds.

### CRAFTING ITEMS

Most of the items in the gear lists can be made using the **crafting** skill.

**RAW MATERIALS:** This column indicates the raw materials needed to craft the item. Raw materials can, in some cases, be other items. If no raw materials are listed, the item cannot be crafted.

**TIME:** The gear tables also list how long it takes to craft the item, assuming the artisan works for two Quarter Days per day. If additional time is spent on the work, the artisan gets a bonus to their roll.

**TALENTS:** Items are classified as _advanced_ if they require a specific talent to be crafted. This is indicated in the Talent column. If no talent is listed, the item is _simple_ and can be crafted by anyone.

**TOOLS:** Many items require specific tools to be crafted. Such requirements are listed in the Tools column. Some items even require certain functions, such as a _forge_, to be made. Read more about functions in Chapter 8. Functions don’t only exist in strongholds – the adventurers can find them in villages and other locations in the Forbidden Lands as well.

### CRAFTING AND LABOR

In the Forbidden Lands workshops are scarce, skilled labor is uneven, and time is the true price.

**WORKSHOP LIMITS:** If the required function or tool set is missing, increase crafting time by 50% and apply -1 to the roll.

**SPECIALISTS:** Advanced items require a specialist. If none is present, crafting is impossible without training or outside help.

**ASSISTANTS:** Large items (armor, crossbows, vehicles) require at least one assistant. Each assistant beyond the first reduces time by 25%, to a minimum of half the listed time with three assistants.

**FITTING TIME:** Armor requires 1-2 extra days of fitting. Poor fit gives -1 to MOVE or -1 Armor Rating until adjusted.

**SEASONAL WORK:** Outdoor or heavy craft slows in winter; increase time by 50% unless indoors with proper functions.

**Rope and Textile Quality:** When purchasing rope, cloth, tents, or clothing, choose a quality tier that affects lifespan, not performance.

- **Rough:** Half lifespan in harsh use; cost -25%.
- **Standard:** Use the table price and lifespan.
- **Fine:** Double lifespan; cost +50%. Requires a skilled maker.

If a skill roll leans on rope or cloth and really strains it (a hard climb, yanking a tent seam), check for wear by quality:

- **Rough:** Damaged on two 💀 (no bonus until repaired); Tears on 3+ 💀.
- **Standard:** Damaged on 3+ 💀 (no bonus until repaired).
- **Fine:** It doesn’t wear out from normal strain during its lifespan unless someone cuts it on purpose; when rope is damaged, 1 damage only damages it, and 2 and more damage tears it.

#### MAKESHIFT TOOLS IN THE WILDS

Every adventurer learns the worth of the ugly little things that never earn a verse in any song. A short shovel. A hand pick. A hammer. A pry-iron. A few wedges, nails, cord, scraps of leather, a knife, a flask of oil. None of it is fine, and none of it is meant to last, but being without such things can break a journey.

`Makeshift Tools` is such a kit. It is not a builder's chest, a mason's roll, or a smith's bench in a sack. It is a rough bundle of field gear for digging, prying, staking, patching, and other mean roadwork. Most such kits are thrown together from offcuts, poor iron, scrap wood, and whatever straps and bits can be spared. That is why they are cheap. That is also why they fail.

Track `Makeshift Tools` with a `D8` Resource Die when bought. Roll the die after any hard or significant use: digging through roots, snow, rubble, or loose earth; prying at lids, boards, doors, or stones; driving stakes; cutting away debris; making rough camp repairs; or patching harness, carts, shields, boats, and the like. On a roll of `1-2`, something splits, dulls, bends, cracks, or is spent.

If a task calls for a proper hand tool and you have only `Makeshift Tools`, you may still attempt the work. With a `D8` Resource Die, the roll is at `-2`. With a `D6` Resource Die, the roll is at `-3`. This only applies when the kit can stand in for the same general kind of tool. It never replaces a forge, a workshop, or a true craftsman's set.

When the Resource Die for `Makeshift Tools` is lost, treat the kit as broken. The shovel has split, the pick has come loose, the wedges are ruined, the cord and nails are gone, or too many little losses have finally made the whole bundle worthless.

The tools listed later in this chapter are proper implements for craft and heavy labor. `Makeshift Tools` is the traveler's version: rough, mixed, cheap, and never enough for long.

### MELEE WEAPONS

The Forbidden Lands have emerged from 260 years of isolation which has had an impact on the development and availability of weapons. For one, radical limitations in trade has made the raw materials for production very scarce in some villages, and those that have a supply of raw materials have been unable to distribute their produced weapons. Another factor is the survival of the weaponsmiths and armorers traditions and skills through nearly ten generations of isolation.

This usually means that simpler weapons in terms of production and materials, are more widespread after the Mist. Axes, maces, clubs, daggers and spears are more widespread than swords, as an example. Maces and axes are sometimes made from lower quality steel, iron, or even bronze, while most longer swords require both higher quality steel and craftsmanship to make them effective.

The Mist has also put an end to warfare and massed combat, and the focus of weapon development has been on small skirmishes, personal defense and defense against monsters. Swords are great for fighting against other armed opponents, but axes and polearms are usually better against oversized monsters. So even though polearms aren’t deployed in units on the battlefield, they still have had a role in defending against monstrous attackers.

It is worth remembering that both the dwarves and elves seems to have been largely unaffected by the mist, and that they would have better access to whatever weapons and armor they would prefer. But for the other kin we must keep in mind that not everyone is running around with long swords and plate armor.

#### KNIFE

Knives are tools that every self-respecting adventurer should have in their belt, and they can serve as a back-up weapon in a pinch. Knives have single-edged blades usually between 3-10 inches in length. The grip is made of two halves of wood, bone, or horn riveted and glued to either side of the tang. There is usually a small round guard between blade and grip to prevent the hand slipping onto the blade. As a tool, a knife is made for cutting, but in combat you need to stab with it to do any significant damage. All kin and cultures have knives, often of different styles.

By-knives are knives that come with another sword, usually an arming sword, that has its sheath attached to the sheath of the weapon. They are popular among adventurers, and sometimes also come with a "pricker", a short and sharp nail or needle with a small grip used as a fork, to clean your teeth, or as a tool.

#### DAGGER

Daggers are common weapons that come in a variety of different shapes and sizes. They usually have double-edged blades between 8-12 inches long. Some of the other types you can find are:

#### SHIV

A shiv is a crude stabbing blade, often made from a broken tool, a nail, or a fragment of steel filed to a point. Most have no guard and little to no grip, and are wrapped in leather or cloth for use. They are easy to hide and easy to make, but they break or slip in the hand more often than purpose-built knives.

#### SAI/PARRYING DAGGER

Parrying daggers are used in the off hand to trap and control an opponent's blade. Some are narrow, with strong knuckle guards; others are trident-like, such as the sai, with side prongs meant to catch and lever. They are heavier and more robust than a common dagger, and are primarily a defensive weapon.

#### SEAX

The seax is a broad, single-edged knife-sword used by common folk and raiders who want a cheap, rugged blade. It is longer than a knife but simpler than a shortsword, often with a straight spine and a simple wooden grip.

The lack of a guard makes parrying unsafe; a seax cannot be used to parry.

#### FALCHION

The falchion uses a cruciform hilt, but its blade has one straight, blunt spine while the sharp edge widens toward the tip. The tip can be shaped in a variety of ways depending on the maker and tradition.

#### SHORTSWORD

Short swords cover a variety of older sword designs with blades between 15-24 inches long. They fall somewhere between knives and arming swords, and remain popular as a sidearm for those that cannot afford an arming sword, or for whom those swords are too large to wield in one hand.

Fang blades are traditional goblin swords with a single-edged, fang-like blade effective for slashing and stabbing. Belifar daggers, also called cabbage-cutters, are halving swords with double-edged blades that widen near the tip and bronze hilts. Hargas are straight, single-edged knives with a diagonal point and simple grips, an old Aldernal design. Falenards resemble short arming swords with crossbar guard and pommel formed from the same piece of metal as the blade. Orcish short swords resemble a shorter cross between a falchion and a scimitar, usually with a cross-guard. Dwarves and elves also have shorter versions of arming swords, but they are not very common.

#### BROADSWORD

The dwarven broadsword is a variant of the arming sword, with straighter edges and a more abrupt point than an arming sword. It is more suited for slashing than stabbing, since the balance point lies closer to the cutting edge.

#### SWORDBREAKER

The swordbreaker is a specialized parrying dagger with a deep notched spine, designed to bind or even break a lighter blade in close fighting. It is rare outside dueling cultures and the hands of wealthy bodyguards, and its use requires practice and a steady off-hand.

#### RAPIER

The rapier is a long, slender civilian sword built for thrusting. Its blade is narrow and stiff, and the guard is often a complex cage of rings and bars to protect the hand. It excels in duels and street fights, but is less suited to hacking through armor or heavy shields.

#### LONGSWORD

The longsword is designed for fighting in two hands, so it has a wide grip commonly three hand-widths wide. The blade is between 35-44 inches long. It is still slender and well-balanced, meaning it can be gripped in one hand for extending reach for single attacks or lunges, or for grappling with the off-hand, but it is not sustainable to wield as a one-handed weapon over time.

The bastard sword is a variant of the longsword with a slightly shorter grip and blade, a hybrid between an arming sword and a longsword. The grip is about two hand-widths wide, and the blade has a lighter taper toward a more defined tip, adding mass toward the cutting edge at the expense of some stabbing efficiency. The choice between bastard swords and longswords is usually a matter of personal preference and fighting style, but the shorter bastard sword is a bit less cumbersome in the field while remaining effective against monsters and beasts.

#### 2H LONGSWORD/CLAYMORE

Two-handed longswords and claymores are long-bladed swords meant for powerful cuts and extended reach. They are heavier than a standard longsword, with long grips for leverage and a broad guard. In the field they are favored by champions and guards who expect to face multiple foes or monstrous threats.

#### TWO-HANDED SWORD

The two-handed sword, or greatsword, is too heavy to wield in one hand. It resembles the longsword, but is longer and heavier, with a blade length of between 45-55 inches, and a grip that is usually four hand-widths wide. Greatswords often have a ricasso, an unsharpened length of blade close to the guard, sometimes with a second guard above it. This allows half-swording, gripping the blade to handle the weapon more like a polearm and give more power to thrusts and parries.

#### TUCK

The tuck is a rare and specialized sword. It is basically a longsword, but the blade is slender and thick, usually hexagonal or diamond shaped, with no edges. It is a long, rigid steel bar with a point for piercing armor and is usually used with the half-swording technique. The tuck is sometimes called a rich man's spear and is most often used by warriors in plate armor trying to kill other men in plate armor.

#### SCIMITAR

The word scimitar was first used to describe the curved light swords of the elves, but it has expanded to cover all similar designs. Scimitars are single-edged, often with a short double edge near the point. The blade is thinner than an arming sword and curved back for better slashing. The scimitar is widespread among orcs, elves, and Aslene riders, and the curved blade offers an advantage when attacking with momentum from horseback.

#### HANDAXE

The hand axe or hatchet is a tool, often used as a weapon in a pinch. Its head is usually a thick wedge-shape made to chop wood, not armor or flesh, but a blow from a hand axe can still be lethal and is something to be avoided. Orcs also often bring meat cleavers as backup weapons; these heavy, square-shaped blades count as hand axes in combat.

#### BATTLEAXE

The battle axe has a handle of around 2 feet and a metal head wedged onto the handle. Unlike a woodcutting axe, the head is not thick and wedge-shaped, but slender to allow for a broader cutting edge without adding too much weight, and for cutting through armor and flesh. Some heads have a spike on the back. Double-headed axes are usually ceremonial in nature but are sometimes seen in combat as well. The battle axe is top-heavy and balanced for impact force, and is usually used with a shield.

#### TWO-HANDED AXE

The two-handed axe, or great axe, has a longer handle than the battle axe, usually 4-5 feet long, and a larger, heavier head. It is designed to be wielded with two hands. It resembles the battle axe in most respects, but it is more head-heavy and requires greater strength to wield efficiently.

#### MACE

The mace is an ancient and effective weapon, and a natural progression from the simple wooden club. It consists of a 1 1/2-2 foot hardwood handle with a metal head. The standard head has stubby pyramidal protrusions to focus percussive force and gain purchase on the target. Flanged heads use ribs to transfer force, while sculpted heads are cast to resemble demons or beasts but remain knobby enough to grip armor and transfer impact. Mace heads are often cast bronze, which is easy to work and inexpensive, making the mace popular and common.

Dwarven maces use hollow-tube metal handles instead of wood, often with flanged or sculpted heads brazed to the tube. The metal handle is sturdier, but once bent or kinked it is harder to repair and costlier to replace.

#### MORNINGSTAR

The morning star resembles the mace and warhammer, with a wooden handle about 1 1/2-2 feet long, but it has a round, heavy head set with spikes. The heavier head makes it almost useless as a defensive weapon, and it is usually paired with a shield, but it is brutal and effective against armor. This makes it a favorite among orcs in particular.

#### WARHAMMER

The warhammer has a metal percussive head fitted to a wooden handle, similar in size to a mace. Unlike a mace, it has a hammerhead on one side and a spike or beak on the other, fastened with metal bands along the handle. It is often made of hardened iron or steel to pierce armor with the back. It is top-heavy and not easy to use defensively, although the beak can hook and deflect an opponent's weapon. It is most often used with a shield.

#### FLAIL

The flail is made from a wooden handle about 2 feet long, with a solid metal head attached by a chain slightly shorter than the handle. The head is usually a ball with short studs, similar to a mace head, to help transfer force to the target. It is difficult and tiring to wield, but the chain adds momentum and makes it harder to parry and defend against. The flail is not very widespread but can be found among all kin and cultures.

#### TWO-HANDED FLAIL

The two-handed flail places a heavier striking head on a longer haft, giving it greater reach and momentum. It is a specialist weapon, often built for intimidation or tournament display, and requires room and training to use without injuring the wielder.

#### WHIP

Whips are long braided lashes used to drive animals or signal at a distance. In combat they are used to harry, entangle, and sting, relying more on reach and timing than on raw force. A combat whip usually has a reinforced thong and a weighted fall.

#### BLADED WHIP

A bladed whip is a dangerous modification that adds metal edges or hooked barbs to the lash. It tears flesh easily but is fragile, costly, and illegal in many settlements. These are prized by cruel duelists and feared as instruments of punishment.

#### RUST CENSER

Rust brothers sometimes wield their ceremonial censers as weapons in combat. These count as flails, but if the attack deals damage and you roll three ⚔️ on the attack roll, the target starts burning.

A burning person takes 1 point of damage at the end of their turn until they roll a successful Move test (slow action) to put the fire out.

#### WOODEN CLUB

The wooden club is a sturdy piece of wood, a well-turned hardwood truncheon, a peasant's flail, a roughly formed bludgeon, or any improvised blunt weapon. It is widespread among more primitive kin like Saurians, Whiners, and Ogres, and serves as a weapon of last resort for peasants and poorer folk, or a less-than-lethal option for town guards and slavers.

#### LARGE WOODEN CLUB

Large wooden clubs are heavier two-handed versions of the basic club, often rough-hewn from hardwood and used for brute force rather than finesse.

#### HEAVY WARHAMMER

What the dwarves call a warhammer differs from the common warhammer. It has a shorter handle, about 1-1 1/2 feet long, made from a hollow iron tube with a single sledge-like stone block for a head. Dwarven stone singers shape and create these heads through their rituals, and they hold great spiritual significance. Larger, two-handed versions are also in use and are called dwarven mauls.

#### STAFF

The simple staff, or quarter staff, is a hardwood staff usually 6-7 feet long, sometimes with metal caps on the ends. Its use is widespread, and it is surprisingly effective, often used for non-lethal combat practice and contests.

#### SHORT SPEAR

Spears are ubiquitous and extremely versatile weapons. They are wooden shafts with a spearhead, made for thrusting and for using their length as an advantage in combat. The short spear is usually about 6-7 feet long. In combat it is primarily used with a shield in ranked infantry formations and gripped in one hand. It is also used as a hunting weapon and may be thrown up to NEAR distance.

#### LONG SPEAR

Long spears are usually 7-9 feet long and designed to be used with two hands. The extended reach provides added defense, making it suitable to use without a shield. Long spears have heavier heads and sometimes a cross-guard below the head. They are too heavy to throw, but some are designed for hunting game like boar or bear where they are set against a charging animal.

#### PIKE

The pike is a very long spear, at least 8 feet and up to about 12 feet. It is usually reinforced with metal bands along the shaft. The length makes it unwieldy and heavy, but it excels in ranked formations where several ranks can attack at once. In the Forbidden Lands it is often used as a counter to giant or flying monsters, carried on wagons and used as a "use and drop" weapon before switching to something more sensible.

#### HALBERD

Halberds fall under the same category as bills and other polearms developed from farm tools. The bill is the most common of these, fashioned from a billhook mounted on a pole with an attached spike at the end. These polearms are popular among militias and guards, and are often chosen by warriors expecting to face monsters or mounted opponents.

#### BILL

The bill is a polearm developed from the billhook, a broad sickle-like tool mounted on a pole with an attached spike. It is commonly used by peasant militias and in ranked infantry formations. The bill is popular among warriors expecting to face monsters or mounted opponents.

There are other polearms in the same family, such as the glaive, vouge, or early forms of halberds, most of which were developed from farming tools. The bill is the most common of these, but all sorts of heads can be found on polearms.

#### POLEAXE

The poleaxe mounts an axe head, a spearpoint, and a spiked hammerhead on the end of a long wooden shaft about 5-6 feet long. It offers options to engage both armored and unarmored targets. The shaft is reinforced near the head with metal bands, and the bottom end is often capped in metal for pommeling.

The poleaxe is a sophisticated weapon, often found in the hands of knights or professional warriors and designed for single combat against armored opponents.

#### TRIDENT

Tridents are spear-like weapons with three prongs, often associated with hunting or fishing but adapted for war by some cultures. They function like spears in use, relying on reach and thrusting attacks.

### RANGED WEAPONS

The scarcity of resources in the Forbidden Lands has not impacted the manufacture of ranged weapons in the same way as melee weapons and armor. Throwing weapons can be made quite easily, and only the heaviest crossbows require quality steel for the bow. However, some of the spanning and trigger mechanisms of the crossbow requires a lot of skill to manufacture, and therefore the bow is more widespread than the crossbow.

It seems like the southern Alderlander regions and the dwarves tend to use crossbows, and the Aslene, Ailenders and other kin prefer to use normal bows, but this is a general trend and not a rule. There seems to have been more skilled crossbow-makers among the Alderlanders and dwarves, but they are found all over the Forbidden Lands.

Be aware that it is not possible to compare the draw weights of bows and crossbows, as bows are much more effective mechanically, with a much longer power stroke than a crossbow.

#### ROCK

There are three types of rock: igneous, sedimentary, and metamorphic. If you throw a decent sized rock at someone, you might hurt them.

#### THROWING KNIFE

Throwing knives are fairly heavy, flat double-edged blades, with a wide, blunt tang to balance them. They are mostly used for recreational or entertainment purposes, but in skilled hands they can do some damage to an unarmored opponent.

#### THROWING AXE

Throwing axes have sleek, arched heads and are designed to be hurled overhead at advancing or charging enemies. This tactic was often used by dwarves and orcs during the Alder wars, but axe throwing has since spread across the Forbidden Lands as recreation, hunting, and combat practice.

#### THROWING SPEAR

Javelins, or throwing spears, have been used for hunting and war since immemorial. These spears are specifically balanced for throwing and are still in widespread use today. The Saurians are particularly fond of the javelin.

#### SLING

Slings are still used for hunting and recreational target practice. Their lack of efficiency against armor has made them less used on the battlefield, but they remain common among halflings and goblins, and are sometimes used by slavers as a less-than-lethal option.

#### BLOWGUN

The blowgun is a simple tube, often of cane or hollowed wood, used to launch light darts with a sharp breath. It is nearly silent and easy to conceal, and is most effective for delivering poisons or for hunting small game.

#### BLOWGUN DARTS (IRON)

Iron blowgun darts are small metal needles, balanced for flight and meant to pierce clothing and skin. They are stronger and more reliable than wooden darts, and are often carried in waxed rolls or bone cases to keep their points sharp.

#### BLOWGUN DARTS (WOODEN)

Wooden darts are cheap and quick to make, often hardened in fire and tipped with bone or sharpened wood. They are less durable, but they travel well and can still deliver a poison if coated properly.

#### SHORT BOW

The short bow is the simplest type of bow. It is a self-bow made from a single wooden stave, often a natural laminate taken where the heartwood meets the sapwood for added strength. The notches for the string are often reinforced by horn. It is usually about 4-5 feet tall, with a draw weight around 50-60 lbs.

#### RECURVE BOW

The recurve bow is a short bow whose tips curl away from the user, giving additional mechanical force and range. This design came to Ravenland with the Quards but has been adopted by other kin and cultures. It is usually made from composite materials, though the elves have a method of making a recurve self-bow. Composite material does not tolerate humidity well because the glue degrades in damp conditions.

Recurve bows pack a better punch than short bows even though they may be shorter, typically 2-5 feet long with draw weights around 60-80 lbs. They are popular for mounted shooting and among shorter kin such as goblins and elves.

#### LONGBOW

The longbow, or war bow, is a large self-bow usually made from yew and about 6-7 feet long. It may have a draw weight of between 80-120 lbs, making it effective even against many types of armor. It takes practice and training to draw and loose effectively, but in able hands it is formidable, with a higher rate of fire than crossbows.

#### LIGHT CROSSBOW

The light crossbow is not a very powerful bow, but it is good enough for combat and is primarily considered a hunting weapon. It is quick and relatively easy to make and is found everywhere.

#### HEAVY CROSSBOW

The standard crossbow is larger, with a heavier stock and stronger bow, usually made from composite or steel. The draw weight is usually around 400-500 lbs, and it typically requires a spanning aid such as a doubler belt or goat's foot lever. It is used for combat or military purposes, is easy to train soldiers to use, and can be kept loaded for long periods, though it has a lower rate of fire than bows.

#### WINDLASS CROSSBOW

The windlass crossbow is the most powerful personal ranged weapon in existence. It has a heavy stock and a steel bow attached with steel brackets. Draw weight can reach around 1000 lbs and it cannot be spanned without significant mechanical aid.

The windlass is a manual winch that attaches to the stock. Two ropes and hooks pull the string evenly via pulleys. The cranks are turned until the bow is spanned and the string is seated, then the winch is removed. This must be repeated for each shot. The windlass crossbow is not practical at short range, but it delivers a devastating ranged attack before melee or against flying monsters.

#### THROWN WEAPON STACKING

Up to six units of a thrown weapon with the light property counts as a single light item. If the stack increases between seven and twelve units, the stack's weight increases by one category (typically, from light to normal weight). If the units exceed a dozen, the exceeding units form a second stack that counts as a new item in the character's inventory.

#### BUCKLER

The buckler is a small, circular metal shield up to about 14 inches in diameter. It has a boss with a central grip and a metal rim. Some are simple discs, others are decorated or reinforced. It is too small to be effective against missiles but is a good defensive option for close combat and is widely used, especially among human kin.

Buckler as a weapon: the buckler does not gain normal shield advantages when parrying and cannot parry missiles. Talents that affect shields do not apply to the buckler.

#### SMALL SHIELD

Small shields are under 24 inches in width or diameter and are used for personal defense. Most are fastened to the arm with straps, though older hand-grip styles still exist. Straps are often adjusted to personal preference and may be double-strapped for different uses, such as horseback or on foot. Small shields are made from planks of wood, usually covered in fabric or hide, and sometimes edged with a hide or metal rim. They are often painted in geometric shapes in vibrant colors.

#### LARGE SHIELD

Large shields come in the same forms as small shields, only larger and sturdier. Their main advantage is in ranked combat, where shields overlap to form a wall and offer better protection against missiles. They are more cumbersome to transport and use in single combat, which is why some adventurers still prefer smaller shields.

#### TOWER SHIELD

The tower shield is a 6-foot-tall and 3-foot-wide rectangular shield with a grip, a viewport, and legs so it can stand when set down. It is used to cover troops advancing on defensive positions and is not a usual tool for adventurers.

It counts as cover with armor rating 6 from missiles coming from the front. It takes a fast action to set down, and a fast action to pick up again. While set, the wielder cannot move without avbandoning the shield. It cannot be used for parrying or attacking. It is heavy and needs both hands to carry. It costs 20 silver.

The tower shield provides the same cover for up to one additional person who is sharing space with the wielder.

#### LEATHER

Leather armor, or a leather jack, is supple armor that covers the torso, upper arms, and thighs. It uses several layers of leather over critical areas with padding stitched inside. It is often supplemented with boiled leather bracers and greaves. Well-maintained, it offers flexibility, low noise, and water-resistance, making it a favorite among outdoorsmen and in communities with surplus leather.

#### STUDDED LEATHER

Reinforced leather armor is leather armor strengthened with mail pieces sewn to the leather (voiders), or with metal plates, splints, and boiled leather. It is often combined with metal guards for the lower arm and greaves. It is common in communities where wool, plant fiber, and metal are hard to find, but leather is abundant.

#### GAMBESON

Gambeson is padded armor made from several layers of thick fabric and heavy padding, fitted tightly to the body and covering the torso, arms, and thighs. It is flexible, comfortable, and quiet. The outer layer is often wool for water resistance, but it can get heavy if soaked. It is common where plant fibers or wool are readily available.

#### BRIGANDINE ARMOR

Brigandine consists of small plates riveted to the inside of a vest of supple leather or heavy fabric, worn over a padded arming doublet that reaches to the upper thigh. It often includes spaulders and vambraces and sometimes plate greaves. Brigandine offers good protection with only a modest loss of mobility and stealth and is popular among soldiers who can afford it.

#### CHAINMAIL

Mail armor consists of a sleeved hauberk reaching to mid-thigh, with mail chausses protecting the lower legs. It is often reinforced with plate vambraces, spaulders, greaves, and poleyn. Mail is worn over a padded arming doublet and often with a tabard or surcoat.

#### PLATE ARMOR

Plate armor consists of large overlapping steel plates worn over a padded arming doublet with mail voiders. It includes a breast plate, back plate, gauntlets, arm and leg plates, and other pieces. It is heavy and fatiguing over long periods, and requires assistance and time to suit up. Plate is usually only available in larger towns with specialized smithies and access to good steel.

#### ARMING DOUBLET

Brigandine, mail, and plate armor are all described as being used with an arming doublet. The arming doublet, or padded jack, is a light form of padded armor or gambeson, worn under heavier armor for comfort and added protection against blunt force.

Sometimes the arming doublet is also used to anchor or attach other armor pieces, and in the case of plate armor it has mail voiders sewn to it in critical areas. This reduces the weight compared to wearing a full suit of mail underneath heavier armor, while still covering weaknesses with mail.

There are many occasions when an armor wearer removes the overlaying armor and just wears the doublet, such as in camp, while traveling, onboard boats, or for crossing rivers. It is the GM's call to penalize players for wearing full armor in situations like this, but at the GM's discretion, players may remove their main armor and still count the arming doublet as having Armor Rating 2 while losing their Heavy or Move -2 penalties.

#### STUDDED LEATHER CAP

The padded cap or coif is made from layers of fabric and padding, sometimes with an exterior layer of leather. It can be worn under normal hoods or headwear.

#### OPEN HELMET

Open helmets are basic helmets in a variety of cultural styles. They sit on the upper skull and do not offer much protection for the neck and sides of the head. They do not impede breathing, vision, or hearing significantly. They are padded or lined with leather on the inside. Variants include nasal helmets, eye-ring helmets, turtle shell helmets, and kettle hats with flared brims.

#### MAIL COIF

The mail coif is a hood of mail that fits snugly over the skull and covers the chin, neck, shoulders, and upper chest with an opening for the mouth, nose, and eyes. It is worn over padding with a small steel skull cap beneath. Wolfkin often favor coifs because they adapt better to their head shape.

#### CLOSED HELMET

Closed helmets cover more of the head and often parts of the face and neck. Bascinets are formed from a single steel plate, with a conical top to deflect blows, and cover the temples, upper jaw, and ears. They often include an aventail, a padded mail piece covering chin, neck, and shoulders. Other variants include the older Bergand helmet with jointed cheek guards, heavy orcish helmets with eye slits, and elven helmets that leave the bowstring side open.

#### GREAT HELM

Great helms are enclosing helmets that offer maximum head protection. Common types include:

> #### ARMOR AND CLOTHES OF UNDEAD
>
> One of fantasy's greatest mysteries is the surprising efficiency of armor and weapons wielded by undead warriors supposedly long dead and decomposed into skeletons. Maybe the same sorcerous forces that animate the living dead are also preserving their equipment, or maybe even the undead wax, oil and hone their weapons?
>
> In reality most armor would be completely useless, mail would be rusted solid, and any leather or cloth would be rotted and brittle. A plate armor would fall apart due to all the straps collapsing. Axes and swords without edges are pretty much just maces, and wooden handles would be rotted to the point that they snap with any significant impact. This would depend on the age of the encountered undead of course.
>
> It is up to each GM to choose to suspend disbelief and include sharp weapons and functioning armor in the inventory of skeletal opponents, or to reduce the efficiency of 300-year-old armor and weapons to some extent.

**Flint knapped arrows:** These work just like iron head arrows. Remove 1/2 iron, smith, and Forge from the requirements, but add 1 unit of flint, obsidian, or glass.

**Masterwork Arrows/Bolts:** You can make masterwork arrows and bolts. These are made just like when you make weapons with a bonus amount of weapon dice, except that masterwork arrows add this as a skill modifier when shooting. Add a resource die for wax, when crafting the ammunition

**Bone weapons:** These weapons are like their metal-headed counterparts, but they become fragile, taking damage even on non-pushed rolls. For weapons that generally use no more than 1/2 iron to make, replace all that metal with one unit of bone. For weapons that generally use more than 1/2 iron, replace each metal unit with one unit of wood and one of bone. So, a bone sword would be wooden but with teeth or sharpened bone along its edge. Remove Smith and Forge from requirements and reduce crafting time from weeks to days and days or less to a Quarter of a Day.

**Flint knapped weapons:** These work as bone weapons but replace the bone with an equal amount of flint, obsidian, or glass. Due to their sharpness, they deal +1 damage.

**War bow:** These are made like other bows but require double the material and an extra work day. They deal +1 damage, but when you shoot, your rolled Agility dice cannot exceed your current Strength, and your marksmanship level is capped to your level of might.

**Composite Bow:** Composite bows are made of sinew, wood, and horn. Only short bows can be made into composite bows. They gain +1 weapon die and increase their range in one category. Add 1 unit of horn and 1 glue to the required crafting materials. Also increases the construction time from 1 day to 1 week. Composite bows will take damage from moisture unless you waterproof them with 1/4 unit of wax.

**Composite Crossbow:** These slightly larger crossbows replace their metal limbs with composite layers of sinew, wood, and horn. Replace all required iron with equal horn, wood, and glue. They aren't any better than regular crossbows, but lose the Smith and Forge requirements. These crossbows will take damage from moisture unless you waterproof them with 1/4 unit of wax per used glue.

**Rawhide backing:** You can make self-bows (non-composite bows) less likely to break by backing them with a layer of rawhide if you are a rank 3+ bowyer. This uses 1/4 unit of rawhide, and grants a +1 crafting die. Such bows will now take damage from moisture, unless you also waterproof them with 1/4 unit of wax.

**Firewood:** Same as wood, although made out of wooden pieces or wood-like pieces not fit to be used for crafting, but is suitable for making a fire. You can find 3 units of firewood for every 2 units of normal wood you would find during a Quarter Day's work.

**Charcoal:** 1 unit can replace 2 units of wood when used as fuel. Burns at a higher temperature than wood.

**Black coal:** 1 unit can replace 2 units of wood when used as fuel. Burns at a higher temperature than wood. Released gases make it unsuitable for cooking and open fire.

**Coke:** 1 unit can replace 3 units of wood when used as fuel. Burns at a higher temperature than charcoal and black coal. Unsuitable for cooking under open fire.

**Copper:** It is easy to use and gives +1 bonus to any CRAFTING roll. It is too soft to be used in arms and armor. Not safe for food, milk, or anything that is lightly acidic.

**Lead:** It is very easy to work with and grants +2 bonus to any CRAFTING roll. It is however too soft to be used in tools, arms, and armor and quickly melts if left near a fire. Items made of lead tend to be heavier than those made of iron.

**Tin/Zinc:** Used to make bronze/brass. Metals that tend to suffer from prolonged moisture, like mat in brigandine, are often coated with a layer of tin to avoid rusting, as well as drinking containers made out of metal.

**Bronze/Brass:** More expensive than iron and cannot match steel. Not safe for food, milk, or anything that is lightly acidic. Looked down upon by the Rust Church. Weapons and armor can replace iron with bronze/brass. It can be master worked as long as the total bonus does not exceed half of what you normally can make, round down.

**Silver:** More expensive than iron and cannot match steel. It is however easy to work with and gives a +1 bonus to any CRAFTING roll. Silver inlays (using 1/10 of metal used to craft) can be added when crafting a weapon or armor, to make it more stylish. This doubles the production time, but can give a +1 bonus to MANIPULATION. Weapons and armor can replace iron with silver. It can be master worked as long as the total bonus does not exceed half of what you normally can make, round down.

**Gold:** Items crafted by replacing iron with gold use twice the material and double the object's weight (unless the item is Tiny). It is very easy to use and gives +2 bonus to any CRAFTING roll. It is too soft to be used in arms and armor. Gold inlays (using 1/10 of metal used to craft) can be added when crafting a weapon or armor. It can then give a +1 bonus to MANIPULATION.

**Iron:** Often contains slag and high amounts of carbon. It could be variants of pig iron, cast iron, and low-quality wrought iron. The slag makes it easier to work with and lowers its melting point compared to other variants of iron, but it breaks easily. It also rusts easily and can be damaged from prolonged exposure to moisture.

**Wrought iron:** Considered a high-quality wrought iron. It is made by repeatedly beating and folding a pig iron bloom, to force out the molten slag. This will however oxidize most of the carbon, making it weaker than steel. A good smith will often reheat edges in something rich in coal and hammer those, turning those parts into mild steel. Weapons and armor can replace iron with wrought iron, this gives a -2 modifier to the CRAFTING roll, but a rank 2+ smith halves the penalty. The item will gain +1 Weapon Bonus/Armor Rating. It can be further master worked as long as the total bonus does not exceed what you normally can make.

**Steel:** Made by re-adding carbon to wrought iron under intense heat. Weapons and armor can replace iron with steel, this gives a -4 modifier to the CRAFTING roll, but a rank 3+ smith halves the penalty. The item will gain +2 Weapon Bonus/Armor Rating. It can be further master worked as long as the total bonus does not exceed what you normally can make.

**Crucible steel:** A striped composite steel made in a crucible heated in a forge originally developed in the Drawn Lands, but at least known by some Aslene. The mix of components lowers the melting point, allowing the ingredients to partially fuse. It mechanically works just like steel, but can add a natural +1 bonus to MANIPULATION due to its beauty.

**Dwarven steel:** Dwarves often add rare minerals to their finest steels: chromium, molybdenum, and nickel among others. This makes the steel immune to rust and increases its toughness. The added toughness however makes it harder to work with. Considered heretic by the Rust Church, but unless you are an inventor or alchemist it would be very hard to tell it apart from regular steel. Weapons and armor can replace iron with dwarven steel, this gives a -6 modifier to the CRAFTING roll, but a rank 4+ smith halves the penalty. The item will gain +3 Weapon Bonus/Armor Rating. It can be further master worked as long as the total bonus does not exceed what you normally can make.

**Caltrops:** These can be scattered into a NEAR zone. The zone then counts as rough terrain, and on a failed roll the target takes 1 damage, ignoring armor, but roll any item dice from boots as if it were armor rating. If damaged, any movement actions or MOVE rolls count as a slow action for 1 Day. A successful HEALING roll can negate this effect.

**Plough:** Farming tool. If using the "Field" and "Garden" stronghold functions, it is added as a requirement.

**Ironcrow:** The medieval version of a crowbar. Can grant a +2 bonus to MIGHT rolls made to bend open things.

**Whetstone:** Allows you to forge the Smith and Forge requirement when you want to repair a metal-headed melee weapon that has taken a maximum of one point of item damage.

**Tannin:** A leather worker is considered to make their own tannin as part of their work and it is not a resource you need to keep track of. It is often made of finely crushed tree bark and if needed it could be considered made from firewood. If you use the "Mill" function, a dedicated mill can help produce tannin (crushing spare tree bark and so on), you can gain a +1 bonus to all Tanner craft rolls.

**Grease:** If smeared on your skin, it grants you a +2 bonus to endurance vs cold weather, but smells really bad. If you use the "Hygiene" die, reduce your Hygiene die by 1.

**Glue:** This is kept as granules or flakes. You apply it after it has dissolved and heated in water. It has an indefinite shelf life as long as it is kept dry.

**Leather:** Regular leather is made just as rawhide, but adds the step of tanning, which changes the leather's chemical composition, preventing it from spoiling. It is after that stretched and softened. Leather will to some degree absorb water, but will not be damaged by it. Leather can be hardened through baking. That makes it tougher but stiffer. Baking is often superior to boiling leather, as that causes the leather to react with the water, plasticizing it, which causes shrinkage and lowered toughness.

**Heavy/Studded/Lamellar leather:** Treat this as extra layered leather held together with studs, it uses 4 instead of 2 units of leather. Lamellar leather is made of hardened strips of leather, but it uses similar stats. You may replace the 1/2 unit of iron with studs procured from a smith. You can then replace the requirement of Smith and Forge with that of a hammer.

**Rawhide:** Rawhide is made by taking a skinned pelt, soaking it in lime and water or urine, then fleshing and dehairing it (potash can help this process). Then finally drying it. Rawhide is tougher than leather, but very stiff. It is sensitive to moisture. If you spend a turn (15 minutes) in rain, or a day in sweaty or high moisture conditions, roll armor rating dice and deal damage on each X. Roll no more than once per day. You can make it resistant to water (but not fully waterproof - so swimming in it will still damage it), by adding 1/2 a unit of varnish per unit of rawhide. Hardened leather can be replaced with rawhide. Shields made of rawhide are especially good to make with rawhide as the shrinking, stiffness and toughness together will better hold it together if used, gain a +2 bonus to such craft rolls. It should be protected from moisture.

**Wax hardened leather:** By heating leather and wax and then putting the two together, you can fully saturate the leather with wax. This will stiffen the leather and make it more or less waterproof - making it good at containing liquids, like in waterskins. This avoids plasticizing, but the wax will to a slight degree lower piercing resistance, so for armor the end result will be similar to baking.

**Glue hardened leather:** Leather can become very hard and tough by moistening it, heating it and impregnating it with animal glue. As animal glue is soluble, it will however become sensitive to moisture unless varnished.

**Glass composite leather:** This can be made kind of like glue impregnated leather, but with a layer of crushed glass in between the flesh side of two leather sheets. You can minimize sensitivity to moisture with varnish. Hardened leather armors can replace their leather with glass composite leather, this gives a -4 modifier to the craft roll, but a rank 3+ tanner halves the penalty. The item will gain +2 Armor Rating. It can be further master worked as long as the total bonus does not exceed what you normally can make. It should be protected from moisture.

**Buckskin:** Instead of tanning, you can oil leather (the animal's own brain contains enough oil for this) and smoke it. The leather will absorb water, but will not be considerably damaged by it. The leather can become very smooth, but will smell of smoke. It lacks the chemical composition of regular leather and cannot be hardened. Leather armor or studded leather armor can replace their leather with buckskin, this gives a +2 modifier to the craft roll, but the items will lose 1 point of armor rating.

**Fur:** Made in a similar fashion to buckskin. Leather armor or heavy/studded leather armor can replace their leather with fur, this makes the armor count as winter clothing.

#### INVENTION DESCRIPTIONS

**Acid:** Most commonly sulfuric acid, also known as vitriolic acid. It is concentrated by boiling off excess water.

**Arrows, Ampule:** These arrows have glass ampule heads that can be prefilled with poisons or other substances. Doubles the target's armor rating. Will not poison a target if it fails to deal any damage. If filled with acid, then the first point of damage will be dealt as acid damage and any non-natural armor rating will be reduced by 1. Poison/acid not included in price.

**Arrows, Explosive:** Front part of arrow shafts filled with explosives that detonate if they hit something hard, like bone at great speed. Loud and can frighten animals, deals +1 damage, but doubles target's armor rating.

**Arrows, Fire:** Requires an easily accessible fire within ARM'S LENGTH. Take a -2 penalty to hit. Count the first point of damage dealt as fire damage. An additionally rolled X can, instead of dealing extra damage, make a hit target catch fire. This makes them take 1 damage after each round, unless snuffed out with a slow action and a successful MOVE roll.

**Arrows, Viscid Fire:** Requires an easily accessible fire within ARM'S LENGTH or must be made with DETONATORS. Take a -2 penalty to hit. Count the first point of damage dealt as fire damage. A hit target will catch fire, making them take 1 damage after each round, unless snuffed out with a slow action and a successful MOVE roll.

**Bombs:** These work just like thrown weapons, with range NEAR and no item bonus. They do not deal damage on a hit or from scoring extra hits, cannot be parried. Roll a resource die to see if they are consumed from use. Bombs attached with detonators must not be thrown, they can also be buried and then work just like if they were mines. Roll a SURVIVAL vs SCOUTING roll whenever an enemy steps into such a zone to see if they detonate.

**Bomb, Acid:** Inflicts 1 damage to target every round, for 1D6 rounds, or until the victim is doused with a unit of water. Armor helps against the damage, but any damage blocked by non-natural armor decreases the armor rating by the same amount. Roll a die for every target within arm's length, on a X they also are hit.

**Bomb, Smoke:** Can only be used if you have an accessible fire within ARM'S LENGTH when taking the draw or attack action or must be fitted with DETONATORS. A 5-meter area (target and surrounding SHORT zone) is enclosed in smoke for 2D6 rounds. Ranged attacks into a zone with smoke are modified by -2 and cannot, as a general rule, hit anything through the zone. If the targeted zone is flammable then it will also ignite.

**Bomb, Fire:** Can only be used if you have an accessible fire within ARM'S LENGTH when taking the draw or attack action or must be fitted with DETONATORS. Thrown against 1 target. Deal 1 fire damage on a hit and roll a die against all targets within arm's length, on a X they also take 1 fire damage. All hit targets and any nearby flammable objects keep taking one point of fire damage after each round until the fire is put out with a successful MOVE roll (slow action). Armor helps against the initial damage only.

**Bomb, Viscid Fire:** As the regular fire bomb, but deals 2 initial fire damage and rolls 2 dice against targets within ARM'S LENGTH. All targets also take a -2 penalty to MOVE rolls made to put the fire out.

**Bomb, Quicklime:** Deals 2 Agility damage to the target and 1 Agility damage to all within ARM'S LENGTH. Armor has no effect, but damage is reduced with an ENDURANCE roll. The inhalation causes nausea and troubled breathing. A target that breaks from this also becomes blinded for a turn (15 minutes). The effect of this blindness is the same as for being in total darkness. Deals no damage to undead and non-living objects, and will only deal damage if a target's head is within the impact zone.

**Bomb, Explosive:** Can only be used if you have an accessible fire within ARM'S LENGTH when taking the draw or action or must be fitted with DETONATORS. Loud, may scare animals. Deals 4 damage to the target and roll an equal amount of damage dice vs all targets within NEAR range (dealing damage per X). Armor rating protects as normal.

**Detonator:** Made from distilling urine into phosphorus or by making a fulminating mercury/silver mixture. Explodes when smashed. Can remove the need for match cord on things like explosives. As an inventor, you probably add something to prime them just before throwing, to avoid unnecessary risk of detonation from tripping or so.

**Distiller:** An apparatus that is used to separate substances by selectively boiling and condensation, also without the substances ever coming in direct contact with the fire that is used to heat it up.

**Explosives:** These could be in multiple forms, almost all use nitric acid, made from sulfuric acid and saltpeter. A feasible explosive is ammonium nitrate. It uses ammonia (can be made from urine) and is enhanced with a tiny bit of oil. Explosives can be used to destroy locked doors.

**Firearms:** Firearms require you to first have a lit match cord in order to use them. This burning cord can be pressed into the firearm's gunpowder hole as you pull the trigger. When making a masterwork firearm, you can exchange a +1 item bonus with a flintlock system, this makes a striking pin hit a frizzle and opens a hatch so that any gunpowder inside ignites. It removes the "requires lit match cord" from the prerequisite, but the weapon cannot fire if wet. Add a resource roll for flint when crafting the weapon. When making a masterwork firearm, you can exchange a +2 item bonus with a caplock system, this removes the "requires lit match cord" and reduces the time a load action takes (from slow to fast or from a fast to a free action). You have to add detonators and a resource roll for copper when making "gunpowder and bullets" for the weapon.

**Gunpowder:** Burn fast or even explodes if in confined spaces. Does not work if moist. Ruined if immersed in water as the saltpeter then wash out.

**Liquor:** See the "Recreational drugs" for more uses.

**Match cord:** This can act as a very slow burning fuse. When lit, roll a resource die each turn (15 minutes). It can be tweaked or shortened to burn faster.

**Perfume:** This is both a mild antiperspirant, germ reducing and can hide smell. Roll a resource die to gain a +1 bonus to Manipulation for a Quarter Day or to increase hygiene if you use the "Hygiene" die.

**Rubber:** Can be extracted from some vegetable roots. A rank 2+ Tanner can replace half the amount of leather when making shoes by taking a -2 Craft penalty. If so is done, the shoes will grant an extra +1 item bonus. Can also be used to make slingshots.

**Saltpeter:** Produced by distilling potash and calcium nitrate from urine moistened straw and dung. This often requires that you have access to a barn in active use or one that for a long time has been used by animals. You can then make one Lore roll per week to extract one unit per rolled X.

**Smelling salts:** Can be used as a resource for a +2 bonus to a Heal Other Agility or check made to heal Agility or to wake an unconscious person.

**Turpentine:** Can be used as lamp oil, but burns with a strong odor. Can be used to produce varnish.

**Viscid Fire:** This substance sticks to surfaces and is not as easy to wash off even with soap. If ignited, it burns hot and is very hard to extinguish.
