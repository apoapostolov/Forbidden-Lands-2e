## Politics of the Forbidden Lands

> _The messenger had mud to his thighs and blood dried black at one cuff. He knelt in the rushes before the hall fire, not because anyone asked it of him but because the ride had taken what was left in his legs._
>
> _"Fenwick has shut the gate," he said. "No grain carts through. No ferry rope lowered. They say the ford is theirs until spring and any man from Stonewall can test it with his own teeth."_
>
> _Lord Ordovast sat with one hand on the table and the other on the pommel of a sword he had not yet drawn. The room waited. Behind him stood the steward with the levy tallies, the shrine-priest with the seal chest, and a hired captain called the Gristle who watched the room the way soldiers watch a field before the horn._
>
> _"Who else?" Ordovast asked._
>
> _"Ash Mill will not send wagons until Fenwick bends. Duskbridge says they were promised safe passage and did not get it. The Raven Sisters want satisfaction for the boy hanged at the ford."_
>
> _That was politics in the Forbidden Lands. Not speeches, not scrolls. Grain halted on a road. A ferry rope left hanging. Three places deciding, in the same hungry week, that a lord's hand had grown lighter than it used to be._
>
> _Ordovast looked at the Gristle. "How many men to open the road?"_
>
> _"Enough to make them lower the rope," she said. "Twice that if you want them to lower it next month as well."_
>
> _No one in the room mistook the answer for wit. It was simply the shape of the problem laid bare._

### What This Chapter Is

A battle does not begin when the first horn sounds. It begins months earlier, when a lord asks more grain than a village will bear, when an oath is sworn before bad witnesses, when a road goes unsafe, when a shrine speaks against a ruler, or when a hired band is paid to make a refusal costly. This chapter gives you the rules for those earlier turns, when power is still measured in dues, hostages, ferries, old rights, and the number of men who will actually answer when called.

Use it when a power in the Ravenlands is more than one captain and more than one village. A faction can be a petty crown, a castle lordship, a shrine federation, a merchant league, a clan confederacy, a free company in permanent service, or a dark dominion held together by dread.

Use this chapter to:

- create a faction from scratch
- stat a faction that already exists in your campaign
- track what settlements it rules, protects, taxes, or bleeds
- run oaths, hostage bargains, tribute, charters, tolls, and feuds
- call levies, hire mercenaries, and gather campaign stores
- decide what a faction can truly put in the field before Chapter 12 begins

Do not use these rules for every loud reeve or every village elder with six cousins and a cudgel. Use them when one hand can close a gate in Fenwick, demand grain in Ash Mill, and promise protection at Duskbridge in the same hungry week. If the same power can harm, reward, or command in more than one place at once, it is large enough to deserve a faction sheet.

Use **JOURNEYS** for local standing, hospitality, rumor, and first arrival, **THE STRONGHOLD** for buildings, works, and local infrastructure, **MERCENARIES OF THE FORBIDDEN LANDS** for one company and its internal life, and **BATTLES & SIEGES** once armies are assembled and the matter is decided in open war.

### Key Terms

**Seat** — The place from which a faction rules: hall, castle, shrine, market town, hidden court, or moving camp.

**Basis of Rule** — Why people obey: blood right, old custom, conquest, protection, wealth, cult sanction, oath confederacy, or dread.

**Retainers** — Permanent sworn fighters, house guards, shrine guards, or similar long-service men.

**Levy** — Temporary military service drawn from settlements, kin groups, or oath-bound dependents.

**Tributary** — A settlement or lesser power that pays because refusal costs more.

**Vassal** — A lesser power bound upward by oath, hostages, land, marriage, or shared war.

**Feud** — Open hostility between factions, tracked from cold grievance to mustered war.

**Campaign Burden** — Food, labor, coin, wagon duty, and passage demands placed on settlements once a faction turns from rule to war footing.

These terms are not ornament. They are the working parts of rule. A tributary that stops fearing the collector may become a rebel's friend by next market day, and a feud is not a mood but a count of how near men are to saddling horses.

## The Faction

> _The map on the table was not a map in the learned sense. No scales. No neat border line. It was a hide with charcoal marks: Fenwick at the river bend, Ash Mill under a grain glyph, Duskbridge marked by a square and the little hanging loop Gest used for bridges. Around each mark were smaller signs — three strokes for men, circles for stores, a hook for the ferry, a cross where the shrine stood._
>
> _The Gristle watched Ordovast move a black river-stone from Stonewall to the ford and understood, before he spoke, that he was not deciding whether to send men. He was deciding whether he still ruled there at all._

### What a Faction Is

A faction is a durable power in the world. It is not just a named ruler. It is a seat, a claim, a body of dependents, a means of collection, a means of coercion, and a way to turn obedience into armed force.

A faction may be:

- a castle lordship
- a petty crown
- a militant faith
- a clan confederacy
- a merchant road league
- a free company in permanent service
- a cult domain
- an undead or demonic power with subjects enough to matter

If it cannot collect, compel, protect, punish, or raise men beyond one household, it is not yet a faction for the purposes of this chapter, no matter how proud the banner above its door.

You can still use these rules for powers that are small in land but wide in consequence. One shrine that draws tithes from three villages, one toll hall with hired guards, or one mercenary camp that has begun taking grain instead of wages may already be a faction. The question is never whether the seat is grand. The question is whether the power can make trouble in more than one place before nightfall reaches its own fire.

### Creating a Faction

Build a faction in this order.

1. **Name the seat.** Where does this power stand?
2. **Choose the basis of rule.** Why do people obey?
3. **Choose a faction tier.** Thin, Established, or Dominant.
4. **Assign attributes and skills.** Give the faction strengths that match its history.
5. **Choose talents.** Two to four are enough.
6. **List its holdings.** Settlements, roads, shrines, bridges, ferries, strongholds, markets.
7. **State its resources.** Treasury, stores, labor, levy, retainers, hired bands.
8. **State its current aim.** What is it trying to do right now?
9. **State its present weakness.** What is beginning to fail?

Build in that order and do not skip the last two lines. A faction's aim tells you what it will spend itself on. Its weakness tells you where the knife already is. Two lordships with the same numbers do not play alike if one is trying to reopen a ford before freeze and the other is hiding a disputed heir in a shrine cellar.

### Faction Tier

Choose one of the following as a starting frame.

| TIER | ATTRIBUTE ARRAY | SKILL ARRAY | TALENTS |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Thin Power** | 3, 3, 2, 2 | 2 skills at 3, 4 at 2, 6 at 1 | 2 |
| **Established Power** | 4, 3, 3, 3 | 4 skills at 3, 6 at 2, 6 at 1 | 3 |
| **Dominant Power** | 5, 4, 4, 3 | 6 skills at 3, 6 at 2, 4 at 1 | 4 |

Leave every other faction skill at 0.

**Thin Power** is one hard seat, one real circle of dependents, and a reach that ends quickly once bad luck begins. **Established Power** can survive one bad turn without showing its ribs. **Dominant Power** is large enough to act in one valley while trouble is already ripening in another. Use the highest tier sparingly. Most powers in the Ravenlands claim more than they truly hold.

### The Faction Sheet

Record the following.

- Name
- Seat
- Type
- Basis of Rule
- Current State
- 4 attributes
- 16 skills
- Talents
- Directly ruled settlements
- Protected settlements
- Tributaries
- Vassals and allies
- Strongholds, roads, bridges, shrines, ferries, markets, mines, or similar key holdings
- Treasury
- Stores
- Campaign Points
- Available labor
- Available levy
- Retainers and hired bands in service
- Standing with important settlements and factions
- Active feud states
- Current aim
- Current weakness

You do not need to rewrite the whole sheet every turn. Holdings, basis of rule, and talent ranks change rarely. Standing, feud, treasury, stores, campaign points, and which settlements are turning cold are the things that move most often. If you want less bookkeeping, keep one current sheet and note only what changed this week in the margin.

### Treasury and Stores

Track both with one of four ratings.

| RATING | TREASURY | STORES |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Bare** | Coin exists only in immediate hands. A bad week is a crisis. | Granaries and stores are thin. Campaigning is short and ugly. |
| **Thin** | The faction can meet ordinary duties, but not shocks. | Enough for routine obligations and one short push. |
| **Sound** | The faction can pay retainers, bribe, repair, and weather trouble. | Enough for a serious muster or a brief siege. |
| **Deep** | The faction can sustain shocks, buy loyalty, and fund a campaign. | Granaries and warehouses are heavy enough to feed a real host. |

Step a rating down when the faction pays for something large without replacement: ransom, bad harvest, campaign stores, emergency repairs, bought peace, several weeks of hired troops. Step it up only through clear gain: harvest, tolls, windfall, conquest, rich marriage, reopened trade, or several quiet turns of sound rule.

Do not let Treasury stand in for Stores or the other way round. A lord may have silver from bridge tolls and still watch his horses founder for want of oats. A shrine may have full granaries and no coin to stop hired swords from prying open the altar chest. When one is high and the other is low, play the strain honestly.

### Labor, Levy, and Retainers

Track the available strength behind the faction. These are not ornamental notes. They tell you what a faction can attempt before the dice are lifted, and what kind of war it can sustain before the villages begin to empty.

| RESOURCE | RATINGS |
| --- | --- |
| **Labor** | Light, Working, Heavy |
| **Levy** | None, Small, Ready, Heavy |
| **Retainers** | None, Handful, Company, Strong Garrison |

**Heavy** labor means roads can be repaired, mills cleared, walls patched, and carts loaded in a hurry, but someone is not in the field that day. **Ready** levy means spears exist, names are counted, and households know what they owe; it does not mean they will march gladly. **Strong Garrison** means there are enough hard men in one place to make weaker neighbors think twice before the first blow is struck.

## Basis of Rule

> _The priest's hand shook only when he set the seal down. Not while he preached. Not while he named three households defiled by refusal. Only when he pressed wax to wood and felt, in the room behind him, the silence of men who might obey because they believed him or because they feared the crowd more than they feared the gods._

Choose the main basis of rule. Every faction has traces of others, but one should be primary.

| BASIS OF RULE | WHAT IT RESTS ON | FAVORED SKILL | MANDATE IS DAMAGED WHEN... |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Blood Right** | lineage, inheritance, old names, remembered claims | Claim | succession is disputed, heirs are slighted, or old right is mocked publicly |
| **Old Custom** | precedent, local law, accepted forms, known dues | Decree | the ruler breaks custom, ignores judgment, or overturns what should have stood |
| **Protection** | patrols, defended roads, justice, safe harvests | Accord | the ruler fails to protect those who paid for protection |
| **Conquest** | force remembered, fear of defeat, visible strength | Rite or Assault | weakness shows openly, or a challenge goes unanswered |
| **Wealth** | stores, markets, coin, credit, bought loyalties | Accord or Traffic | the coin dries up, tolls fail, or the market turns elsewhere |
| **Cult Sanction** | shrine authority, tithes, sermons, sacred law | Rite | the priesthood splits, the rites fail, or sacrilege stands unanswered |
| **Oath Confederacy** | mutual war-bonds, hostages, negotiated service | Accord | oaths go unpaid, hostages are mishandled, or one sworn ally is left unsupported |
| **Dread** | terror, exemplary punishment, monstrous force | Decree or Assault | the ruler is seen to hesitate, fail, or forgive where fear expected blood |

A faction may gain a second basis later. Do not start with two unless the history truly requires it.

The basis of rule tells you what a faction sounds like when it justifies itself. Blood Right speaks of fathers, graves, and inheritance. Protection speaks of wolves on the road and barns that did not burn. Wealth points to open market stalls and full ledgers. Dread rarely explains anything at all. When a basis is damaged, the injury should be public: a judgment refused, a convoy robbed in daylight, a challenge unanswered before witnesses.

## Faction Attributes

### Mandate

Mandate is the accepted right to command. It measures legitimacy, oath-weight, public obedience, and the willingness of lesser powers to comply. A faction with high Mandate does not need to shout to be obeyed.

### Iron

Iron is armed weight. It measures retainers, levy strength, coercive force, readiness for violence, and the ability to keep fighting after loss. A faction with high Iron can make refusal expensive.

### Road

Road is reach. It measures scouts, couriers, patrols, ferries, bridges, trade movement, redeployment, and how far a faction's hand can actually travel. A faction with high Road can act beyond its own doorstep.

### Hearth

Hearth is the lived base of rule. It measures grain, coin, labor, workshops, repair, relief, and the ability to restore what war and weather have spent. A faction with high Hearth can survive its own victories.

When you are unsure which attribute is really under strain, ask what kind of failure the faction is suffering. If people no longer believe, it is **Mandate**. If men will not stand, it is **Iron**. If orders cannot reach, it is **Road**. If the mills are broken, the carts late, and the granaries hollow, it is **Hearth**.

### When an Attribute Reaches Zero

| ATTRIBUTE AT 0 | RESULT |
| --- | --- |
| **Mandate** | One ruled settlement, vassal, priestly circle, or captain openly refuses command, defects, or demands new terms now. |
| **Iron** | The faction cannot call further levy, impose new force, or sustain offensive action until Iron recovers. |
| **Road** | Distant action collapses. Couriers fail, remote garrisons drift, and the faction's reach contracts toward the seat. |
| **Hearth** | Stores, treasury, labor, or repair fails hard. Campaign action stops until the base is restored. |

Do not soften these effects. The point of faction play is that rule breaks in visible ways.

## Faction Talents

> _The old wall at Stonewall had been repaired four times in living memory and built three times before that, each layer laid by men who assumed the next bad year would come from outside. They were wrong as often as they were right. A wall still remembers both kinds of siege._

Faction talents are long habits of rule. They are not tricks. They are the way a power has learned to survive, the habits a realm falls back on when weather, debt, rebellion, and war all press in at once.

### Path of Blood Right

**Rank 1:** When you Press a Claim based on inheritance, memory, or old title, add one extra success if you roll any success at all.

**Rank 2:** When an heir, hostage, or marriage tie is produced before witnesses, hostile Standing related to succession improves by 1 step for this turn.

**Rank 3:** One named retainer household or allied lineage stays firm when Mandate would otherwise fall to 0.

**Rank 4:** Rival claimants take +1 difficulty to turn one of your ruled settlements against you.

**Rank 5:** Once per season, a public Rite tied to lineage restores 1 Mandate without a recovery turn if it is witnessed and uncontested.

### Path of Tribute

**Rank 1:** Regular dues from one ruled or protected settlement can be collected without a roll if Standing there is not negative.

**Rank 2:** The faction can hold one tributary settlement at normal tribute without advancing Feud this season.

**Rank 3:** When forced collection succeeds, take one extra unit of goods, coin, or service before the settlement begins to hide stores.

**Rank 4:** Two tributaries may be linked under one collector route; handling both no longer costs an extra minor act.

**Rank 5:** Once per campaign week, one forced collection may be treated as ordinary tribute for supply purposes, but Standing still falls. The cost is delayed, not erased.

### Path of the Iron Muster

**Rank 1:** Levy answers faster. The first week of a Muster state raises one additional contingent from ruled ground.

**Rank 2:** One levy contingent can be held an extra campaign week before the first service-roll is required.

**Rank 3:** The first failed Discipline roll in a campaign week counts as a mixed success instead: the troops hold, but Iron takes 1 damage.

**Rank 4:** One reserve captain or proven sergeant may keep a second contingent steady without the ruler's direct presence.

**Rank 5:** When demobilizing after a campaign, reduce the usual Standing loss from hard service by 1 at every ruled settlement that supplied levy.

### Path of Stone and Timber

**Rank 1:** Works tied to roads, bridges, palisades, or field camps take one fewer turn-step to establish in play.

**Rank 2:** A damaged stronghold or bridge restores 1 extra effect step whenever a Works roll succeeds.

**Rank 3:** Camps, ditches, and forward stockpiles count as one extra relevant asset die for Siegecraft or Stores.

**Rank 4:** One stronghold under this faction always counts as prepared ground when Chapter 12 begins there.

**Rank 5:** Once per season, prevent 1 Hearth damage from war or weather by consuming timber, stone, and labor already on hand.

### Path of the Caravan

**Rank 1:** Safe-conduct, toll, and trade passage acts using Traffic gain +1 success if any success is rolled.

**Rank 2:** One road, ferry, or bridge under the faction's control counts as protected trade ground; minor raids there do not immediately reduce Treasury.

**Rank 3:** A caravan or convoy may serve as courier relay. Treat one distant settlement as one step closer for Relay this turn.

**Rank 4:** When Stores are moved by road or river under guard, the first attempt to cut that supply line each campaign week takes +1 difficulty.

**Rank 5:** One major contract, toll-season, or charter bargain each season raises Treasury one step if it resolves cleanly.

### Path of the Shrine

**Rank 1:** A public Rite before a shrine, relic, or acknowledged priest adds one asset die.

**Rank 2:** One settlement under cult sanction may ignore a first minor grievance this season if the shrine publicly supports the ruler.

**Rank 3:** Fanatical or sworn troops raised through the shrine gain +1 Discipline for one campaign week.

**Rank 4:** Sanctuary, interdict, or public condemnation can be used as a political act with Accord or Rite instead of Assault.

**Rank 5:** Once per season, a holy proclamation may turn one allied or neutral settlement into protected ground without taking a separate Grant Protection act.

### Path of Hidden Knives

**Rank 1:** Intrigue rolls to gather blackmail or private dirt gain one extra success if they succeed.

**Rank 2:** A forged message, bought guard, or bribed ferryman counts as an asset die once per turn.

**Rank 3:** Once per season, force an enemy faction to re-roll a successful Relay or Accord act and keep the worse result.

**Rank 4:** A quiet removal, theft, or vanished courier can advance a feud without openly naming your faction as the source.

**Rank 5:** Once per campaign, cancel one enemy minor act entirely by revealing or triggering work you already set in motion.

### Path of Horse and Hound

**Rank 1:** Scout and courier acts using Watch or Relay count one terrain step easier than normal.

**Rank 2:** Mounted or fast-moving contingents under this faction arrive one campaign step sooner than ordinary levy.

**Rank 3:** Retreating enemies are harder to shake. A successful pursuit or road-cut automatically worsens their Feud or Standing position by one step.

**Rank 4:** One hostile courier line or road movement per campaign week may be intercepted before it reaches its destination.

**Rank 5:** Once per battle campaign, turn a broken enemy flank into a pursuit advantage when Chapter 12 begins, if your fast troops were present.

### Path of Terror

**Rank 1:** Intimidating compliance through Decree or Assault takes one lower difficulty when the target has already seen blood.

**Rank 2:** Occupied settlements do not test immediate revolt on the first week of occupation.

**Rank 3:** One public punishment per season restores 1 Iron or prevents 1 Discipline loss, but Standing drops by 1 in one additional nearby settlement.

**Rank 4:** A faction acting under dread may hold a tributary one extra turn at negative Standing before it snaps to open Feud.

**Rank 5:** Once per season, force immediate compliance from one lesser power without a roll. Afterward, advance Feud by 1 and reduce Mandate by 1 unless the faction's basis of rule is Dread.

## Faction Advancement

Factions do not grow by finding one old master in a tower. They grow by surviving hunger, swallowing lesser powers, drilling men until obedience becomes habit, buying new officers, hardening roads, and learning from every humiliation that did not quite kill them. Track **Campaign Points (CP)** for every faction that matters across more than one turn, and award them only when something real has changed in the world.

A faction gains no CP for an ordinary success on the dice. It gains CP when it has widened its hold, broken a rival's design, survived a dangerous season, won a campaign, or absorbed land, stores, and loyalty that used to belong to someone else. Spend CP only at the end of a **Season Turn**, after a **Campaign Week**, or in the quiet between campaigns—never in the middle of an act, battle, or siege.

### Awarding Campaign Points

| RESULT | CP |
| --- | --- |
| **Minor success** | **1** |
| **Major success** | **2** |
| **Significant victory** | **5** |
| **Great conquest or absorption** | **10** |

Read those results broadly. A minor success might secure a useful pact, reopen a road, or hold what would likely have been lost. A major success might gain a new settlement bond, force lasting obedience, or turn a local balance of power. A significant victory might win a campaign objective, force surrender, crush a rival move, or establish a new political reality. Great conquest or absorption is what happens when a faction destroys, conquers, or truly swallows an enemy seat, its roads, its stores, or its tributaries.

Do not pay twice for the same event. If one victory already earned **5 CP**, do not also pay **2 CP** because a road opened on the way.

### Spending Campaign Points

A faction needs no **Teacher** to improve. Its teachers are defeat, profit, blood, drilled labor, and the memory of what nearly broke it. Spend CP only when the fiction explains the change: a conquered toll road may justify **Traffic**, a season of hard service may justify **Discipline**, a stronger cult hold may justify **Path of the Shrine**, and absorbed villages may justify **Hearth** or **Mandate**.

Raise skills, attributes, and talents one rank or step at a time. A faction can never raise an attribute above **5** through CP, and no skill or talent should exceed the ranks already used elsewhere in this chapter.

Example: Stonewall breaks Fenwick, secures the ferry, and forces the river road open again. That may be **5 CP** for the victory itself. If those points are spent on **Traffic**, the meaning is that the reopened ferries and tollmen now answer more cleanly than before. If the same points go into **Path of Tribute**, the victory taught a different lesson: not how to move goods, but how to keep lesser places paying after the fear of battle begins to cool.

### Raising Skills with CP

| --- | --- |
| **0 → 1** | **2** |
| **1 → 2** | **4** |
| **2 → 3** | **6** |
| **3 → 4** | **8** |
| **4 → 5** | **10** |

## Faction Rolls

### The Roll

A faction act is resolved with:

**attribute + skill + one asset die + modifiers**

The asset die is a normal d6. It represents a relevant stronghold function, shrine, market charter, road net, named lieutenant, hostage, or prepared advantage. A faction normally gains **one** asset die, not more. If several advantages apply, choose the most important one.

### Difficulty and Opposition

Most faction acts are rolled against a difficulty from 1 to 4.

| DIFFICULTY | MEANING |
| --- | --- |
| **1** | resistance is weak, or the faction is acting within accepted right |
| **2** | the matter is disputed, expensive, or exposed |
| **3** | the act is hard, risky, or runs against another strong interest |
| **4** | the act strains the faction's actual reach, legitimacy, or stores |

When another faction actively resists, both sides roll. The higher number of successes wins. Ties favor the defender or the present order.

### Extra Successes

Extra successes matter.

- In diplomacy, they improve terms.
- In collection, they widen compliance or increase yield.
- In intrigue, they deepen penetration or reduce traces.
- In military preparation, they speed concentration or improve readiness.

Do not count extra successes if the act would not logically widen.

Do not roll just because a faction exists. Roll when another will, another shortage, or another armed hand pushes back. Routine rule at good Standing can be read from the sheet. Dice belong on the table when a road is cut, an oath is doubted, the granary is thin, or two powers want the same thing before the week is out.

### Pushing

Factions may push rolls.

When a faction pushes:

- **banes on base dice** damage the relevant attribute
- **banes on asset dice** damage, expose, or poison the asset: a bridge wrecked by haste, a priest alienated, a steward compromised, a hostage mishandled, a settlement turned cold

Examples:

- A hard Yield roll wrings food from a village, but Hearth takes damage because the land and stores were spent too deep.
- A Rite roll raises zeal for war, but Mandate takes damage because the priesthood has stirred something that obeys poorly.
- A Relay roll forces men and horses too hard, and Road takes damage because the courier line is blown open after the message arrives.

### Recovery

Faction attributes recover slowly. At the start of a **Season Turn**, recover 1 point in up to two damaged attributes if the fiction supports it.

- **Mandate** recovers through kept promises, sound judgment, visible justice, remission, witnessed mercy, or victory that still looks lawful.
- **Iron** recovers through rest, replacement, repaired arms, fresh horses, new captains, and ending hard service.
- **Road** recovers through repaired crossings, patrolled roads, rebuilt courier lines, fresh scouts, cleared snow or flood wreckage.
- **Hearth** recovers through harvest, reopened trade, repaired works, relief, new labor, or ordinary quiet after strain.

A faction in active Campaign state rarely recovers more than 1 attribute in a season. During active **Campaign** state, assume recovery is paused unless the faction fully breaks contact, withdraws from field action, or clearly spends real stores, coin, and labor on emergency repair. A campaigning power may recover 1 damaged attribute only by paying for it in the fiction: fresh officers, replacement mounts, rushed repairs, bought grain, hired healers, or similar costly measures.

## Rule, Obligation, and Settlement Bonds

> _The elder of Ash Mill took the cup with both hands and did not drink. That was answer enough. A woman who trusts your protection drinks while the offer still hangs in the air. A woman who is measuring what it costs her sets the cup down first and asks what her sons owe in spring._

### Settlement Status

Every important settlement stands in one of these relations to a faction.

| STATUS | WHAT IT MEANS | WHAT THE SETTLEMENT OWES | WHAT THE FACTION OWES |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Ruled** | direct authority, steward, garrison, or accepted lordship | tax, labor, levy, ordinary obedience | judgment, defense, and the maintenance expected of rule |
| **Protected** | local leadership remains, but protection is purchased or sworn | tribute, provisions, warning, shelter, agreed aid | protection, patrol, arbitration, and restraint |
| **Tributary** | dues are paid because refusal is dearer | food, coin, hostages, passage, labor | little beyond delay of open violence |
| **Vassal** | a lesser power bound upward | attendance, service, men under arms, oath obedience | backing, legitimacy, aid when feud hardens |
| **Occupied** | held by force with garrison, hostages, or terror | whatever can still be taken | nothing stable beyond pressure |
| **Allied** | independent, but linked by mutual need | aid as agreed | reciprocal aid and non-aggression |

A settlement can change status. That is most of politics.

### Political Instruments

These are the common tools of power.

**Protection Pact** — A settlement pays because another power keeps road, ford, harvest, or shrine safer than it would otherwise be.

**Fealty Oath** — A lesser ruler, captain, or shrine binds upward.

**Hostage Compact** — A child, sibling, sworn man, or ward is kept as proof that an agreement still lives.

**Betrothal or Fosterage** — Long peace bought through kinship rather than immediate obedience.

**Market Charter or Toll Right** — Authority over crossing, bridge, ferry, road, or market day.

**Temple Sanction** — A shrine, priesthood, or cult makes rule cleaner or dirtier in the eyes of the faithful.

**Outlawry** — A person or household is stripped of ordinary protection and may be seized, hunted, or denied shelter.

### Standing and Reputation

Use the **Standing** rules from **JOURNEYS** for factions as well as fellowships. A faction may hold Standing with:

- settlements
- other factions
- priesthoods
- merchant circles
- kin groups
- named captains or companies

Use **Reputation** the same way. A faction can be feared, respected, cursed, or welcomed in a region long before its banner is seen. At faction scale, read Standing broadly.

Standing is local memory made visible. It is the ferryman who still lowers the rope at dusk, the elder who sends a boy running ahead with warning, the priest who speaks the ruler's name without spitting first. When Standing falls, do not describe a number changing. Describe the cup set down untouched, the gate opened a moment too slowly, the toll demanded in a new tone.

| STANDING | MEANING |
| --- | --- |
| **+2** | allied, trusted, or warmly received |
| **+1** | favorable, useful, or treated as rightful partner |
| **0** | neutral, wary, or merely customary |
| **-1** | cold, obstructive, or ready to resist |
| **-2** | hostile, shut, or on the edge of feud |

### Feud Track

Track the state of hostility between factions.

| STEP | STATE | MEANING |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **0** | Cold Peace | no open movement, but pressure may already be there |
| **1** | Grievance | insult, claim, refused due, broken word, border trouble |
| **2** | Pressure | hostages demanded, roads watched, bribes, raids, hard bargaining |
| **3** | Armed Feud | retainers gather, roads become dangerous, outposts are tested |
| **4** | Mustered War | open campaign, siege, or battle is close enough to count |

Advance the track when insult is not repaired, when dues are forced, when roads are cut, when blood is spilled, or when occupation begins.

Reduce it through:

- public compensation
- witnessed apology backed by real payment
- hostages returned cleanly
- marriage or fosterage that both sides accept
- real service rendered to the aggrieved party
- one full quiet season without contact

Do not reduce Feud simply because time passed if the injury remains visible.

## The Faction Turn

> _By midwinter the work of rule had turned small again. Not war-horns. Not banners. One missing wagon from Duskbridge. A priest asking whether the tithe would be taken in grain or coin this year. Two brothers in Ash Mill claiming the same strip of river meadow and both of them too proud to be wrong in front of the same witnesses._
>
> _That was the shape of power when it was healthy enough not to march._

A faction acts in one of four states.

| STATE | WHAT IT LOOKS LIKE |
| --- | --- |
| **Peace** | ordinary dues, local judgment, patrols, gifts, trade, small repairs |
| **Pressure** | spies, bribes, raids, stockpiles, road closures, hostage demands |
| **Muster** | levy called, stores gathered, roads secured, captains summoned |
| **Campaign** | armies in the field, regular burden, cut supply, siege, open war |

### Season Turn

Use this in **Peace** and **Pressure**.

Resolve each major faction in this order.

1. **Recover** — restore damaged attributes where the fiction allows.
2. **Count Dues** — routine taxes, tithes, tolls, and agreed tribute come in.
3. **Check Oaths** — note what is due, disputed, or close to failing.
4. **Choose Acts** — one major act and one minor act.
5. **Resolve Consequences** — Standing shifts, Feud shifts, status shifts, stores rise or fall.

### Campaign Week

Use this in **Muster** and **Campaign**.

Resolve each active faction in this order.

1. **Pay and Feed** — decide whether troops, mercenaries, and roads are actually sustained.
2. **Move or Concentrate** — march, redeploy, escort stores, call reserve, cut roads.
3. **Take One Campaign Act** — raid, invest, reinforce, negotiate surrender, cut supply.
4. **Check Burden** — apply extraction, hunger, disease, or local resentment.
5. **Escalate or Hand Off** — if a real battle begins, go to **BATTLES & SIEGES**.

### Major and Minor Acts

A faction normally takes:

- **one major act** per Season Turn
- **one minor act** per Season Turn
- **one campaign act** per Campaign Week

Minor acts are the small things that support rule: send gifts, dispatch riders, hear petitioners, shift a small garrison, issue safe-conduct, inspect a bridge, gather quiet dirt. Major acts change the political map: call levy, press claim, hire a band, grant protection, impose tribute, take hostages, occupy a place, settle or break a feud. If a faction clearly lacks the reach to do two things at once, do not let the turn economy lie for it.

If the player characters are the knife-edge of the act, let the faction roll set the field rather than settle every detail. A successful **Grant Protection** roll may mean the village is willing to hear terms at all, but the fellowship still has to escort the first grain carts through wolf-country. A failed **Call Council** roll may still leave one priest or widow willing to talk in private after dark. The faction rules set pressure. The characters bleed inside it.

## Revenue, Tribute, and Burden

> _The grain was measured in silence. That was the part the steward hated most. Not the sacks. Not the arguments. The silence of a household watching the scale and knowing the hand on the other side had counted their winter before they did._

### Ordinary Dues

A stable power does not collect at war rate.

Ordinary dues are:

- tax
- rent
- tithe
- toll
- labor days
- wagon service
- shelter rights
- market percentages

Collect ordinary dues with **Hearth + Yield**.

| SITUATION | DIFFICULTY |
| --- | --- |
| ruled settlement, positive Standing | 0 |
| ruled settlement, neutral Standing | 1 |
| protected settlement, terms clear | 1 |
| dues raised above custom | 2 |
| tribute disputed, or settlement already strained | 3 |

On success, the dues come in. On failure, one of the following happens:

- the settlement pays short
- the settlement delays openly
- Standing falls by 1
- Feud advances by 1 if the matter was already heated

A short payment is not just arithmetic. It means some household hid sacks under wet hides, some widow swore the sheep died of rot, or some reeve decided this was the season to learn how much weakness lived in the lord across the river.

### Campaign Burden

Campaign burden is different. It is what a faction takes once war, mustering, or occupation has begun. Use the burden ceilings already established in **BATTLES & SIEGES**. Campaign burden is counted over **seven consecutive days**. When a settlement provides three times its population in supplies, that total is the outer acceptable pull for one campaign week before real local harm begins.

A settlement can be asked for, per week:

- up to **three times its population in supplies** without immediate collapse
- up to **five times its population in copper** as the outer acceptable cash demand
- more than that only by destructive over-extraction

Chapter 11 decides **who is asked** and **what it costs politically**. Chapter 12 tells you the hard physical ceiling.

### Burden Levels

| BURDEN | EFFECT |
| --- | --- |
| **Light** | no immediate harm; the settlement still lives its own life |
| **Hard** | Standing -1 unless the settlement is fiercely loyal; next Settlement Turn worsens by 1 |
| **Crushing** | Standing -2, Feud +1, one household or local power holder gains Need +1 or Heat +1 if you use village play |
| **Ruinous** | use the starvation, collapse, and deterioration rules from Chapter 12 immediately |

**Hard** burden is the point where ordinary rule begins to taste like occupation. **Crushing** burden is where names are remembered. If you are unsure whether the demand crossed the line, ask who will still speak for the faction after the wagons leave.

### Tribute and Tolls

Use **Mandate + Accord** or **Hearth + Yield** when tribute is recognized and grudgingly accepted. Use **Iron + Assault** or **Mandate + Decree** when tribute is imposed by open pressure. Toll rights rely on **Road + Traffic**. A toll road that is not watched is only a place where armed men take coin until someone stronger arrives.

### Over-Collection

Over-collection has visible costs.

- Standing falls.
- Mandate is damaged.
- Feud advances.
- Future levy weakens.
- Trade turns away.
- Villagers hide stores, flee, or invite a rival power in.

Do not make the political cost vague. Name the harmed household, the shuttered market, the empty byre, the closed gate.

## Political Acts

> _The elder did not agree. He yielded. Those are not the same thing, and only fools fail to count the difference._

Use these acts as the main procedures of faction play.

They do not replace scenes. They tell you what stands when the scene ends. Use them when the question is not whether the conversation happened, but whether the village yielded, whether the oath held, whether the hostage truly bought obedience, or whether the collector came back with sacks and bruises instead of excuses.

### Grant Protection

A faction binds a settlement under rule or protection. Roll with **Mandate + Accord**.

| SITUATION | DIFFICULTY |
| --- | --- |
| settlement asked for protection | 1 |
| settlement is afraid and exposed | 1 |
| settlement has another suitor or old protector | 2 |
| settlement distrusts the faction | 3 |

On success, the settlement becomes **Protected**. With 2+ extra successes, it may become **Ruled** if the fiction supports direct lordship. On failure, Standing falls by 1 and the settlement seeks another shield.

Protection that is never proved rots fast. If the faction won this act because the village feared raiders, the first missed patrol, burned barn, or robbed cart should test that new bond immediately.

### Collect Due

A faction gathers ordinary dues, delayed payment, or tithe. Roll with **Hearth + Yield**.

On success, coin, grain, labor, or transport comes in as expected. On failure, payment is short, delayed, or actively contested.

Use this act for ordinary collection, not for wringing blood out of a place already on the edge. If the sacks come in under spearpoint, or the villagers watch from behind barred doors, you are no longer collecting due. You are imposing tribute or burden, and the cost should be paid elsewhere on the sheet.

### Press a Claim

A faction asserts inheritance, old boundary, temple right, or lawful possession. Roll with **Mandate + Claim**.

On success, the claim becomes politically real. Another faction must now answer it or suffer Standing loss where the claim matters. On failure, the claimant looks grasping, weak, or false, and Mandate takes 1 damage if the claim was pressed publicly.

A claim is strongest when it can be pointed to: an old cairn, a marriage chest, a witness too stubborn to lie, a shrine book that still carries the right seal. If there is nothing to show, the roll is still possible, but the result should feel thinner and more exposed.

### Swear Fealty

A lesser power binds upward. Roll with **Mandate + Accord**.

On success, the lesser power becomes **Vassal** or **Ally** as negotiated. Hostages, marriage, or witnessed oaths can add one asset die. On failure, the negotiation sours, and Feud may advance if either side pushed too hard.

Fealty is easiest to win from the afraid, the isolated, or the recently saved. It is hardest to win from a power that still believes it has another road open. If you want the oath to last, say what was promised upward and what was promised back down.

### Give Safe-Conduct

The faction guarantees passage, convoy, or legal peace along a named route. Roll with **Road + Traffic**.

On success, trade, messengers, or travelers move under the faction's name. A second failure to protect the same route damages Mandate as well as Road.

Safe-conduct is not a speech. It is men at the ford, torches at the ferry, one hanged robber left where the next two can see him, and a merchant who reaches the next bridge with every chest still nailed shut.

### Take Hostage

The faction secures an agreement with a ward, hostage, or important dependent. Roll with **Mandate + Accord** or **Road + Intrigue**. Use Accord when the hostage is given under terms. Use Intrigue when the hostage is seized or manipulated into place. On failure, the act turns uglier than intended, and Standing falls by 1 with the other party even if the hostage is taken.

**Hostages from Chapter 12.** Hostages taken through siege surrender terms, coercive village collection, or occupation rule count as normal hostage assets under this chapter. They carry the same political weight as any other hostage and interact with Feud, Standing, and occupation checks accordingly. Returning them cleanly — after a truce, on good terms at the end of a campaign — can reduce Feud by 1 or improve Standing by 1 where the fiction supports it. Harming them, losing them through negligence, or executing them under duress worsens Feud or Standing by 1 even if the death was not deliberate: the other side makes no distinction between malice and carelessness when the body comes back.

**Hostage lifecycle.** The rules above handle the taking. Keep track of these states during long campaigns:

- **Escape** — the hostage leaves without formal release. Treat as a failed Take Hostage act with all its Standing consequences.
- **Rescue** — an enemy raid, intrigue, or field action frees the hostage. This is an enemy act using Road + Intrigue or a military operation in Chapter 12.
- **Death by neglect or illness** — the hostage dies in custody through no deliberate act. Treat as hostage harm for Standing and Feud purposes unless the holding faction can demonstrate the death was beyond their control and the other side accepts that. They usually do not.
- **Formal return** — the holding faction releases the hostage as part of a truce or political act. Use Send Gift or Bride-Price or Negotiate Truce or Peace to structure the return.

Hostages work because everyone can picture the face in the room where the doors are barred. If that face becomes distant, hidden, or uncertain, the compact weakens. Let messages, visits, tokens, and rumors matter. A hostage no one has seen in months is halfway to a corpse in the minds of the other side.

### Call Council or Moot

The ruler gathers powers, households, priests, or captains and tries to hold the room together long enough to decide something. Roll with **Mandate + Rite** or **Mandate + Decree**.

On success, judgment or agreement stands for now. On failure, factions leave angrier than they arrived, Mandate takes 1 damage, and one local power holder chooses a side openly.

Councils fail more often from pride than from law. When this act goes bad, do not make the failure abstract. Name the man who walked out, the widow who laughed in the wrong place, the priest who refused the cup, or the captain who touched sword-hilt before the room was done talking.

### Judge or Outlaw

The faction declares punishment, blood price, sentence, or exclusion. Roll with **Mandate + Decree**.

On success, the judgment holds. On failure, it looks partial, weak, or unlawful, and if the punished side has kin or backing, Feud advances.

Good judgment is one of the few ways a faction can spend Mandate and still come away richer in it. Bad judgment does the opposite. It teaches every watching household that law is only another name for whichever hand reached the bench first.

### Send Gift or Bride-Price

A faction tries to soften grievance, open alliance, or buy patience. Roll with **Mandate + Accord**. This act always costs Treasury or Stores if it is to matter. On success, Standing improves by 1 or Feud reduces by 1. On failure, the gift was too poor, too late, or read as insult.

### Hire Mercenaries

The faction buys force that is not bound by blood or custom. Roll with **Hearth + Stores** to see whether the faction can actually sustain the terms, then use the hiring rules in **MERCENARIES OF THE FORBIDDEN LANDS**.

A faction with **Thin** or **Bare** Treasury may still hire a band. The question is whether it can keep the band after the first promises are spoken. If exact pay matters, use the daily soldier salaries from **BATTLES & SIEGES** for the force being hired and count the price across seven days. A band held under retainer for a full campaign week will usually step **Treasury** down one rating unless the faction has **Deep** Treasury, a rich charter behind it, or captured pay to spend. On a failed hire-sustain roll, the band serves for `D3 + 1` weeks before demanding harsher terms, fresh plunder, or release.

Mercenaries let a weak faction strike above its blood and land for a little while. They also put iron in the field that has no love for the banner feeding it. If pay is late, if plunder thins, or if a better purse rides in from the next valley, the lesson comes due fast.

### Spy or Suborn

The faction works quietly inside another power. Roll with **Road + Intrigue**.

On success, choose one:

- gain a real fact
- worsen enemy Standing at one place by 1
- force an enemy act to take +1 difficulty next turn
- steal or corrupt one asset die before it is used

On failure, traces are found. The target may advance Feud without needing open proof.

Use intrigue when the pressure must stay deniable. If public humiliation, open threat, or armed seizure would serve just as well, use another act. Intrigue is for the gatekeeper with gambling debts, the ferryman with two sons under another man's roof, the priest who drinks alone after compline.

### Call Levy

The faction turns right, fear, or obligation into bodies. Roll with **Iron + Muster**. The result does not create men from air. It activates the levy the faction truly has available. See **Retainers, Levy, and Mercenaries** below. On failure, the levy arrives late, thin, or unwilling, and Mandate or Iron takes 1 damage depending on why.

When levy is converted to troops for Chapter 12, use Mandate to judge willingness.

| MANDATE | LEVY CONDITION |
| --- | --- |
| **5** | eager or well-backed levy; if the settlement or troops already merit a quality bonus, keep it |
| **3-4** | ordinary levy; no extra change |
| **2** | reluctant levy; remove one quality edge or reduce willingness in the fiction |
| **1** | fraying levy; remove one quality edge and treat morale as brittle from the first hard reverse |

Every extra week of levy service is paid twice: once in the field and once at home. Fences go down. Lambing is missed. Mill work slows. A ruler who keeps levy too long may still win the campaign and come home to poorer villages, harder faces, and fewer sons willing to answer next spring.

### Raid

The faction hits a road, farm belt, granary, ferry, shrine, or outwork without committing to full battle. Roll with **Iron + Assault** or **Road + Watch**. Use Assault for direct violence. Use Watch when the raid is really a sharp interception or border bite. On success, choose one:

- reduce enemy Stores one step
- worsen enemy Standing by 1 in one settlement
- advance Feud by 1 in your favor
- seize immediate goods or labor

On failure, the raid bloodies itself, hits the wrong place, or hardens resistance instead.

### Invest a Place

A faction begins siege, encirclement, or coercive isolation. Roll with **Iron + Siegecraft**. On success, the place is under investment. From the next Campaign Week onward, apply campaign burden and supply pressure. If both sides are actually in position and the action becomes open siege, go to **BATTLES & SIEGES**.

### Negotiate Truce or Peace

A faction tries to stop a feud before it becomes ruinous. Roll with **Mandate + Accord**. Difficulty is the current Feud step. On success, reduce Feud by 1. With 2+ extra successes, reduce it by 2 if real concessions were made. On failure, the enemy reads weakness, stalling, or dishonor.

### Failure Must Bite

A failed political act should never mean only nothing. On failure, choose or roll a real consequence.

- Standing falls.
- A lesser power stalls openly.
- A rival gains ground.
- Feud advances.
- An asset is strained.
- A settlement pays, but grows cold.
- The work succeeds, but at attribute damage after a push.

Politics that never wounds is not politics. It is weather talk.

## Retainers, Levy, and Mercenaries

> _The levy arrived in three shapes. Men with old helmets and good spears from the hall farms. Hunters from the birch ridge with bows and bad boots. And six boys carrying axes that had been wood-tools the day before. The steward counted all of them as men. The captain counted only the first two groups as soldiers._

### Sources of Force

A faction fields force from five places.

1. **Retainers** — permanent sworn fighters.
2. **Levy** — temporary service drawn from settlements or kin groups.
3. **Specialists** — riders, scouts, engineers, healers, shrine men, hunters.
4. **Mercenaries** — hired companies from Chapter 12 of Book 01.
5. **Unnatural or Fanatical Forces** — cult zealots, thralls, undead, monstrous troops.

### What Settlements Can Provide

This chapter decides what a settlement can credibly put in the field. Chapter 12 decides how that force fights once formed.

| SETTLEMENT | WHAT IT CAN PROVIDE |
| --- | --- |
| **Outpost / Hamlet** | guides, scouts, shelter, 1 small watch detail, no formed troop |
| **Village** | 1 short-service levy contingent, or 1 skirmisher contingent if the place lives by hunt or border work |
| **Large Village** | 1 infantry contingent and 1 support contingent, or 1 stronger contingent if well-equipped |
| **Town** | 1 trained infantry contingent, plus 1 skirmisher, support, or mounted contingent if infrastructure exists |
| **Stronghold** | permanent garrison, better troop quality, officers, and the ability to sustain real campaign work |

### Levy Service

Levy is not free. It removes hands from field, flock, ferry, and workshop.

A village levy gives **one campaign week** of ordinary service without further strain if the cause is accepted and the season permits it.

Each additional campaign week requires **Iron + Discipline** or **Mandate + Muster**.

| RESULT | EFFECT |
| --- | --- |
| success | levy remains in field one more week |
| failure | Standing -1 at the supplying settlement |
| pushed success | levy stays, but either Mandate or Hearth takes 1 damage |
| 0 successes on a push | levy deserts, straggles, or turns home in pieces |

A protected or tributary settlement gives shorter leash than a ruled one. A vassal may give longer if the oath is real.

### Retainers

Retainers do not vanish with harvest week. They are the hard core. A faction with **Retainers: Handful** can defend its own seat and enforce judgment nearby. A faction with **Retainers: Company** can hold roads, escort dues, and begin Campaign state without first begging villages for men. A faction with **Strong Garrison** can absorb one bad turn of levy failure without losing Iron immediately.

### Infrastructure and Force Quality

Stronghold functions matter.

| FUNCTION OR HOLDING | WHAT IT IMPROVES |
| --- | --- |
| **Barracks / Quarters** | larger permanent garrison |
| **Training Grounds** | better infantry quality |
| **Shooting Range** | skirmishers, bowmen, crossbowmen |
| **Stables / Pasture** | cavalry, mounted couriers, mounted pursuit |
| **Forge / Mine / Market** | reliable arms, armor, upkeep |
| **Temple / Shrine** | fanatics, oath men, cult levy, holy sanction |
| **Road / Bridge / Ferry / Dock** | concentration speed, supply, messenger work |
| **Walls / Keep / Tower** | stronger garrison posture, siege capacity |

If the holding is not there, do not let the faction field the improved troop as though it were.

### Mercenary Integration

Not all strength comes from obedient subjects.

A faction may:

- hire a band for one task
- retain a band for a season
- station a band as garrison support
- fold a band into a host without owning its Loyalty

Use **MERCENARIES OF THE FORBIDDEN LANDS** to build the band exactly. Use this chapter to decide why the faction hired them and what happens when the purse grows light.

A mercenary band changes politics the moment it appears. Villages that would endure their lord's own men may balk at foreign boots in the yard. Rival powers may read the hire as weakness, ambition, or proof that war is now certain. Even before swords cross, the act of hiring tells the countryside what kind of season is coming.

### Converting to Chapter 12 Troops

When you hand a faction to **BATTLES & SIEGES**, use this logic.

In this chapter, a **contingent** is the political source of men. In Chapter 12, that contingent becomes a **troop** once its exact size and quality are set.

- **Village levy** becomes levy infantry or skirmishers.
- **Large village with Training Grounds** becomes trained infantry.
- **Town with Shooting Range** adds trained skirmishers.
- **Stable or horse-rich land** can add cavalry.
- **Mercenary band** contributes the troop type its own chapter says it truly is.
- **Shrine-raised zealots** may count as fervent troops where appropriate.
- **Undead, cult, or monstrous power** converts according to the creature or troop entries of Chapter 12.

If a faction's **Mandate** is low, reduce levy quality or willingness. If **Iron** is low, reduce numbers or remove reserve. If **Road** is low, have one contingent arrive late or split. If **Hearth** is low, begin the campaign with worse supply or weaker repair. A single contingent is enough to use Chapter 12 if it is facing another formed force, but a faction built on one troop alone cannot sustain long Campaign state without quick reinforcement, easy retreat, or outside support.

This is the handoff point where politics becomes bodies. Do not smooth over what the sheet already told you. A faction that reached war by fear, late dues, and broken roads should feel ragged when the banners finally appear.

## Logistics and Campaign Burden

> _A lord who thinks war is men soon learns it is wagons. A lord who thinks it is wagons soon learns it is grain. A lord who thinks it is grain soon learns it is the villages that grow it, and that villages remember better than armies do._

Before a faction enters open war, answer four questions.

1. **Who feeds this force?**
2. **Who pays it?**
3. **What base does it draw from?**
4. **What road, river, ferry, or escort keeps it supplied?**

If you cannot answer all four, the faction may still march. It simply marches toward trouble already inside itself.

### Base and Supply Line

Every campaigning faction needs:

- a **base** — stronghold, fortified town, major shrine, secured camp, or supply town
- a **line** — road, river, pack trail, ferry chain, or convoy route

Use **Road + Relay** to establish or protect the line. Use **Hearth + Stores** to stock the base well enough to matter. Failure on either roll means the campaign begins already thin.

A weak supply line is not only a military weakness. It invites betrayal. Carters vanish. Ferrymen decide the river is too high tonight. Villages that seemed obedient yesterday suddenly remember old cousins on the other side of the feud.

### Cutting Supply

A faction may target another's line with **Road + Watch** or **Road + Intrigue**.

On success, choose one:

- delay a convoy by `D3` days
- spoil or seize one stores movement, usually `D6 x 10` supply units when quantity matters
- force the enemy to spend its next campaign act restoring the line
- worsen one nearby settlement's Standing with the target by exposing its weakness

Extra successes let you combine two of these results if the fiction allows it.

The target may restore the line with **Road + Relay**. If another faction is actively holding the cut, treat it as an opposed roll. Ties favor the line already in place.

Once armies are truly in the field, use Chapter 12's exact supply rules.

### Disease, Waste, and Hard Country

Poor **Relief** is not a color note. It kills campaigns.

Whenever a faction campaigns in:

- marsh
- plague country
- winter roads
- long siege
- refugee-heavy ground

...and neglects Relief, failure should cost Hearth, Iron, or both. Hard campaigning without Relief means:

- camp sickness
- wounded men lost for want of care
- frightened refugees clogging roads
- rot in food and leather
- burial neglected, fear rising behind the line

Use these losses to show a campaign coming apart from the inside. Men do not need an enemy spear to disappear. They can be swallowed by marsh fever, infected wounds, stolen rations, and the simple fact that no one made room for the living or the dead.

### Occupation

A faction may turn a place from **Ruled**, **Protected**, or **Tributary** into **Occupied** if it cannot or will not govern more gently. Occupation requires:

- men left behind
- regular food and pay
- some answer to resistance

Treat occupation as Campaign state even if no field army is moving. Occupation always creates Hard burden. A bad occupation becomes Crushing fast.

**Occupation modes.** Chapter 12 identifies three immediate ways a place is held after it falls:

- **Oaths and hostages** — selected people are held as insurance. The settlement complies unless the hostages are freed or the oaths break. The first attempt at organized resistance, refusal, or smuggling in the next campaign week fails automatically unless the hostages are already freed.
- **Garrison rule** — soldiers are quartered inside. Compliance is enforced by presence. Works while pay holds and the garrison is large enough to suppress organized resistance.
- **Terror rule** — visible punishment discourages resistance. The settlement does not revolt in the next campaign week, but Standing and burden consequences compound faster.

These modes are not exclusive. A garrison using hostages and making examples of early resistance is using all three.

**Default status after conquest.** Any place taken by assault is **Occupied** by default. A place that surrendered before breach, and whose surrender terms were accepted and kept, may become **Protected** or **Tributary** at the GM's judgment — clean surrender and assault are different enough that the same status need not follow from both. Mercy after an assault changes burden and Standing; it does not change the status from Occupied until a later successful political act formally elevates it.

**Weekly Occupation Check.** Once per campaign week, the occupying faction determines whether the occupation holds. Choose the method in use and make the corresponding roll:

- **Oaths and hostages:** Mandate + Accord
- **Garrison rule:** Iron + Discipline
- **Terror rule:** Iron + Assault

Set difficulty from the settlement's current condition.

| Difficulty | Condition |
| --- | --- |
| 1 | Burden is Light and Standing is neutral or better |
| 2 | Burden is Hard, or Standing is cold |
| 3 | Burden is Crushing, recent sack occurred, or a hostage was mishandled |
| 4 | Pay has failed, garrison is thin, or a rival is actively stirring resistance |

On success, the occupation holds for now.

On failure, choose one:

- Open refusal of dues — no collection this week
- Messenger or smuggling traffic breaks through — outside powers learn the settlement's condition
- Households flee — Hearth takes 1 damage from lost labor
- Sabotage — one stronghold function, road, mill, or ferry is disrupted for 1D3 weeks
- A hostage is lost, escaped, or freed by someone inside the garrison
- The settlement shifts from Occupied to active Feud — suppression now requires a military act, not a political one

**Path of Terror, rank 2** provides one immunity per campaign: the first failed occupation check does not advance the settlement to active Feud. The failure result is still chosen from the list above; only the shift to open Feud is deferred that one time.

Occupation never ends when the gate opens. It ends when people start bringing dues without an armed escort, when the wells are no longer poisoned in rumor, and when the children of the place stop counting which house last burned. Until then, the war is merely wearing a quieter face.

## From Politics to War

> _By the time the first shield wall locked, the road had already been lost twice in council, once in a priest's mouth, and once again in the barn ledger where Ash Mill's grain was crossed out in charcoal and not replaced._

### When Chapter 12 Begins

Move into **BATTLES & SIEGES** when any of the following becomes true.

- two real armies are in position to fight
- a place is fully invested and the next question is wall, gate, or relief
- supply lines and quarter-day army movement matter more than faction acts
- the PCs are acting as generals, champions, or officers inside a battle

Feud at **4: Mustered War** does not always mean Chapter 12 has begun. It means armies are gathered and war is openly intended. If both sides still maneuver, threaten, raid, negotiate, or try to break the other's support before committing, remain in Chapter 11. Move to Chapter 12 only when the matter is about to be decided by battle, siege, or relief in the field.

### What Chapter 11 Exports

Before Chapter 12 begins, know these things.

- Who is the general?
- Which named captains or officers are present?
- What contingents and troop types are fielded?
- What quality are they?
- What base feeds them?
- What line of supply connects base to army?
- Are nearby settlements feeding willingly, grudgingly, or under force?
- Are these troops levy, retainers, mercenaries, zealots, or monsters?

If you know that, the handoff works.

### After the War

When Chapter 12 ends, return here. Battle aftermath should change faction play.

- lost troops reduce levy and retainers
- pillaged villages change status and Standing
- unpaid armies turn into grievance, banditry, or mutiny
- broken roads damage Road
- damaged walls and mills damage Hearth
- a defeated ruler may lose Mandate without losing the seat immediately

**War Reckoning.** After any major battle, siege fall, or failed campaign, run this step before returning to normal faction turns. Apply each item that is true.

| What happened | Faction damage |
| --- | --- |
| A contingent was destroyed or routed | Iron −1 per destroyed major contingent |
| Soldiers were not paid at campaign end | Mandate −1; see When Payment Fails in Chapter 12 |
| Villages were pillaged, sacked, or burned | Hearth −1 per village lost; each becomes Occupied or worse |
| A key road, bridge, or ford was cut or destroyed | Road −1 |
| Walls, mills, granaries, or foundries were damaged | Hearth −1 |
| The campaign ended in public defeat or surrender | Mandate −1; Feud advances by 1 with the victor unless terms were clean |
| Terms were sworn and then broken | Mandate −2; all active negotiations become Demanding |
| The army committed atrocities in occupied territory | Mandate −1; all occupied settlements increase occupation difficulty by 1 |

Then set the status of every settlement that changed hands during the campaign using the default status rule above under Occupation.

A faction that does not reckon is a faction that has not finished the war. The things that did not get counted do not disappear. They appear later, at worse interest.

A campaign is only complete when you account for the ground it passed over.

## Final Use

Build the faction. Mark its seat. Mark its roads. Mark the places that feed it. Mark the places that fear it. Then ask what it wants badly enough to spend Mandate, Iron, Road, or Hearth for.

That answer is the beginning.
