## Encounters

> _Himeon leaned in to poke the fire, casually grabbing his bow in the process._
>
> _“Something watches us and hungers,” he said. Sanarda looked around while drinking from her flask. Hairy spider legs clambered among the darkening foliage overhead. “I wasn’t tired anyway.”_

This chapter details a number of random encounters that you can use during the adventurers’ journeys across the Forbidden Lands. Use the table on the next page by rolling D66 and enter the result in the column indicated by the terrain type of the hex that the adventurers are currently in. Each encounter has a number attached to it. Find the entry with the corresponding number, and resolve the encounter.

Typically, don’t roll when the adventurers enter a hex with an adventure site in it, as they will have plenty to do there anyway!

### Scouting

The adventurers generally have a chance to spot a threat from an encounter before it’s upon them. During journeys, the adventurer who KEEPS WATCH gets to make a SCOUTING roll. Make a straight roll, not an opposed roll unless the threat is actively ambushing the adventurers. Success generally means that the lookout notices the threat while it’s still at a safe distance. The adventurers can then decide whether to show themselves, set up an ambush, or to SNEAK away undetected.

Failing the SCOUTING roll means that the threat is up close and personal before the adventurers notice it.

### Reoccurring Encounters

Sooner or later, you will roll an encounter that you have already rolled before. When this happens, choose one of the options below:

- Continue the encounter.
  - The adventurers meet the same people or creature again.
- Change the encounter. The adventurers meet a similar creature or person, but it’s different in some way.
- Re-roll.

### **0** No Encounter

Time passes without any significant encounter. Describe the surrounding nature – the rustle of leaves in the forest, the howling winds across the plains, the mist in mountain valleys, or the soft rain against the adventurer’s faces. For the moment, they are alone in their journey across the Forbidden Lands.

**TERRAIN TYPES:** All

### **1** the Blood Mist

> _The crickets stop first. Then the small breath of wind dies, and the cold settles into the wool of your cloak as if it had been waiting there all along. You smell iron before you see anything. When you turn, the red is already at the height of your knees, moving against the slope of the ground._

A pocket of living Blood Mist has settled in this hollow. It rises in a slow ring around the party from every direction at once, the way water fills a cupped hand. If the lookout spots it before it closes, each adventurer may make a MOVE roll to climb a tree, scramble onto a boulder, or claw up onto a roof beam — anything that lifts them above the crimson. A failed roll, or no warning at all, leaves the adventurer waist-deep in the Mist. Another MOVE roll each round may carry her clear.

An adventurer inside the Mist must make an INSIGHT roll each round. Failure costs 1 point of Empathy as the longing for hearth and kin floods her. She remembers a face she will not see again. She tastes a meal her mother cooked. If she is Broken, the longing wins; she sits down in the red, folds her hands in her lap, and waits to die. The others must drag her out by the wrists.

When the Mist has fed long enough on the party's grief, a Bloodling forms inside it and attacks (see page 78).

**TERRAIN TYPES:** All

### **2** Duel in the Woods

> _Steel rings, then a bellow, then steel again. The bellow is in orcish. The reply, in elvish, is louder. Through the alders you see them: an orc with a smith's shoulders, a tattooed elf no taller than his hammer arm, both panting, both grinning, both bleeding from the lip._

The elf Heldemin is a wandering sap carver who has spent three winters trying to be allowed near the Roka clan without being killed. The orc smith Alachran is the reason she is still breathing. When she heard him sing over his anvil, she understood that no song carries between their peoples without a body to carry it, and she has talked him into staging a duel-play they mean to perform in both camps before the snow.

They rehearse this far from any road because the orcs would call her a witch and the elves would call him a beast. The blows are real enough to bruise; the cuts are placed where blood will not stop the show. If the adventurers approach openly, Heldemin lowers her sword first and Alachran a beat later. They will trade bread and questions. They will not say where the Roka camp lies. If the party is hostile or careless with words about kin, Alachran's grin closes; the rehearsal becomes a fight he intends to win.

If the adventurers help them — finding a barn, a barrow, any roof where two kins might sit down to a first audience — both will remember it. So will whoever burns the barn down afterward.

**TERRAIN TYPES:** Plains, Forest, Hills

### **3** the Orcish Fugitive

> _A drum first. Then the chant under it: Msshamassh, gukkull, gukkull. Old words for older shame. They come around the bend in no formation at all, but no straggler steps out of the rhythm. Two of them carry a stripped birch pole on their shoulders. From the pole hangs a third orc, wrists and ankles lashed, head down, eyes open._

The trussed orc is Sargah. He left the clan in the spring to find what was on the other side of the rivers. The party is bringing him back to be judged before the clan-fire, and they would rather lose three of their number than the prisoner. There are twice as many of them as adventurers, with stats as on page 64.

Sargah is not gagged. If anyone meets his eye, he speaks the common trade-tongue clearly: he ran because his cousin was promised to him as wife and he would not take her. He says this without expecting to be saved. He says it because he wants someone outside the clan to know.

The escort will trade words but not the prisoner. They have a half-day's road ahead and they are tired. If pressed they will say his crime is desertion, not the wedding. If the adventurers try to free him, the escort fights to the last. If the adventurers let the procession pass, Sargah's clan will hear the tale from him within the week — that strangers stood in the road and chose not to act. The clan will remember both choices.

**TERRAIN TYPES:** Plains, Forest, Dark Forest, Hills, Quagmire, Ruins

### **4** Death from Above

> _A shadow crosses the grass twice before you understand what is making it. The third time it crosses, a sound comes with it — not a hawk's cry, not an eagle's, something with weight in its throat. The grass behind you flattens in one beat of wings. You feel the air pushed against your back._

The adventurers have wandered into the hunting circle of a gryphon. The first pass is a measuring dive — close enough to test their nerve, never close enough to commit. If the party turns and walks back the way they came at a steady pace, the gryphon paces them on the wing and lets them go at the line of its territory. Running breaks the truce; running shows it the back of the neck.

If they hold their ground, draw bows, or push deeper into the hunting circle, the gryphon stoops in earnest and fights to the death. It does not flee from wounds. This is its nest country, and somewhere in the rocks it has young to feed.

Read more about gryphons on page 102.

**TERRAIN TYPES:** Plains, Hills, Mountains, Lake, Ruins

### **5** the Harpies' Feast

> _They argue before they attack. The voices come down from the rocks above, three of them, sharp and overlapping in a tongue that has too many vowels for a throat to hold. The argument is about whose turn it is to eat first. The argument is about you._

The three are Krag, Mag, and Serag, sisters by hatching, starved by a thin season. They have been watching the road since dawn and have decided. They drop together from the high rocks and split the party between them, going first for whoever stands alone or limps. Their combined Strength is 9 (see page 104).

Mid-fight, one of them — usually Mag, who is the greediest — spots silver, a polished buckle, the gleam of a coin pouch through torn cloth. She breaks off and screams the word for treasure. The other two hear her and the fight changes shape. They will turn on each other for a single coin if a coin is thrown far enough. They will follow it down a cliff. They will not all come back up. Adventurers who throw silver may keep it, if they are willing to climb back down for it through the carrion.

**TERRAIN TYPES:** Plains, Hills, Mountains, Lake, Marshlands, Quagmire, Ruins

### **6** the Horse

> _A stallion stands in the road, ears back, reins trailing in the dirt. Saddle still cinched. Saddle blanket dark on one shoulder where blood has dried into the wool. Its mouth works on a bit it cannot spit out. There is no man on it and no man dead near it._

The horse belonged to the noble Lavide, ambushed on this road two mornings ago. The body was dragged off into the brush and stripped, but the horse — combat-trained and skittish in the hands of strangers — broke its tether and has been circling the place since. A successful ANIMAL HANDLING roll quiets it. A failure means it must be cornered and held; it will kick.

In the saddlebags is a VALUABLE find and a rose-shaped sigil of cast bronze. Anyone in the lowlands who knows nobility knows the rose; it is the mark of Lavide's house, and the house keeps a small castle within two days' ride. Returning horse and sigil intact earns ownership of the horse and a PRECIOUS find. Returning the sigil with the horse sold elsewhere — and being later recognized by the steward — is a different conversation.

**TERRAIN TYPES:** Plains, Forest, Ruins

### **7** the Massacre Lure

> _You smell the blood from the bend before you see it. A dozen bodies in the road, some still moving — a woman in chainmail trying to lift a sword that weighs more than her arm now does, a wagon on its side, a young man with clean hands who sees you, drops to his knees, and begs._
>
> _"Robbers! They took the horses. Have you water? Is one of you a healer? Brother, no — brother, stay with me—"_

The scene is staged. The blood is real but it came from a slaughtered ox; the entrails spilling between the "victims" are the ox's, slipping warm against the slavers' necks. None of these people are hurt. The leader, Harwa, lies face-down with a bronze horn under his chest. When he winds it, the wounded rise.

A SCOUTING or INSIGHT roll catches the wrongness — the wagon's traces are cut clean rather than torn, no horse-tracks lead away, the woman in mail is breathing too steadily, and the boy with clean hands has sword-callus on his palm. Anyone who wades in to help with their weapon shouldered is exactly the prey the trap was built for.

When the horn sounds, the slavers swing cudgels — they want bodies whole and selling. A captured adventurer wakes hours later in chains, in the slow line bound for the slave-village of Grindbone (see the _Raven's Purge_ campaign book). Anyone who breaks and runs is not pursued. The slavers tally the catch, lift the wounded ox into the wagon, and roll on before the smell brings wolves.

### **8** the Great Insulter

> _A voice carries from a long way off, and at first you take it for a man arguing with himself. As you draw nearer the words sharpen, and they are not arguments but accusations. Above the road, on a platform of lashed pine no higher than a barn-roof, a small orc stands with his arms wide. He has been shouting at the empty road for hours._

The orc is Khardnal, of the Urhur clan, and he calls himself the Great Provoker. He believes the rage of his kin pushed the Blood Mist back across the rivers, and that the Mist will return the day people forget how to be furious. He has taken it upon himself to test every traveler.

When the party comes within hearing he names them — cleverly enough that he must have been watching the road for half a day. He calls the dwarf a tunnel-rat who left his dead behind. He calls the elf a corpse who has not yet noticed she is dead. He calls the human soft, oath-breaking, Mist-fattened. The point is not insult. The point is to find which of them still carries the rage that holds the Mist back. Whoever answers, in his mind, is worth saving.

If the adventurers walk on without answering, Khardnal climbs down and follows them for a stretch, still shouting, until he tires. If they answer in words, he will sit and trade rage-stories until satisfied. If they answer with steel, he is delighted; he draws axe and shield, calls on his BERSERKER talent, and fights to the death praising whoever cuts him for being in touch with their proper anger.

Khardnal has stats like a typical Urhur orc (see page 64) with the BERSERKER talent.

**TERRAIN TYPES:** Plains, Forest, Quagmire, Ruins

### **10** the Fox

> _A song travels with you for the better part of the morning. A man's voice, low and dry as if from a throat unused to speaking, singing words you do not know about purple flowers and a place where the sky touches the earth like a closing lid. The song stops when you stop. It begins again when you walk. At a turn in the path you find a fox sitting on its haunches in the moss, watching. The song does not start again._

The fox is a demon. It calls itself Grelf, though it has not said so to anyone in a long while. It chose the fox-shape for the same reason it chose the song: to be looked at without being recognized. It is curious about the party — about which of them carries grief, which of them sleeps badly, which of them prays. It will follow them.

Grelf does not speak in fox-form unless directly addressed and then only if it judges the speaker worth the effort. Its voice when it answers is the voice of an old soldier who has watched too much. It does not lie. It does not warn. It will sit just outside the firelight when the party makes camp, and in the morning the fox will be at the edge of the clearing, the same fox, watching.

When Grelf decides it has seen enough, it sheds the fox in one motion and stands. Use the tables for creating a demon on page 80. The grief it has been measuring becomes the door.

**TERRAIN TYPES:** Plains, Forest, Dark Forest, Quagmire

### **11** the Forgotten Prince

> _A cairn of river-stones the size of a barrow, stacked deliberately and never finished. Moss covers the lower courses; the upper stones still show the marks of the hands that placed them, four hundred winters ago. Whoever raised it meant the man underneath to be remembered, then walked away and forgot._

Under the stones lies an Alderlander prince whose name has not been spoken since the Alder Wars. He was buried with his horse and the two knights who fell beside him, with a silver necklace at his throat (worth 2D6 silver coins), an old broadsword at his hand, and a piece of rusty chainmail folded over his chest.

The stones can be lifted in an hour by two strong backs. The grave-goods are exactly what the songs would promise. The prince has waited a long time for company. The moment a hand closes on the silver, he wakes and rises as a Death Knight, and he does not know the war is over.

You can read more about Death Knights on page 86.

**TERRAIN TYPES:** Plains, Forest, Dark Forest, Hills, Mountains, Marshlands, Quagmire, Ruins

### **12** the Hungry Robbers

> _Six men step out of the brush as if they have been waiting since dawn, which they have. Their clothes do not fit them anymore. The big one in front has a black beard split down the middle by an old scar, and his sword is held the way a man holds a sword he is not certain he can lift twice. He spits in the road first, before the words come._
>
> _"Wot manner o' dirt are ya? Wot are ya doin' 'ere? Dontcha know who I am?"_

A band of starving robbers, equal in number to the adventurers, led by Argam Twainbeard. They were farmers two summers back; their lord raised the rents, the rains failed, and the road was the only door open. They have not eaten properly in eleven days. Argam knows it. The thinner ones know it better.

A show of force will be answered with a show of force — they will swing first, even badly, because hungry men have no second move. But food shared, or even offered, changes the air. They will sit at the same fire and trade what they have: a name, a story of the village they came from, the road's recent dead. By morning they may follow at a distance hoping for more, or they may slip off into the trees with the party's oats.

If the party kills them all, two boys hiding in a thornbreak watch it happen. They will tell the story in the next village in their own way.

### **13** the Plague Brother

> _A body in the grass, days dead. The crows have done what crows do, and so have the foxes. What remains is the shape of a man in a brown robe, the cloth darker where it stuck to him as he died. The wind is wrong here. It carries the sweetness of a cellar that has not been opened._

The dead man was a Rust Brother named Largas, who fell out of favor with his master and became the experiment. They infected him with a demonic plague and set him loose to see how far he would run. He ran four days. He died here, on his side, with his hand pressed to his bronze medallion as if it could hear him.

Any adventurer who touches the corpse — to bury it, to search it, to drag it from the road — risks the infection. The plague has weakened to a Virulence rating of 3, but it is no kinder for that. While it runs, the victim sees demons in the faces of her friends. She knows they are her friends. She sees demons anyway. She will sleep badly. She will, in the third night, swing at someone she loves.

On the body is a simple bronze medallion with the Rust sigil. Selling it is dangerous. Wearing it is worse.

**TERRAIN TYPES:** Plains, Forest, Dark Forest, Hills, Mountains, Marshlands, Quagmire, Ruins

### **14** Flagellants

> _The drum first, slow and not in time with anything alive. Then the wet sound, which you do not place at first, of leather striking skin. They come over the rise as a long thin line: men and women in black, their backs already wrong under the cloth, blackberry-thorn ropes wound around their arms and necks. Some have no tongues. Some have no eyes. At the head walk three women in red carrying a pole; what hangs from the pole is iron, and is not a sigil any of you know. Behind them, a wagon with a frame, and on the frame, hooked through the skin, the volunteers._

The procession is led by Mother Catyala. Once she stood high in the Rust Church; now she leads a breakaway sect that holds the Blood Mist was the gods' first warning and its lifting was the second. She preaches that the Forbidden Lands have until the third warning to atone, and that atonement is paid in flesh — not the flesh of others, but one's own. Her people came to her willingly. They believe what she believes.

Mother Catyala will speak gently to the adventurers. She will offer to take their gear and weapons and clothes, to wrap them in thorns, to walk them with the column to the next village, where they may begin. Refusal does not anger her; she has heard refusal before. The danger is the iron symbol on the pole. It carries the ILLUSION spell. An adventurer who looks at it must make an INSIGHT roll. Failure means the adventurer begins, slowly, to see the world as Mother Catyala sees it: every face shadowed by judgment, every untorn skin a debt unpaid. The effect lasts until the symbol is destroyed or out of sight.

The column will not fight to defend itself. It will pray. The villagers in the next valley have been turning them away with rocks; one of these days the rocks will be enough.

**TERRAIN TYPES:** Plains, Hills, Quagmire

### **15** Beggars

> _They are at the road before you see them. Twenty or so people in what was once clothing, the gray of long sleeping outdoors. They do not crowd. They do not speak. They stand on both sides of the path with their hands open at waist height, and they wait._

These are the survivors of Freygrove, a smallholding burned by adventurers three weeks ago over a quarrel no one in the village understood. The houses are ash. The well-rope was cut. They have been walking since.

They ask nothing aloud and refuse nothing offered. Bread, copper, a name to remember the dead by — it goes into the same open hand. Most carry small things: a wooden spoon, a child's shoe, a clay pipe with no tobacco. They do not have it in them to be grateful or angry. If asked what happened, the eldest woman will name the band that did it. Whether the party knows the name, or wears it, will decide whether the road is still walkable on the way back.

**TERRAIN TYPES:** Plains

### **16** the Dwarf Balloon Pilot

> _High in a pine, where no nest belongs, hangs a wooden cage on cables. It is shaped like a large basket and braced with iron, and a counterweight of stones in a netted bag swings beneath it on a fouled rope. The whole contrivance has slipped from its anchor and lodged in the upper branches. Inside the cage, something moves. From the high boughs comes the click of a crossbow being span._

The basket-cradle belongs to the dwarf Andred, who has not set his feet to the ground in eleven years. He was on the safe side of the rockfall when his wife and three children were buried alive in the Hugel galleries. He listened from the other side of the stone as they suffocated, one after the next, and could not dig fast enough. When the silence came, he climbed up out of the mountain and swore on the pickaxe of his line that he would never again stand on the ground that had eaten them.

Since then he has lived in cradles, in tree-blinds, in lashed platforms; he winches between trees with grappling iron and counterweight, and on bad nights he sleeps in his harness. To eat, he sets down on the roofs of cottages by night and takes what is unguarded — a sack of grain, a copper pan, a knife from a windowsill. He trades the spoils at remote crossings for what he cannot steal. The villagers he steals from call him the Hanging Thief and have started leaving traps on their roofs.

A cable has parted, the cradle has crashed into the pine, and Andred has broken his leg below the knee. The bone has not pierced skin but the leg will not bear weight, and he cannot reach his counterweight to descend. He needs help to splint the leg, free the cradle, and rig a new line. He is terrified the party will rob him. He has three loaded crossbows laid out within reach and a battleaxe within arm's length, and he will use the first crossbow before he finishes hearing the offer of help. He has cause to be afraid: the basket holds one SIMPLE and one VALUABLE find of stolen goods (roll on the "in a lair" tables on page 188), and any villager from twenty leagues around would call them theirs.

### **17** Wedding

> _Music carries through the trees long before the clearing opens — a fiddle, a hand-drum, a deep voice singing a marching song with the words changed. In the glade are tables set with bread and small beer, a stage of new pine boards, garlands of bound rowan. At the edge of the clearing, in white dyed with onion-skin yellow, an orc woman and a human man hold each other's wrists and wait for the priest._

The orc Tregga and her human Robert have both fought in wars they will not name. They are done. They have called every kin they still trust to this clearing to witness an oath neither of their peoples wants spoken. The food is honest. The wine is thin. Whoever the party is, they will be invited to the table.

Not every guest came in good faith. Three from each side have agreed, in private, that the wedding is an abomination. They will pick a fight with each other during the vows — staged but loud, blood drawn early — and trust the rest of the guests to choose sides on instinct. They want a brawl that will leave too many wounds for the marriage to survive in memory. If the brawl spreads, two of the saboteurs are ready to put a knife in Robert during the press.

The adventurers can join either side, intervene before the first blow, or do nothing and let the morning sort itself. Tregga will see who stood where. So will Robert's brother, who is one of the saboteurs.

**TERRAIN TYPES:** Forest

### **18** the Demon Baker

> _The wind brings the smell of bread before you see the smoke, and that is wrong, because there are no fields here, no grain road, no one to buy from. The cottage sits low among root-tangled birches, with no path leading in or out. Heavy smoke pushes from the chimney. The two small windows are warm with light. From inside comes the wet, rhythmic slap of dough being worked._

The cottage belongs to Melkar, a stout halfling with a thin red beard. The village of Pernty banished him for theft — a chieftain's diamond he still carries in a wooden chest beside his bed (worth 1 gold). On the road north he found a cluster of black, blood-warm rocks in the bottom of a dry stream and built his oven from them. He did not know the rocks were demon-touched until the first batch of bread came out of the oven breathing.

Melkar will be charm itself. He will sit the adventurers down to tea, to honey-cakes, to a soft white loaf with butter that is the best food they have eaten in a long road. The food is not poisoned. The food is, in fact, exactly what it appears to be. Behind the curtain that divides the cottage, in the cellar trapdoor, in the cold cupboard, in the iron chests under the bed, are the rest of his work: small misshapen things made of dough and rendered fat and the tallow of corpses Melkar has dug up by night. They have stub limbs. They have raisins set into the dough where eyes should be. They twitch. They whisper to each other in a language no halfling speaks. Twenty of them are armed already, with paring knives and iron skewers wedged into their dough hands. They are waiting for him to call.

If the adventurers find one and Melkar sees them find it, he tells them quickly that he has been gifted, that he can make life, that he wants only to live in peace with his children. If they draw steel, he calls every loaf in the cottage. If anyone tries to bake using Melkar's oven, the demon in the rocks will reach for them.

- **DEMONIC LOAVES:** Melkar's tallow-and-dough children are handled as a single creature. Strength equals the number of loaves divided by 5 (round up). They have no skills. Their attacks have Weapon Damage 1 (puncture, knife, or skewer).
- **SECOND ENCOUNTER:** The next time the adventurers pass this way, the cottage is too small for what is in it. ### **30** Teramalda

Guntermann's men prefer payment to fighting; they have done this for a season and have learned the difference between travelers who will weep and pay and travelers who will start counting crossbow bolts. They will not budge below three silver. They will accept gear of equal worth. They will laugh at any threat that is not backed by drawn steel and a believable face. If steel is drawn, they sound the horn and brace behind the sandbags. They have killed eleven travelers this season and lost two of their own; they consider the math acceptable.

### **38** the Infected

> _A small flat-bottomed boat drifts at the edge of the reeds, no oars, no pole. In it a heap of blankets stirs. A hand comes out of the heap, stops, falls back. Then a face. The face is the wrong color. The skin around the eyes is the deep blue of a bruise three days old, and weeping._

The woman in the boat is Linnaya. Her village set her in the boat at first light and pushed it into the current; this is how the lake-villages send the Blue-Plagued away when the Rust priest is too far to come and burn them. She has not eaten in two days. The blisters on her chest and throat have begun to leak; she has lost her voice; she is bright with fever and lucid in flashes. She would like very much to be told that she is going to live. The party will not be able to give her that.

Making contact — boarding, touching, breathing the boat's air — is the risk. The Blue Plague has Virulence rating 5. The Blood Mist did not invent every horror; some of these were here before.

### **39** the Stowaway

> _A white sail crests the headland, and a second behind it. A bulky merchant cog opens around the point under shortened canvas, half a dozen sailors moving easy on her deck. One of them lifts his hat to you across the water and shouts something that sounds like a welcome._

The Moon Apple runs spices, salted meat, and bolts of dyed cloth from the south shore to the north. The crew is bored and the captain is hospitable; Sertold will heave to and let the party come aboard, will trade news for a cup of southern wine, and will, if the party seems decent and they ask, set out two extra plates and a hammock for the night. He has no harm in him.

Down in the rope locker behind the spice barrels lives the goblin Meroggha. Meroggha is on the run from his own elder brother, who is the clan-chief two days east and an unfussy man about loose ends. He has been a stowaway four days, eats what he steals at night, and has marked the party's gear from the moment it came over the rail. When the party leaves the Moon Apple, Meroggha leaves with them — in a pack, under a tarp, in the bottom of a horse-bag. He will not announce himself for another day or two. He will be very grateful when he does. He will also, by the next sundown, be a problem his brother's people are tracking.

### **40** Death from Below

> _The water around the boat begins to break for no wind. Pale green light moves under the hull, slow and wide. By the time you have your hands on something to hold, the first tentacle is over the rail — thicker than a man's thigh, suckers the size of dinner plates — and the second is coming up the other side._

A giant squid has the boat. It will take it under if it can get the leverage, and once under there is nothing the party can do that matters. Half the tentacles — four of the eight — must be cut off in roughly the time it takes to ship two real waves; after that the squid loses interest in this particular meal and slides back into the dark. ### **41** the Trader of Souls

> _A small sailboat rides the still water with ten oil lamps lit along its rails — too many lamps, the kind of vanity a peddler uses to draw the eye on a dark lake. A lyre plays from somewhere amidships. A pennant snaps at the masthead in colors that mean nothing in this country. Three figures aboard. The middle one stands and waves you in._

The trader is Vergevil. The two with him are his sisters Ginda and Aneta (stats as typical soldiers, page 183), neither smiling. The cargo is the usual peddler's clutter — a bolt of thin cloth, a box of horn cups, a small chest of foreign coin — except for one shelf along the cabin wall, where two dozen small green-glass bottles stand corked with wax and tied to the rack with twine. Each bottle has something inside it that moves like smoke; held to the lamp, each gives back the faint sound of a voice or a slow breath.

Vergevil bought the lot from a hill-man who claimed they were trade-goods of the old time, and sells them now as relics of power for ten silver each. He believes his own story half the time. He will trade them for blades, gemstones, or any object older than himself. What he is selling are the souls bound by a sorcerer's BIND SOUL spell (see page 139 in the _Player's Handbook_) some long time ago — he does not know the spell, does not know the cost of breaking it, and does not particularly want to.

If a bottle is uncorked the soul escapes; the bottle is then a bottle. A druid with the RESURRECTION spell (see page 124 in the _Player's Handbook_) can give the freed soul into an animal's body, or set it as a second voice in a sentient mind. Roll D6: on 1–3 the soul keeps its old self and acts with reason; on 4–6 the soul has gone mad in the glass, and acts as madness chooses.

### **42** the Lizard Hunters

> _A click in the mist to your left, sharp and patterned, like a tongue against a back tooth. Then the same click answered from your right. Then from behind. Shapes resolve out of the gray — short, broad-shouldered, gray-green skin pebbled like wet stone, yellow eyes set wide, mouths full of small teeth. Spears held low and easy. They have been watching you for a while._

A hunting band of Saurians, equal in number to the party plus D6, has worked the party up the slope into the open. Their leader is Sheeerz. He stops three spear-lengths off and considers. When he speaks the words are flat and sibilant and the trade-tongue in them is broken from disuse: "Walk-meat. Stop. Sheeerz looks. Sheeerz decides."

Sheeerz lost his clutch-parents to a human hunting party as a hatchling. He has not forgotten. He does not love humans, dwarves, or elves; he tells them apart by smell and the matter of food. Sheeerz's people have not yet decided whether the soft-skinned upright things are meat, threat, or trade. The decision will be made today.

If the party stands quiet and shows open palms, Sheeerz will talk — short hard sentences, long pauses, the spear never lowered. Trade is possible: salt is wanted, iron more, songs not at all. If the party reaches for steel, the Saurians take the slope back into the mist faster than the party can follow and come back at dusk with three times the number.

### **43** Buried Alive

> _Cries from across the rubble — a woman's voice, a child's, then a man saying her name in the flat way a man says it when he has stopped hoping. You come up between two roofless walls into what was once a courtyard. Seven elves stand around the rim of a deep pit, blue feathers braided into their hair and bowstrings loose in their hands. Two of them are working shovels. The pit is half-full already. From the bottom, four faces look up: a mother, a father, two children. They have stopped trying to climb._

The family is the Ylendidra. Six months ago they were taken in for hiding three orcs the Council had sentenced to death; six weeks ago they slipped their guards and ran. The bounty hunter Bluefeather and her six were tasked with bringing them home alive. Two days ago, in a coppice west of here, the mother put a kitchen knife into Bluefeather's shoulder while Bluefeather was reading the writ aloud. Bluefeather has decided not to bring them home.

The shovels are slow. The work will take an hour at the rate it is going. The family knows the rate. They are not crying anymore for help; they are crying because crying is what is left.

Bluefeather will not be talked off the work. She is a professional. She has the writ; the writ is signed; if it is murder the Council will rule it murder and not before. She will accept the party walking past. She will not accept the party intervening. Her six are good archers and the courtyard has clear sight-lines on three sides.
