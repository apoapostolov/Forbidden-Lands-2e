elcome to Xiphos Games Studio's **Towns & Villagers** complement for **Forbidden Lands** . WIn this book you will find elements, tables and references to enrich the Adventure Sites creation method and add more aspects and details to the world.

### Recommendations When Using This Rulebook

As with the Scenario creation rules in the Gamemaster's Guide, this book works best with some advance preparation.

We have decided to create this complement because the original creator for Forbidden Lands seemed a bit basic and lacking in certain details that can greatly enrich the gaming experience.

It's perfectly possible to roll the dice and improvise how the results affect the game world, but everything will work better and more smoothly if you prepare some villages and towns in advance and get them ready for the game session.

We believe that the most important aspects of an Role Playing Game are its characters. For this reason, we will put special effort in a creator that allows obtaining NPCs that are very diverse in appearance, personality and abilities, making life easier for Gamemasters when it comes to giving life and personality to their campaigns in the Forbidden Lands.

Imagine that the players do not want to wait for you to make all your rolls and fill in the necessary gaps. Use these methods within the session as a last resort when a need arises that you couldn't foresee.

In addition to character creation, you can find bonus content for taverns and other important places in a town, as well as many more encounter ideas and adventure seeds.

While there should always be room for improvisation, your villages and towns have never been better defined, at least by random, simple and quick methods.

This is a tool to have on hand whenever adventurers visit a new adventure site. Each character, each location can be a new opportunity to make a game session memorable.

### Locations

Having established the general situation of the town, the next step is to go into its different locations. To do this, you must roll the dice as many times as indicated in the **town size** table.

You can do this process while the characters are moving into the settlement and discovering it, but this can take unnecessary time while the players wait for the result, as well as prevent you from planning a good adventure seed.

For each location, roll the dice and determine both its **size** and its current **state** in the second table on the next page. This will help your creativity when it comes to narrating the atmosphere and the possible problems or virtues of the establishment, and even of the town itself, depending on what structure it is.

The **quality of service** table follows the same line. For locations that do not have people working, you can interpret the result as its qualities from an architectural or urban point of view, or directly its real usefulness due to how it is constituted.

Finally, launch to determine the **profitability** of the localization. This can be interpreted as the amount of iron from a mine, the quality and quantity of water from a well, or the profits from a tavern. However, it can also represent the beauty of a square, in the case of locations that do not have direct profitability.

### Interpret the Rolls

With a random location generation method your main job is to make a creative interpretation of the results. In a linear and basic way, not every result will make sense, but with a little effort very interesting combinations can emerge.

If you cannot find a logic to the internal situation of the place, look for it in external factors: if the tavern does not receive enough maintenance, but has good service and is prosperous: Could it be that the money is going away in some way? Could they be extorting the owners? Will they give much of their profits to a cult? Are they financing someone or something? It smells like adventure!

### Raw Materials

In the Forbidden Lands there are a large amount of raw materials waiting to be exploited by its inhabitants. These resources are a great boost to the economy of any town.

However, some sites are more valuable than others, not all raw materials are present everywhere or in the same amount. To determine the presence of these resources there is a table: **availability of natural resources** .

Roll a D66 for each column to find out if the resource exists in the settlement hexagon and its abundance.

If in this first roll the result corresponds to the first row, the resource does not exist. This is compelling and self-explanatory for the case of stone, iron, silver, and gold. In the case of wood, the trees are not abundant enough or their species is not suitable for the uses required by civilization. In the case of pastures, the land is not fertile or fertile enough, nor can it ever be due to soil or climate conditions.

### Related Locations

Each resource can only be exploited through the right locations and workers. If a resource exists nearby, the town has the corresponding location: crop fields for pasture, a logger's hut for wood, a quarry for stone, or a mine for iron, silver, or gold.

### Resource Exploitation

The natural resources table shows one resource die on each row. That is the abundance of the resource in the town hexagon.

To find out if the resource continues to exist in the same abundance, roll a resource die if it is being exploited. Each roll of 1 reduces the resource die by 1 level. When the resource is on D6 and you roll a 1, the resource is gone.

You must do this every three months if there were locations in operation for at least half of that time, exploiting that resource.

### Recover Resources

While the trees can be depleted and the land rendered unproductive, it is true that they can recover with time and effort.

This is only possible if the initial roll of the resource did not result in 0, that would mean that it is already unrecoverable or that it never existed, for some reason..

So, if the resource of **pasture** or **wood** was exhausted by the exploitation of the Stronghold, it is possible that you return every six months of not using locations that wear them down.

Roll a die: If the result is 5 or 6, return the D6 of the resource die.

Do not forget to make all the rolls described before for each location.

### Resource Proliferation

Both **pastures** and **wood** can proliferate given enough time. If you have no resource depleting locations and the resource is already in the hexagon, roll the resource die one level higher than the current one every six months. If the result is the maximum for the die (6 for D6, 8 for D8, etc), then the resource die increases to the next level.

Of course, the different characters in the Forbidden Lands and various events can cause the proliferation of resources to be assured and automatic. Do not let these rules limit the story you want to tell or the consequences of the actions of the adventurers or other individuals.

### Discover Resources

What would happen if the hexagon had a juicy deposit of silver but no one had yet discovered it? Starting roll after roll of Exploration would be foolish. It is better for the Gamemaster to figure out how to get the knowledge to the characters.

There may be a legend that talks about it, or a worker stumbles upon the source of the resource by chance (or did he already know?). Work on the most interesting opportunity that comes to mind.

### Profession and Skills

In many cases it will be necessary, or at least interesting, to know the character's greatest qualities, their level of experience in different disciplines and their ability to influence the world.

The PCs might have to deal with the random characters spawned from this tool or need some of its services. In order to generate characters that are as unique or characteristic as possible, it is necessary to have tables that allow obtaining interesting results.

That's what the **profession** table is for. Use it when the character's profession isn't obvious from the narrative situation or a specific need for the story.

Each profession has an Attribute column, which indicates a 1 point bonus to that Attribute for the character. This isn't exactly how it works for PCs, but it reflects the advantage of being in that profession for a lifetime.

Also, there are the Skill columns. The so-called **1st skill** is the main one for the professional. To know the points of the character in that Skill you must roll the D66 in the **professional experience** table. With that value determined, assign the **2nd skill** by subtracting 1 point from that original result and the **3rd skill** by subtracting 2 points.

This result also modifies the character's **reputation** roll on the table below.

Nothing stops you from rolling the dice to get the **2nd** and **3rd skill** if you want more randomness. In such cases subtract only 1 from the result of both.

When you need to know another proficiency of the character that is not in these three columns, you can refer to the **non-professional experience** table that is right next to it.

You may have noticed that with this method characters can have up to 6 points in a Skill. This reflects unbelievable levels of proficiency that are sure to be in the Forbidden Lands in dribs and drabs. As with high Attribute values, take the opportunity to highlight these talented individuals.

### Means and Objectives

Finally, it remains to determine the objectives of the character and how he thinks he should fulfill them, what are his methods to achieve it.

Also, what are the resources available to do so? Are you a wealthy person? Where his money comes from and how he manages it is up to you, but it is a good reference to take into account when interpreting his actions and motivations.

In the Forbidden Lands, sooner or later everyone will need to use their Equipment dice. The character's wealth determines what equipment he can afford. Use the Equipment modifier from the table above and apply it to the Equipment results for both weapons and armor.

Unless you need a very specific description, simply having the number of dice will suffice for the character's participation in combat or other type of circumstance where they must use tools.
